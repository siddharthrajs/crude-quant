Big Oil's integrated approach pays off as refining margins skyrocket
Iran war lifts refining margins, spurs volatility
Shell, BP cite importance of integrated approach
Majors eye capital discipline amid uncertainty

FACTBOX: US crude, LPG gain footprint in Asia amid shipping and supply risks
Elevated freight has changed comparative economics
South Korea, Japan sharply boost US crude purchases
US LPG flows to India surge more than fourfold

Western oil and gas majors were carried to yet another bumper quarter in Q3 by soaring refining margins -- even as upstream production continued to suffer from the Middle East conflict -- as their large integrated businesses, spanning the entire oil value chain, continued to pay dividends.
After the US-Israeli war on Iran began in late February, supermajors saw rocketing crude prices fill company coffers. As the war dragged on, denting Middle East production and raising freight costs, those with large trading teams were able to leverage the extreme volatility.
The third quarter was refining's turn. With product flows through the Strait of Hormuz at a minimum -- even as crude flows out started to pick up -- and stocks low across the board, diesel cracks repeatedly hit record highs. Majors in both Europe and the US put their refineries into overdrive.
Posting its earnings last week, BP said its refining margin hit $46.70/b in Q3, up almost threefold year over year, helping lift profits to multi-year highs despite flat upstream production and reduced throughput from its Gelsenkirchen divestment.
Shell saw a similar effect, with a record $42/b refining margin, while TotalEnergies' European margin more than doubled quarter over quarter to $33.20/b.

US crude and LPG flows to Asia are rising sharply as refiners and consumers seek to reduce their exposure to disruptions affecting Middle Eastern production and shipping routes, particularly around the Strait of Hormuz.
While the shift is more pronounced in North Asia, where South Korea and Japan have sharply increased US crude purchases, South Asian buyers are also expanding access to US supplies, with LPG flows witnessing an exponential rise.
However, US barrels are not replacing Middle Eastern crude outright. Instead, refiners are combining traditional Gulf supplies with US, Canadian and Latin American grades to improve supply resilience, manage crude-quality requirements and secure timely deliveries. The willingness to pay a premium for Atlantic Basin cargoes has increased as freight, insurance and replacement costs rise for shipments exposed to disruption.
"Longer voyages, higher freight and different crude qualities all add to cost and complexity, so US supply works best as a complement to Gulf barrels rather than a substitute," said Premasish Das, executive director for oil analytics at S&P Global Energy CERA.
India's oil procurement strategy also illustrates a broader shift. The country has increased both US crude and LPG purchases while building inventories and processing more crude to meet domestic festival demand and international demand for middle distillates.

Chinese refineries halt gasoil exports immediately amid tight supply: sources
Domestic stockpiles hit lowest since 2023
Gas stations in Guangxi stop fuel services
Many Chinese refineries have suspended exports of gasoil to foreign countries with immediate effect to meet domestic demand amid tight supplies, refining sources and traders told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, Nov. 5.
"We were alerted to prioritize domestic supply amid tight supplies; in other words, we have to suspend gasoil exports to other country to avoid domestic shortages deepening," said a south China-based source with a leading gasoil exporting refinery in the state-run sector.
Six refineries that previously exported to foreign countries now all skip the outflow, despite some continuing to supply Hong Kong and Macau, according to sources at the refineries.
China was estimated to export about 800,000 metric tons of gasoil in November, including the barrels sent to Hong Kong and Macau, amid new export quota allocation, Platts reported earlier.
With the suspension, the volume will fall, except for barrels destined for Hong Kong and Macau, while the resumption will depend on the domestic supply level, the refining sources added.
China's gasoil exports reached 1.85 million mt (459,000 barrels/day) in September, a 43-month high, while October outflows were estimated at about 1.1 million mt.
Eight refiners, including two that focus on gasoline exports, and three local oil product traders said domestic gasoil supply is extremely tight.
China's gasoil stock fell about 6% month over month to 10.48 million mt on Oct. 30, the lowest level since Jan. 6, 2023, when local information provider JLC began providing data.
According to a local trader, the wholesale price of gasoil reached 10,800 yuan/mt ($217/b) on Nov. 3, exceeding the retail price ceiling of 10,025 yuan/mt in Guangzhou, the capital city of Guangdong province, suggesting tightness.
The wholesale price fell to 10,500 yuan/mt on Nov. 5 due to a slump in crude oil prices, the trader said, adding that supply remained tight, supporting the price despite stable demand.
A second Guangdong-based refiner said some gas stations in neighboring Guangxi province had suspended refueling services, prompting wholesale traders to come to Guangdong to seek special approval to secure 1,000 mt for shipment back to Guangxi.
"Supplies needed to safeguard people's basic needs should not be diverted to exports for profit," said a Guangxi-based refining source.
Meanwhile, the other clean oil products, including gasoline and jet fuel, can be exported as planned, the sources added.
-- Oceana Zhou - Oil Market Specialist, Daisy Xu - Oil Market Specialist

Hormuz oil shipping unlikely to recover 100% even after peace achieved: NYK CEO
NYK expects Hormuz effectively closed till March 2027
Cape of Good Hope route continues as Suez bypass
ONE gradually increasing number of Red Sea transit: NYK CEO
Oil shipping via the Strait of Hormuz is unlikely to recover 100% even after a peace deal is achieved as oil companies step up their alternative oil procurement, President and CEO Takaya Soga of Japan's Nippon Yusen Kaisha, one of the world's largest ship operators, said Nov. 5.
"Even if peace is achieved with regard to Hormuz, I do not think things will simply return 100% to the way they were before. I believe that the diversification efforts currently underway will inevitably remain in place to some extent," Soga told an earnings press conference.
"With risks of this magnitude having actually materialized, oil companies and others are now working very hard to hedge those risks by developing alternatives and increasingly diversifying their sources of supply," Soga said.
When asked to comment on the situation in the Middle East following the US midterm elections, Soga said, "In reality, there are likely to be a host of issues that will not simply disappear even if peace is achieved."
"For example, there are believed to be a considerable number of mines in the Strait of Hormuz and there is also the question of whether the Iranian government will impose a surcharge on vessels transiting the strait. I think resolving these various issues will take a considerable amount of time," he said.
"Therefore, even if the situation moves toward peace, we do not believe that the conditions necessary to resume transit through the Strait of Hormuz will be put in place immediately. We therefore expect the situation in which vessels refrain from transiting the strait to continue for some time."
Soga's remarks came as NYK Line expects the Strait of Hormuz will effectively be closed until the end of March 2027 as a basis for its income forecast for fiscal year 2026-27 (April-March), Chief Financial Officer Takuji Banno said Nov. 5.
"As for Hormuz, when we announced our previous forecast in August, we explained that it was based on the assumption that the situation would continue through the end of September and that passage through the Strait would become possible thereafter," Banno told the earnings press conference.
"This time, however, we have significantly revised that assumption and recast the forecast on the basis that passage through the Strait of Hormuz will remain impossible through the end of March this fiscal year," Banno added.

bypass the Suez Canal to continue through the end of March 2027, although some container ships have begun gradually resuming transit through the Suez Canal.

TAIWAN DATA: September crude imports rise 17.6% YOY as US, Qatari supply increases
US overtakes Saudi as Taiwan's top crude source
Qatari shipments resume after three-month gap
High freight costs limit February purchase plans
Taiwan's crude oil imports rose 17.6% year over year in September as higher US and Qatari arrivals more than offset steep declines in Saudi Arabian and UAE supply, data from the Ministry of Economic Affairs' Energy Administration released Nov. 2 showed.
Imports totaled 26.427 million barrels, down 1.2% from August's 26.753 million barrels but above 22.464 million barrels a year earlier. On a daily basis, September imports averaged 880,907 b/d, up 2.1% from August's 863,011 b/d, according to the data.
The US became Taiwan's largest crude supplier in September, with arrivals increasing 57.7% month over month and 11.5% year over year to 6.520 million barrels. Its share of total imports rose to 24.7% from 15.5% in August.
Qatar ranked second, supplying 5.876 million barrels, or 22.2% of imports, after no arrivals were recorded from June through August. That compared with 476,072 barrels in September 2025.
Despite the recovery in US arrivals in September, traders

said elevated freight rates were constraining purchases for the current February-arrival cycle.
WTI Midland volumes for February 2027 arrival had thinned as high freight costs kept the arbitrage window closed, Singaporebased crude traders had said Sept. 17. Trade data compiled by Platts also showed relatively slower sales of light sweet US crude to Asia in the new trading cycle compared with previous months.
Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, assessed Dirty USGC-China VLCC 270,000 mt rates at $222.22/mt on Nov. 4, down $11.11/mt day ovet day; Dirty USGC-Singapore Suezmax 130,000 mt was assessed at $200/mt, down $15.38/mt over the same period.
Earlier buying activity during September included CPC's procurement of four Aframax cargoes of WTI Midland crude from Gunvor for December delivery, at premiums of about $27/b to Platts Dated Brent crude assessments, CFR Taiwan, the traders said.
CPC was also heard to have bought two Aframax cargoes of Argentina's Medanito crude for December delivery, one each from PetroChina and TotalEnergies, at premiums in the $22s/b to Platts Dated Brent crude assessments, CFR Taiwan, they added.
Formosa Petrochemical bought 2 million barrels of Oman crude through a tender for December loading and delivery, at a premium of about $5/b to Platts November Dubai crude assessments, on an FOB basis plus freight, market sources had said Oct. 16.
In September's import data, Oman was Taiwan's third-largest supplier, with volumes rising 20.8% month over month and 99% year over year to 3.981 million barrels. Kuwaiti arrivals were broadly steady from August at 3.869 million barrels, up 11.9% year over year.
Saudi Arabian supply fell 57.6% month over month and 60.5% year over year to 2.380 million barrels. UAE arrivals declined 53.2% from August to 1.880 million barrels, while Iraqi volumes dropped 67% to 1.922 million barrels.
Refinery intake fell 4.9% month over month to 3.395 million metric tons of oil equivalent in September, although it rose 9.5% year over year, the data showed.
Over January-September, Taiwan's crude imports declined 4.9% year over year to 216.036 million barrels, while refinery intake fell 6.7% to 29.133 million metric tons of oil equivalent.

BP trims stake in Norway's Aker BP amid portfolio rethink
Sells 5% Aker BP stake for $1.06 billion
Iran war fuels demand for Norwegian oil
CEO O'Neill refocuses on core oil, gas
BP has sold off almost a third of its stake in Norwegian producer Aker BP, it said in a statement Nov. 5, as part of a portfolio simplification process under new CEO Meg O'Neill.
The British supermajor said it had sold a 5% stake in the company - one of Norway's largest oil and gas producers - out of its 15.9% shareholding, leaving it with an interest of approximately 10.9%.
The sale, at a price of NOK 311 per share, has raised NOK 9.83 billion, BP said, or $1.06 billion. The firm has entered into a 90-day lock-up for its remaining shares, it said.
The company, which is undergoing a strategic rethink under O'Neill - who took over in April to right the ship after a largely unsuccessful shift into clean energy - said the proceeds will go towards strengthening its balance sheet.
The company is therefore set to divest around $9 billion in 2026, some $1 billion higher than its previous guidance.
"Since Aker BP's creation a decade ago, it has executed a successful growth strategy that has led to significant value creation," O'Neill said in the statement.
"In recent months, it has further increased its value through a combination of its strong performance and the favorable macro environment. We believe now is the right time for us to realize part of this value to further strengthen BP's balance sheet, our first priority as we build a simpler, stronger, more valuable BP."
The US-Israeli war on Iran has sent crude and refined product prices soaring this year, with Middle Eastern facilities forced to close and commodity flows through the critical Strait of Hormuz severely hampered.
As a result, Norwegian producers such as Aker BP have been able to sell cargoes into Europe at hefty premiums to Dated Brent, which Platts last assessed at $96.16/b on Nov. 4, down from a recent peak of over $135/b in early October. Platts is part of S&P Global Energy.
Aker BP, whose portfolio includes a slew of Norwegian fields and a stake in the 755,000 barrel/day Johan Sverdrup field said it achieved a liquids price in Q3 of $102/b, up from $70/b a year earlier.
The company produced 374,000 b/d of oil equivalent in the quarter, it said last month. It also recently agreed to join forces with rivals Equinor and Var Energi to explore for frontier standalone oil and gas developments on the Norwegian Continental Shelf.
The Aker BP share sale comes just days after BP reported soaring profits in the third quarter and tapped Shell's chemicals head to run its downstream division.
O'Neill has simplified BP's structure into two core divisions, upstream and downstream, and signaled the company would refocus on oil and gas.

Russia's state-run ship classifier wins partial court victory against EU sanctions
Court annuls EU recognition withdrawal ruling
Transaction, port entry bans remain in place
EU has 70 days to appeal court ruling
Russia's state-owned ship classification society has won a partial legal victory against EU sanctions that could make it easier for the organization to resume European activities if sanctions are eventually lifted.
Following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Brussels banned financial transactions with the Russian Maritime Register of Shipping and the entry of ships under its classification into EU ports, while stopping recognition of the validity of its inspections, surveys and certification, among other restrictive measures.
On Nov. 4, the EU Court of Justice annulled the bloc's withdrawal of recognition, ruling the measure was disproportionate because a temporary suspension could have achieved the same objective.
The court said in a statement that following a full withdrawal, RMRS would have had to undergo a new recognition procedure if sanctions were lifted, rather than having its earlier recognition restored.
In a statement Nov. 5, RMRS confirmed the court ruling but added that the EU's provision will remain in effect for now as Brussels has two months and 10 days to appeal against the ruling.
In any case, the ruling would not restore normal European operations for RMRS. The court upheld the bulk of the EU's restrictive measures, including transaction bans. It also ruled inadmissible RMRS's challenge against the port entry ban.
St. Petersburg-based RMRS is Russia's largest ship classification society. Russian crude exports reached 116 million barrels on 142 tankers in October, and RMRS provided classification services to 77 of them, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea and Maritime Risk Intelligence Suite data.

Permian producers raise 2026 oil output guidance on efficiency gains
APA boosts 2026 output target to 124,000 b/d
Permian Resources lifts oil guidance by 7%
Permian Basin oil and gas producers APA Corp. and Permian Resources both increased their full-year oil production guidance as efficiencies boosted output above expectations, according to earnings calls held Nov. 5.
Permian Resources is increasing its full-year oil production midpoint to 202,000 b/d, a 7% increase from February, driven largely by improved operations, company executives said during the company's earnings call.
Permian Resources entered the year in no-growth mode, but then pivoted, William Hickey, the company's Co-CEO and director, said. "Sometime in mid-March, we decided this is a very unique moneymaking environment, and we are now going to project to do over 10% year-over-year organic growth in the same year."
In response to higher oil prices earlier this year, Permian Resources increased production by investing in the shorter-cycle projects in its portfolio, James Walter, Co-CEO and director of the company, said on the call.
It is unclear whether the company will continue in growth mode next year, Hickey said. The company will wait before deciding whether the return environment justifies growth, Hickey said.

The Panama route could improve the economics of US crude. South Korea's customs authorities are working to extend preferential treatment under the South Korea-US free trade agreement to US crude moved through Panama's Petroterminal de Panamá pipeline. The arrangement would support the transfer of crude from Panama's Atlantic coast to its Pacific coast before loading it onto tankers bound for Asia.
The Panama route could reduce the journey from the US Gulf Coast to East Asia from approximately 55 days via the Cape of Good Hope to about 40 days. Estimated VLCC transportation costs could decline from $28.3 million to $24.3 million.
Petroterminal de Panamá's trans-isthmian pipeline is about 130 km long and moves crude between Atlantic and Pacific terminals. Its reported capacity is approximately 10 million barrels per month, providing an alternative to the longer route around southern Africa.
Refiners are adapting their logistics as well as their sourcing strategies. South Korean authorities have simplified documentation requirements for certain US crude shipments transiting neighboring countries, while discussions with Panama could enable wider use of the Pacific route.

The Platts Dated Brent benchmark fell below the $100/b mark on Nov. 4 for the first time in two months as Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, assessed the Dated Brent benchmark at $96.16/b Nov. 4, down $9.035/b from the previous close..
The decline came on a combination of an oversupplied North Sea crude market and a retreating forward market after Democrats took control of both houses of Congress in the US midterm elections.
The benchmark was last assessed below $100/b on Sept. 2 at $98.485/b, Platts data showed.
The Dated Brent physical differential, which reflects the most competitive differential among the grades in the basket for each day of the assessment, also sank $2.13/b on the day to a discount of $2.885/b against the North Sea Dated strip Nov. 4. This was its lowest value since May 2020.
The decline in outright prices tracked a slide in the forward market through the session, with futures prices retreating as market participants assessed the impact of the US midterm elections, which saw Democrats taking control of both houses of Congress.
Platts assessed the front-month January ICE Brent futures contract at $98.53/b at the London close Nov. 4, down $5.07/b or 4.89% from its previous settlement.
"The flat price has already shown the [bearish] sentiment," said a Europe-based North Sea crude trader, commenting on the pricing impact of the elections.
Analysts noted that the election results and diplomatic progress in the Middle East have both led to an unwinding of some geopolitical risk premium in the crude futures market.
"The market increasingly appears to be betting that Trump may seek to put the Middle East conflict behind him following the election setback," said Ole Hansen, Head of Commodity Strategy at Saxo.

Iranian crude imports slump 80% YOY
Iraqi crude leads replacement barrels
China's independent refineries imported 1.16 million metric tons, or 275,000 barrels/day, of Iranian crude in October, the lowest monthly volume since Platts began collecting the data in January 2022, as supplies dried up.
October Iranian crude imports to China dropped 80% year over year and 49% from September, according to data collected by Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, and released Nov. 4.
Only five Iranian crude cargoes were discharged during October, down from 13 in September, the data showed.

The refiners imported a combined 3.12 million mt of crudes from Iraq and the UAE in October, nearly four times the 790,000 mt imported in September, according to Platts data.
Iraqi crude accounted for the bulk of the increase, with imports rising to about 2.31 million mt in October from 520,000 mt in September.
Taking into account the frequent buyers, including the private mega refineries, the independent sector imported 5.33 million mt of crude from the UAE and Iraq in October, rising from 3 million mt in September, Platts data showed.
"I think those Basrah crudes can almost offset the loss of Iranian crudes," said a trader in Shandong.
Basrah Medium crude for December delivery was offered at premiums of about $13-$14/b to ICE Brent crude futures on a DES Shandong basis Nov. 4, broadly stable from a week earlier, according to two trader sources.

Former engineer pushes stricter utility oversight
Targets reduced flaring and orphan wells
Democrat Jon Rosenthal won the Nov. 3 election for the vacant commissioner's seat at the Texas Railroad Commission, defeating Bo French (Republican) and Arthur DiBianca (Libertarian).
The Railroad Commission (RRC), despite its name, regulates oil and gas drilling, pipeline safety, natural gas utilities, surface mining and underground injection control in the country's largest oilproducing state. No Democrat had won an RRC seat since 1995.
The Associated Press called the race for Rosenthal early Nov. 4. Rosenthal led French 52.6% to 45.5%. DiBianca had received 1.9%, with 93% of the vote completed.
Rosenthal is a four-term state representative in the Texas Legislature and a career oilfield mechanical engineer.

For gas utilities, Rosenthal's priorities include stricter prudence reviews on utilities to cover costs and greater transparency in gas utility billing.
For wells, his priorities include reducing flaring, "turbocharging" orphan-well plugging, and raising bonding requirements for new drilling permits. He also promotes job training to support small operators with technical assistance in installing capture and compression, as well as new technologies such as geothermal and carbon dioxide storage.
Rosenthal would like to add community liaisons in every region; upgrade pipeline safety; and require site-specific emergency plans, alarms and public notification for releases of sour gas and hazardous chemicals.
