Indian companies could play a larger role in Venezuela's energy sector as the country's oil production increases, importing more of its heavy crude for processing while exploring new opportunities to invest in upstream projects, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Oct. 20.
Speaking ahead of a meeting with Reliance Industries Ltd.'s executives at the company's Jamnagar refining complex, Rubio said India's extensive refining capacity positions it well to process additional Venezuelan crude as production increases, according to a US State Department statement.
"They have the capability to refine heavy crude. They've done so in the past, and I think as more and more Venezuelan oil comes online, it's something they can do," Rubio said, referring to Indian refiners.
Rubio added that energy cooperation is both an economic and a national security issue. Refining capacity is essential for turning crude into usable energy, while reliable energy supplies are critical to economic stability.

South Korea rejects calls for higher diesel exports, but refiners work to meet global demand
Export volumes remain capped at 2025 levels
Refiners receive strong demand for more spot cargoes
Seoul uses MOPS for domestic price cap policy
The South Korean government is holding firm on ensuring adequate domestic supplies of clean oil products by capping exports at 2025 levels, rejecting the refining sector's proposal to increase overseas sales, although local refiners remain committed to meeting strong global demand as much as possible, according to the trade ministry and industry sources.
South Korea -- Asia's top supplier and net exporter of transportation fuels and middle distillates -- has met its contractual supply obligations to term customers across the Asia-Pacific region so far this year. The government and refiners' trading teams have worked together to secure sufficient feedstock crude through supply diversification and alternative logistical strategies, product managers at major refiners in Seosan, Ulsan and Seoul told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, during market discussions over Oct. 19-21.
However, demand for additional spot supplies of clean oil products from South Korean refiners has surged, particularly for diesel, with the Asian crack spread for the essential middle distillate reaching record highs in the second half of 2026, according to the product managers and the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Resources.
The second-month Singapore gasoil swap crack against Dubai crude swaps reached a record $83.02/barrel on Sept. 16. It averaged $72.97/b in September, compared with $64.71/b in August and the 2025 average of $18.16/b, Platts data showed. The crack spread was last assessed at $66.91/b on Oct. 20.

PetroChina to restart Dalian Petrochemical without Russian crude
Dalian refinery restarts with Mideast crude
200,000 b/d train targets end-Nov startup
Relocation project slows amid restart push
PetroChina's Dalian Petrochemical will restart its refinery in northeast China without Russian ESPO crude, three sources with knowledge of the matter told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, Oct. 21.
Seaborne Middle Eastern crude is expected to arrive in Dalian later this month for the restart, one of the sources said, while pipelined Russian ESPO would not be readily available in the short term.
A second source said crude cargoes from Brazil were under consideration.
The refinery, which was shut in July 2025 under PetroChina's relocation strategy, had previously considered restarting operations earlier this year when deeply discounted Russian crude made processing economics attractive.
Dalian Petrochemical was previously almost fully reliant on ESPO crude, but the pipeline volumes have been redistributed to other PetroChina refineries following the shutdown, Platts reported earlier.
PetroChina has yet to respond to an official request for comment from Platts sent on Oct. 20.

Diesel exports fell 3.4% year over year to 129.82 million barrels in the first eight months of the year.
Among recent spot market activities, GS Caltex offered 300,000 barrels of jet A-1 fuel for loading over Nov. 6-10.
Stable domestic retail prices The export controls are intended to help ensure adequate
domestic supplies and support the government's fuel price ceiling, introduced on March 13 to limit increases in domestic gasoline, diesel and kerosene prices, MOTIR said.
Maximum prices are calculated using average domestic supply costs, changes in Mean of Platts Singapore assessments and applicable taxes. The government will compensate refiners for losses incurred under the program after it ends and will lift the price ceiling and export restrictions once international oil markets stabilize, MOTIR added.
Together with fuel tax reductions, the measures helped bring average retail diesel prices down to 1,843 won/liter ($1.28/liter) in the second week of October, down 8.1% from 2,006 won/liter five months earlier, according to MOTIR. Gasoline prices fell 7.7% to 1,857 won/liter over the same period.

Japan to introduce kerosene import subsidies from Oct. 21 until March 20, 2027
Japan subsidizes kerosene import-wholesale gap
Hokkaido retail kerosene hits record ¥155.7/l
Kerosene imports drop 42.9% YOY
Japan will provide financial subsidies for the difference between kerosene import prices, including surcharge/freight and domestic kerosene wholesale prices, to refiners and oil product importers from Oct. 21, 2026, to March 20, 2027, according to a document distributed by the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry during an Oct. 20 briefing seen by Platts, part of S&P Global Energy.
A METI official confirmed the document on Oct. 21. Refiners and oil product importers are required to keep supplying kerosene by the same amount in the same month of the previous year to receive the subsidies, the document showed. They are also required to apply to METI for subsidies and provide import and domestic wholesale prices. The definition of imports, such as the time of unloading or customs clearance, will be determined later, the official told Platts. METI will calculate a monthly average of import and domestic wholesale prices for the subsidies, the official added. Minister of Economy, Trade and Industry Ryosei Akazawa said on Oct. 16 that the government will set up three new policies to stably supply kerosene amid higher domestic kerosene prices. First, the government will request refiners, importers and wholesalers to supply kerosene cargoes by the same amount as the same month of the previous year to consumers or business partners. The second policy is a scheme to help refiners sell kerosene cargoes directly to consumers, and the third is a subsidy for the difference between import and domestic wholesale prices.

"METI will likely work out details later on a case-by-case basis, while discussing with refiners or oil product importers," a trader familiar with the matter said.
Record high prices The average weekly retail kerosene price in Hokkaido, Japan's
northernmost region, rose 24.5% year over year to ¥155.7/ liter ($0.98/l) on Oct. 19, hitting a record high for the second consecutive week, while the national average was ¥142.9/l, according to METI data published Oct. 21.
"The announcement of the policy package has taken effect in Hokkaido as a rise in the kerosene price was reduced to ¥0.2/l week over week in comparison to ¥3/l for the previous week, though we will keep a close eye on the situation," Takehiko Nagai, Director of Industrial Safety Policy Division in METI, said at a press briefing in Tokyo Oct. 21.
The average assessment price of kerosene rose 46.3% year over year on Oct. 19 to ¥119,500/kl across the Chiba, Kanagawa, Chukyo and Hanshin regions, according to Platts calculations.
The Japanese Consumers' Cooperative Union in Hokkaido issued a statement Oct. 13 asking the national and Hokkaido governments for an emergency policy to tackle the disruption in kerosene distribution and higher kerosene spot market prices, according to the union website.
The statement said record-high wholesale prices raised the regional retail price above ¥190/l, higher than retail gasoline at about ¥170/l, due to a lack of imports, refiners' control of kerosene supply and a restriction of kerosene supply from other prefectures.
Japan's largest refiner ENEOS Corp. has yet to respond to a request for comment, while Idemitsu Kosan Co. Ltd, the second-largest refiner, was immediately unable to be reached for comment.
A spokesperson for Cosmo Oil Co. Ltd., the third-largest refiner, declined to comment on the union's statement.
Kerosene consumption in Hokkaido was 2.19 million kiloliters in fiscal year 2025-26 (April to March), the largest among all 47 prefectures in the country, according to the Petroleum Association of Japan.

UK approves high-profile Jackdaw gas development
Adura says gas supply can start `within weeks'
Court had invalidated earlier approval
Potential new approach with Rosebank decision due
The UK government has approved the Jackdaw offshore natural gas development in the North Sea, it said Oct. 21, allowing one of the country's most contentious energy projects to start up within weeks and signaling a new, more pragmatic approach from Downing Street.
"The OGA [Oil and Gas Authority] granted consent [for Jackdaw] on Oct. 21, 2026. Accordingly, the project may proceed," the government said in a decision document.
The development is operated by Adura, a joint venture between Shell PLC and Norway's Equinor.
"Today's approval clears the way to allow Jackdaw to start supplying gas to British homes, power stations and businesses within weeks," Adura said in a statement Oct. 21.
The operator has said Jackdaw would increase the UK continental shelf's natural gas production by 6% at its peak.
Court challenge Jackdaw had been approved by the prior Conservative UK
government in 2022. However, a court in 2025 found that the initial government consent was invalid because the regulatory approvals process should have taken into account emissions resulting from the consumption of the associated oil and gas.
The 2025 ruling also invalidated the government's consent for another previously approved, Adura-operated project in the UK offshore, the Rosebank oil and gas field, for emissions-calculation reasons. Jackdaw and Rosebank will ultimately provide 10% of UK natural gas supply, according to Adura.
The court in 2025 allowed work to continue on both projects but required resubmission of the environmental impact assessments and forbade starting production until the projects

were re-approved. The UK government is still considering a decision on Rosebank
after Adura submitted further information on the development. The Jackdaw decision was formally taken by new energy
secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh, who was given the key job by Prime Minister Andy Burnham after he took office in July.
"This decision will support the country's energy security with homegrown gas this winter and help minimize the UK's use of imported higher emission fuels as we continue our transition to renewables and nuclear," Fahnbulleh said in a statement on the Jackdaw move. "The decision is compatible with our legally binding climate commitments, and we remain resolute in ending new licenses for oil and gas and achieving net zero by 2050."
The previous incumbent, Ed Miliband, who is now foreign minister, was accused by the oil and gas industry of dragging out the decision. Miliband had been the face of the UK's net-zero ambitions.
Even before the Jackdaw approval, Burnham had signaled a more pragmatic approach to the UK North Sea, which is widely regarded as an economic lifeline for the northeast of Scotland.
Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch welcomed the decision, while accusing the government of dithering, while Zack Polanski, leader of the left-wing Green Party, said it amounted to "climate vandalism."
The approval was also cheered by labor union Unite. "Whilst we of course support net zero, we cannot have a jobless transition, abandon our energy security or offshore our carbon responsibilities. That is why we have argued for this decision on Jackdaw," Sharon Graham, its general secretary, said Oct. 21. "We simply cannot let go of one rope until we have hold of another."
The move to greenlight Jackdaw comes as UK gas prices -- like those across Europe -- have soared to recent highs ahead of winter, amid continued trade disruptions linked to the war in the Middle East.
Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, assessed the month-ahead UK NBP gas price at 85.564/megawatt-hour on Oct. 20. The benchmark is around its highest point since December 2022.
Softer approach Meanwhile, industry players have been closely watching
Jackdaw for signs of a softer approach to oil and gas, after complaining for years about regulatory uncertainty, a challenging tax environment and bans on new exploration. The Energy Profits Levy, which has raised the headline tax rate to 78%, has driven a wave of consolidation and exits from the North Sea by international oil companies.
"Approving Jackdaw is the right decision, but this does not stop the damage being done to the North Sea. And it appears Rosebank remains stuck in political limbo," said an Ineos spokesperson in a statement, calling for the Energy Profits Levy to be amended in the UK chancellor's upcoming budget.

Iran says it opened 'special routes' for Iraqi ships through Hormuz
Iraq says crude exports at 3.4 million b/d in Oct
Iran's Araghchi says negotiations with US ongoing
Crude, product exports remain below pre-war levels
Iran has opened "special routes" for Iraqi ships through the Strait of Hormuz to help Iraq mitigate the cost of the ongoing crisis in the waterway, Iranian foreign minister Abbas Araghchi said in an Oct. 20 interview with Iraqi state news.
In March, Iraq said the country was in talks with Iran to allow some tankers carrying Iraqi crude to pass out of the Persian Gulf through Hormuz.
Iraq's state oil marketer SOMO and the oil ministry declined to comment on the matter Oct. 21.
"Tehran is aware of the extent of the losses and economic burdens that Iraq has incurred as a result of the developments in the strait," Araghchi said. "The Iranian side has made efforts to mitigate these costs ... [including] opening special routes for Iraqi ships to pass through the Strait of Hormuz."
The comments come as a tanker was "struck by an unknown projectile" 30 nautical miles off the coast of Iraq's port city Al Faw on Oct. 20, according to the UK Maritime Trade Operations.
UKMTO said there was no reported damage or environmental impact and advised vessels to transit with caution.
So far in October, Iraq's crude exports have averaged 3.4 million barrels/day through the Strait of Hormuz, according to comments from Iraqi oil minister Bassem Mohammed Khudair carried by state news INA Oct. 20.
S&P Global Commodities at Sea data shows that in the first

10 days of October, Iraq was loading around 3.06 million b/d of crude, but that rate has slowed in the last 10 days to 690,900 b/d. Loadings at ports inside the Persian Gulf have slowed recently as attacks on ships mount. Ship tracking data may also fail to capture the true number of loadings at ports due to high volumes of "dark loadings."
Iraq was hit particularly hard when regional war broke out at the end of February and the Strait of Hormuz was effectively shut. Iraq, heavily reliant on oil revenues to fund its state coffers, had to quickly shut in oil production with export routes blocked and only limited storage options.
Iraq's crude production has since recovered, pumping 3.9 million b/d in September, according to the latest OPEC+ survey from Platts, part of S&P Global Energy. The month before the war, Iraq produced 4.22 million b/d.
The country relies heavily on its southern terminals at Basrah, with a 3.5 million b/d capacity, to export its Basrah Medium and Basrah Heavy crude grades, but the crisis in the Strait of Hormuz saw export levels plummet to just 523,000 b/d in June. Export volumes have recovered since then, climbing to 2.78 million b/d in September, CAS data shows.
The data shows exports of oil products have also been severely affected by the war.
Ongoing negotiations In the interview, Iran's Araghchi said negotiations with the US
to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and end the war were ongoing through Qatari mediators.

KPC in talks with `strategic allies' to move hydrocarbons through pipelines: CEO
KPC pursues pipeline deals with Gulf allies
Kuwaiti oil output reaches 2 million b/d
Gulf crude shows lower carbon than US oil
Kuwait Petroleum Corp. is in talks with "strategic allies" in the Persian Gulf about moving hydrocarbons, including crude oil, through pipelines, and progress is "extremely positive," CEO Shaikh al-Sabah said Oct. 21.
"We're in the engineering phases of some of them," Sabah told the Dow Jones Energy Forum. "We're very serious about moving it through. And even the financing is going to be international efforts."
KPC was in talks with Saudi Arabia and the UAE on pipeline and port options and was looking to increase domestic storage and stocks at its overseas refineries, including in Japan and South Korea, Platts reported earlier this month. Sabah noted at the time that concerns were growing around refined product supply shortages and elevated prices.
KPC estimated that around 6 million b/d of refined products previously produced in the Gulf have left the market. Before the war, Kuwait was Europe's biggest jet fuel supplier and the second-largest diesel supplier.
KPC is also looking for foreign investment in projects, including pipelines and offshore fields, the CEO said.
In July, KPC announced that its wholly-owned Kuwait Oil Co., responsible for crude and pipelines within Kuwait, signed a $16 billion lease and lease-back agreement involving its domestic and export pipeline network for 20.5 years with a consortium of international infrastructure and institutional investors Blackstone, Brookfield and KKR.
That agreement had been agreed to on Feb. 27, one day before the Iran war began, so it was delayed to allow investors to understand the new outlook, Sabah said.
It ended up being Kuwait's largest energy infrastructure partnership at the time and the largest foreign direct investment ever in Kuwait, according to the statement of the deal. It was the first time global investors put capital into Kuwait's midstream

infrastructure, according to the company. The investment was in line with KPC's plans to reach 4 million
barrels of crude production per day by 2035, according to the statement.
A 50% increase in energy demand expected by 2050 cannot be met by renewables and every other source of energy unless crude oil is included, and crudes from the Persian Gulf have lower carbon intensity than crudes in the US, Sabah said at the Oct. 21 forum.
"Any way you look at it, energy demand is increasing," he said. Maybe 75% of energy demand can be met by the other sources, he said. "So then the question becomes whose oil is it going to be." Each barrel of crude from the Gulf is associated with about 5 kilograms of CO2, he said. In the US, by comparison, the carbon intensity of shale is closer to 15 kg to 20 kg per barrel, and deepwater crude is well over 30 kg per barrel, he said.
"We have the lowest cost barrel. We have the lowest carbon intensity barrel. And that's where the pressure, I think, will come in," he said. "It's not just on cost, it's on carbon intensity."
-- Claudia Carpenter

TURKEY DATA: Iraq-Turkey crude pipeline flows up 72% in September
ITP flows average 261,800 b/d in Sept
Ceyhan-Kirikkale flows down up 19% from July
BTC flows up 10% on month
Crude oil flows through the Iraq-Turkey pipeline (ITP) totaled 7.854 million barrels in September, averaging 261,800 b/d, data published by Turkey's state pipeline operator, Botas, showed Oct. 20.
The average daily flow was up 71.9% compared to August, when flows were the lowest recorded since March, when the pipeline was halted for a period during negotiations between the Iraqi government and oil producers in Iraq's Kurdistan region.
Monthly and daily flows for September were the highest reported since flow restarted in September 2025, and were 16.3% up on the previous highest flow of 217,433 b/d reported in November 2025.
The sudden sharp rise follows the agreement between Turkey and Iraq signed Aug. 1, under which operation of the line will continue under the existing ITP inter-governmental operating agreement for a further year, pending the conclusion of an agreement for a new operating agreement, and other agreements on possible cross-border gas and power trading.
Under that agreement, Baghdad undertook to increase ITP flow to about 1 million b/d but did not specify when it expected that level to be reached.
Flow through the line has been limited since restarting in late September 2025, with producers in Iraq's Kurdistan region preferring to sell the bulk of their output to local refiners instead of to Iraq's state oil marketing authority, SOMO, for export.
In addition to volumes from Kurdish producers, which are reaching 1 million b/d, that level will require substantial output from Iraq's northern Kirkuk oil fields, which are estimated to hold about 3 billion barrels of reserves and are being refurbished by BP under a March 2025 contract with Baghdad.
Subsequent agreements saw ConocoPhillips take a 42% stake in the project and Turkey's state upstream operator TPAO 15%.
Turkey's energy minister Alparslan Bayraktar said Oct. 18 that TPAO would begin to receive its share from the country's Kirkuk oilfields in 2027.
The ITP consists of two parallel strings with a total capacity of about 2.4 million b/d. The Iraqi section of the larger pipeline, with a nameplate capacity of 1.5 million b/d, was damaged during the Islamic State insurgency in the 2010s and is undergoing rehabilitation.
Flows from Ceyhan via the Ceyhan-Kirikkale pipeline to Tupras's 108,000 b/d Kirikkale refinery in September were 111,133 b/d, down 4.3% from August and up 27.3% year over year.
The Kirikkale refinery was designed to be supplied with Iraqi crude arriving at Ceyhan via the ITP, the reopening of which had been expected to see the plant return to refining primarily Iraqi crude.

Tupras has not confirmed how much crude arriving at Ceyhan via the ITP is being supplied to Kirikkale following the line's reopening.
According to the latest official Turkish data, in August, Tupras imported 183,438 mt of Iraqi crude via the ITP, down 39.5% from July.
BTC flows rise Flows of Azeri crude to Ceyhan through the Baku-Tbilisi-
Ceyhan pipeline totaled 16.078 million barrels in September, or an average of 535,933 b/d, up 9.9% from August when flow was down 4.6% month over month.
The 1 million b/d BTC pipeline mainly carries crude produced by the BP-operated ACG oil fields together with volumes of Azeri crude from fields operated by Azerbaijan's state oil company Socar, and volumes of crude from operators in Kazakhstan and Turkmenistan.
On Aug. 19, BP announced it had started a planned 11-day maintenance program on the Central Azeri platform, which it said would reduce output from the ACG field during the period.

KPC in talks with `strategic allies' to move hydrocarbons through pipelines: CEO
KPC pursues pipeline deals with Gulf allies
Kuwaiti oil output reaches 2 million b/d
Gulf crude shows lower carbon than US oil
Kuwait Petroleum Corp. is in talks with "strategic allies" in the Persian Gulf about moving hydrocarbons, including crude oil, through pipelines, and progress is "extremely positive," CEO Shaikh al-Sabah said Oct. 21.
"We're in the engineering phases of some of them," Sabah told the Dow Jones Energy Forum. "We're very serious about moving it through. And even the financing is going to be international efforts."
KPC was in talks with Saudi Arabia and the UAE on pipeline and port options and was looking to increase domestic storage and stocks at its overseas refineries, including in Japan and South Korea, Platts reported earlier this month. Sabah noted at the time that concerns were growing around refined product supply shortages and elevated prices.
KPC estimated that around 6 million b/d of refined products previously produced in the Gulf have left the market. Before the war, Kuwait was Europe's biggest jet fuel supplier and the second-largest diesel supplier.
KPC is also looking for foreign investment in projects, including pipelines and offshore fields, the CEO said.

Speaking ahead of a meeting with Reliance Industries Ltd.'s executives at the company's Jamnagar refining complex, Rubio said India's extensive refining capacity positions it well to process additional Venezuelan crude as production increases, according to a US State Department statement.
"They have the capability to refine heavy crude. They've done so in the past, and I think as more and more Venezuelan oil comes online, it's something they can do," Rubio said, referring to Indian refiners.
Rubio added that energy cooperation is both an economic and a national security issue. Refining capacity is essential for turning crude into usable energy, while reliable energy supplies are critical to economic stability.
India imported about 300,000 barrels/day of Venezuelan crude in 2019, but volumes have since declined to about 25 million barrels in 2024. In 2025, five VLCCs -- one per month -- discharged Venezuelan crude at the Port of Sikka, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea data.

FUJAIRAH DATA: Oil product stockpiles rebound as light distillates jump record 72%
Fujairah inventory climbs 6.2% to 7.247 mil barrels Light distillates rebound from record low Heavy distillates rise 12% to two-week high
Oil product stockpiles at the UAE's Fujairah rebounded 6.2% in the week ended Oct. 19, led by a record 72% jump in light distillates such as gasoline and naphtha, according to Fujairah Oil Industry Zone data published Oct. 21.
Total inventory rose to 7.247 million barrels, marking the first gain in four weeks, the data showed. Stocks remain 65% below the Feb. 23 level before the onset of the Iran war, as disruptions to ship traffic through the Strait of Hormuz continue. Fujairah is located on the UAE's east coast, just outside the Strait of Hormuz.
Light distillate stocks recovered to 1.304 million barrels from a record low of 760,000 barrels a week earlier, according to FOIZ data. The inventory increase was the largest recorded in FOIZ data shared with S&P Global Energy since 2017.
Stocks of heavy distillates, used as ship fuel and for power generation, rose 12% to 3.682 million barrels, representing a two-week high, while middle distillate inventories declined 18% to 2.261 million barrels after hitting their highest level since the start of the war a week earlier.
Since the war began, heavy distillate stocks have dropped 53%, while middle distillate inventories have fallen 23% and light distillate stocks have declined 87%.
Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, assessed Fujairahdelivered marine fuel with 0.5% sulfur at $982/metric ton on Oct. 20, the highest since Oct. 13, while Fujairah-delivered highsulfur fuel 380 CST was assessed at $739/mt, the lowest since Oct. 13.
