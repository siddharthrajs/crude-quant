PetroChina diversifies crude supply, shipping amid Middle East disruptions: exec
Middle East disruptions reshape China's crude sourcing
Russian, South American barrels partly fill supply gap
PetroChina uses controlled fleet, pipelines to secure supplies

COP31: UN climate summit opens as energy markets, diplomacy face stress test
Clean power push takes center stage in Antalya
Electrification pace must quadruple to hit 35% by 2035 goal
Carbon funding shortfall threatens Article 6 progress

PetroChina has tapped diversified crude supplies, controlled shipping capacity and its global trading network to maintain oil flows amid Middle East disruptions that have tightened supply and pushed up freight costs, Luo Yizhou, general manager of PetroChina International Co. Ltd., said at the 15th China International Oil and Gas Trade Congress in Shanghai on Nov. 8.
The company's international trading arm accounted for 18.1% of China's crude imports over January-September 2026, making its response significant for crude flows into the world's largest importing market, Luo said.
Middle East tensions this year at one point disrupted more than 12 million barrels/day of crude flows and triggered more than 16 million b/d of oil production cuts within the Gulf, according to figures cited by the executive. Freight rates on some routes surged as much as tenfold.
The impact extended beyond Middle Eastern routes, as increased use of tankers for ship-to-ship transfers in the region reduced available tonnage elsewhere and pushed up freight rates globally.
The disruption has also reshaped China's crude sourcing. The share of China's crude imports from the Middle East fell 12.5% year over year over January-September, while the shares from Russia and South America rose 6% and 5.2%, respectively, according to customs data cited in the speech.

China extends US tariff suspension, keeps LNG at 25%, crude at 20%
24-percentage-point tariff suspension extended for two months
Separate US LNG, crude tariffs remain in place
China monitors potential US Section 301 tariff shift
China will extend for two months the suspension of a 24-percentage-point additional tariff on US-origin goods while retaining a 10% reciprocal tariff, according to a Nov. 9 announcement by China's State Council Tariff Commission.
The measure takes effect at 1:01 pm on Nov. 10 and applies to imports covered by the commission's 2025 Announcement No. 4. China said the extension implements the consensus reached by the US and Chinese leaders in Busan and is intended to provide a relatively stable and predictable policy environment for businesses.
This means US LNG remains subject to a combined 25% additional tariff, comprising the retained 10% reciprocal levy and a separate 15% tariff imposed on US LNG in February 2025.
US crude also remains subject to a combined 20% additional tariff, including the retained 10% levy and a separate 10% crudespecific tariff.
Coal, however, was included in a tariff-reduction framework announced in September, although implementation remained subject to completion of domestic legal procedures, Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, reported Sept. 28.
The rates refer to additional tariffs imposed as part of China's trade countermeasures and do not necessarily represent all duties or charges payable on an import.
The retained 10% additional tariff also continues to apply to other covered US goods, including commercial soybeans. China separately announced tariff relief in September for a range of US agricultural products including corn, wheat, sorghum, soybean oil and meal and animal proteins, while excluding commercial soybeans, Platts reported earlier.
Energy flows remain constrained Regular customs-cleared imports of US LNG into China
effectively halted after Feb. 6, 2025. Individual US-origin cargoes subsequently reached Chinese terminals in July and August 2026, although the trading and logistics arrangements did not necessarily result in the LNG being cleared into China's domestic market.
Chinese buyers holding flexible US FOB supply have instead been able to resell, swap or reload cargoes to manage their exposure to Chinese import tariffs, Platts reported previously. Market participants have also said that demand and cargo economics, rather than tariffs alone, would determine whether direct US LNG imports resume.
US crude flows have also been constrained by the additional

20% tariff. Chinese refiners had gone about nine months without US crude imports before considering a resumption during the Middle East supply crisis in March 2026, Platts reported earlier.
Trade truce extended The latest tariff extension comes after the US and China
agreed to extend their broader trade truce, previously scheduled to expire on Nov. 10, until Jan. 10, 2027, giving the two sides additional time to address outstanding trade measures.
The State Council Tariff Commission said Nov. 9 that China had noted repeated US statements about potentially using Section 301 tariffs to replace tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA), which had been ruled invalid.
China will continue monitoring subsequent US measures and adjust its tariff countermeasures as appropriate, according to the commission.

AKR to buy out BP's stake in Indonesian fuel retail joint venture
Buys BP's 49.9% stake in APR for $14.2M
BP still focused on Tangguh LNG operations
Comes amid BP global portfolio rethink
Indonesia's AKR Corporindo will take full ownership of fuel retailer PT Aneka Petroindo Raya (APR) after two of its subsidiaries agreed to acquire BP's 49.9% stake in the joint venture for $14.2 million, the company said Nov. 9.
In an Indonesia Stock Exchange filing, AKR said its subsidiaries PT Anugerah Krida Retailindo (AKRIDA) and PT Andahanesa Abadi (ADH) had jointly agreed to acquire 11,444,565 shares held by BP Global Investments Limited, representing 49.9% of APR's issued and paid-up capital.
The shares were valued at $14,221,500 under a share purchase agreement signed by the parties.
AKRIDA previously held 50.1% of APR, with BP owning the remaining 49.9%. Upon completion of the transaction, AKRIDA will hold all but one share of APR, while ADH will own the remaining share, giving the AKR group full ownership of the company, it said.
BP and AKR signed a joint venture agreement in April 2017 to develop a fuel retail business in Indonesia. The venture established APR, which operates under BP AKR Fuels Retail, according to the company's website.
BP's divestment is part of its ongoing portfolio reshaping strategy, aimed at supporting growth and improving performance across its business, James Tehubijuluw, BP Indonesia's country head, told Platts Nov. 9. New BP CEO Meg O'Neill, who took over in April, has set out plans to create a simpler, stronger and more valuable company.
"BP has agreed to divest its 49.9% interest in PT Aneka Petroindo Raya (APR) to PT Anugerah Krida Retailindo (AKR). As a result of the transaction, BP will exit the joint venture," Tehubijuluw said.

China's INE crude futures volume drops 46% MOM to four-month low in Oct
Oct traded volume reaches 3.46 million lots
Delivery volume surges tenfold in October
The volume of crude oil futures traded on the Shanghai International Energy Exchange declined 46.15% month over month to 3.46 million lots in October from 6.42 million lots in September, reaching a four-month low, exchange data showed Nov. 6.
The INE crude oil futures contract (SC) is a physical settlement contract priced and traded in yuan, having first been listed in March 2018. One lot is equivalent to 1,000 barrels.
Following suit, the total traded volume for the front-month November SC futures contract was at 606,556 lots in October, decreasing 74.52% from the 2.38 million lots that the then frontmonth October SC futures contract had recorded in September, the exchange data showed.
The INE crude futures contract comprises crudes of mostly medium, sour quality, including grades such as Dubai, Upper Zakum, Murban, Oman, Qatar Marine, Basrah Light, Basrah Medium, Tupi, Buzios, Djeno and Shengli. These are delivered to various storage facilities across coastal China, such as Zhoushan, Dalian and Shandong.
"The overall trading volume was contracting over the month as activity of short-term speculative capital declined," said a source with a futures company.
October also saw fewer trading days in China due to the Golden Week holiday.
Crude throughput at China's state-owned refineries faces further downward pressure in November as high feedstock costs and weakening margins prompt some plants to consider voluntary run cuts on top of scheduled maintenance, Platts reported earlier.

December cash Dubai assessments averaged $110.73/barrel in October, down from an average of $113.711/b for front-month November cash Dubai in September, while the Dubai cash-futures spread -- a measure of the market structure -- averaged $8.10/b in October, down from $21.60/b in September, data from Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, showed.
Meanwhile, total open interest for the futures contract was 63,195 lots Oct. 30, up 1.02% from 62,556 lots at the end of September.
Contrastingly, the open interest for the front-month November SC futures was at 1,295 lots as of Oct. 30, down 34.60% from the front-month October SC futures at 1980 lots Sept. 30.
The crude oil delivery volume in October was at 1,980 lots, or 1,980,000 barrels, a tenfold rise from that delivered over September.

Europe, Middle East and Africa
Operations at Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline unaffected by fresh attack: SPA
Pipeline sustains attack, fire extinguished
Saudi loads 2.8 mil b/d at Yanbu in October
Capacity expansion planned beyond 7 mil b/d
Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline, a key conduit linking eastern fields with the western port of Yanbu, came under attack again on Nov. 9, although there was no impact on operations, state news reported.
In a statement, the Saudi Press Agency quoted an official source at the Saudi energy ministry as saying the 7 million barrel/ day pipeline was "targeted" in the Medina region, "resulting in a limited fire that was extinguished by emergency response teams."
No injuries or fatalities were reported, the official said, adding that "emergency response teams and specialized technical teams responded immediately following the attack and took the necessary measures to secure the site and ensure its safety."
It did not speculate on who was responsible for the attack. Representatives for Saudi Aramco did not immediately respond to a request for comment from Platts Nov. 9. The 1,200 km pipeline -- which is connected to the huge crude processing facility in Abqaiq -- represents an alternative export route for Saudi crude flows in the event of disruptions through the pivotal Strait of Hormuz, which hosted 20 million b/d of crude and products prior to the US-Iran war in February. After war broke out, the kingdom quickly rerouted flows through the East-West Piepline, but it came under attack by drones in early September, Riyadh said, resulting in a days-long shutdown. By Oct. 6, the pipeline was back pumping 5.8 million b/d, according to state media. In an earnings call on Nov. 3, meanwhile, Saudi Aramco CEO Amin Nasser told analysts that the pipeline is undergoing preliminary engineering work for a "major" expansion to take capacity to "much more" than its existing 7 million b/d. The kingdom exported 4.8 million b/d of crude oil in October, 2.8 million b/d of it from Yanbu, according to data from S&P Global Commodities at Sea. Prior to the US-Iran war, which constrained flows through Hormuz, Saudi Arabia exported around 6.8 million b/d, according to CAS. Last week, Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, reported that Saudi Aramco had lowered its December official selling price differentials for its crude grades bound for Asia, Europe, and the Mediterranean, citing a notice from the producer. Aramco set the December OSP differential for its flagship Arab Light bound for Asia at a $7/b discount to the Oman/Dubai average, declining by another $2/b from the previous month, the notice said. Super Light, Extra Light, Medium, and Heavy's December OSP differentials also decreased by $2/b.

COSCO plans 24-VLCC fleet expansion through newbuild orders, leasing deals
Chinese state-run tanker firm orders 10 VLCCs
14 VLCCs to come from six Chinese lessors
Fleet adds dual-fuel ships for emissions cuts
COSCO Shipping Energy Transportation plans to add another 24 Very Large Crude Carriers to its fleet through newbuild orders and bareboat charters, the world's largest tanker operator said on Nov. 9, betting on future growth of crude transportation amid strong market conditions.
In regulatory filings, the Shanghai- and Hong Kong-listed company said that 10 newbuild VLCCs were contracted at two Chinese yards while unveiling plans to lease another 14 from six Chinese banks, all of them due to be delivered by the end of 2030.
"The Group is responding to the trend of growing global crude oil transportation demand by adding scarce VLCC tanker capacity, expanding fleet scale and capturing market opportunities," the Chinese state-backed company said.
The large investment has come as VLCC rates have reached record highs this year, boosted by increased ship requirements as ton-mile demand grows during shipping disruptions at the Strait of Hormuz.
The Platts Global VLCC Index for scrubber-fitted, eco ships jumped from $218,080/day on Feb. 27 to a record $1,195,157/day on Oct. 9 before easing back to $1,044,559/day on Nov. 8, still a historically high level.
Platts is part of S&P Global Energy.
Deals Through a subsidiary, COSCO Shipping Energy has spent
9.482 billion yuan ($1.42 billion) ordering 10 VLCCs from Yangzhou COSCO Shipping Heavy Industry and four from Dalian Shipbuilding Industry Co., according to the filings.
Yangzhou COSCO -- ultimately controlled by China COSCO Shipping, parent of COSCO Shipping Energy -- will deliver four ships between August 2029 and November 2030. The other four ships are due to be delivered by Dalian Shipbuilding between September 2029 and May 2030.
Separately, COSCO Shipping Energy said it plans to bareboat charter 14 newbuild VLCCs from six Chinese financial leasing companies for periods of up to 20 years. Total lease payments will be capped at 17.4 billion yuan, with the tankers expected to be delivered progressively by the end of 2030.
The leasing counterparties are BOCOM Financial Leasing, ICBC Financial Leasing, ABC Financial Leasing, BOC Financial Leasing, CITIC Financial Leasing and Minsheng Financial Leasing. They will provide four, three, three, one, two and one VLCCs, respectively.
"Supplementing VLCC capacity through bareboat leasing can effectively reduce overall financial costs and one-off capital investment," COSCO Shipping Energy said.

Alternative fuels Of the 14 leased VLCCs, 10 will have LNG dual-fuel propulsion,
while the remaining four will be conventionally powered. Following simple retrofits, the six Yangzhou newbuilds will be able to run on LNG, while the four Dalian vessels could be powered by LNG or methanol, according to the company.
The efforts to run on fuels with lower greenhouse gas emissions than conventional, oil-based fuels came as COSCO Shipping Energy "actively respond to the global shipping decarbonization trend, conform with the company's sustainable development strategy and help improve the vessels' competitiveness throughout their full life cycle," the company said.
"Through the addition of VLCC tanker capacity, the Group will optimize the age structure of its international tanker fleet, enhance the fleet's intelligent and green development, and improve fleet operating efficiency," COSCO said.

Czech oil players to replace remaining Russian volumes in state reserves
50% of Czech stocks are Russian crude
CPC Kazakhstan blend selected for storage
Stocks support Litvinov, Kralupy plants
Czech oil refinery, transportation and state storage companies are working together to replace the remaining Russian oil held in the country's strategic stockpiles - which amounts to around half - a spokesperson for its state reserves authority told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, on Nov. 9.
On Nov. 6, Czech oil refiner Orlen Unipetrol, state-owned oil pipeline company Mero, and the State Authority for Management of Strategic Reserves signed a cooperation agreement paving the way for a phase-out of Russian crude from the country's reserves.
"We currently have around half [Russian] oil in reserves for Unipetrol's refineries," Jakub Linka, an SSHR spokesman, said in a telephone interview, with the remainder "a Norwegian oil mix [including] Johan Sverdrup for the Litvinov refinery, and an Azeri mix for the Kralupy refinery."
He added: "The memorandum is a first step in replacing the remaining Russian oil which amounts to half, 45 days out of the European Union's obligatory 90-day oil reserve."
Linka said a further agreement still needs to be signed by the three parties setting out the specific timelines and other details of the oil reserves switch.
Orlen Unipetrol has selected a CPC Kazakhstan-type oil blend as its preferred crude to be kept in the state reserves for possible emergency use, as it can be used by both Czech refineries.
The larger Litvinov refinery is capable of processing oil with a higher sulfur content, while Kralupy uses a lower sulfur mix.

QatarEnergy takes stake in Chevron oil block offshore Namibia
Chevron's stake in PEL 82 falls to 55%
Block sits in Walvis Basin
Namibian officials eye first oil from 2030
QatarEnergy has reached an agreement to acquire 25% of an offshore exploration block in Namibia's Walvis Basin from a subsidiary of Chevron, according to a QatarEnergy statement on Nov. 9, marking its fifth acreage acquisition in the emerging oil producer.
Chevron's operated interest in Petroleum Exploration License 82 will be reduced to 55% as part of the deal, which is subject regulatory approvals, QatarEnergy said. Meanwhile, Custos Energy and state-run Namcor E&P will also each hold a 10% interest, it said.
The agreement covers blocks 2112B and 2212A in Namibia's Walvis Basin, which has in recent years played second fiddle to the Orange Basin, where huge discoveries by TotalEnergies and Shell in 2022 triggered an intense competition for acreage by some of the world's largest oil companies.

BP farmed into a trio of Walvis Basin blocks earlier this year, reflecting increased interest from international oil companies.
"We are pleased to further strengthen our relations with Chevron through this agreement, and to expand and diversify our footprint in Namibia," Qatari energy minister Saad al-Kaabi said in the statement.
The exploration license covers about 11,444 square kilometers in water depths ranging from 350 meters to 2,300 meters, QatarEnergy said. Chevron said previously that it was considering drilling an exploration well on PEL 82 in 2026.
The deal is the latest in a string of Namibia acquisitions by the Gulf national oil company. QatarEnergy already holds stakes in Shell's prolific PEL 39 block, TotalEnergies' PEL 56 -- which contains the huge Venus oil discovery -- and Chevron's PEL 90, where an exploration well is being lined up for late 2026. Norway's Equinor recently farmed into PEL 90 as well.
Namibian officials are hoping to achieve first oil in around 2030 and estimate that the country holds over 11 billion barrels of crude oil.
However, negotiations are still ongoing with TotalEnergies over terms for Venus, which faces technical challenges, from extreme water depth to a high gas-oil ratio, according to the French firm, which is yet to take a final investment decision on the 150,000 barrel/day project.
Long reliant on refined product imports, Namibia hopes to see an economic overhaul once it starts exporting crude. Its oil is understood to be light sweet, meaning it could eventually compete with grades like Nigeria's Bonny Light.

REFINERY NEWS: PDVSA CRP complex rises to 32.5% of capacity
Refineries: Amuay, Cardon, Puerto La Cruz and El Palito, Venezuela
Owner: PDVSA
Capacity: Paraguana Refining Center (Amuay 645,000 b/d; Cardon 310,000 b/d); Puerto La Cruz 187,000 b/d; and El Palito 140,000 b/d
Duration: Ongoing
Venezuela's 955,000 b/d Paraguana Refining Center was operating at 310,000 b/d, or 32.5% of its capacity, as of Nov.9, up from 30% reported on Oct. 29, according to the PDVSA technical report reviewed by Platts, part of S&P Global Energy.
The increase in the processing rate is due to greater availability of crude oil and the execution of maintenance work on units and plants.

Russian crude exports fall on G7 tankers as Greek operators flee for better earnings
Greek tankers exit Russian routes for profits, security
G7 tankers' market share in Russia hits 7-month low
Non-G7 fleet maintains shipments amid relief talk
Russian crude exports on G7 tankers fell sharply in October as Greek operators were enticed by strong earnings elsewhere and steered away from Black Sea conflicts, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea and Maritime Intelligence Risk Suite data.
The data shows tankers flagged, owned or operated by companies based in G7 countries and their allies, or insured by Western protection and indemnity clubs, loaded roughly 906,000

barrels per day of Russian crude in October, or 24.2% of total exports.
The share was the lowest in seven months. In September, G7 tankers lifted 1.2 million b/d, or 29.2% of total exports.
Shipments by tanker operators in Greece, the top shipowning nation within G7/EU, more than halved to 8.3 million barrels in October from 17.3 million barrels in September, according to CAS/ MIRS. October's loadings were the lowest since December 2024.
The development came as tanker rates were around their all-time highs in mainstream markets, boosted by larger ship requirements from offshore transfers east of the Strait of Hormuz and high risk premiums during the ongoing US-Iran war.
October's average Suezmax time-charter-equivalent earnings for transporting Russian crude from the Baltic to West Coast India was $46,257.92 per day lower than the Global Suezmax Index, compared with a discount of $23,225.69/day in September, according to Platts assessments for non-scrubber, non-eco ships. Platts is part of S&P Global Energy.
In a research note published last month, CAS analysts said four Greek-owned Aframaxes and four Suezmaxes had migrated from Russian to mainstream trades where earnings environments were strong.
Greek tanker firms have also been avoiding operations in the Black Sea, with the CAS/MIRS data showing only one Greekoperated tanker lifting Russian crude in the region last month, compared with 11 in other regions.
The International Maritime Organization said more than 100 ships might have been hit in the Black Sea and Sea of Azov this year as part of the Russia-Ukraine war, potentially killing 38 seafarers.
Platts assessed the additional war risk premium for crude exports from the Black Sea at $5/b on Nov. 6, the highest since the assessment began in October 2022.

China's INE crude futures volume drops 46% MOM to four-month low in Oct
Oct traded volume reaches 3.46 million lots
Delivery volume surges tenfold in October
The volume of crude oil futures traded on the Shanghai International Energy Exchange declined 46.15% month over month to 3.46 million lots in October from 6.42 million lots in September, reaching a four-month low, exchange data showed Nov. 6.
The INE crude oil futures contract (SC) is a physical settlement contract priced and traded in yuan, having first been listed in March 2018. One lot is equivalent to 1,000 barrels.
Following suit, the total traded volume for the front-month November SC futures contract was at 606,556 lots in October, decreasing 74.52% from the 2.38 million lots that the then frontmonth October SC futures contract had recorded in September, the exchange data showed.
The INE crude futures contract comprises crudes of mostly medium, sour quality, including grades such as Dubai, Upper Zakum, Murban, Oman, Qatar Marine, Basrah Light, Basrah Medium, Tupi, Buzios, Djeno and Shengli. These are delivered to various storage facilities across coastal China, such as Zhoushan, Dalian and Shandong.
"The overall trading volume was contracting over the month as activity of short-term speculative capital declined," said a source with a futures company.
October also saw fewer trading days in China due to the Golden Week holiday.
Crude throughput at China's state-owned refineries faces further downward pressure in November as high feedstock costs and weakening margins prompt some plants to consider

voluntary run cuts on top of scheduled maintenance, Platts reported earlier.
December cash Dubai assessments averaged $110.73/barrel in October, down from an average of $113.711/b for front-month November cash Dubai in September, while the Dubai cash-futures spread -- a measure of the market structure -- averaged $8.10/b in October, down from $21.60/b in September, data from Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, showed.
Meanwhile, total open interest for the futures contract was 63,195 lots Oct. 30, up 1.02% from 62,556 lots at the end of September.
Contrastingly, the open interest for the front-month November SC futures was at 1,295 lots as of Oct. 30, down 34.60% from the front-month October SC futures at 1980 lots Sept. 30.
The crude oil delivery volume in October was at 1,980 lots, or 1,980,000 barrels, a tenfold rise from that delivered over September.
