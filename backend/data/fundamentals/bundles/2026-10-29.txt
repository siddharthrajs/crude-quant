Europe set to tap 42 mil barrels of oil stocks: French minister
EU ministers coordinate release over three months
France to release 10 mil barrels of diesel 'at cost'
Move follows Iran war, intense US pressure

Record refining margins, strong output lift Shell profits to 4-year high
Third-quarter earnings double on year to $10.81 bil
Refining margins hit record $42/b on Iran war
Depleted inventories to keep prices high: Sawan

European countries will release more than 42 million barrels of strategic oil reserves in the next three months, with 10 million barrels of diesel hitting the French market "at cost" in the next few days, France's economy minister, Roland Lescure, said Oct. 29.
Speaking on arrival at a gathering of EU ministers in Luxembourg, Lescure said officials were pushing ahead with "the liberation of strategic reserves of petrol and diesel," following a G7 decision on Oct. 2 to release 100 million barrels of oil in response to soaring fuel prices stemming from the US-Iran war.
"Less than a month later, Europe has coordinated a very important release of strategic reserves," Lescure said. "We are going to release more than 42 million barrels altogether in the next three months, and half of this is going to be diesel."
Diesel cracks hit record highs this year amid constraints on the export of crude and products through the critical Strait of Hormuz. Lescure said diesel was "already at the heart of our challenges." The Oct. 29 meeting was focused on energy security ahead of the more challenging winter months.
"France is playing its part fully," he said, noting that Paris would release 10 million barrels, all of it diesel, into the French market in "the next couple of days," bringing down the price of diesel at the pump.

Shell plc's indicative refining margin hit a record $42/barrel in the third quarter, while its profits doubled year over year to the highest level since 2022, as strong LNG output, trading and elevated prices buoyed earnings, the company said Oct. 29.
Shell said strong profits reflected "operational performance in a continued volatile and elevated commodity price environment with record refining margins."
Like its rival majors, Shell has seen profits surge in the aftermath of the Middle East war, which choked flows of key commodities exiting the Middle East from late February, sending oil and gas prices and refined product cracks soaring.
Shell said the company's refineries processed 1.21 million barrels/day of crude in the third quarter, down slightly from the second quarter, with refinery utilization of 96%.
"There is a lot more to do," CEO Wael Sawan told an earnings call, noting that the company was leveraging data and AI "more than ever before" in its refineries.
In Shell's core upstream oil and gas division, output fell 22,000 barrels of oil equivalent/day to 1.802 million boe/d in the quarter, due to "production sharing contract effects and field decline," offset partly by new production in the US and Brazil, it said.

South Korea to boost diesel exports amid strong global demand, robust cracks
Seoul raises diesel export cap to 110%
Crack spreads hit record high $83.02/b
Refining capacity 1.1 mil b/d above demand
South Korea will allow refiners to increase diesel exports to alleviate supply shortages in Asian and global markets, as ample domestic supplies give Asia's top middle-distillate supplier greater flexibility to capitalize on robust crack spreads and export margins.
The South Korean government said it will permit additional diesel exports from November, provided that refiners fulfill domestic supply obligations and maintain adequate inventories, according to an Oct. 29 statement from the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Resources.
"Export volumes will be allowed at up to 110% of the corresponding month a year earlier, compared with the existing ceiling of 100%," the trade ministry said.

Strong demand Demand for South Korean kerosene from Japan has
strengthened ahead of winter, while Australian buyers continue to seek additional spot diesel cargoes.
Inquiries for incremental gasoline, jet fuel and diesel supplies from customers and trading companies on the US West Coast and in Indonesia, New Zealand and Europe have also continued, product marketers and sales managers at two refiners based in Ulsan and Seosan told Platts on Oct. 29.

The ministry introduced export restrictions on March 13, alongside a domestic petroleum price cap, to secure local supplies amid concerns over crude import disruptions and heightened geopolitical tensions in the Strait of Hormuz. The restrictions applied to gasoline, diesel and jet fuel/kerosene.
The policy shift reflects stable feedstock crude procurement despite ongoing tanker-flow disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, while the country's major refiners have maintained relatively high run rates to meet essential fuel demand from domestic and AsiaPacific customers, according to the ministry and feedstock and product managers at three refiners based in Incheon, Ulsan and Seosan who spoke to Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, during market discussions Oct. 29.
South Korea has about 3.4 million barrels/day of refining capacity, well above its domestic petroleum demand of about 2.3 million b/d. The surplus capacity supports its role as a major supplier of transportation fuels and middle distillates

South Korean shipments of refined products to top buyer Australia rose 18.4% year over year to 72.97 million barrels over January-September, while exports to Europe reached 15.44 million barrels over the same period, marking record-high shipments for the first three quarters, the latest data from staterun Korea National Oil Corp. issued Oct. 28 showed.
In spot activity, South Korea's GS Caltex sold 300,000 barrels of jet A-1 fuel for loading over Nov. 12-14 at a discount of about $1.30/b to the whole-month November average of the Mean of Platts Singapore jet fuel/kerosene assessments, FOB Yeosu, according to three Asia-based trade sources.
The refiner also sold 300,000 barrels of 50 ppm sulfur gasoil for loading over Nov. 21-25 at a discount of around $5-$6/b to the loading-month average of the MOPS 10 parts per million sulfur gasoil assessments, FOB Yeosu, according to three Asia-based market sources.

Platts assessed the second-month Singapore gasoil swap crack against Dubai crude at a record high of $83.02/b on Sept. 16. The crack spread averaged $72.97/b in September, compared with $64.71/b in August and the 2025 average of $18.16/b, Platts data showed. Platts assessed the crack spread at $59.31/b on Oct. 28.
The FOB Singapore second-month jet fuel/kerosene crack spread against Dubai swaps has averaged $49.96/b so far in 2026, more than double the 2025 average of $17.32/b, according to Platts assessments.

India's crude sourcing decisions are driven by energy security needs and its refiners are under no pressure to reduce purchases from any source, India's foreign ministry spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal told reporters in New Delhi Oct. 28, replying to a question on whether India was cutting Russian crude purchases under pressure from Washington.
"On energy sourcing, we are very clear -- our energy sourcing is done by our companies. "Obviously, it is based on a national interest," Jaiswal said.
Russian crude shipments to India rose sharply in the first half of 2026, increasing from 1.17 million barrels/day in January to a peak of 2.43 million b/d in June, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea data. Flows eased to 2.05 million b/d in July, 1.98 million b/d in August and 1.64 million b/d in September but remained well above January levels, according to CAS data.
"The oil companies take into consideration commercial conditions that are available, market conditions that are there at that point in time, technical specificities, as well as the global situation when they are sourcing or importing energy from abroad," Jaiswal said.
India has not experienced any pressure from the US administration to reduce crude imports from any source, Indian petroleum minister Hardeep Singh Puri said at an industry event in Goa Oct 27, responding to whether the country's refiners were under pressure to cut Russian crude purchases.

New allocation lifts 2026 export quotas to 42.06 million mt
Unlikely to fully use quotas amid tight domestic supply, high costs
December exports expected to top 5 million mt: analysts
China has likely allocated 5.65 million metric tons of clean oil product export quotas in its fourth batch of the year, lifting the 2026 total by 1.9% year over year to 42.19 million mt, five sources with knowledge of the matter told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, on Oct. 29.
In 2025, Beijing allocated 41.41 million mt of quotas for gasoline, gasoil and jet fuel exports, taking into account a 640,000-mt quota swap from fuel oil in early November.
Market sources said quota availability was more than sufficient for the rest of the year, citing the 12.6% year-overyear export reductions in the first three quarters, lower refinery throughput expected in November and December, in addition to declining oil product inventories. Chinese refineries are required to prioritize domestic demand over export economics.
S&P Global Energy CERA analysts estimated that China would need to export at least 2 million barrels/day (around 7.72 million mt) of clean oil products over the final two months of the year to fully utilize the quotas. However, they added that oil product inventories were too low to support such export levels.

Indonesia, UAE discuss oil supply of potentially up to 30 million barrels
UAE proposes crude delivery to Jakarta: minister
SE Asian country imports 1 million barrels daily
B50 biodiesel to cut foreign oil needs by 25%
Indonesia and the UAE are discussing the potential supply of up to 30 million barrels of oil, Energy and Mineral Resources Minister Bahlil Lahadalia said Oct. 28, noting that the talks were preliminary.
"It is still a possibility. Everything is still at the preliminary stage," Lahadalia said. Details on the delivery period were not provided.
Djoko Siswanto, the chief of state-owned oil and gas regulator SKK Migas, said at an industry event Oct. 27 that the UAE had raised the possibility of supplying up to 30 million barrels of oil to Indonesia. Siswanto said the proposal was conveyed by the UAE ambassador during a meeting he attended alongside representatives of Mubadala Investment Co. The meeting also covered the final investment decision for a gas project in Andaman, Aceh.
Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, contacted the UAE embassy in Jakarta for comments Oct. 29, but had yet to receive any confirmation at the time of publishing.
Siswanto said the supply of up to 30 million barrels of crude oil came "without cost" and without requiring working capital, with shipping also to be provided free of charge, state-owned news agency Antara reported Oct. 27. Siswanto did not elaborate on the proposed cooperation mechanism, financing, delivery schedule or precisely which costs would be waived, Antara reported.
Indonesia is seeking to strengthen energy security as the Southeast Asian country is heavily reliant on imported oil and fuels.
Indonesia consumed about 1.6 million barrels/day of fuel, while domestic oil production stood at about 600,000 b/d, leaving about 1 million b/d to be covered by imports, Lahadalia said.
Lahadalia said the planned introduction of B50 biodiesel, comprising a 50% palm oil-based component, could reduce import requirements by about 250,000-300,000 b/d, leaving about 700,000-750,000 b/d to be met through imports.
-- Mia Pei, Anita Nugraha
KSA says ships cited over Hormuz violations not operated by Sinokor
Ships sub-chartered to overseas oil firm: KSA
South Korea imports 62% crude from Middle East
Sinokor Merchant Marine Co. Ltd. does not directly operate the vessels recently identified by Iran as allegedly violating transit rules in the Strait of Hormuz, the Korea Shipowners' Association said Oct. 29 in a statement.

According to information confirmed by the Korea Shipowners' Association with Sinokor officials, the vessels were not owned by Sinokor, the KSA said.
Instead, they had been sub-chartered to an overseas oil company. The ships were also crewed entirely by foreign nationals, with no Korean seafarers on board, it said.
The association added that it could not identify the vessels or the overseas oil company that chartered them.
Sinokor declined to comment directly when approached by Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, on Oct. 29. A company spokesperson referred Platts to the statement issued by the KSA.
Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority had said Oct. 27 in a statement that vessels belonging to the Sinokor shipping company have committed the highest number of passage violations over the past three months, and appropriate enforcement measures have so far been taken against at least seven of them.
"All cargo owners, stakeholders and insurance service providers are hereby warned that as of Nov. 1, legal action will be taken against all vessels of this company," the authority said in a statement on X.
For South Korean shipping and oil companies, the issue is particularly significant because South Korea -- Asia's third largest crude oil buyer -- relies heavily on seaborne imports of various sour crude grades from the Middle East, feedstock managers at major refiners based in Seosan and Ulsan told Platts Oct. 29.
"Any detention threat, regulatory dispute or delay could increase insurance, security and freight costs, even when cargoes continue moving," the Ulsan-based refinery feedstock manager said.
South Korea imported 456.4 million barrels of crude from the Middle East in the first nine months of 2026, making up 62% of the total shipments of 732.8 million barrels over the same period, data from state-run Korea National Oil Corp. issued Oct. 29 showed.
Sinokor Merchant Marine, also known as Janggeum Merchant Marine, is a major South Korean regional shipping and marinelogistics operator. The company provides container liners, oil tankers and related logistics services across a network spanning 16 countries, according to the company's official website.

TotalEnergies captures high margins as refinery throughput rises
Refinery output rises 9% quarter over quarter
European margins more than double to $33.20/b
Satorp facility returns to normal capacity
TotalEnergies SE expects refinery production to stay elevated in the fourth quarter of 2026 and the European refinery margins to remain "quite strong" into the next year, the company's CEO, Patrick Pouyanné, said Oct. 29.
"I think the refining margin in Europe will remain quite good, quite strong during the first half of 2027, because we embark the consequences of prices, low inventories," Pouyanné said during a call with investors. "Beyond, it's very difficult to say what will happen," he said, adding that it is "one of the most complex questions" for the company of what view to take on refining margins for the next year.
TotalEnergies expects its refinery throughput in Q4 to stay roughly unchanged from the third quarter, with the 465,000 barrels/day Satorp in Saudi Arabia refinery returning to normal operations, despite a 20-day turnaround at its Normandy refining and petrochemical platform.
The company processed an average of 1.55 million b/d of crude across its refining network in Q3, a 9% increase quarter over quarter and 5% year over year.
"The increase of nearly 10% refining throughput enabled the company to fully capture historically high refining margins in Europe and worldwide, while oil trading activities outperformed at a level comparable with the two previous quarters," TotalEnergies said.
The quarter-over-quarter increase was primarily driven by higher refinery throughput in France, which rose to 426,000 b/d, up from 354,000 b/d in Q2, although French volumes were still lower than throughput of 481,000 b/d in the same quarter last year.
The main year-over-year increase was driven by a jump in

refinery throughput in the rest of Europe, which rose to 688,000 b/d, up from 595,000 b/d in the third quarter of 2025 and 684,000 b/d in Q2 this year.
TotalEnergies said the quarter-over-quarter increase in refining production was driven by all refineries operating well in Europe and the US, as well as the progressive return of Satorp to its nominal capacity.
France's 220,000 b/d Donges refinery completed its major maintenance July 16.
TotalEnergies' global refinery utilization rate increased to 88% in Q3, up from 80% in Q2 and 84% in Q3 2025. In chemicals, steam cracker utilization was 75%, up 4 percentage points from the previous quarter. In Europe, the company reached an average refining margin of $33.20/barrel, more than double the $13.50/b in the previous quarter and almost four times higher than in 2025.
TotalEnergies' sales of petroleum products were roughly unchanged quarter over quarter, at around 1.22 million b/d, but down from 1.27 million b/d in Q3 2025. The volumes excluded trading and bulk refining sales, the company clarified.
The decrease in product sales reflected "demand elasticity in a high price environment," TotalEnergies said.
The company said its downstream business reached "a record quarter," with adjusted net operating income of $4.1 billion, a 77% rise from Q2.
Despite very high profits, TotalEnergies is not considering expanding its refining capacities, Pouyanné said. The current situation rather encouraged it "to maybe consider maintaining these capacities."
"We will not change our strategy, you know, fundamentally, oil and gas upstream, its integration and maintaining," he said. "Downstream [for us] as a cash flow, and I think today's a super cash flow, so that's good for us."

hydrocarbon production to grow by more than 3% year over year in the fourth quarter, the company said in the quarterly statement.
"The impact of the conflict on production is currently decreasing compared to the third quarter and represents less than 5% of the company's total production, primarily related to limited exports of LNG volumes via the Strait of Hormuz," TotalEnergies said.
TotalEnergies produced 1.4 million barrels of oil equivalent/day of oil, including bitumen, in Q3 out of 2.47 million boe/d of overall hydrocarbon output. "A gradual return-to-normal oil production in the Middle East" drove the quarter-over-quarter increase in output, the company said.

Austria's OMV raises 2026 refining margin forecast by $5/b after strong Q3
Lifts 2026 margin outlook to $25/barrel 2026 refinery utilization expected over 90% Company did not lift any UAE cargoes in Q3

European Commission launches dialogue with European refineries
EC seeks to boost European refinery output Domestic capacity down 10% after slew of closures Industry urged to increase biofuel production

Austria's OMV expects its European refining margin to average $25/barrel for the full year 2026 following an exceptionally strong Q3, up from the previous forecast of $20/b, the company said during its quarterly results presentation Oct. 29.
"I've been in this business 30 years and I've never seen refining margins as high as they were in Q3," CEO Emma Delaney said during a call with investors.
The Middle Eastern conflict, the Russia-Ukraine war, and very low middle distillate inventories contributed to the volatility of refining margins in Q3, according to Delaney, with the volatility expected to continue.
"When we look back to 2022-2023 and the spikes in refining margins at that time, it did take some time to unwind. And in Q4 in particular so far, what we are seeing at the moment, refining margins are bouncing around a lot," she said.
OMV, which operates refineries in Austria, Romania and Germany, expects its facilities' utilization rates to average above 90% for the full year 2026. No major maintenance is expected in the fourth quarter at any of OMV's sites, according to Delaney.
The company said it expects its hydrocarbon production this year to average around 290,000 barrels of oil equivalent per day.

The European Commission has initiated a dialogue with European refiners to discuss short-term responses to the ongoing disruptions caused by the conflict in the Middle East as well as the medium-term outlook for the sectors' competitiveness, it said in a statement Oct. 28.
"With a challenging geopolitical reality and pressing security needs in Europe, we must ensure reliable supplies for our citizens, businesses and defense," the statement said.
The EC also said following the first meeting held with the refining industry Oct. 28 that "we will work to increase outputs from refining assets in Europe," adding that "the industry has made some suggestions that we need to assess."
The EC noted that after two decades of refinery closures in Europe, which have already reduced domestic capacities by around 10%, divestments have accelerated and "this crisis is a wake-up call."
A host of European refineries have shut down over the past years, with closures especially pronounced around the years 2010-2012 and the early 2020s. Refineries have closed across the EU, including in France, Italy, Portugal and Finland, as well as in non-EU countries like Norway and the UK.

The increase would push commodity prices to their secondhighest level since 2013, led by a 32% surge in energy costs. Prices are forecast to retreat 7% in 2027, but that outlook assumes no major escalation in the Middle East, a recovery in Russian oil production, fewer constraints on Black Sea shipments and manageable crop losses from El Niño.
Energy markets have suffered the most severe disruption. The Middle East conflict has delivered what the report called a "historic shock to energy supplies," while attacks on infrastructure and shipping have exposed the vulnerability of export routes through and around the Strait of Hormuz and the Red Sea. Although Gulf crude exports have recovered to near pre-conflict levels, regional flows of refined products remained at less than half their previous level in September.
Global oil production is expected to fall by more than 5% in 2026, more than offsetting increased output outside the Middle East. Brent crude, which reached about $110/barrel in mid-October, is forecast to average $91/b this year before declining to $79/b in 2027. The projections loosely aligned with S&P Global Energy's outlook which forecast Brent crude to achieve an average price of $95.43/b in 2026, before decreasing to $88.40/b in 2027.
The supply shock would have driven prices even higher

without a projected 2.5% decline in oil demand this year -- the third-largest contraction after the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 and the 1980 oil crisis. The market has also relied heavily on inventories, which were drawn down by an average of about 2.4 million barrels/day from March through September.
Food markets face a similar combination of geopolitical and climatic pressures. Food commodity prices are projected to rise 10% in 2026 and a further 7% next year as the war between Russia and Ukraine disrupts Black Sea shipments of grains and oilseeds. Together, the two countries account for about 30% of global wheat exports.
Meanwhile, the 2026-27 El Niño has a high probability of becoming the strongest since comparable records began in 1950. The World Bank warned that severe droughts and floods in unexpected locations could damage production of rice, soybeans, coffee, cocoa, and palm and coconut oils.
Under an adverse agricultural scenario combining prolonged export disruptions with stronger-than-expected El Niño effects, food commodity prices could rise 12% above the baseline forecast in 2027 and surpass their 2022 level. Grain prices could climb 20% above baseline, worsening inflation and food insecurity, particularly in developing economies.
Metals prices are also reaching record highs. The World Bank's metals and minerals index is projected to climb 25% this year, driven by supply constraints and growing demand for aluminum, copper and tin from renewable-energy infrastructure, electric vehicles and data centers. Precious-metal prices are forecast to rise 40%, with gold and silver both on course to set annual records as investors seek protection from geopolitical instability.
Copper prices are forecast to average 33% higher year over year in 2026, underpinned by geopolitical, operational and weatherrelated disruptions. Prices are then expected to decline 2% in 2027 and 4% in 2028 as supply conditions improve modestly.

PetroChina expects oil prices to stay elevated on geopolitics in Q4: executive
Middle East conflicts drive supply concerns
PetroChina targets 8 million mt shale oil annually
Q3 refined product exports up 27% year over year
International oil prices are expected to remain elevated in the fourth quarter of 2026, potentially edging higher from Q3, a company executive said during PetroChina's Q3 briefing conference call on Oct. 29.
CFO Wang Hua said geopolitics remained a key driver of oil prices, with conflicts in the Middle East increasingly becoming more complicated and prolonged. Disruptions to global oil supplies and historically low inventories were also expected to keep oil prices elevated.
The anticipation came despite Q4 being a traditionally seasonally weak period for oil consumption, with demand expected to decline from the previous quarter, according to Wang. Coordinated releases of strategic oil reserves by several countries and growing expectations of further interest rate hikes by the US Federal Reserve could also put pressure on oil prices, he added.

Dated Brent averaged $96.99/b in Q3, down from $103.85/b in Q2, according to data from Platts, part of S&P Global Energy.
In the longer term, Wang said the energy transition is expected to push oil consumption into a plateau. However, concerns over energy security and demand for inventory replenishment would continue to provide support to oil prices.
Against this backdrop, Wang said PetroChina would continue to increase domestic reserves and production, restore output from its Middle East projects and raise production from projects outside the Middle East to ensure stable supplies of lowcost crude.
Vice President Yang Weisheng said the company would continue to step up exploration and development during the 15th Five-Year Plan period (2026-2030), with annual capital expenditure to remain above Yuan 40 billion. Annual shale oil production is targeted to exceed 8 million mt, while the company's total crude oil output would remain above 107 million mt/year, accounting for more than half of China's crude production, Yang said.
In Q3, PetroChina recorded oil and gas equivalent output of 456.2 million barrels, up by 1.3% quarter-over-quarter, according to the company's results.
In the first three quarters, it produced 1.38 billion barrels of oil equivalent of oil and gas, including domestic output of 1.2e billion boe, up by 1.1% year over year, and overseas output of 128.6 million boe, the report showed.
Product exports Wang also said overseas refining margins for oil products
remained relatively high, with gasoil and jet fuel cracks at about $60/b.
The Asia/Singapore jet fuel crack averaged at $64.67/b as of Oct. 29, up from $55.82/b in September and $59.68/b in August, Platts data showed. The monthly average gasoiil cracks in Asia/ Singapore remained hovering at high levels, although retreating to $62.72/b as of Oct. 29 from $63.98/b in September and $67.72/b in August, according to Platts data.
PetroChina will continue to conduct oil product exports in an orderly manner and on market-based principles, in line with government policy and taking into account domestic product supply and sales as well as export economics, Wang said.
Chinese refineries are required to prioritize ensuring domestic supplies rather than the export economy, while they were newly awarded 5.56 million mt quotas for clean oil product exports in the fourth batch quota allocation, Platts reported.
PetroChina exported 13.41 million mt of oil products and bonded bunker fuel in the first nine months of the year, including 5.98 million mt in Q3, up about 27% year over year, according to Wang.
Its crude throughput rose 11.1% quarter over quarter to 346.9 million barrels in Q3, while its refining margin reached about $12/b, up about $3/b from the previous quarter, Wang said.

Indonesia, UAE discuss oil supply of potentially up to 30 million barrels
UAE proposes crude delivery to Jakarta: minister
SE Asian country imports 1 million barrels daily
B50 biodiesel to cut foreign oil needs by 25%
Indonesia and the UAE are discussing the potential supply of up to 30 million barrels of oil, Energy and Mineral Resources Minister Bahlil Lahadalia said Oct. 28, noting that the talks were preliminary.
"It is still a possibility. Everything is still at the preliminary stage," Lahadalia said. Details on the delivery period were not provided.
Djoko Siswanto, the chief of state-owned oil and gas regulator

SKK Migas, said at an industry event Oct. 27 that the UAE had raised the possibility of supplying up to 30 million barrels of oil to Indonesia. Siswanto said the proposal was conveyed by the UAE ambassador during a meeting he attended alongside representatives of Mubadala Investment Co. The meeting also covered the final investment decision for a gas project in Andaman, Aceh.
Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, contacted the UAE embassy in Jakarta for comments Oct. 29, but had yet to receive any confirmation at the time of publishing.
Siswanto said the supply of up to 30 million barrels of crude oil came "without cost" and without requiring working capital, with shipping also to be provided free of charge, state-owned news agency Antara reported Oct. 27. Siswanto did not elaborate on the proposed cooperation mechanism, financing, delivery schedule or precisely which costs would be waived, Antara reported.
Indonesia is seeking to strengthen energy security as the Southeast Asian country is heavily reliant on imported oil and fuels.
Indonesia consumed about 1.6 million barrels/day of fuel, while domestic oil production stood at about 600,000 b/d, leaving about 1 million b/d to be covered by imports, Lahadalia said.
Lahadalia said the planned introduction of B50 biodiesel, comprising a 50% palm oil-based component, could reduce import requirements by about 250,000-300,000 b/d, leaving about 700,000-750,000 b/d to be met through imports.

European countries will release more than 42 million barrels of strategic oil reserves in the next three months, with 10 million barrels of diesel hitting the French market "at cost" in the next few days, France's economy minister, Roland Lescure, said Oct. 29.
Speaking on arrival at a gathering of EU ministers in Luxembourg, Lescure said officials were pushing ahead with "the liberation of strategic reserves of petrol and diesel," following a G7 decision on Oct. 2 to release 100 million barrels of oil in response to soaring fuel prices stemming from the US-Iran war.
"Less than a month later, Europe has coordinated a very important release of strategic reserves," Lescure said. "We are going to release more than 42 million barrels altogether in the next three months, and half of this is going to be diesel."

NNPC lowers most of Nigerian crude oil OSPs for November cargoes
NNPC cuts 34 of 37 crude grades for November
Usan drops $13.46/b, largest decline overall
Bonny Light falls $4.79 to 2-cent discount
Nigerian National Petroleum Co. lowered most of its crude oil official selling prices, also known as NSPs, for November-loading cargoes, according to a copy of the pricing document seen by Platts on Oct. 28.
Of the 37 crude oil grades listed in the document, 34 declined from their October levels. Obe rose by 37 cents/barrel to a $1.07/b premium to benchmark Dated Brent, while Okwuibome Condensate finished up 8 cents/b to a $2.20/b premium.
Usan fell the most across the full suite of Nigerian grades, with its NSP set at a $13.13/b discount, down $13.46 month over month.
Flagship grade Bonny Light saw its NSP fall by $4.79 to a 2-cent/b discount, while Forcados, which is regularly Nigeria's largest export grade, saw its NSP set at a $1.34/b premium.

CHINA DATA: Refiners eye Nov run cuts as crude costs tighten margins
At least five refiners plan Nov run cuts
Sinopec throughput may fall about 5% MOM
Tight product supplies support PetroChina runs
Crude throughput at China's state-owned refineries faces further downward pressure in November as high feedstock costs and weakening margins prompt some plants to consider voluntary run cuts on top of scheduled maintenance, refinery sources told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, Oct. 27-29.
At least five state refiners under Sinopec Group said they planned to lower utilization rates in November to limit refining losses, while PetroChina Company Limited's throughput is expected to be supported by relatively tight domestic gasoline and gasoil supplies and the return of capacity from maintenance.
A Sinopec refinery in South China plans to cut its utilization rate to about 80% in November, from about 93% in October, as high freight and insurance costs worsen processing economics.
Sinopec could cut crude throughput by up to 5% to about 4 million barrels/day in November, according to sources familiar with the matter.
About 820,000 b/d of state-owned refining capacity, mainly at Sinopec plants, will be under scheduled maintenance in November, up from 744,000 b/d in October, Platts data showed.
Utilization rates at Sinochem and China National Offshore Oil Corporation are expected to remain largely stable, while maintenance at PetroChina Guangdong Petrochemical Co Ltd is not scheduled to begin until early December.
Sinopec refiners weigh deeper cuts "All refiners want to cut throughputs, but the obligation to
secure oil product supply in the domestic market has determined the operating rate," a South China refinery source said.
The refinery expects to operate at about 70%-75% of capacity to meet its domestic supply obligations.
Chinese refiners have been facing widespread processing losses amid relatively high crude prices, elevated freight costs and steep crude differentials, while domestic oil product price adjustments have lagged increases in feedstock costs, according to another South China refinery source.
"It would not be surprising to see state-owned refiners voluntarily cut runs under the current economics," the source said.
"Margins are deeply negative. Luckily, we will be undergoing maintenance next month," a state-owned refinery source in East China said. "With freight and insurance costs remaining high, refiners have little incentive to raise runs."
The pressure could intensify in December, as another stateowned refinery source in East China said, "Run rates will probably have to fall further in December as there won't be enough crude available."

State refinery throughput eases in Oct The combined crude throughput of 49 Chinese state-owned
refineries was expected to fall to 8.15 million b/d in October, from 8.39 million b/d in September, Platts data showed.
Throughput stood at 8.13 million b/d in August, 7.77 million b/d in July and 7.75 million b/d in June.
The 49 refineries, with a combined capacity of 10.7 million b/d, comprise the majority of Sinopec and PetroChina's refineries, as well as CNOOC's Huizhou Petrochemical and Sinochem's Quanzhou Petrochemical.
October throughput translates into an average utilization rate of 76.1%, down from a six-month high of 76.4% in September.
Tight supply supports PetroChina's runs PetroChina faces less pressure to cut November runs, with
company sources pointing to relatively tight domestic gasoline and gasoil supplies.
China's gasoil inventories fell about 4% month over month to 10.78 million mt as of Oct. 23, the lowest since Jan. 6, 2023, local information provider JLC data showed.
"From this point of view, refiners should not cut throughputs," a PetroChina refinery source in North China said.
Refiners that rely more heavily on imported crude face greater operational pressure because China's domestic gasoline and gasoil pricing mechanism does not fully reflect changes in crude costs, making it more difficult to pass higher feedstock costs through to consumers, the source said.
PetroChina's average refinery utilization rate recovered to about 84.7% in October from about 82% in September.
Its 200,000 b/d North China Petrochemical refinery, Huabei Petrochemical, is expected to return to typical operations in the second half of October following scheduled maintenance, supporting PetroChina's overall crude throughput in November.
PetroChina's Dalian Petrochemical is also preparing to restart units associated with its 200,000 b/d crude distillation unit, which is expected to come online toward the end of November, refinery sources said.
Independent refiners also cut runs Weak refining margins have also driven smaller independent
refiners in eastern Shandong province to cut utilization toward the end of October, according to JLC. Their weekly utilization rate fell to 50.7% as of Oct. 21 from 53.3% Sept. 30.
Theoretical refining margins deteriorated to a loss of 436 yuan/metric ton as of Oct. 21 from a profit of 694 yuan/mt at the end of September, according to JLC.
"The run rate has been falling the whole month and is likely to fall further in November, maybe slightly below 50% to about 49%," a JLC analyst said.
China's private mega-refiners also trimmed crude runs in October amid higher feedstock costs.
Zhejiang Petroleum & Chemical, Hengli Petrochemical (Dalian), Yulong Petrochemical and Shenghong Petrochemical were expected to process a combined 1.5 million b/d of crude in October, down from 1.52 million b/d in September, the data showed.

Platts to amend freight calculation for Oman, Murban CFR North Asia crude assessments Nov. 2
Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, will amend the freight calculation used in its daily Oman and Murban CFR North Asia crude assessments, effective Nov. 2, 2026.
Under the change, Platts will use the daily VLCC Gulf of Omanto-Far East freight assessment in $/mt (DOOEW00), which was launched Aug. 3, in its calculation of the outright price of Oman and Murban delivered into North Asia at 4:30 pm Singapore time

(0830 GMT). The change will better reflect the shipping economics for the
two grades, with Murban primarily loading from Fujairah port and Oman from Mina Al Fahal port outside the Strait of Hormuz.
Currently, the freight calculation is based on the daily VLCC Persian Gulf-to-Far East freight assessment in $/mt (TDAAB00).
Platts first proposed the change in a subscriber note published Sept. 28, 2026.
The freight calculation will continue to use the conversion factor for metric tons to barrels, as applied to the grades, in line with their prevailing qualities. The conversion factors are currently 7.32 for Oman and 7.62 for Murban.
In line with this change, Platts will also use the daily VLCC Gulf of Oman-to-Far East worldscale freight assessment (DIOEW00) in the freight calculation for Murban in its Indonesian Minas, Cinta and Widuri crude market parity prices.
Under the current MPP methodology for Minas, Cinta and Widuri, Murban -- as one of five basket grades -- is normalized for delivery to Singapore, accounting for freight. This is currently calculated using the daily VLCC Persian Gulf-to-Singapore worldscale freight assessment (PFAOD00).
