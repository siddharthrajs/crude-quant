Equinor lifts 2026 output growth forecast as high prices boost earnings
Company now expects to grow output 4% in 2026
Strong prices see earnings double YOY in Q3
Output to dip in 2027, Rosebank approval expected

Baker Hughes, NOV see service pricing pressure as offshore demand builds
Offshore rig rates approach $500,000/day
Freight costs climb 20% year over year
2027 pricing gains expected to offset inflation

Norway's state-backed energy giant Equinor has raised its 2026 production growth guidance from 3% to 4%, while high commodity prices stemming from the US-Iran war doubled its pre-tax earnings year over year in the third quarter, it said in results posted Oct. 28.
Equinor said its oil and gas production was up by 2% year over year in Q3 to 2.166 million barrels of oil equivalent/day, split roughly evenly between oil and gas. That compares to 2.13 million boe/d in the same quarter of 2025.
"High production in Q3 enabled us to capture additional value from higher prices. Based on safe and efficient operations, we are increasing our 2026 guidance for oil and gas production growth from 3% to around 4%," Anders Opedal, Equinor's CEO, said in a statement.
The Q3 increase was driven by a 21% production hike in Equinor's international business to 323,000 boe/d, fueled by additional production from its Adura joint venture with Shell in the UK and the Bacalhau asset in Brazil, as well as reduced turnaround activity, Equinor said. Bacalhau is now producing at its 220,000 b/d plateau.
Meanwhile, production was stable across Equinor's much larger Norwegian Continental Shelf portfolio, with new fields like Halten East and Verdande and new wells elsewhere offsetting natural declines and outages at Johan Castberg and Troll. New fields added 70,000 boe/d in the quarter, the company said.

Baker Hughes Co. and NOV Inc. executives Oct. 28 separately pointed to the prospect of higher oilfield service and equipment prices in 2027 as international and offshore investment strengthens, even as Middle East disruptions raise logistics costs and constrain near-term activity.
The executives, on their companies' third-quarter earnings calls, described producers continuing to prioritize recovery and efficiency over a broad-based expansion of drilling.
Baker Hughes expects global upstream spending to rise at a low- to mid-single-digit rate in 2027, led by international and offshore markets, after broadly flat spending this year. NOV said limited spare capacity in high-specification equipment could tighten markets even with a modest increase in operator activity.
"Pricing is the next leg," NOV CEO Jose Bayardo said during this company's call, adding that service companies and drilling contractors would need better pricing before committing more capital to their fleets. Bayardo said he would be surprised not to see a material improvement in 2027, although the timing remained uncertain.
Baker Hughes CFO Ahmed Moghal similarly said product and service pricing would help offset inflation in its Oilfield Services and Equipment business next year. Neither company quantified a broad increase in customer service prices.

India to boost oil output, expand storage, diversify crude imports to shield consumers: Minister Puri
Energy diplomacy to play a key role during turbulent times
Multi-fuel strategy to help to ensure energy security
India will seek to strengthen its energy security by increasing domestic oil and gas production, expanding storage and diversifying import sources, as the country prepares for continued growth in energy demand and heightened risks to global supply chains, Petroleum Minister Hardeep Singh Puri said Oct. 27.
In addition, India's growing energy needs will require a diversified, multi-pathway strategy that combines hydrocarbons, gas, renewables, biofuels, nuclear power, and emerging technologies rather than relying on any single fuel or technology.
"By increasing domestic production, improving storage and diversifying imports, India will be able to protect Indian consumers from future price shocks that other parts of the world face," Puri told a conference in the western city of Goa.
The minister said energy security now extends beyond securing physical supplies. It also requires countries to manage geopolitical conflicts, shipping chokepoints, volatile prices and sudden disruptions while keeping economies moving.
He added that India had maintained steady energy imports during recent supply-chain disruptions, which he described as evidence of the success of the country's energy diplomacy.
"India continues to expand its energy partnerships, reduce dependence on limited sources and safeguard its energy security," he said.
The country had reduced its exposure to individual suppliers over the past decade by expanding the number of countries from which it sources crude oil to 41 from 27, Puri said. The country has also built 5.33 million metric tons of strategic petroleum reserves and increased refining capacity to 267 million mt per annum from 215 million mt, Puri said.
Other measures include expanding the number of fuel outlets to more than 100,000 from about 52,000 and increasing LPG import terminals to 22 from 11. Domestic LPG production had also risen to about 55,000 mt/day from around 34,000 mt/day, he said.
Energy diplomacy Puri said India's agreement in January to source 10% of its LPG
requirements from the US had proved crucial during the recent global energy crisis.
"We are further strengthening energy ties with Algeria, Canada, East Asian nations and Qatar to ensure reliable supplies," Puri said.
The Middle East accounted for about 93% of India's LPG imports in January-October 2025, but that share has fallen to 56% in the same period this year. US supply has risen more than fourfold in the same period, from 1.2 million mt to over 5 million

mt, taking its share from about 7% to 35%, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea data.
"For LPG, the big change this year is that India has become much less dependent on a single supply region," said Charles Kim, associate director at CAS.
"India's procurement strategy is increasingly about balancing economics with supply resilience. Middle Eastern barrels will remain competitive because of their proximity, but Indian buyers are putting more value on having alternative sources available when disruptions occur," he added.
Upstream push The government is also seeking to raise domestic oil and
gas output through initiatives, including the Samudra Manthan program, which Puri described as a major initiative to unlock India's offshore hydrocarbon potential.
The initiative includes opening nearly 1 million sq. km of previously designated "No-Go" areas for exploration, potentially expanding opportunities for domestic exploration and production, he added.
Puri said this would strengthen domestic exploration and production and help reduce India's dependence on imported hydrocarbons. Higher domestic output, combined with larger reserves and refining capacity, would give the country greater flexibility during periods of international disruption.
The federal Cabinet approved investments of up to 840 billion Indian rupees ($8.8 billion) over five years in late July to advance offshore oil and gas exploration under Samudra Manthan, or the National Offshore Exploration Scheme. The initiative is expected to catalyze reserve accretion of more than 600 million metric tons of oil equivalent by fiscal year 2030-31 (April-March), the petroleum ministry said July 31.
The government will fund up to 50% of eligible deepwater exploration drilling costs, capped at $71 million per well. By partially covering these costs, the scheme could significantly improve the economics of frontier exploration.
Alternate fuels India was also using alternative fuels to reduce the pressure
on its import bill. The expansion of E20 fuel, which blends 20% ethanol with gasoline, is providing additional income to farmers while helping reduce energy imports, Puri said. The GOBARdhan scheme similarly aims to convert agricultural residue and organic waste into compressed biogas.
India will therefore need to balance affordability, supply security and sustainability across a broad energy mix. Its longterm objective is not to replace one dependency with another, but to build a diversified system capable of absorbing price and supply shocks while supporting economic growth, he said.
"For a country of India's scale, success cannot mean choosing one fuel, one technology or one pathway. It means building an energy system that is secure, affordable, diversified and increasingly sustainable," Puri added.

INTERVIEW: Omega targets Taroom oil, gas growth amid Australia's supply concerns
Omega appraises multibillion-barrel potential
Flow tests yield 452 b/d light sweet crude
Company assessing infrastructure, partnerships
Omega Oil & Gas sees Queensland's emerging Taroom Trough as a potentially significant source of domestic oil and gas for Australia, though further appraisal is needed to establish the resource mix, productive capacity and infrastructure required to commercialize the play, company chairman Martin Houston told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, in an interview at Singapore International Energy Week 2026.
"It's a significant game changer for Australia, which is why the Queensland government is so excited about it," Houston said, referring to the broader Taroom Trough resource opportunity.
Houston said early indications suggested Omega's large acreage position alone could hold multibillion-barrel-of-oilequivalent potential across oil and gas, though the company is still appraising the fields and has not established commercial volumes across the acreage.
Houston highlighted the significance of the potential new supply to Australia's broader focus on energy security.
"One of the things that the Minister of Energy in Queensland and certainly the federal government has been talking about is greater liquids self-sufficiency," Houston said. He added that the Queensland government was encouraging both short-term responses through storage and longer-term measures involving the development of new supply and, potentially, refining.
"We see ourselves playing a key role, producing more domestic oil into the refining capacity in Australia," Houston said. "Even using a longer-term normalized oil price of $70-$75/b, we believe any of the projects would be economic within that range."
Canyon has already demonstrated flows of very light crude. A 2025 flow test of Canyon-1H achieved peak rates of 452 b/d of 49.5 degrees API oil and 0.60 MMcf/d of associated gas from a 650-m lateral, according to the company. Its sustained 24-hour rates were 321 b/d of oil and 0.472 MMcf/d of gas.
Houston described the produced liquid as a "very light, sweet crude" at 49.5 degrees API.
The extent to which oil rather than gas ultimately drives development remains uncertain. "We know we have got light oil. We know we have got gas. In what proportions, what quantities, and so on, and how the fields will be produced is all a matter for the future," Houston said. "We are still appraising the fields."
Omega is now testing how widely the petroleum system extends across its acreage, the company said in an Oct. 21 statement.
Canyon North-1 started drilling in October as the third well of Omega's 2026/27 appraisal program and the first well drilled in the ATP 2081 joint venture, in which Omega holds 45% and operates alongside Tri-Star (30%) and Beach Energy (25%). The well followed Canyon-4, where Omega reported a 589-m gross oil-and-gas-bearing sequence containing 197 m of aggregate net pay, which the company described in the statement as its best

Canyon well to date. Canyon North-1 is designed to test whether the play extends
north of Omega's PCA areas. Omega said the Canyon Sandstone demonstrates continuous geology from the Canyon wells to Tasmania-1 and can be correlated approximately 40 km farther northwest to Fantome-1.
"What is becoming clear in the Taroom Trough is that this is not just one horizon," Houston said. "There are multiple horizons, or benches, with multiple potentially productive layers that still need to be tested."
"Geologically, we believe this is a very extensive hydrocarbon province with multiple possible reservoirs, consistent with what is seen, for example, in the Permian Basin in the US."
Omega expects an upgraded resource assessment in the first quarter of 2027, followed by one or two horizontal wells, with flow testing scheduled for the first half of 2027, according to its Oct. 21 announcement.
"The long-term production test should give us a much better understanding of productive capacity, the resource potential, and the relative proportions of oil and gas," Houston said. "Our intention is to move toward early production as soon as we can, whether oil or gas."
Infrastructure hurdle Converting those resources into production will also depend
on determining how oil and gas reach the markets. "From our perspective, the immediate task is to understand what infrastructure already exists and what needs to be built," Houston said. "If wells are put on production after the drilling campaign, we need to establish what infrastructure is required for both oil and gas."
Houston said existing gas pipelines were around 40-100 km from the company's acreage, while oil would be trucked during the testing phase. He added that government and industry were collaborating on future infrastructure requirements.
The Queensland government has designated the Taroom Trough a "Prescribed Project," with a dedicated development plan led by the Office of the Coordinator-General to streamline approvals and facilitate infrastructure development, Omega said in April. The company's Canyon acreage is also located near established infrastructure and regional market access points.
Houston added that as an E&P company, Omega's "job is to find and produce the hydrocarbons."
"We expect to grow as the business expands, but ultimately a development of this scale may require companies with larger balance sheets, access to markets and infrastructure capabilities," he said.
Omega has already broadened its operated Taroom footprint through partnerships. In September, the Queensland government selected an Omega-led joint venture with Tri-Star and Beach as the preferred tenderer for the 1,138-sq-km PLR2026-1-10 land release. Omega holds 45% and operatorship, with Tri-Star at 30% and Beach at 25%, according to a Sept. 17 statement.
Houston put Omega's current position at 2,986 sq km of gross operated acreage and around 2,100 sq km of net acreage.

China clean oil product exports likely to fall 15% MOM in November: sources
Refineries cut November exports amid supply tightness Gasoil shipments drop 30% as margins weaken production Fourth quota batch release sparks market speculation
China's refineries were likely to export up to 3.13 million metric tons of clean oil products in November, down 15% from an estimated 3.7 million mt in October, according to four sources close to the matter on Oct. 28.
The November export ceiling comprised about 284,000 mt of gasoline, down from about 400,000 mt in October, while gasoil would fall 30% to 770,000 mt and jet fuel would fall 6% to about 2.07 million mt, they added.
Previously, market sources expected Chinese refineries to slash their clean oil product exports in November, led by gasoil, due to tight domestic supplies, while weak refining margins weigh on production, Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, reported Oct. 26.
Meanwhile, there was market discussion about China releasing the fourth batch of clean oil product export quotas.
The third batch was allocated in the end of September at 4.54 million mt, bringing the total allocation for 2026 to 36.54 million mt, down 10.38% year over year, Platts reported earlier.

Chinese independent refiners turn to Iraqi crude as Iranian supplies dry up: sources
Iraqi crude purchases surge 8M barrels Basrah grades fetch $11-15/b premiums
China's independent refineries are increasing purchases of Iraqi crude as Iranian barrels vanished from the market, refiners, traders, analysts and bankers told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, on Oct. 28.
At least five infrequent buyers of Iraqi crude have bought 6 million barrels as of Oct. 28 for November delivery, while another private refiner, which had emerged in the market earlier for September delivery, continued to buy Iraqi crude, taking at least 2 million barrels, they said.

They added that Basrah Heavy cargoes were sold at premiums slightly above $11/b against ICE Brent futures on a DES Shandong basis, while Basrah Medium cargoes fetched premiums of $13$15/b on the same basis.
A Shandong-based trader said Iranian crude buyers were looking at Iraqi Basrah Medium and Basrah Heavy as alternatives to Iranian Light and Iranian Heavy, as the sanctioned barrels had almost vanished from the market.
A second independent refiner said refineries that usually process Russian crude would continue to stick with Russian grades, adding that ESPO was being offered at about $24-$25/b against ICE Brent Futures, on a DES Shandong basis.
"There are some similarities among the specifications of the Middle Eastern grades, which can be processed in trains that were previously used for Iranian crude," a Shandong-based independent refiner said, adding that plants can produce asphalt from Basrah Heavy, which is also an alternative to Venezuelan Merey.
Independent refineries usually take cargoes on a delivered basis to avoid managing shipping and insurance issues.
Market sources warned that cargoes loaded in the Persian Gulf face logistical uncertainties due to the conflict in the region, requiring refiners to have backup feedstock plans in case a cargo cannot be delivered.
With an increasing number of buyers from the independent sector entering the market, Iraqi crude imports are set to rise in November, a Shandong-based market analyst said. "We got quite a few inquiries from trading houses about issuing letters of credit for Iraqi barrels, which had not happened before," a Singaporebased banker said.
China's independent refineries imported about 2.73 million metric tons, or 20 million barrels, of Iraqi crude in September, with the majority going to a private mega-refining complex that has never taken sanctioned crude, according to data compiled by Platts.
The September volume accounted for about 22.3% of the sector's total crude imports of 12.22 million mt in September, Platts data showed.

European gas vulnerability When it comes to the European natural gas market, Reitan
warned Oct. 28 that the continent is "vulnerable" heading into winter, with record-low gas storage and elevated prices amid the ongoing conflict in the Middle East.
"It is a vulnerable situation; we need to be prepared for a lot of volatility," he said.
European gas storage levels are trailing those recorded at the same time in recent years. EU-wide storage was 74.9% full as of Oct. 26, according to the latest data from Gas Infrastructure Europe. That was the lowest level for this time of year on record, going back to 2011, GIE data showed.
Lower storage levels typically mean that weather shifts spur larger price movements, Reitan said. "The weather will have a significant impact over the next few months," he said.
While market watchers widely expect this year's global El Niño weather pattern to result in a generally warmer winter, Reitan said that this bearish expectation is already priced into the forward curve.
"People are aware of that effect, and everyone knows that it possibly will have an effect," he said. "Meaning that cold spells ... will typically create a lot of volatility in this situation."
European gas prices have surged to recent highs over the past month. Platts assessed the month-ahead Dutch TTF gas benchmark at 95.83/megawatt-hour on Oct. 27. The index is near its highest level since December 2022.
Reitan subsequently said separately during an Oct. 28 call with analysts that while there is a potential for high prices to

stoke demand destruction, Equinor has also seen more interest from market players to buy gas longer-term.
"We see actually an increasing appetite for long-term gas," he said on the call.
The interest comes as Europe faces stiffer competition with buyers in Asia for scarcer spot LNG cargoes due to the wartime disruptions hampering transit through the Strait of Hormuz.
"We see actually fairly solid demand [in Asia] for LNG, even at the current prices," Reitan told analysts.
Even as oil exports from the Middle East have largely rebounded toward pre-war levels, Reitan said Equinor expects LNG flows through Hormuz to remain "very, very limited" until there is a resolution to the conflict.
"There is a significantly different risk related to an LNG ship than a crude tanker," he said.

Russia seeks closer energy ties with African countries
Russia implements more than 30 energy projects in Africa Cooperates with African countries through OPEC+, GECF Libya asks Russian companies to return to oil projects
Russia wants to expand existing energy cooperation with African countries, including on upstream oil and gas production projects as well as through OPEC+, officials said Oct. 27 and 28.
Russian President Vladimir Putin said that Russia is already implementing more than 30 promising energy projects in 16 African countries, during the Russia-Africa Economic and Humanitarian Forum in Moscow.
"We have a shared interest in ensuring that Africans have access to affordable, sustainable, and environmentally friendly energy sources, and in supporting the development of domestic energy markets across Africa, including in sectors where Russia possesses unique technologies," he said, according to a Kremlin statement.
Russian Energy Minister Sergey Tsivilyov said that Russia is ready to help Africa to create hydrocarbons hubs, the Tass news agency reported Oct. 28.
"To enable the import of hydrocarbons, logistics infrastructure within African countries has to be established to facilitate onward transport of these hydrocarbons to end consumers," Tsivilyov told reporters, according to Tass.
"At the same time, there must be development based on Africa's own existing resources--specifically, the expansion of hydrocarbons extraction," he added.
Russia works with 8 African countries in the OPEC+ coalition. This includes six OPEC members - Algeria, the Republic of Congo, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Libya and Nigeria. Sudan and South Sudan are part of the non-OPEC group. Africa's OPEC+ members produced a total of 4.48 million b/d in September, according to the Platts OPEC+ crude production survey.
During a meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin on Oct. 27, Mohamed al-Menfi, Chairman of the Presidential Council

of the State of Libya, said that Libya wants to expand cooperation with Russia across a wide range of sectors, including energy, gas, infrastructure, reconstruction and security.
"We hope that Russian oil companies will return to Libya and contribute to the advancement of this vital sector of our economy," he said, according to a Kremlin statement.
Russia's Tatneft earlier signed four production service agreements with Libya's National Oil Corp. on reserves in Libya's Ghadames and Sirte basins, but work has been interrupted multiple times due to security concerns.
According to the Kremlin, Putin added during the meeting: "Our cooperation within multilateral frameworks, particularly the Gas Exporting Countries Forum, whose summit in Moscow we both just attended, as well as OPEC+, enables us to join efforts in developing measures to safeguard global energy security."
Russia hosted the GECF leaders' summit on Oct. 27. African GECF member states include Algeria, Egypt, Equatorial Guinea, Libya and Nigeria.

UK extends fuel duty discount until July 2027
Discount may save drivers £120 annually on fuel
European nations implement similar tax relief
The UK government extended a 5 pence per-liter discount on fuel duty until July 2027, the country's Chancellor of the Exchequer, John Healey, said on Oct. 28.
The duty on gasoline and diesel was cut by 5 pence per liter in March 2022 to address the energy price crisis triggered by Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine. The initial measure was repeatedly extended by successive governments.
"With the war in the Middle East still driving high fuel prices, I am extending these tax cuts for motorists through the winter and spring to July, supporting households, hauliers and farmers," Healey said during his Budget Statement update in the House of Commons.
The tax cut, most recently extended by Prime Minister Keir Starmer in May, was due to expire at the end of the year. During the May announcement, the government also granted a 12-month road tax holiday to hauliers.
The duty cut should save an average UK driver about £120 per year, the UK government said in a statement on May 20. Hauliers are saving £600 for a typical heavy truck and £912 for the largest vehicles on the road, according to the statement.
This was at least the third announcement in the past seven days by a European government regarding motor fuel price support. However, the support measures are typically of shorter duration.
The Czech government said on Oct. 26 that it extended its current motor fuel regulation measures and reduced the excise duty on diesel fuel for an additional month, through the end of November. The Czech reduction in excise tax on diesel is 2.35 koruny per liter ($0.11 per liter). Italy also extended its motor fuel state support on Oct. 22, but none of the measures are expected to continue into 2027.
Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, assessed the European diesel benchmark ultralow sulfur diesel 10 ppm CIF Northwest

Europe Cargo price at $1,498/metric ton on Oct. 27, below a peak of $1,642.25/mt on Sept. 15. In contrast, the price averaged $714.72/mt in February before the Middle East war began.
UK diesel consumption in 2025 averaged 573,000 b/d, while gasoline consumption averaged 299,000 b/d, together accounting for about 65% of the country's total oil products demand, according to S&P Global Energy CERA.

US EPA proposes rolling back elements of methane rules, restore flaring option
EPA restores flaring, cuts monitoring rules
Changes save $45B, boost oil and gas output: agency
Small producers seek relief from Biden emissions rules
The US Environmental Protection Agency proposed Oct. 27 to roll back elements of its oil and natural gas methane regulations, restoring flaring as a compliance option and eliminating thirdparty monitoring provisions that drew industry objections during the Biden administration's 2024 implementation push.
The proposal would revise standards for new and existing facilities, known as OOOOb/c, and establish separate requirements for certain well sites. EPA estimated the changes would save consumers and businesses $45 billion and support an additional 210 million barrels of oil production and 1.41 Tcf of gas production. The agency's release did not specify the period covered by those estimates.
The changes would move beyond the compliance extensions and narrower technical revisions already adopted by the Trump administration, targeting requirements governing associated gas disposal, large emissions events and equipment operation.
The moves were first announced by US President Donald Trump at an Oct. 27 campaign rally in Texas.
"Tonight, I'm officially terminating Biden's expensive and unnecessary methane regulations to save consumers and energy producers," Trump said. He said Democratic regulations had "crushed small oil and gas operators."
Flaring, monitoring changes EPA proposed restoring flaring or other control devices
to achieve 95% emissions reductions as the best system of emission reduction for associated gas from oil wells. The agency argued that requiring gas to meet a sales threshold is impractical in producing areas lacking sufficient infrastructure.
The proposal would also rescind the super-emitter program, which allows EPA-certified third parties to identify large emissions events for further investigation. EPA cited legal concerns and questioned whether the program was necessary given other practices available to identify and address significant releases.
That provision was a flashpoint when the underlying rule was published in March 2024. Platts reported at the time that the Western Energy Alliance considered the program vulnerable to legal challenge, while Texas had filed suit seeking to block the broader regulations.
EPA is also considering two approaches to separate well-site standards: categorizing sites by production as marginal or nonmarginal, or by resource type as conventional or unconventional. Marginal wells account for roughly four-fifths of producing wells nationwide, according to the agency.
Other proposed changes would distinguish between design and operating requirements for covers and closed-vent systems, allow repairs under the operating requirements, and provide

additional flexibility for control-device monitoring. In 2024, the Biden EPA estimated the rule would reduce oil
output by about 41.1 million barrels in 2038, or about 1% of total US production, while significantly reducing pollution from US oil and gas production.
Reversing compliance The proposal marks a reversal from the implementation effort
that followed publication of the Biden-era rule on March 8, 2024, when industry lawyers warned producers to prepare immediately for complex and overlapping methane-control, reporting and emissions-charge requirements.
The original framework distinguished between new or modified sources and existing operations, making construction dates, equipment changes and emissions levels important to compliance obligations and investment decisions. Lawyers also warned that revised emissions calculations could increase reported methane releases even without operational changes.
Since announcing reconsideration in March 2025, EPA has extended several compliance deadlines and finalized April 2026 revisions addressing temporary flaring and monitoring the heating value of gas sent to combustion devices. In May, the agency clarified circumstances in which flaring could continue at new oil wells after the phaseout deadline.
Industry support for federal methane regulation was not uniform. While criticizing European methane regulation proposals as too onerous, ExxonMobil CEO Darren Woods publicly urged the Trump administration to maintain key features of the US regulations. Mike Sommers, President of the American Petroleum Institute, the US's largest oil and gas trade group, told Platts' Capitol Crude podcast in 2024 that the industry welcomed a federal standard to provide a consistent "measuring stick" for operations.
However, groups representing smaller producers have pushed for the changes. In late September, the National Stripper Well Association, which says it represents "the interests of the smallest and most economically vulnerable oil and gas operators in the US," said wells that produce less than 15 barrels of crude or 90,000 TCf of gas per day should be exempt from methane regulations.
"It is an unwise use of EPA time, talent, and treasure to reduce stripper well emissions," the association wrote in a release.
On Oct. 28, Independent Petroleum Association of America President and CEO Edith Naegale thanked Trump and EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin for the proposal.
"As originally written, the regulations would have placed an outsized burden on independent producers and could have threatened the continued operation of hundreds of thousands of marginal wells nationwide," Naegale said in a statement. Canada proposes clean-fuel rule changes to support ethanol, canola coprocessing
Government amends regulations for biofuels
Carbon capture gets incentives at plants
Refineries boost canola oil coprocessing
Canada has proposed amendments to its Clean Fuel Regulations aimed at strengthening domestic low-carbon fuel production and supporting investment in carbon capture at ethanol plants and the coprocessing of canola oil and other biobased feedstocks at refineries.
The government said in a release Oct. 27 that the proposed changes are intended to narrow the competitiveness gap facing Canadian producers, encourage additional clean-technology investment and reduce compliance costs for fuel suppliers.
Canada did not provide specific estimates of the additional biofuel production, feedstock demand or credit generation expected from the amendments.
The Clean Fuel Regulations require gasoline and diesel suppliers to progressively lower the life cycle carbon intensity of the fuels they supply. Companies can comply by reducing emissions at refineries, supplying lower-carbon fuels, or acquiring credits generated by qualifying projects and fuel use.

Since the regulations took effect, the credit market has grown to more than C$3 billion, supporting investment across low-carbon fuels, agriculture, forestry and clean technologies, according to the government.
Ethanol CCS, canola demand The proposed amendments are intended to support projects
that capture and store carbon dioxide from low-carbon fuel facilities, including ethanol plants.
Carbon capture can reduce the lifecycle emissions intensity of ethanol by preventing fermentation-derived CO2 from being released, potentially increasing the fuel's credit-generating value under carbon-intensity-based programs.
The amendments would also encourage refineries to coprocess renewable feedstocks, including canola oil, with crude oil. Six Canadian refineries have already undertaken coprocessing projects under the Clean Fuel Regulations, the government said.
Growth in low-carbon fuel production has also supported the construction of new canola-crushing plants supplying feedstocks to renewable diesel and other fuel facilities.
Canada consumes almost 200 million liters/day of gasoline and diesel, providing a large compliance market for lower-carbon fuels, according to the government.
The proposed rule changes complement Canada's Biofuels Production Incentive program, which is accepting applications until December 2027.
Support for Canadian producers The program provides up to C$372 million in production-based
support for Canadian biodiesel and renewable diesel producers, aimed at maintaining domestic capacity and strengthening feedstock and fuel value chains.
The government said the Clean Fuel Regulations have also encouraged investment in renewable natural gas and hydrogen production.
"Our government designed these proposed amendments to support Canada's low-carbon fuel sector and ensure resilient long-term emissions reductions," Julie Dabrusin, Minister of Environment, Climate Change and Nature, said as per the release.
The detailed implications for biofuel credit values, imported fuels and crop-based feedstock eligibility will depend on the final regulatory text following consultation.
