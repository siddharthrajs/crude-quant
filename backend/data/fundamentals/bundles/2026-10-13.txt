Russia could boost diesel exports to 3.5 mil mt/month by year-end: Novak
Lifting of export ban follows US sanctions waiver
Russia prioritizes domestic market
Russia could increase its diesel exports to global markets to as much as 3.5 million metric tons/month by the end of 2026, Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said Oct. 12.
"Overall, we have the capacity to increase diesel export volumes. By the end of the year, we could reach export levels of 3 million-3.5 million mt/month to global markets -- matching precrisis figures," Novak said in an interview with television channel Rossiya-1.
The Russian government partially lifted a diesel export ban Oct. 10 after negotiations with the US administration.
The US Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control issued a sanctions waiver on Oct. 9, allowing the sale, delivery and import of Russian-origin diesel fuel, including importation into the US. The license is effective until April 7, 2027.
Russian diesel and gasoil exports averaged about 3.3 million mt/month over January-May, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea, roughly in line with the volumes exported in 2025. However, exports declined from June, reaching 479,000 mt in September, the lowest monthly volume on record, according to CAS data, which dates back to 2016.
Additional Russian supplies to global markets "will help increase volumes and partially balance supply and demand," Novak said. "[This is a] trend we are already observing, including in global diesel prices, which have adjusted downward by about 5%-6%."
Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, assessed the European diesel benchmark price ULSD 10 ppmS CIF NWE cargo at $1,450/ mt on Oct. 12, down from $1,496.5/mt on Oct. 8, before the announcement of the partial ban relief.

Landlocked Midwest refiners expand pipelines to reach coastal markets
Laurel Pipeline expansion moves Midwest fuel east
Western Gateway targets California supply gap
Midwest refining capacity grows to 24% of US total
US Midwest refiners have long faced a structural problem: large, sophisticated refineries producing more gasoline, diesel and jet fuel than the regional market can absorb, with limited pipeline and waterborne access to move surplus barrels to higher-priced coastal markets.
Two infrastructure developments -- the expansion of Buckeye Partners' Laurel Pipeline system into the US Atlantic Coast and the proposed Western Gateway pipeline connecting Illinois to Los Angeles -- are now beginning to change that calculus.
The comparison with the US Gulf Coast is instructive. Few regions combine such diverse crude access, dense pipeline connectivity, enormous storage capacity and world-scale export infrastructure in one integrated system.
The USGC became the world's most important refining center not simply because it built large refineries, but because an unusually deep midstream network -- anchored by the Colonial Pipeline's roughly 5,500-mile system from Houston to New York Harbor, alongside Explorer, Plantation, Buckeye and Magellan -- allowed those plants to source almost any crude and sell products into almost any major market.
That combination gave Gulf Coast refiners exceptional scale, flexibility and multiple outlets for every barrel. It is precisely that model that Midwest refiners are now attempting to replicate.

CHINA DATA: Independent refiners face Iranian crude shortage as Sept imports hit four-year low
Iranian crude imports drop 57% in September
Supply shortage forces refineries to cut
Alternative barrels cost $25/b above Brent
Imports of Iranian crude by China's independent refineries hit a four-year low in September, and are likely to trend lower in October due to the ongoing US blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, refinery and trade sources told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, on Oct. 13.
Iranian crude deliveries slumped 57.3% month over month to about 2.2 million metric tons (537,000 barrels/day) in September, the lowest since January 2022, when deliveries were 2.18 million mt, according to Platts data.
As a result, the sector's Iranian crude imports in the first nine months of 2026 fell 16.5% year over year to 46.9 million mt, the data showed.
"It's hard to predict how much Iranian crude will arrive in October, especially since most carriers switch off their AIS when passing through the Strait of Hormuz," a Shandong-based analyst said. "I expect the volume to be just over 1 million mt."
The sharp drop in Iranian crude availability has become the dominant concern for China's independent refining sector.
Market sources said Iranian crude cargoes, which typically move to China from floating storage near Malaysian waters, have become increasingly scarce as a result of the Middle East conflict.
High prices The tighter supply picture has driven Iranian Light offers
to about $25/b above ICE Brent on a DES Shandong basis, putting the grade close to the premium commanded by Russia's ESPO crude.
Other alternative barrels remain equally expensive. Brazilian Buzios crude was recently offered at premiums of around $25$27/b to ICE Brent on a DES Shandong basis, amid longer voyage times and elevated freight costs, traders and refiners said.
"Some of those refineries that used to buy Iranian crudes have switched to Basrah barrels, but that is far from sufficient, so some refineries will have to further cut utilization rates," a trader said.
Since late September, small independent refineries have secured about 7 million barrels of Middle Eastern crude, while private mega-refineries have purchased another 4 million barrels, according to trade sources.
The volume was slightly below the 867,000 b/d (3.55 million mt) of total imports by the whole independent sector from the Middle East, excluding Iranian barrels, Platts data showed.
Iraqi Basrah Medium crude for November delivery was traded at premiums of about $17-$18/b to ICE Brent futures

on a delivered Shandong basis, while Basrah Heavy changed hands at about $12/b above ICE Brent, two Shandong-based refinery sources said. These cargoes were bought by about eight independent refineries.
"There were no other cheap feedstock choices for independent refineries besides those Middle Eastern crudes, as Iranian crude supplies are apparently diminishing," a trade source in Qingdao said.
Runs cut despite improving margins Refiners, traders and analysts said high feedstock costs, along
with improving exports and increased deliveries from the Middle East, have led to cautious feedstock procurement to avoid a sudden slump in oil prices, despite stronger refining margins.
Most refineries have been operating at reduced levels since mid-September and will likely cut rates further late this month due to the limited feedstock, sources said.
According to local energy information provider JLC, average refining margins for processing imported crude rose to 694 yuan/ mt as of Sept. 30 from 410 yuan/mt a month earlier.
"The refining margins were still quite good recently, so there is no need for them to shut down at the moment," another analyst in Shandong said.
However, persistent feedstock shortages could trigger additional shutdowns next month as refiners struggle to secure replacement barrels, the analyst said.
-- Daisy Xu - Oil Market Specialist, Oceana Zhou - Oil Market Specialist

India cuts windfall gains tax on diesel, jet fuel exports
Strong product exports boost India's oil terms of trade: CRISIL
India's middle distillate exports rose to a one-year high in September, supported by robust overseas demand as European diesel inventories continued to decline, creating scope for shipments from the South Asian supplier to remain elevated in the coming months, refining sources and analysts said Oct. 13.
Additionally, New Delhi's lowering of export duties in September will continue to encourage private Indian refiners to tap overseas markets, at a time when tighter global product supplies are increasing pressure on policymakers amid surging prices worldwide.
"India's increase in product exports in September coincided with tightening European diesel inventories, which are expected to reach their lowest year-end level in more than a decade," said Saransh Katiyar, liquid bulk analyst at S&P Global Commodities at Sea.

This marks a sharp reversal from the consecutive declines in petroleum exports recorded over the previous three fiscal years.
"India's oil terms of trade have improved since the onset of the West Asia conflict," said Dharmakirti Joshi, chief economist at CRISIL. "While crude oil import prices have risen sharply, export prices of refined petroleum products have increased even faster. Export volumes have also recovered in recent months after an initial disruption caused by the conflict."
"While exports to some markets have faced headwinds this fiscal year, shipments to newer markets have accelerated, partly offsetting the weakness. These markets are likely experiencing supply shortages from traditional suppliers such as West Asia and Russia amid geopolitical disruptions and reduced refining capacity," Joshi added.

Japan to provide P&I, compensate cargo, hull cover for crude, oil products, LNG transport
To provide LNG shipping insurance if foreign reinsurance is lost
Three bills to enhance stability of crude oil, other fuel supplies
JOGMEC to provide subsidies based on CCS cost-carbon price gap
Japan is set to provide protection and indemnity cover and partially compensate cargo and hull insurance payments for the transportation of crude oil, oil products and LNG, as part of a bill approved by the Cabinet on Oct. 13, a senior official at the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry said.
The bill is one of three approved by the Japanese Cabinet earlier Oct. 13 to enhance the security of the country's crude oil supply, among others, amid the conflict in the Middle East.
"The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism will revise the existing specified tanker act. The current law covers crude oil, and the scope will be expanded to include petroleum products such as naphtha, as well as liquefied natural gas," the METI official told a press briefing.
METI will also introduce similar support measures for hull insurance and cargo insurance, the official added.
In response to the Middle East situation and to ensure a stable supply of energy to Japan, METI said the government will establish a program under which it will partially compensate nonlife insurance companies for the funds required to pay insurance claims under marine property insurance, such as cargo and hull insurance, covering the transportation of crude oil, oil products and LNG, even in cases where the provision of reinsurance is prohibited.
"In the event foreign insurance companies become unable to provide reinsurance coverage, a situation could arise where the cargo is available but cannot be transported," the official said. "To address such circumstances, the government will establish a mechanism to fill the gap left by the unavailable foreign reinsurance coverage."
Japan introduced a law in 2012 to compensate P&I cover as a sovereign guarantee for each tanker carrying Iranian crude amid EU sanctions against Iran -- an initiative that remains in effect to date. However, Japan suspended its imports of Iranian oil in May 2019.
The Japan P&I Club, formally known as the Japan Ship Owners' Mutual Protection & Indemnity Association, provides insurance cover of up to about $9.8 billion per tanker.
METI Minister Ryosei Akazawa said earlier Oct. 13, "Today, the Cabinet approved three bills: a bill concerning the rationalization of the use of designated sea lanes to ensure the stable transportation of crude oil among others; a bill on special measures to secure the fulfillment of specified cargo insurance contracts and specified vessel insurance contracts; and a bill to partially amend the Act on the Japan Organization for Metals and Energy Security."

"In light of the structural changes taking place in the global energy landscape, including the recent situation in the Middle East, it is necessary to strengthen the resilience of Japan's energy supply and demand structure," Akazawa told reporters.
"Through these three bills, we aim to fundamentally enhance the stability of procurement by diversifying the sourcing of crude oil and other fossil fuels and reducing associated risks," he added.
The Cabinet's approval comes after Japan formulated an energy policy package on Aug. 26, under the direction of Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, to bolster the resilience of the country's energy supply and demand system through Green Transformation (GX) initiatives known as POWERR GX.
Other bills The first bill, which concerns the rationalization of the use
of designated sea lanes to ensure the stable transportation of crude oil and oil products, importers such as refiners, petrochemical producers and trading houses will receive grants when they procure crude oil and oil products from suppliers located outside the Strait of Hormuz region, the official said.
To receive the grant, these companies will be required to formulate a designated sea lane rationalization plan that sets out details such as procurement targets, the types of crude oil covered, shipping routes and the duration of the plan, the official added.
The government will then review and certify the plan, after which JOGMEC will provide grants covering a portion of the costs associated with supply diversification, the official said.
The funding for these grants will come from contributions paid by refiners and other importers, which JOGMEC will use to finance the grant program, the official added.
Regarding the bill amending the JOGMEC Act, the official said the recent situation in the Middle East has underscored the critical role that alternative pipelines can play in ensuring Japan's crude oil procurement.
The proposed measure would allow JOGMEC to provide equity investments and debt guarantees for such pipelines, which are not associated with specific oil and gas fields, as it can currently provide equity investments and debt guarantees only for pipelines associated with oil and gas fields, the official added.
As part of the amendments to the JOGMEC Act, JOGMEC's mandate will also be expanded to include equity investments and debt guarantees for projects involved in overseas biofuel production, the official said.
"Demand for biofuels and other alternative fuels is expected to increase due to regulations in Japan and overseas," the official said. "It is important to ensure ample supply so that biofuel supply and demand can be brought into proper balance."
JOGMEC's functions will also be expanded to include equity investments and debt guarantees for smelting and refining projects that process used materials, such as spent batteries and other recyclable metal resources, the official said.

Nigeria's Dangote refinery swings to $4.7 billion net profit in 2026
Surge in profits contrasts with $117 million loss in 2025
Higher run rates and surging margins buoy profits
IPO to support refinery expansion plan
Nigeria's Dangote Petroleum Refinery and Petrochemicals FZE took home a net profit of $4.7 billion in the first nine months of 2026 as soaring refining margins linked to the Middle East conflict helped to transform the company's balance sheet from multimillion-dollar losses last year, it reported Oct. 13.
The company's results represent an additional $2.88 billion in net income for the third quarter of 2026 on top of the $1.82 billion of post-tax profit it previously reported for H1. It marks a record return for the refining business, which incurred a net loss of $117 million in the first 9 months of 2025, according to its figures.
"Our third-quarter performance demonstrates the successful transition from commissioning and ramp-up activities to sustainable commercial operations," said a statement from David Bird, the refiner's CEO, who spotlighted higher throughput rates and operational reliability.
After commissioning its 650,000 b/d facility in Lekki, near Lagos, in January 2024, the company was previously forced to repeatedly halt production on the site's main gasoline unit, the residue fluid catalytic cracker, through 2025, and saw its utilization capped at around 70% until 2026, according to previous estimates from Bird.
In February 2026, it subsequently scaled crude processing at the refinery to its intended 650,000 b/d for the first time, before subsequently raising the site's capacity to 700,000 b/d. It has since run at full capacity for every month except July, according to official data from Nigeria's downstream regulator.
The surge in production transformed the refiner into a major international fuel exporter at a time when prices have jumped to all-time highs. According to assessments by Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, the price of West African gasoline on a FOB basis was $1,267/mt on Oct. 13, compared to just $696/mt the previous year and close to a recent all-time high.
In the first nine months of 2026, the refining business saw its earnings before interest and tax rise more than eightfold, from $628 million to $5.5 billion, the company reported. A "significant improvement in refining economics" additionally grew its EBITDA margin to 25% from 6.8% the previous year, it said.
The company has previously shared plans to further slash its

operating costs and boost margins, including by incorporating heavier crude grades and residues into its feedstock mix. As part of a major expansion project for the site, it is also constructing a second refinery train before 2030, a development that would make the facility the largest in the world.
The new Dangote earnings report comes as the company remains in the throes of a landmark IPO, launched in midSeptember and due to close on Oct. 27. The listing is expected to raise more than $1.6 billion for the Nigerian business, which has targeted reaching 10 million retail subscribers.
"With our expansion program already underway, we remain focused on increasing refining capacity and broadening our product portfolio," Bird said in his statement. "These results reflect the strength of our integrated business model and provide a solid foundation as we progress through our IPO," he said.

Cameroon launches oil bid round in effort to double production
Covers three basins including Rio del Rey
Country's oil output stands at 50,000 b/d
Eyes share of renewed IOC exploration interest
Cameroon has launched a new six-block oil and gas licensing round in an effort to secure a new upstream investment and double its production, after seeing its regional rivals sign up some of the world's largest companies in recent months.
The round was formally launched by the Central African country's national oil company, Société Nationale des Hydrocarbures (SNH), on the sidelines of the African Energy Week conference in Cape Town.
It features two blocks in the Rio del Rey Basin, two in the Douala/Kribi-Campo Basin and two in the Logone Birni Basin, SNH said, with a mixture of onshore and offshore acreage.
Pierre Eric Nkoa Nkoa, deputy exploration manager at SNH, told the conference that the results would be unveiled in June 2027.
Meanwhile, a source at the energy ministry told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, on Oct. 13 that Cameroon was currently producing 50,000 barrels/day of crude, but hopes to double it to 100,000 b/d through the licensing round.
The country's last acreage auction wrapped up in March and was dominated by US player Murphy Oil, which secured four blocks, but drew limited interest from international firms.
Cameroon has underperformed its peers in recent years, even as changing narratives about the energy transition and wars in Europe and Iran have driven a new wave of exploration activity along Africa's Atlantic coastline.
Gabon, Namibia, Cote d'Ivoire, Liberia and others have signed exploration deals with oil majors since last year, while established producers like Nigeria and Angola have secured significant new upstream investments.

Galp Q3 upstream output from Brazilian offshore rises 14% YOY
Refining margin hits record-high $33.80/b
Oil increases to 90% of production mix
Portuguese integrated energy group Galp Energia SGPS, SA said Oct. 12 its Q3 working interest upstream production from the Brazilian offshore rose 3% from Q2 and was up 14% year over year at 131,000 boe/d.
To reach the updated target of around 130,000 boe/d for all of 2026 announced in July, Galp needs to keep its Q4 production at around 133,000 boe/d, following Q1 volume of 129,000 boe/d and Q2's 127,000 boe/d.
Figures for Q3 are preliminary, with full results to be announced Oct. 26.
The ratio of oil in the production mix has increased to 90% from 88% in Q2 and 86% in Q3 25, Galp said in the regulatory filing.
Galp has a 20% stake in the Equinor ASA-operated Bacalhau field. The project has been ramping up during 2026 toward a production target of 220,000 boe/d set for year-end, according to Galp co-CEO Maria Joao Carioca's statement made July 27.
Equinor did not respond to Platts' Oct. 13 request for comment.
Galp also announced Oct. 7 that a consortium with Equinor as operator with a 70% share and Galp with a 30% share, was awarded a new block in Brazil's latest tender, the Rodocrosita block, which lies adjacent to the Bacalhau development in block BM-S-8.
The field has "future optionality to explore potential synergies with the Bacalhau project," Galp said.
Record margin Downstream, Galp reported a record-high refining margin of
$33.80/b in Q3, roughly doubling from $16.80/b in Q2 and more

than tripling in annual comparison from $9.50/b, according to the preliminary figures.
Galp operates the 228,000 b/d Sines refinery. The hydrocracker at Sines refinery started a planned maintenance Sept. 24, with the work scheduled to last 37 days.
Refinery throughput in Q3 was 22.2 million boe, down 2% year over year and down 2% quarter over quarter. Throughput for the first nine months of 2026 stood at 63.5 million boe, preliminary figures showed.
The company said in July that it expected full-year throughput between 80 million and 85 million barrels.
Oil product sales totaled 2.0 million mt in Q3, down 1% year over year, but up 10% quarter over quarter, the preliminary figures showed.
Galp's gas and LNG supply and trading volumes increased 26% year over year to 21.4 TWh in Q3, it said, lifted by a 33% yearover-year increase in trading volume to 15.8 TWh.

Saudi Arabia signs agreements with 5 countries for closer energy ties
MOUs with Mongolia, Tanzania, Cyprus, Mauritius, Sierra Leone
Deals cover oil, gas, renewables
Saudi Arabia has signed memoranda of understanding with Mongolia, Tanzania, Cyprus, Mauritius and Sierra Leone to deepen cooperation on energy security, investment and clean energy, according to multiple statements from the state-owned Saudi Press Agency on Oct. 12.
Collectively, the five agreements signed at the WPC Energy Congress in Riyadh establish frameworks for future projects, investments and technical cooperation across oil and gas, refining, petrochemicals and renewable energy, according to the statements.
The agreement with Mongolia covers oil and gas, refining, petrochemicals, electricity and grid interconnection, renewable energy, energy efficiency and storage. It also includes cooperation on circular carbon economy technologies, as do Saudi Arabia's agreements with Tanzania and Mauritius, one of the statements said.
Saudi Arabia and Tanzania will also cooperate on refined product supply and related infrastructure, while assessing opportunities in LNG, oil and gas exploration, petrochemicals, electricity and renewable energy, while the agreements with Mauritius and Cyprus cover oil and gas, refining, petrochemicals, electricity, renewable energy and storage, the statements added.
In Sierra Leone, Saudi Arabia will work to deepen cooperation on renewable energy and expand access to clean LPG cooking solutions, according to another Oct. 12 statement from the Saudi Press Agency.
Further details on specific projects were not provided. -- Lauren Holtmeier

Output at Iran's shared Yaran oilfield jumps 20% on pump upgrades: report
Yaran output reaches 21,721 b/d
Four-well pump overhaul drives production recovery
Production at Iran's onshore Yaran oilfield, shared with Iraq, has increased 20% since the start of the current Iranian year (March 21), driven by pump upgrades across multiple wells, project manager Hossein Mohseni-Zonouzi said Oct. 12, according to a report from the oil ministry news service Shana.
Output at Yaran rose to 21,721 barrels/day in total, with the northern part producing 14,175 b/d and the southern section contributing 7,546 b/d, Mohseni-Zonouzi said.
"The total oil produced at the Yaran oilfield in the first six months of this [Iranian] year reached 3.434 million barrels," he said.
The increase followed the replacement of electric submersible pumps in three wells at the northern side and the employment of a new pump at well 7, Mohseni-Zonouzi said, adding that the field uses 26 submersible pumps.
In 2024, Iran said its target for Yaran, which has 1.7 billion barrels of oil in place, was 33,000 b/d. The Yaran oilfield is part of the West Karun region in southwest Iran that straddles the border with Iraq.

77 wells already re-entered so far this year Local firms focused on well re-entries after IOC exits New bid round offers 40 blocks for development
Nigeria's upstream regulator said late Oct. 11 that it has set its sights on restoring around 788,000 barrels/day of shut-in oil

production as part of a broader push to reach its output target of 3 million b/d by 2030.
The chief executive of the Nigerian Upstream Petroleum Regulatory Commission, Oritsemeyiwa Eyesan, said that between January and September at least 77 oil wells had been successfully re-entered, adding that the commission has sanctioned drilling programs for 128 wells.
Nigeria has struggled to meet its production targets in recent years, with field maturation, theft and vandalism, and underinvestment, particularly in exploration, eroding output.
Many oil wells have been abandoned by producers due to high operating and security costs, compounded by the recent onshore divestments by international oil majors.
However, local producers, including Seplat Energy and the Renaissance consortium, which purchased onshore and shallowwater assets from ExxonMobil and Shell respectively, have set about re-entering shut-in wells.
"Our key priority is simple - restoring the more than 788,000 barrels per day of shut-in production identified across 63 operators," Eyesan was quoted as saying. "This is besides the licensing rounds and accelerating [contract] approval processes."
The NUPRC on Oct. 7 launched a fresh licensing round with 40 oil and gas blocks on offer, which it hopes will eventually add 1.74 million b/d to Nigeria's oil production capacity and 13.9 billion cubic feet/day to its gas capacity, once its fields are fully developed.
Nigeria's oil production, including condensates, averaged 1.7 million b/d in August, according to government estimates.
