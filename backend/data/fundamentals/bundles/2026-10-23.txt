Russian diesel tanker heads to New York as Kremlin claims regular shipments
Malta-flagged tanker should carry 40,000 mt fuel
Agioi Fanendes left Primorsk Oct. 22: CAS

US waiver eases risks for India, China, but won't alter Russian crude flows

The Malta-flagged medium-sized tanker Agioi Fanendes is heading to New York carrying Russian diesel, according to S&P Global Energy Commodities at Sea data, with the Kremlin expecting deliveries to continue "on a regular basis."
The Kremlin's spokesperson, Dmitry Peskov, said Oct. 23 that the first tanker carrying Russian diesel was sent to the US.
"The first tanker has, in fact, set off toward its destinations -- to its buyers -- and [subsequent shipments] will continue on a regular basis," Peskov told journalists, according to TASS. He did not clarify the name of the vessel sent to the US or who the buyers of the delivery are.
The Agioi Fanendes tanker left Russia's port of Primorsk on Oct. 22 and is expected to reach New York around Nov. 6-7, according to CAS data. It is unclear who the buyer and the seller of the vessel might be.
The first diesel tanker from Russia to the US since 2022 is expected to carry about 40,000 metric tons, Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said Oct. 21.
Eastern Mediterranean Maritime, which manages the Agioi Fanendes, according to its website, could not be reached for comment Oct. 23.
While the vessel remains in the Baltic Sea, it can still divert its destination.

The US waiver on Russian crude has reduced uncertainty for Indian and Chinese refiners, giving them greater confidence to continue purchasing economically attractive Russian grades while keeping alternative supply options open in case the situation changes, analysts and industry sources said Oct. 22.
The waiver is expected to preserve, rather than materially alter, Russian crude flows to India and China. While it reduces the possibility of a sudden disruption to global oil supplies, the potential for the waiver to be reversed means political and compliance risks would continue to influence trade, shipping and pricing decisions.
"For refiners in India and China, the waiver is really about reducing the uncertainty rather than changing the crude supply picture," said Premasish Das, executive director for oil analytics at S&P Global Energy CERA. "Russian barrels were already flowing into these markets, but the waiver removes an immediate concern and gives refiners more confidence to continue buying grades that make economic sense."
"However, as the legislation remains in place, refiners may still want to keep alternative supply options open in case the situation changes," Das said.

Indonesia's Prima Energy acquires stake in UK's Jadestone
Prima Energy buys 28.3% of Jadestone
Deal expands portfolio across four countries
Jadestone produced 20,000 boe/d in 2025
Indonesia's PT Prima Petroleum Energi, known as Prima Energy, has acquired a 28.3% stake in London-listed Jadestone Energy, the company said in a statement Oct. 23, as it looks to further grow its Southeast Asian oil and gas business.
Prima, founded in 2021, holds two production sharing contracts in Indonesia -- Bawean and Northwest Natuna -- both with 100% interests. It focuses on maximizing value from mature assets while pursuing development opportunities, it said.
The Jadestone deal gives it interests in a slew of oil and gas assets across Australia, Indonesia, Malaysia and Vietnam, most of them offshore. The UK firm is listed on the AIM market in London, and has 10 assets in total, Prima said, adding that Jadestone is valued at £228 million ($305 million).
"Our acquisition of a material stake in Jadestone is an exciting development for our company as we look to build our presence within the industry more widely across Southeast Asia while we continue to progress our work on our Indonesian projects," Pieters Utomo, Prima's chief executive, said in the statement.
Jadestone representatives did not respond to a request for comment from Platts, part of S&P Global Energy.
Prima added that the investment reflects its deepening commitment to exploration and production in Southeast Asia and builds on its existing presence in Indonesia.
Northwest Natuna is the largest undeveloped heavy oil discovery in Indonesia, according to Prima, with an expected production of 20,000 barrels/day of crude. Bawean pumps 2,000 b/d currently, it said.
Jadestone produced just under 20,000 b/d of oil equivalent in 2025 across its portfolio, a record level and 14% higher than 2024, it said, thanks to strong performance across its Asia-Pacific assets. It guided production of up to 21,000 boe/d in 2026.
Prima Energy's sister company, the Soechi Group, focuses on shipping and marine service, especially the transportation and storage of oil and gas, and is expected to support Prima Energy's expansion into offshore operations, it said.

China's Zhoushan overtakes Fujairah to become third-largest global bunker port
Zhoushan bunker supply reaches 8.54 mil mt in 2025
Singapore retains top spot with 56.18 mil mt
Asian ports gain amid shifts in global trade flows
China's Zhoushan overtook Fujairah to become the world's third-largest bunkering port in 2025, highlighting the growing

prominence of Chinese ports in the global marine fuel market, according to rankings released at the ninth International Petroleum and Natural Gas Enterprises Conference in Zhoushan, Zhejiang province, Oct. 22.
Zhoushan supplied 8.54 million mt of marine fuel in 2025, placing it behind Singapore and Rotterdam but ahead of Antwerp-Bruges and Fujairah, according to the rankings.
Singapore maintained its position as the world's largest bunkering hub, with sales rising 3.8% year over year to 56.18 million mt. Rotterdam ranked second with 9.36 million mt.
Antwerp-Bruges ranked fourth at 7.62 million mt, while Fujairah slipped behind Zhoushan to fifth place with 7.27 million mt.
Zhoushan had ranked fourth globally in the previous ranking, closely trailing third-placed Fujairah, making its move into third place a notable shift in competition among the world's major bunker hubs.
Zhoushan strengthens bunker position Zhoushan's bonded bunker fuel sales rose 10.1% year over
year in 2025 on a comparable basis, according to data presented at the conference, extending growth at one of China's key international bunkering centers.
The port also supplied 515,500 mt of marine fuel to the domestic market during the year.
The world's 10 largest bunkering ports supplied a combined 116.7 million mt of marine fuels in 2025, up 3.3% year over year, indicating resilient bunker demand despite uncertainties surrounding international trade and financial policies.
Zhoushan was among several Asian ports to record higher bunker sales during the year, alongside Hong Kong, Busan and Shanghai. The rankings highlighted the growing importance of Asian bunkering markets as shifts in global trade flows and shipping routes reshape competition among major fueling hubs.
Chinese suppliers maintain strong presence Chinese companies also featured prominently among the
world's largest marine fuel suppliers in 2025. Chimbusco ranked first with sales of 28.16 million mt, broadly
unchanged from 28.2 million mt in the previous year, according to the conference data.
Sinopec Fuel Oil Sales Co. ranked third, with 2025 sales of 21.27 million mt, down 10.6% from 23.8 million mt a year earlier, according to the data.
The top 10 global marine fuel suppliers recorded combined sales of 167 million mt in 2025, down 1.91% from 2024.
The supplier ranking was based on marine fuel volumes sold directly to end-users, including contracted volumes and deliveries completed through third parties, while excluding inter-company trading volumes.

SINGAPORE DATA: Heavy distillate stocks rebound 4.5% to four-week high
Imports rise 75% week over week
Middle Eastern inflows nearly double
Exports from Singapore up 226%
Singapore's commercial stockpiles of heavy distillates rebounded 4.5% to 18.858 million barrels in the week ended Oct. 21, following six consecutive weeks of inventory draws, Enterprise Singapore data showed Oct. 22.
However, stocks were 18.1% lower in the week ended Oct. 21 than in the corresponding week a year earlier, the data also showed.
Weekly residual fuel stocks in Singapore have averaged 20.709 million barrels so far in 2026, compared with a weekly average of 22.8 million barrels in 2025 and 19.7 million barrels in 2024, according to Enterprise Singapore data.
The weekly build in Singapore's inventories was partly driven by a 74.9% week-over-week increase in imports, which totaled 965,469 mt in the week ended Oct. 21.
About 44.3% of the imported volume came from within Asia, with Indonesia and India supplying 77,144 mt and 63,765 mt of heavy distillates, respectively. Arrivals from Thailand jumped to 116,665 mt from 31,926 mt in the preceding week.
Imports from the Middle East -- the UAE, Syria and Saudi Arabia -- increased 91.1% week over week to 297,519 mt in the week ended Oct. 21.
Singapore also saw 64,920 mt of arrivals from the Netherlands, after receiving no European inflows for two weeks.
Meanwhile, fuel oil exports from Singapore more than tripled from the preceding week to 440,316 mt in the week ended Oct. 21.
Of the total, 208,378 mt was bound for China, up from 84,062 mt the week before.
Singapore's inventory data covers only stocks at onshore terminals. Enterprise Singapore describes heavy distillates as "residues," which include cracked and straight-run fuel oil and low-sulfur waxy residue.
In the downstream market, spot trading discussions at the Singapore bunker hub were steady in the week to Oct. 22, with healthy supply and demand noted for November, according to trade sources.
Firmer cargo prices on Oct. 22 raised concerns that premiums could rise, prompting last-minute buying for October deliveries, two Singapore-based traders said.
Limited barging slots for the rest of October kept premiums elevated, with mostly pocket slots available. Additional offers for October deliveries hinged on vessel schedules, with delays

potentially freeing up more supply, a Singapore-based trader said. Platts assessed the Singapore-delivered 0.5%S marine fuel
bunker premium over benchmark FOB Singapore marine fuel 0.5%S cargo prices at $86.02/mt on Oct. 22, up 38% week over week and the highest in about two months.
Platts assessed the Singapore-delivered 380 CST HSFO bunker premium over FOB Singapore 380 CST HSFO cargo prices at $95.97/mt, up 11% week over week and the highest in about seven months.
Premiums for low-sulfur fuel oil ex-wharf cargoes were steady to slightly firmer Oct. 22, with first-half November loadings heard traded from the low $40s/mt to $45/mt, according to trade sources.

FACTBOX: Houthi attacks on Saudi oil, gas assets fuel price risk premium
Brent futures top $116/b as Houthis claim fresh strikes
Yanbu, East-West Pipeline critical amid Hormuz disruption
Saudi crude rerouting north via Suez as Red Sea risks climb
Oil prices have climbed as renewed Houthi attacks raise risks to Saudi Arabia's oil infrastructure and the export routes the kingdom has increasingly relied on amid disruption to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.
Saudi crude continues to reach international markets, including via Yanbu, the Suez Canal and Egypt's SUMED pipeline, but the latest attacks have put the market on edge over both production infrastructure and alternative export routes out of the Middle East.
"The threat is no longer one route or one front: Iran, the Houthis in Yemen and militias in Iraq can all put Middle East oil infrastructure at risk," according to the latest crude oil market outlook from S&P Global CERA.
The following are key facts about the attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure and their market impact:
Infrastructure Repeated Houthi strikes have targeted Saudi Arabia's core
production and export facilities, raising fears of disruption to crude flows.
Yemen-based Houthis on Oct. 22 claimed an attack on Saudi Aramco facilities in Yanbu and said a fire broke out at the targeted site. Saudi authorities and Aramco have not confirmed an attack or damage as of Oct. 23. Aramco did not respond to a request for comment Oct. 22.
Yanbu is home to Aramco's 400,000 barrels/day Samref refinery, its 400,000 b/d Yasref refinery and the 228,000 b/d Yanbu refinery, as well as crude export infrastructure.

Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline, which is critical for rerouting crude exports toward Saudi Arabia's Red Sea port of Yanbu by bypassing the Strait of Hormuz, was shut following Sept. 10 drone attacks. In early October, flow rates through the 7 million b/d capacity pipeline had climbed to 5.8 million b/d after repairs had been carried out, Saudi energy minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman said.
The SUMED Pipeline in Egypt carries up to 2.5 million b/d northbound from the Red Sea to the Mediterranean, supporting Saudi crude flows toward European and other markets.
On Oct. 19, the Houthis said they had targeted Aramco facilities in Yanbu and the Shaybah oil field with "ballistic and cruise missiles and drones," adding that fires had erupted at the sites. A few days prior, Houthis attacked Aramco sites in Dhahran, home to the company's headquarters, and in Riyadh, as well as an Aramco administrative building.

Aramco has committed at least 24 million barrels of crude for ship-to-ship transfers off Sohar, Oman, to Asian customers in September and October.
The increase in traffic through Suez "closely coincides with escalating Houthi attacks targeting Saudi shipping near the Bab al-Mandab strait," CAS said in a Oct. 20 report. The Houthis declared a naval blockade on Saudi shipping on July 20.
Aramco had shifted crude flows to Yanbu on the Red Sea after shipping disruptions through the Strait of Hormuz curtailed exports from its east coast terminals.
Saudi crude shipments moving north through the Suez Canal reached about 653,000 b/d over Oct. 1-19, according to CAS data, accounting for the majority of northbound crude flows.
Saudi Arabia's visible gross crude loadings have rebounded sharply in October, as Yanbu exports recovered following a pipeline attack that disrupted flows in September, according to CAS data Oct. 19.
In its Oct. 14 monthly report, the International Energy Agency said Saudi Arabian crude supply in September had surged 2.3 million b/d month over month to 8.3 million b/d, the highest level since February, as production recovered from multiple attacks on oil infrastructure.

Eni bumps up 2026 oil and gas growth target to 6%-7%
Production hits 1.83 million boe/d in Q3
Natural gas output up 10% on year, liquids down 2%
2026 FIDs seen adding 600,000 boe/d of new output
Italy's Eni SpA expects its oil and gas production to grow 6%7% in 2026 after upgrading its output forecast for a second time this year, it reported Oct. 23.
The Italian energy major beat its expectations in the third quarter by producing 1.83 million barrels of oil equivalent/day, up 7% from the previous year and 1.79 million boe/d the previous quarter, it reported in an earnings statement.
Gains in third-quarter output came exclusively from natural gas, where production grew 10% year over year to 5.148 billion cubic feet/d, mostly due to organic growth in the Republic of Congo, Indonesia, Malaysia and Norway. By contrast, the company's liquids output was down 2% year over year at 845,000 b/d.
Speaking on an investor call, CFO Francesco Gattei said that growth came primarily from activities in Congo, Libya, Cote d'Ivoire, Mexico, Ghana and its new Searah joint venture with Malaysia's Petronas.
The stronger production result came despite a "complex operating environment in the Middle East," previously estimated as accounting for 3% of Eni's output, as well as constraints on Kazakh exports, Gattei said.
"2026 was clearly a very strategic turning point for us. We've

continued our phenomenal exploration success, but we also, with strategic agreements in Venezuela and Argentina, we've accessed very large resources," he said.
Projects sanctioned in 2026 are expected to deliver about 600,000 boe/d of new production by 2030, not including Venezuelan volumes. As a result, the company steadily upgraded its growth outlook since the beginning of the year, initially targeting a 3%-4% increase in production volumes compared to 2025.
In an effort to capture future growth and favorable commodity prices, the company has pushed to fast-track new developments in South America, Africa and Southeast Asia, Gattei said, signaling key FIDs this year of Baleine Phase 3 in Cote d'Ivoire, Kutei North and Gendalo & Gandang in Indonesia, Cronos in Cyprus, and Greater Paj in Angola.
With 1.23 billion barrels of new discoveries in the first nine months of 2026, the company flagged an "outstanding" result for its exploration division, which achieved a technical success rate of 80% at a discovery cost of under $1/boe.
Upstream expansion Key milestones include the company's September agreement to
revive Venezuela's giant Junin 5 oilfield under revised contractual terms with the state oil company, PDVSA, which it estimates will give it access to 35 billion barrels of certified oil in place.
An earlier agreement on the country's Perla field gave Eni control over exports and revenues from the project, opening the door to a new phase of LNG expansion and allowing Eni to roughly double its production, Gattei said.
Eni additionally snagged a new exploration license on the Sapukala block in Indonesia's prolific Kutei Basin, and entered Uruguay's oil sector in August by inking a new agreement with Uruguay's national oil company ANCAP and MIWEN to enter the country's OFF-5 exploration block.
By the end of 2026, the company also expects to make a final investment decision on a large-scale LNG export project in Argentina, centered on the country's Vaca Muerta formation, after applying to join the country's Large Investment Incentive Regime (RIGI).
At the same time, the company sold a 10% stake in its flagship Baleine project in Côte d'Ivoire to the Azeri state-owned company Socar, a move it said would help optimize its upstream portfolio through its "dual exploration" approach.
Refining In the downstream sector, the company continued to see solid
refining margins, which strengthened from $8.3/b in the second quarter to $28.1/b in the third. Strength came mostly from middle distillate crack spreads, which continued to tick higher due to disruptions to Middle Eastern oil flows, the report said.
Throughput at Eni's Italian refineries increased to 4.14 million metric tons in the third quarter from 3.37 million in the second, while volumes processed outside Italy, including at the UAE's Ruwais refinery, continued to be affected by shipping disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz.

Wholesale downstream fuel sales to external customers in the July-September period were down 5% year-over-year to just under 8 million mt, Orlen said in market filing of selected Q3 operating results.
While Orlen's refining capacity utilization in Q3 was unchanged year on year, it increased by around 8% from the second quarter. Retail fuel sales to external customers fell 2% to 2.94 million mt.

Iraq's September crude exports of 2.48 million barrels/day were largely steady month over month, at roughly 75% of prewar levels, according to port loading data obtained exclusively by Platts, part of S&P Global Energy.
The ongoing crisis in the Strait of Hormuz has created new trade flows, with the UAE emerging as one of the biggest buyers of Iraqi crude in September, importing 619,000 b/d of mostly Basrah Medium. The UAE had, until recently, imported only a rare cargo of Iraqi crude to process at its Ruwais refinery. Since June, UAE imports of Iraqi crude have been rising.

SLB sees exploration rebound fueling growth across all basins
SLB participates in 80% of global wells
Digital business targets double-digit growth
OneSubsea bookings exceed $9 billion goal
Exploration activity is picking up in both mature and frontier basins as operators seek to replace reserves and diversify supply, SLB S.V. said in its third-quarter earnings update on Oct. 23, a trend the oilfield services company expects to help drive growth across every basin and division in 2027.
SLB has participated in the evaluation of about 80% of completed high-impact exploration wells globally so far this year, the company said, with activity emerging in Brazil, East and West Africa, Namibia, Guyana and Suriname.
CEO Olivier Le Peuch said exploration is improving through a mix of infrastructure-led exploration in mature basins and growing activity in frontier basins. He highlighted West Africa, the North Sea, the Gulf of America, Brazil's Pelotas basin, Suriname, Guyana, Namibia, Angola, East India and Indonesia as areas attracting attention or investment, and tied the trend to energy security, reserve replacement and production decline pressures.
The exploration recovery is also feeding SLB's digital business, as customers seek data, reprocessing, imaging and AI capabilities, Le Peuch said. He said the ambition is for digital "to continue to operate at the level of double digit or about or higher," with digital exploration, digital operations and AI as growth drivers.

Le Peuch said the cycle has started and is favorable to SLB's footprint, driven by deepwater, exploration, production recovery and new growth areas such as Venezuela and Argentina. He described it as "broadly an inflection that is characteristic of an upcycle with long duration."
The company expects all divisions and all basins to benefit from growth in 2027 and beyond, Le Peuch said, reaffirming that the company sees the opportunity to grow "in every basin across every division in 2027."

Deepwater remains an advantaged source of new supply, SLB said, with final investment decisions expected to rise by more than 30% year over year in 2026. The company pointed to continued OneSubsea awards, including a recent award in Mozambique for ExxonMobil's deepwater Rovuma LNG development.
Le Peuch said SLB expects to surpass its $9 billion two-year OneSubsea booking target. Bookings in 2025 and 2026 often convert into revenue in 2027 to 2029, which should translate into visible growth in 2027, 2028 and beyond, he said.
In the Middle East, activity rose early in the quarter in the UAE, Kuwait and to a lesser extent Saudi Arabia, with gains in intervention, stimulation and drilling, Le Peuch said. Oman was steady, while Iraq did not see the same uplift.
Activity later adjusted, but SLB now sees things stabilizing, with fourth-quarter activity expected to remain in line with the third quarter under the current level of disruption, he said.
SLB's 2027 outlook already assumes flat Middle East activity at second-half 2026 levels, making any resolution an additional catalyst, Le Peuch said.
Some countries may need to restore production capacity and continue capacity commitments, meaning the region could return to or even exceed pre-conflict activity, he said.
Aramco awarded SLB four integrated well construction contracts covering more than 450 wells over an initial three-year term, which the company said improves revenue visibility.
SLB is mobilizing in the fourth quarter, with activity expected to reach fuller capacity in 2027, he said.
Le Peuch said SLB remains "very excited" about Venezuela, where activity is accelerating after years of limited investment, helped by customer capex increases and agreements by international operators.
SLB started executing another large contract there this quarter and is adding resources and equipment, he said.
Asked about scale, Le Peuch pointed to a historical level of $1 billion in Venezuela and said he did not see why that should not be "a target" or "an ambition going forward."
SLB said its production business now represents more than 50% of its portfolio, giving it a more balanced mix of short- and long-cycle activity.

ChampionX delivered its highest quarterly revenue since its acquisition in the third quarter of 2025, the company said, and Le Peuch said SLB is "very, very pleased" with its performance, citing production chemicals and artificial lift in North America, with further upside internationally, particularly in the Middle East and offshore basins.

September milder in Europe, but colder in Canada and Japan

September was milder in Europe, but colder in Canada and Japan than a year ago, according to data obtained from CustomWeather and gathered by Platts.

A total of 19 cities geographically representative of Europe had 26.1% fewer heating degree days in September than in September 2025. The total for the SeptemberSeptember period was 26.1% fewer degree days than the similar period last year and 35.1% fewer than normal.
