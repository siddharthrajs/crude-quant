US crude oil inventories hit three-week low as exports surge
Commercial stocks fall 3.19 million barrels
SPR hits fresh 44-year low
Weekly crude exports climb 33% to 4-week high
US crude oil stocks fell to three-week lows during the week to Oct. 2, US Energy Information Administration data showed Oct. 7, against a backdrop of stronger exports and refinery demand.
US commercial crude stocks declined 3.19 million barrels over the week to Oct. 2 to 424.13 million barrels, US Energy Information Administration data showed Oct. 7. The draw was the largest since the week ended Aug. 28 and put stocks 0.6% below the seasonal five-year average, marking the first time they have fallen below normal since early August.
An additional 780,000 barrels exited from the Strategic Petroleum Reserves, extending declines for a 28th-straight week and putting stocks there down to 282.98 million barrels, a fresh 44-year low.
Commercial crude stocks declined across all regions outside of the Midwest, where planned and unplanned refinery outages have trimmed local demand. Midwest stocks climbed 1.87 million barrels, EIA data showed, including a 440,000-barrel build in inventories at the NYMEX delivery hub of Cushing, Oklahoma, that left stockpiles there at a 20-week high.
Midwest refinery utilization climbed 2.6 percentage points to 88.2% of capacity but remained well below the September average of around 94% of capacity. On Sept. 26, ExxonMobil restarted its 267,000 barrels per day Joliet refinery in Illinois after a power outage shut the plant unexpectedly on Sept. 13, according to a company spokesperson.

IEA supports priority release of diesel stocks 'to the extent possible'
IEA backs fast-tracked release of March commitment
Members have 200 million barrels of diesel still in stock
European release could leave 57 days of diesel supply: JP Morgan
The International Energy Agency has offered its support for the accelerated release of emergency oil reserves after a G7 commitment to speed up stock draws, but stopped short of setting specific timelines for new diesel and crude supplies to reach the market.
After a meeting of its member governments on Oct. 7, the IEA said it backed the faster release of stocks already agreed upon in March 2026, when the organization committed to draw an unprecedented 400 million barrels of crude and oil products from its collective reserves. Members also supported the prioritized release of diesel "to the extent possible, given the tightness in diesel markets," the statement said.
The IEA announcement follows an Oct. 2 statement from G7 leaders committing to release 100 million barrels of oil via the agency, after pressure from the US to take stronger measures to cool diesel prices. According to the IEA, 100 million barrels of oil represents the volume of stocks committed in March but not yet released, although some countries have overfilled their targets.
IEA member states -- comprising 32 mostly advanced economies -- have so far released 325 million barrels of oil in response to the Middle East supply shock, and the agency remains on standby to release more stocks as needed, according to its statement. Its remaining emergency oil stocks stand at 1.1 billion barrels, of which over 200 million are diesel.

South Korea may cut US crude costs with Panama pipeline FTA waiver
Transit time to drop by 15 days via new route
Country on track to keep Asia's top US crude buyer status
The Korea Customs Service is working to extend the preferential tariff treatment under the South Korea-US free trade agreement to US crude shipped through Panama's Petroterminal de Panamá pipeline, a move that could lower transportation costs and the time taken to move WTI Midland crude to East Asia via the Pacific Ocean passage, the customs agency and several industry sources with knowledge of the matter said during Oct. 2-7.
In an effort to reduce South Korea's dependence on Middle Eastern crude, the customs department has developed and implemented various measures to diversify import sources. For US crude imports, preferential tariffs under FTAs had previously been difficult to apply because the crude was shipped through neighboring countries after loading, potentially violating the "direct transportation" requirement for FTA preferential treatment, the KCS said in a statement Oct. 5.
South Korean refiners regularly purchase WTI Midland and West Texas Light crude from the US Gulf Coast, and the sweet grades are often shipped directly to South Korea on VLCCs. ASEAN net oil product imports to reach 2.2 million b/d by 2031: IEA
Regional demand grows 130,000 b/d yearly
Upstream output drops to 1.6 million b/d
LPG imports double, creating vulnerability
Southeast Asia's net oil product imports are projected to reach 2.2 million barrels/day by 2031 as rising demand outpaces domestic crude production and the capabilities of the region's aging refineries, the International Energy Agency said.
Regional crude import requirements are expected to approach 3 million b/d by the same year, leaving ASEAN increasingly exposed to supply disruptions, freight costs and price volatility at the Strait of Hormuz and Strait of Malacca, according to the IEA's ASEAN Energy Security Review published Oct. 6.
Oil demand is projected to grow by 130,000 b/d annually between 2025 and 2031, led by diesel, jet fuel, petrochemical feedstocks, LPG and marine fuels. Indonesia and Vietnam will account for much of the increase, according to the report.
Southeast Asia is expected to become the largest regional contributor to global oil-demand growth over the medium term, the IEA said. Regional consumption increased by an average of 1.7%/ year in the decade through 2025, almost twice the global average.
LPG represents a particular vulnerability. Regional LPG imports have more than doubled over the past decade to around 500,000 b/d, while domestic natural gas liquids production is declining and alternative supply options remain limited, according to IEA.

Domestic supply declines Upstream production is moving in the opposite direction,
falling from 2.5 million b/d in 2015 to 1.8 million b/d in 2025. "Production is forecast to fall by 150 kb/d from 1.8 mb/d to 1.6 mb/d during 2025-2031, with Malaysia accounting for two-thirds of the drop," said the IEA.
However, the IEA highlighted Indonesia as an exception, with fiscal reforms, enhanced recovery and new condensate production expected to lift output following decades of decline. "Having fallen from approximately 820 kb/d in 2015 to 600 kb/d in 2025, output is projected to rise to around 730 kb/d by 2031, making Indonesia a notable exception among ASEAN producers," the agency said.
ASEAN has around 5.5 million b/d of refining capacity, with crude and condensate processing averaging 4.1 mb/d in 2026, according to IEA data. The region's refining sector continues to face competitive pressure from export-oriented refining centers in the Middle East and China and is expected to see limited further capacity additions through 2031, the IEA said.
Emergency oil stocks The IEA said emergency oil stocks remain the most effective
mechanism for managing a severe physical disruption because commercial inventories are generally intended for normal operations rather than prolonged international shortages.
ASEAN's Framework Agreement on petroleum security provides for consultation, information exchange and voluntary commercial assistance to members facing critical shortages, but practical implementation remains limited.
IEA recommended clarifying emergency procedures, improving regional market data, conducting simulation exercises and exploring commercially based regional storage arrangements for smaller and highly import-dependent economies. It also said dedicated LPG stocks could merit separate consideration because the fuel requires distinct storage and distribution infrastructure.
The IEA noted that oil market balances are expected to flip from undersupplied in Q4 2026 to oversupplied by Q1 2027, with the surplus continuing throughout next year amid a rebound in Middle Eastern production and exports during 2027, providing governments an opportunity to "establish or expand governmentheld stocks of crude and products and absorb part of the surplus."
Biofuel demand to rise 50% The IEA expects Southeast Asian liquid biofuel demand to
increase approximately 50% from more than 360,000 b/d in 2025 to over 550,000 b/d by 2030. Biodiesel will account for 75% of that growth to 2030, with smaller contributions from ethanol, renewable diesel and sustainable aviation fuel.
Biofuel blending already displaces significant gasoline and diesel imports, including volumes equivalent to up to 35% of Indonesia's diesel demand, the IEA said.

Shell flags high refining margins, strong trading result in third quarter
Refining margins rise to $42/b in Q3
Upstream output at 1.74 mil-1.84 mil boe/d
LNG output climbs on ARC Canada deal
Shell expects its third-quarter earnings to be buoyed by higher refining margins and continued strong trading results, it said Oct. 7, even as upstream oil and natural gas production is likely to dip.
In a trading statement released on its website, Shell said its chemicals and products business had seen its indicative refining margin rise from $24/barrel in Q2 to $42/b in Q3, with infrastructure damage and shipping restrictions in the Middle East driving diesel cracks to new records.
Last week, G7 countries agreed to release crude and product stocks from strategic reserves to cushion the impact of high diesel prices.
Strong margins come despite lower refinery utilization, which Shell expects to dip to 93%-97% in Q3, from 102% in Q2, primarily due to low Rhine water levels impacting its Rheinland plant.
Upstream, the company narrowed its production estimate and inched down the top end of its guidance, while lifting production expectations from its integrated gas division, which includes LNG.
Shell expects Q3 upstream production of 1.735 million-1.835 million barrels of oil equivalent/day, it said, compared to 1.824 million boe/d in the second quarter.
Integrated gas should see production rise quarter-overquarter from 631,000 boe/d to 740,000-780,000 boe/d, the company said. That represents a boost from Shell's previous Q3 estimate, reflecting volumes from ARC Resources, its new Canadian acquisition, and Qatar, it said.
"Trading and optimization [are] expected to be in line with Q2," the company said.
Like its rival European majors, the company has seen earnings rise considerably on strong trading results this year, with its traders able to take advantage of volatility stemming from the war in the Middle East.
Platts Dated Brent, a key physical crude benchmark, rose past $144/b in April before edging downwards and has been volatile in recent weeks. Platts is part of S&P Global Energy.
While refining margins have expanded, the company's chemicals division has not seen a similar boost. Shell said it expects a $62/mt quarter-over-quarter drop in chemicals margins in Q3.
Echoing comments of other international oil company heads, Shell CEO Wael Sawan told the Energy Intelligence Forum in London on Oct. 6 that the oil market was close to exhausting some of the buffers - from high pre-war stocks to the quantity of oil on water and strong refining runs - that had prevented

runaway crude and product prices in the early months of the war.
"There is a limit to how much those shock absorbers can continue to take," Sawan said.

Russian tanker operators lift crude exports to highest level since G7 price cap
Non-G7 tankers help Russia lift crude exports
Russian tanker operators play bigger role
Mainstream companies lack incentives to stay in Russia
Russia increased crude exports in September by relying more heavily on non-G7 tankers, particularly those operated by domestic companies, whose shipments reached their highest level since Western nations imposed a price cap.
Tankers flagged, owned and operated by companies not based in G7 countries and their allies, and not insured by Western protection and indemnity clubs, lifted nearly 2.89 million barrels/ day in September, compared with 2.7 million b/d in August, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea and Maritime Intelligence Risk Suite data.
The increase came as overall Russian seaborne exports rose to 4.09 million b/d from 3.82 million b/d in the same period.
While the overall share of Russian crude exports on non-G7 tankers, mainly from the shadow flee established to bypass Western sanctions, showed little change month on month, Russian tanker operators were shouldering more responsibilities for their home country's overseas sales.
Continuing the upward trajectory seen in recent months, shipments rose from 18.6 million barrels in August to 20.6 million barrels in September -- the highest since the G7 and EU began banning maritime service firms from participating in Russian crude exports unless the barrels were sold below a certain threshold in December 2022.
State carrier Sovcomflot's tankers loaded 6.4 million barrels in September, while the remainder were generally lifted by tankers owned by small, little-known companies, according to the CAS and MIRS data. All of the ships were flagged in Russia.

Flag clampdown The International Maritime Organization has launched a
campaign against falsified flag registration in recent quarters. In 2026, some members states -- including Cameroon and the Cook Islands -- told the UN agency that more than 110 ships with a total carrying capacity of 9.78 million deadweight tons were removed from their flag registries or wrongly claimed to be flagged with them.
More than 100 of these were tankers designated by Western authorities for sanctioned Russian trades or other reasons, according to an earlier Platts review of their circulars. Platts is part of S&P Global Energy.
Meanwhile, 107 ships were reflagged in Russia between January 2025 and June 2026, 56% of which joined the Russian registry after previously reporting false flag information following designation by Western authorities, according to a study by the Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air.
"`Shadow' fleet vessels have been funneled into the Russian ship registry as their other options have been slowly closed off," the nonprofit said in a note published in September. "Under diplomatic pressure from sanctioning countries, many open registries began significantly withdrawing their flagging services for `shadow' vessels. With vessels increasingly being removed from open registries, many vessels turned to false flags."
Sanctions impact As for the G7 fleet, Greek-operated tankers loaded 17.3 million
barrels in September, up from 13.6 million barrels in August, according to the data. The increase came as fewer tanker attacks were reported in the Black Sea.
But freight market conditions point to limited financial incentives for mainstream companies to remain in Russian trade going forward. North American output surges 21% YOY
Spanish integrated energy group Repsol SA increased its upstream oil and gas production by 4% year over year to 574,000 barrels of oil equivalent/day in the third quarter, the company said in a stock market filing Oct. 6.
Repsol's downstream margin for the period reached a record high, based on company data.
Upstream output, which reached the largest quarterly total since Q2 2024, was driven by a 21% year year-over-year increase in North American output to 219,000 boe/d, Repsol said, without providing a breakdown. The figures are all provisional, with final volumes to be published Oct. 29.
Repsol and partner Santos in May announced the start-up of production from the Alaskan North Slope Pikka field, which had a production target of 40,000 boe/d during H2 2026.
The Madrid-based company also has producing assets in Pennsylvania, Texas and the Gulf of Mexico.
In its other regions, Latin American net production, which includes Venezuela, was 237,000 boe/d, down 6% year over year, Repsol said, without providing a breakdown.
Europe, Africa and "rest of world" output dropped 33% year over year to 118,000 boe/d, amid disruption to output from Libya's El Sharara field.
On Sept. 22, Libya's National Oil Corp. issued a third force majeure warning since August after the pipeline and the gates of the Zawiya refinery and Libyan state fuel marketer were shut by armed groups. The incidents affected flows from the field.
For the first nine months of 2026, Repsol's average production was about 557,000 boe/d, short of a company forecast of between 560,000 boe/d to 570,000 boe/d for 2026 that it published in July.

Kazakhstan aims to end refined product imports, start exports by 2030
Kazakhstan wants to end fuel imports by 2030
Refineries cover more than 90% of needs
Major refinery expansions planned
Kazakhstan plans to eliminate its dependence on imported refined products and start exports by 2030, by expanding domestic refining capacity, Energy Minister Yerlan Akkenzhenov said Oct. 7.
In 2025, the country's refineries processed 18.4 million metric tons of crude and produced about 15.5 million mt of finished petroleum products, the minister said during a livestreamed address to the Kurultai, Kazakhstan's unicameral parliament. This accounts for more than 90% of Kazakhstan's domestic fuel needs, according to Akkenzhenov.
"We face annual challenges regarding the supply of aviation fuel. At present, we import around 300,000 tons of aviation fuel from the Russian Federation, and the [renewed] agreement enables us to import aviation fuel from Russia duty-free," Akkenzhenov said.
Kazakhstan's parliament ratified Oct. 7 the renewal of the intergovernmental agreement with Russia on the supply of oil and petroleum products from the country. The deal was extended until Jan. 1, 2028, with automatic renewal for five-year periods in the absence of objections from either party.
The agreement provides for the continued annual coordination of indicative balances for the supply of oil and petroleum products, as well as for the duty-free import of petroleum products from Russia to Kazakhstan.
However, the minister hopes Kazakhstan will be able to meet all of its needs in less than four years.
There are currently three large oil refineries in Kazakhstan and around 25 mini-refineries, according to the minister. "But practically none of [mini-refineries] produce fuel that meets the quality standards established by the Customs Union's technical regulations," he added.
The large refineries include the 6 million mt/y Pavlodar, the 6 million mt/y Shymkent and the 5.5 million mt/year Atyrau.
"We plan to increase the capacity of the Shymkent refinery from 6 million to 12 million tons. We expect the feasibility study for this refinery to be completed by the end of this year, at which point we anticipate moving to the practical implementation stage," Akkenzhenov said.
"We also plan to expand the Pavlodar refinery by 2 million tons and are addressing bottlenecks at the Atyrau refinery-- specifically, the construction of additional diesel hydrotreating lines," the minister added, stressing that these additional volumes should enable Kazakhstan to fully meet its domestic refining needs.
By 2030, Kazakhstan is expected not only to meet its own domestic needs but also to export petroleum products to Central Asian countries, according to the minister.
The deadline and volume targets differ slightly from figures

presented by Chairman of the Board of KazMunayGas Askhat Khasenov at a meeting with Prime Minister Olzhas Bektenov Sept. 23. The Pavlodar refinery should be expanded by 3 million mt/y, with completion expected by 2031, Khasenov said at the meeting, according to the official website of the prime minister.
KazMunayGas was not immediately available for comment. The Atyrau refinery will be expanded by 600,000 mt to 6.1 million mt/year, according to the KazMunayGas chairman. Expansion projects at three refineries will increase their combined capacity from 17.5 million mt to 28.8 million mt, according to the statement from KazMunayGas. The 2030 export goal does not include the fourth refinery which is also under consideration, with three areas chosen as a potential future home for the facility. "We are considering several sites within the Republic of Kazakhstan: in the Ulytau, Atyrau, and Mangystau regions. Consultants are currently working on this," Akkenzhenov said. Kazakhstan's government hopes to attract private investment for its new refinery in exchange for guarantees including tax incentives and feedstock supplies, the ministry's director of the oil transportation and refining department, Daulet Arykbayev, told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, Sept. 30. If approved, the 10 million mt/y fourth large refinery is expected to start up by 2033. The ministry plans a "major roadshow" for the project in 2027-2028, according to Arykbayev.

In the day's process, Eni offered a Nov. 19-20 loading, 950,000-barrel cargo of the grade from a $12.95/b discount down to a $19.60/b discount which was then bought by ExxonMobil. IRAQ DATA: Sep crude exports rise 25% on month as Hormuz flows rebound
Exports reach nearly 3 million b/d
Gulf terminal shipments up 321,000 b/d on month
Launched exports via Syrian port of Banias
Iraqi crude exports rose 25% month over month in September, as more Iraqi oil passed through the Strait of Hormuz, according to data from state oil marketer SOMO, obtained exclusively by Platts Oct. 5.
The data showed that exports hit 2.960 million barrels/day in September, up 583,000 b/d from August levels.
Despite the substantial increase, September flows were around 83% of the 3.568 million b/d Iraq exported in February, before shipping restrictions and attacks linked to the US-Iran war disrupted production and exports in the Middle East.
September exports included 2.571 million b/d from the

southern Gulf terminals, up 321,000 b/d month over month. This was up from 2.250 million b/d in August, but is well below the February rate of 3.335 million b/d.
The rise in exports from the Gulf terminals was due to the increased number of tankers carrying Iraqi oil arriving and loading after crossing the Strait of Hormuz. On Oct. 4, Iraqi Oil Minister Basim Mohammed Khudair al-Abadi said "Iraq had endured difficult circumstances in the recent past, but the crisis is beginning to subside," according to an oil ministry statement.
Gulf terminal exports accounted for 70% of the oil passing through the Strait of Hormuz during the month, SOMO chief Ali Shatri said Sept. 3.
September Basrah Gulf terminal exports consisted of 1.968 million b/d of Basrah Medium crude and 603,000 b/d of Basrah Heavy crude. This compares to 1.799 million b/d and 451,000 b/d in August, respectively, the SOMO data showed. Exports remain below February levels of 2.120 million b/d of Basrah Medium crude and 1.215 million b/d of Basrah Heavy crude.
Basrah Medium has a nominal API gravity of 29 degrees, while Basrah Heavy carries a gravity of 23-24 API.
Iraq also increased exports via Ceyhan and the Syrian port of Banias and resumed supplies to Qayara, the data showed.
Ceyhan exports, which consisted of oil from Kirkuk and the Kurdistan region, doubled month over month to 254,000 b/d.
These volumes, pumped via the Kurdistan pipeline, increased from 180,000 b/d in July and 198,000 b/d in February.
This indicates that IOCs in Kurdistan resumed exports of around 120,000 b/d during the month. This resumption was due to attacks on the Kurdistan region ceasing in early August.
In August, all exports from Ceyhan were Kirkuk federal oil pumped through the Kurdistan pipeline, and no oil was supplied by the IOCs working in Kurdistan.
Exports are below the 250,000 b/d that the Kurdistan Regional Government agreed to pump through their pipeline in mid-March. Under that agreement, some 170,000-200,000 b/d of crude from Basrah was to be pumped North, as volumes produced from Kirkuk are not sufficient to meet export and local refinery demand. In August, the fall was attributed by Shatri to shortages of road trucks to transport the oil from Basrah to the northern system.
Exports of heavy Qayara oil resumed in September at a rate of 56,000 b/d. They had been suspended since February, when the export rate was 35,000 b/d. Trucking to Jordan has remained suspended since January.
In its efforts to diversify export routes and increase export volumes, the oil ministry started supplying oil by trucks from Basrah through Syria for export from the port of Banias on the Mediterranean. These supplies averaged 85,000 b/d in September.
Iraqi government oil revenues were hit by a $25-$35/b discount on the OSP to compensate for risks to tankers passing through the Strait of Hormuz .

ICE Dubai crude futures traded volume edges down MOM in September

The volume of ICE Dubai crude futures traded on the Intercontinental Exchange edged 0.1% lower month over month to 2.427 million lots in September, exchange data showed Oct. 6.
ICE Dubai crude futures comprise Dubai first-line futures, Dated Brent versus Dubai first-line futures and Brent first-line versus Dubai first-line futures. One lot is equivalent to 1,000 barrels.
The lower traded volume came as Dated Brent versus Dubai first-line futures volume fell 7.72% month over month to 14,424 lots, and Brent first-line versus Dubai first-line futures volume dropped 2.17% to 817,557 lots, even as Dubai first-line futures volume rose 1.08% to 1.595 million lots, exchange data showed.

US EIA sharply raises oil price forecast on Middle East supply disruptions
Agency lifts Q4 Brent forecast $14/b to $105/b
Tight supply pushes US diesel above $6/gal, pressures crude
Middle East disruptions cut output 4.5 million b/d
The US Energy Information Administration has sharply raised its near-term oil price forecast, projecting crude will average $105/barrel in the fourth quarter of 2026 as Middle East supply disruptions drain global inventories and tight diesel markets amplify upward price pressure.
The EIA's October Short-Term Energy Outlook marked a $14/b upward revision from last month's forecast and followed a surge in Dated Brent to an average of $114/b in September -- a $23/b jump from August's average. The agency forecasts global oil inventories will fall by a further 700,000 barrels/day in Q4 after declining by 1.9 million b/d in Q3, a trajectory that points to sustained tightness in physical crude and products markets through the end of the year.
The agency said oil and product flows from the Middle East were likely to "generally increase" due to "a combination of convoys through the Strait of Hormuz and workarounds to oil exports ... including bypass routes and increased use of shipto-ship transfers." However, the threat of more regional attacks and other market disruptions was likely to hold prices higher for longer than previously forecast.
"Although we expect oil production and flows from the Middle East will increase, we raised our global oil price forecast compared with last month," EIA said. "The attacks on the EastWest pipeline in Saudi Arabia highlight the potential for continued volatility in physical oil flows and oil amid ongoing withdrawals of oil inventories globally."
Meanwhile, "extreme tightness" in diesel markets would continue to raise demand for crude among refiners hoping to meet diesel demand, EIA wrote. The agency forecast Dated Brent to fall to an average of $84/b in 2027.
Diesel pressure Distillate markets are at the center of the current price
rally, EIA said. US retail diesel prices averaged $6.29/gallon in September, and the EIA expects prices to remain above $6/ gal through October before gradually easing to an average of around $4.50/gal in 2027. East Coast distillate inventories stood 32% below their five-year seasonal average in September and are forecast to remain between 20% and 30% below average throughout the winter -- a persistent supply deficit that will keep heating oil and diesel markets exposed to further shocks.
Record diesel prices have become a fixture of US political messaging in recent weeks. On Oct. 5, US President Donald Trump signed an executive order temporarily lifting restrictions that prohibit the use of dyed diesel, which is typically limited to

off-road use and not subject to a federal excise tax, in his bid to help farmers and transporters grappling with fuel costs.
The administration has faced weeks of pressure from Republican legislators and candidates in the Nov. 2 midterm elections to restrict US diesel exports. Trump expressed support for the idea on Sept. 22, one day before US Energy Secretary Chris Wright denied reports that the administration was preparing a 90-day ban on all diesel exports. Instead, Wright said, the administration was hoping to urge refiners to voluntarily reroute some flows and "trying to avoid a blunt hammer" in its attempts to relieve upward price pressure.
A total ban could force American refiners to slash crude runs by nearly 2 million b/d and ultimately drive up domestic gasoline prices, an analysis from S&P Global CERA forecast Sept. 22.
On the supply side, the EIA said that the gradual increase in the use of Middle East shipping workarounds would reduce regional production shut-ins from 4.5 million b/d in Q4 to 2.7 million b/d in Q1 2027.
US crude oil production is forecast to rise from 13.9 million b/d in 2026 to 14.3 million b/d in 2027, EIA said, providing an additional buffer to global supply as Middle East volumes recover.
Winter fuels The EIA's 2026-27 Winter Fuels Outlook, published alongside
the October STEO, presented what the agency called a "mixed" picture for US households, with the burden of higher energy costs falling unevenly across heating fuel types.
"Relatively high natural gas inventories entering the winter season provide a cushion for heating demand, while households that rely on heating oil face higher prices because distillate markets remain tight," EIA Administrator Tristan Abbey said in a statement.
Households that primarily heat with natural gas or propane -- about half of all US households, according to the EIA -- are expected to spend less this winter than last, supported by natural gas inventories that are forecast to enter the heating season 2% above the previous five-year average. Strong production growth is expected to limit price increases even as LNG exports rise, with the Henry Hub spot price forecast to average $3.16 per million Btu in 2027, down 9% from 2026.

Indonesia has lost around 25% of its oil import sources since the conflict in the Middle East began, he said, without specifying which suppliers or volumes were affected.
Singapore was Indonesia's top gasoline supplier, accounting for more than 57% of the total gasoline and blendstocks discharged into Indonesia in 2025, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea data. So far this year, Singapore has accounted for 67% of Indonesia's gasoline and blendstock discharges, while the country has added supplies from Latvia, Brunei and Qatar, none of which loaded gasoline cargoes discharged in Indonesia in 2025.

Currently, the freight calculation is based on the daily VLCC Persian Gulf-to-Far East freight assessment in $/mt (TDAAB00).

The freight calculation would continue to use the conversion factor for metric tons to barrels, as applied to the grades, in line with their prevailing qualities. The conversion factors are currently 7.32 for Oman and 7.62 for Murban.
In line with this change, Platts is also proposing to use the daily VLCC Gulf of Oman-to-Far East worldscale freight assessment (DIOEW00) in the freight calculation for Murban in its Indonesian Minas, Cinta and Widuri crude market parity prices.
Under the current MPP methodology for Minas, Cinta and Widuri, Murban -- as one of five basket grades -- is normalized for delivery to Singapore, accounting for freight. This is currently calculated using the daily VLCC Persian Gulf-to-Singapore worldscale freight assessment (PFAOD00).
