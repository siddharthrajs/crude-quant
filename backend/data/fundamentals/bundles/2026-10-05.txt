Saudi Arabian Oil Co. CEO Amin Nasser said Oct. 5 that replenishing oil inventories while meeting demand could take up to two years, as the world grapples with a major supply crisis as a result of the conflict in the Middle East.
Speaking at the Energy Intelligence Forum in London, Nasser said that existing buffers, including releasing inventories, have cushioned the blow to supply, but that they are finite and emergency reserves cannot fix long-term supply issues.
"Most of that came from onshore commercial inventories, which was the last major tool in the box," he said.
"If this does not normalize soon we'll find out we're building a much bigger hole in terms of inventories reaching a stress level," he said.
Aramco estimates that less than 10% of inventory is available, with most of it pipeline fill, minimum tank levels and needed for operations.

operations since the US and Israel attacked Iran Feb. 28, but Nasser said that infrastructure is intact and the company is restoring operations within a matter of days following attacks -- a restoration rate that is "six times faster" than anywhere else in the world.

South Korea sustains ample APAC clean product exports on stable feedstock supply
Refiners fully secure Oct. crude needs: MOTIR
Diesel exports to Australia jump 18% YTD
Aug clean oil product exports to Europe reach record high
Gasoil crack spreads hit record $83/barrel
South Korea's refining industry expects stable crude procurement through year-end, supported by government measures, enabling major refiners to sustain clean oil product exports to Asia-Pacific customers and help cover essential fuel shortfalls across the region, according to refinery sources, Korea Petroleum Association, and the trade ministry over Sept.29-Oct 5.
South Korea, Asia's top clean oil products supplier and net exporter, has fully secured its feedstock crude needs for October, while crude oil arrivals for the rest of the fourth quarter are expected to reach at least 90% of the previous year's monthly import levels, according to feedstock managers at major refiners based in Ulsan, Seoul and Yeosu, as well as the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Resources in its industry briefing presentation slide for October.

With about 3.4 million barrels/day of refining capacity and domestic oil demand of around 2.3 million b/d, South Korea has about 1 million b/d of surplus refining capacity available for exports, according to data from state-run Korea National Oil Corp. and KPA.
As the global imbalance between clean oil products supply and demand has intensified, South Korea's refining industry has been helping to address diesel supply shortages in various economic partner countries, responding to requests from countries such as Australia and New Zealand, KPA said in its industry report Oct. 2.
In the first eight months, South Korea exported 62.03 million barrels of refined oil products, mostly diesel and gasoline, to Australia, up 18% from the same period a year earlier, the latest data from state-run Korea National Oil Corp. issued Sept. 25 showed.

Through rigorous diversification efforts, plenty of light sweet US crude, as well as heavy sour Canadian and various Latin American grades, are poised to regularly arrive, while 1.1 million b/d of staple Middle Eastern sour crudes have also been secured for the rest of the year, a MOTIR official told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy on Sept. 30.
Backed up by stability in feedstock procurement and inflows, South Korean refiners can focus on fulfilling term-contract commitments for gasoline, diesel and jet fuel to Asia-Oceania customers, while also supplying spot cargoes to regional markets facing acute shortages, middle distillate product managers at major refiners based in Ulsan and Seoul, as well as marketing managers at two South Korean refiners' trading offices based in Singapore told Platts over market discussion sessions over Oct. 1-5.

Shipments of essential fuels and oil products over JanuaryAugust to New Zealand also rose 11% year over year to 16.7 million barrels, KNOC data showed.
Among recent spot market deals concluded in the Asian market, South Korea's second-biggest refiner GS Caltex sold 300,000 barrels of 50 parts per million sulfur gasoil for loading over Oct. 23-27 at a discount of about $1.50/b to the loadingmonth average of Mean of Platts Singapore 10 parts per million sulfur gasoil assessments, FOB.

Robust crack, lower Chinese exports South Korean refiners could find additional opportunities to

Japan sanctions tankers tied to 42.7 mil barrels of Russian oil trade
Japan counters shadow fleet, joining other G7 members
Vessels mainly transport Russian oil to Asia
Asset freezes target 33 Russian entities
Tankers targeted in Japan's first sanctions against the shadow fleet have been actively involved in international oil trades, according to cargo-tracking data, as the G7 continues to tighten scrutiny over ships transporting Russian energy.
On Oct. 2, Japan's foreign ministry announced sanctions on four MR, two LR1, five Suezmax and 20 Aframax tankers as well as two general cargo ships and two roll-on/roll-off ships, joining other G7 members in targeting Russia's seaborne trades over the war in Ukraine.
The tankers have lifted nearly 42.7 million barrels of crude and petroleum products this year, nearly all of them from Russia for shipments to India, China, Southeast Asia and other destinations, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea.
S&P Global Maritime Intelligence Risk Suite data shows the ships were sanctioned by at least one other Western authority before Japan's enforcement, which didn't cover LNG carriers. Japan remains a Russian LNG buyer, mainly from the Sakhalin-2 project.
Western governments had sanctioned a total of 1,226 oil tankers and LPG carriers as of Aug. 31, according to S&P Global Energy Horizons data, and those ships were mainly designated due to their links to Iranian or Russian trades.
Japan's measures impose restrictions on specified service transactions involving the vessels, as well as certain capital transactions related to their sale, purchase, lease or charter, according to the foreign ministry.
The sanctions package also includes asset freezes on 33 Russian entities and nine individuals, export restrictions on four companies based in Turkey and the UAE, and additional controls on goods that could contribute to Russia's industrial capabilities.

Australia forecasts 15% rise in crude imports as exports slow, field output declines
Domestic oil field output to rise before falling
Middle East conflict threatens diesel supply
Brent crude may rise to $135/b
Australia expects crude oil and condensate imports to rise 14.5% in the fiscal year 2026-27, while exports are forecast to fall 7.9%, according to a government outlook released Oct. 2 that also warned that prolonged disruption in the Middle East could tighten diesel supplies.
Imports are forecast to reach about 213,000 barrels/day in the fiscal year ending June 2027, up from 186,000 b/d in 202526, while exports are projected to decline to 227,000 b/d from 247,000 b/d, according to the September Resources and Energy Quarterly released by the Office of the Chief Economist.
The projections indicate lower crude oil and condensate exports as output from mature Australian fields declines, while the country remains reliant on imported fuels. The outlook assumes that Middle Eastern trade flows normalize before fuel markets come under greater supply pressure.
Australia's crude and condensate exports are projected to fall to 213,000 b/d in FY 2027-28 and 182,000 b/d by FY 2030-31, about 26% below FY 2025-26 levels, according to the government outlook.
Imports are forecast to remain near 213,000 b/d from FY 2026-27 onward, exceeding exports from FY 2028-29, the outlook showed.
Domestic crude and condensate production is expected to rise by 2.9% to 233,000 b/d in FY 2026-27, before declining to 221,000 b/d in FY 2027-28 and 194,000 b/d in FY 2030-31, according to the outlook.
Given Australia's much larger finished-fuel import market, government data showed that refined-product imports rose 5% to 54.4 billion liters in 2025-26, equivalent to about 937,000 b/d. Import value increased 25.2% to A$56.6 billion, while domestic refinery product output remained broadly steady at 14.2 billion liters.
The government said damage to Russian refineries, constrained Middle Eastern refining capacity and lower Chinese refinery runs disproportionately lifted refined-product prices while easing pressure on crude markets.
Continued conflict would have an "outsized impact on product markets (particularly diesel)," the government said.

Inventory depletion "If trade continues to be disrupted until inventories begin to
reach very low levels, it is expected that Brent crude oil prices will begin to rise sharply," the government said. "Prices could average US$135/barrel in the December quarter," it said.
The outlook noted that low inventories could cause localized shortages of oil products and disrupt supply chains, as pipelines and storage facilities require minimum operating volumes. Governments could increase purchases from international markets to protect energy security, adding pressure on prices, according to the outlook.
Brent could average $135/b in the fourth quarter of 2026 under this prolonged disruption scenario, compared with $101.11/b in the baseline scenario, the outlook showed.
The government's baseline outlook forecasts Brent falling to $77.50/b in the first quarter of 2027 and $69.58/b in the fourth quarter as transits through the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab al-Mandab Strait normalize. Annual Brent prices are forecast at $95.7/b in 2026, $73.6/b in 2027, and $67/b in 2028, in nominal terms, according to the outlook.
"Once trade resumes through the Strait of Hormuz, prices are expected to decline over a period of several years as production comes back online much more slowly than in the central (baseline) scenario," the chief economist's office said.
The scenarios are benchmarked to Platts Dated Brent -- the global reference price for physical North Sea crude loading 10 days to one month forward -- which was last assessed at $126.955/b on Oct. 2.
Fundamentals Global oil supply is forecast to recover to 108.3 million b/d in
2027 from 100.6 million b/d in 2026, exceeding projected demand of 105.2 million b/d; returning Middle Eastern volumes and supply growth from Brazil, Guyana and Canada are expected to underpin the recovery, according to the outlook.
"Middle Eastern producers are expected to restore production to preconflict levels or higher once transit through the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab al-Mandab Strait resumes," the chief economist's office said. Australia's Queensland government awards petroleum, gas tenders in Cooper-Eromanga Basin, Taroom Trough
Bridgeport, Vintage secure basin acreage
Companies cite supply boost as key objective
Supply expected to lower energy costs
The Queensland government in Australia has awarded petroleum and gas tenders for five areas in the Cooper-Eromanga Basin and Taroom Trough to secure future domestic oil and gas supply, according to a government statement Oct. 2.
According to the Queensland government, this land release initiative gives the resources industry the certainty it needs to invest, unlock new supply in the state, and put downward pressure on prices.
The successful tenderers include Bridgeport Energy (Qld) Pty Ltd for around 2,712 square kilometers across PLR2026-14, PLR2026-1-5, and PLR2026-1-11, and Vintage Energy Ltd for approximately 625 square kilometers across PLR2026-1-3. Both were awarded acreage in the Cooper-Eromanga Basin, the government statement said.
A consortium that includes XST Taroom Pty Ltd, Cooper Energy (VO) Pty Ltd, Imperial Oil and Gas A Pty Ltd, and Eastern States Energy Pty Ltd was awarded a tender to explore for oil and gas over 306 square kilometers across PLR2026-1-7 in the Taroom Trough.
These new tenders are in addition to the three highly prospective acreages announced in September in the Taroom Trough, along with three awarded in the Cooper/Eromanga Basin and one in the Bowen/Surat Basin in 2025, the statement noted.
Minister for Natural Resources and Mines Dale Last said in the government statement that the sheer interest in the tenders and commitment for the new acreage was a "vote of confidence" in the future of Queensland's resources sector.
"We know more supply is the only solution to bring down the cost of energy, and this acreage opens more land for exploration and future projects," Last said.
In the same statement, Bridgeport Energy Managing Director and CEO Christian Lange said the blocks would build on the company's established presence in the Cooper-Eromanga Basin.

"These blocks are a natural extension of that footprint, and we see real potential for future oil and gas production in the area," Lange said.
Meanwhile, Vintage Energy MD Neil Gibbins said the need for a new supply of gas and petroleum liquids has never been more apparent.
"The release of these blocks will enable the identification and testing of new targets in a proven oil and gas province," Gibbins said, adding that awarding these blocks to junior explorers means opportunities that might have been otherwise overlooked or downgraded are addressed by players who are keen to develop those resources.
Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, assessed the November average JKM at $24.769/MMBtu on Oct. 2, down 71.1 cents, or 2.79%, day over day, as the market retreated amid limited prompt buying interest.

Vietnam's PVEP Jan-Sep crude, condensate output rises 30% YOY to 2.35 mil mt
PVEP executes 27 development wells in 9 months
Well intervention adds 700,000 barrels output
Facility uptime reaches 98% across fields
State-owned PetroVietnam's upstream unit, PVEP, boosted crude oil and condensate production by 30% year over year to 2.35 million metric tons (63,097 b/d) in the first nine months of 2026, outpacing Vietnam's overall crude production growth, as aggressive drilling and field optimization efforts helped offset declines at mature offshore fields, according to a PetroVietnam statement Oct. 2.
Of the total, domestic crude and condensate output rose 35% year over year to 2.11 million mt (56,653 b/d), accounting for nearly a third of Vietnam's national crude production of 178,900 b/d over January-September, according to data from PetroVietnam and the National Statistics Office. Vietnam's overall crude production rose 10.5% year over year.
The strong output came despite mature offshore fields putting pressure on production. S&P Global Energy CERA Upstream Perspective, released in September, said most of Vietnam's producing assets have already produced more than 60% of their recoverable volumes and that declining output remains a key challenge for the sector.
PVEP largely attributed the increase to development drilling, rapid tie-ins of new wells, and an extensive well intervention program.
The company executed 27 development wells during the first nine months, completing 18 while nine remained underway. Several wells significantly exceeded production targets, including the KTN-P4 and KNT-P4 wells in Block 09-2/09, which produced around 2,542 b/d and 3,250 b/d, respectively, compared with planned rates of 1,101 b/d and 1,006 b/d, according to the PetroVietnam statement.

Kuwaiti oil production back to around 2 mil b/d
Global buffers to mitigate supply shortages `evaporating'
Plans to expand own strategic tanker fleet
6 mil b/d of Gulf refined products left the market
Kuwait has increased oil output to around 2 million barrels/day, Kuwait Petroleum Corp. CEO Shaikh Nawaf S. Al-Sabah said Oct. 5, after production fell earlier in the year as a result of attacks and shipping restrictions caused by the war in the Middle East.
Kuwait initially cut output to levels required only for domestic consumption, he said, as it was unable to export any volumes, but is now exporting using its own strategic tanker fleet.
"And we have the capacity to go back up to full production and even to our current maximum sustainable capacity of 3 million b/d if we have the export routes available," he said.

Kuwait was producing around 2.6 million b/d before the US and Israel attacked Iran on Feb. 28.
"We learned from the Iraqi invasion and subsequent war in 2003 that we needed both strategic storage outside the Strait [of Hormuz] and a larger fleet that can run shuttle services outside the Gulf," he said.
KPC is in talks with international partners to expand this fleet, either through ownership or various leasing options, he said.
In addition to ship-to-ship transfers, some KPC customers are now bringing tankers into the Gulf, and more will follow, he said.
Buffers `evaporating' Al-Sabah said that alternative routes and strategic storage
have acted as a buffer but cannot replace free navigation via the Strait of Hormuz.
"The world has been using these buffers, but these buffers are quickly evaporating -- whether it's [strategic petroleum reserves] coming down to incredibly low levels, whether it is strategic storage that we have put in countries around the world, also being moved up very quickly," he said. "And ultimately, the resilience of the international system can only hold up for so long."
Concerns are growing around refined product supply shortages and elevated prices, with the US mulling a diesel export ban and restrictions already in place in Russia.
KPC estimates that around 6 million b/d of refined products previously produced in the Gulf have left the market. Before the war, Kuwait was Europe's biggest jet fuel supplier and the second-largest diesel supplier.
"There's just simply not enough refining capacity in the world to make up for shuttered capacity in the Gulf," he said.
"The pipelines that Saudi Arabia and the UAE have are important and strategic, but they deliver crude," he said. "We have a lot of VLCCs in our tanker fleet... we have some MRs as well that deliver product. But what you're seeing on the international market right now is an abundance of crude on the market but not product."
"So moving more LRs, more MRs, I think is really the name of the game in this next period to try to move product out of the Gulf to get the refineries at full production," he said.
KPC is in talks with Saudi Arabia and the UAE on pipeline and port options and is looking to increase domestic storage and stocks at its overseas refineries, including in Japan and South Korea.
The company is also looking for foreign investment in projects, including pipelines and offshore fields. It has announced three offshore discoveries and Al-Sabah said that subsequent drilling has revealed further discoveries.
He called on consumers and producers alike to recognize that the situation requires a strategic response rather than a purely commercial one.

Russia September crude exports rise 8% on month, despite fresh sanctions
Crude exports to unknown destinations surge to 709,000 b/d Crude deliveries to India plunge 59% on month Sept product exports fall 11% on month
Russia exported 4.088 million barrels/day of seaborne crude in September, up 8% month over month and up 3% year over year, despite ongoing sanctions, ship tracking data from S&P Global Energy showed Oct. 5.
US President Donald Trump signed legislation on Sept. 18 allowing him to impose tariffs of up to 100% on goods imported from the five largest importers of Russian crude oil or gas by volume, and to impose sanctions on shadow fleet vessels used to evade sanctions.
Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said Sept. 21 that it "does not help in reviving our bilateral relations, nor is it likely to facilitate successful progress toward a settlement in Ukraine," in a call with reporters broadcast on the Kremlin's telegram channel.
India and China are the biggest buyers of Russian crude. The Indian foreign ministry said it will monitor the situation and is committed to ensuring that the country's energy requirements are met. The Chinese ministry of commerce said Sept. 19 that it opposes unilateral sanctions that lack a basis in international law as well as secondary sanctions.
Russian seaborne crude exports to India in September dropped 59% month over month and 51% year over year to 816,000 b/d, while exports to China climbed 22% month over month and 27% year over year to 1.415 million b/d, according to data from S&P Global Commodities at Sea.
Exports to unknown destinations surged to 709,000 b/d in September, up from 24,000 b/d in August and up from zero a year earlier, CAS data showed. Exports of Russian crude bound

for ship-to-ship transfer operations in the Far East dropped 43% month over month and 56% year over year to 142,000 b/d, the data showed.
Russian Economy Minister Maxim Reshetnikov said Sept. 24 that Russia will produce 494 million metric tons of oil in 2026, according to a government statement. This is equivalent to about 9.92 million b/d and would be a drop of around 3.5% from 2025 levels of 512 million mt.
Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, assessed Russian Urals crude on a FOB basis at Primorsk at $91.85/b Oct. 2, a discount of $35.11/b to Dated Brent. The discount averaged $29.55/b in September, against a five-year average of $20.79/b.
Diesel ban Russia decided to extend a full diesel export ban until the end
of October, according to Russian newspaper Vedomosti, citing two sources familiar with the matter.
Restrictions on producers have been extended to ensure fuel supply to the domestic market, Vedomosti reported.
The ban for non-producers will remain in place until the end of January 2027. The same deadline applies to all gasoline exports, according to the Russian government decree.
Russia's seaborne exports of refined products in September fell 11% month over month and 42% year over year to 1.075 million b/d, amid continued attacks on its refining infrastructure.
Exports to China dropped 56% month over month and 49% year over year to 109,829 b/d, while exports to Egypt grew 18% month over month and 31% year over year to 150,775 b/d, CAS data showed. Egypt is a key transshipment hub for Russian barrels.
Russian presidential envoy for investment and economic cooperation Kirill Dmitriev said that US diesel prices will rise as a result of US sanctions legislation.
"US Congress sanction threats and [Ukrainian President Volodymyr] Zelenskyy's attacks are affecting global energy markets and driving US diesel prices to a projected $8+ a gallon on election day," he said in a post on social media platform X Sept. 20.

Uniper SE has signed a capacity reservation agreement with Syzygy Plasmonics for future sustainable aviation fuel supply from the technology developer's planned NovaSAF facilities in Central and South America.
The agreement, announced Oct. 1 by Houston-based Syzygy, adds to a growing list of offtake and capacity deals underpinning Syzygy's Latin American expansion.
The deal with Germany's Uniper adds a significant European energy trader to Syzygy's commercial roster. This includes Trafigura Group Pte. Ltd., which signed a six-year offtake agreement covering NovaSAF-1 production with an option to lift additional volumes from future facilities, and World Fuel Services, which holds a separate capacity reservation agreement for planned plants across Central and South America.
Platform and project details NovaSAF-1, Syzygy's first commercial-scale project, is under
development at dairy complex in Uruguay, where manure will supply biogas feedstock.
The facility is expected to produce over 350,000 gallons of ASTM-certified SAF annually, drawing power from Uruguay's electricity grid, which is approximately 90% renewable
Syzygy's proprietary Rigel photocatalytic reactor uses renewable electricity to drive light-powered reactions that convert biogas into syngas, which is then upgraded to jet fuel through Fischer-Tropsch and hydrotreating processes.
The company estimates NovaSAF can reach production costs as low as $6/gallon in the US and under 2,000/metric ton in the EU and UK, potentially matching conventional jet fuel prices under favorable conditions.
The platform's carbon intensity can reach as low as 6 grams of CO2 equivalent per megajoule for dairy- and agriculture-wastederived biogas, representing 33% lower emissions.
