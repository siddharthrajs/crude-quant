India-US energy ties in spotlight as Rubio visits amid weaker US crude flows
Rubio, Jaishankar to discuss energy, other issues Oct. 20
Rubio to visit Jamnagar amid rising Venezuelan crude flows
Jan.-Oct. average US crude flows down 43% YOY: CAS

Expanding energy cooperation between India and the US is expected to feature prominently in talks during US Secretary of State Marco Rubio's visit to India this week, with officials, industry sources and analysts citing the revival of US crude flows and broader cooperation in areas such as LPG as key priorities.
Rubio's visit to India -- his second this year -- comes as India seeks competitively priced crude oil and products while avoiding overreliance on any single supplier or region. This makes energy security -- and the role the US can play in supplying crude, LNG, LPG, ethane and other feedstocks -- an important test of whether the two countries can translate their broader strategic partnership into more resilient and commercially viable energy ties, they said.
India's External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar is scheduled to hold talks with Rubio on Oct. 20, ministry spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said Oct. 16, adding that the visit would provide an opportunity to review bilateral relations and discuss ways to further strengthen the Comprehensive Global Strategic Partnership.
"Discussions are expected to cover key areas of bilateral cooperation, including energy, trade, investment, defense, critical and emerging technologies and other areas of mutual interest. The two sides will also exchange views on regional and global developments," Jaiswal said.

Saudi Arabia's visible gross crude loadings have rebounded sharply in October, as Yanbu exports recovered following a pipeline attack that disrupted flows in September, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea ship-tracking data collected Oct. 19.
Loadings over Oct. 1-10 averaged 7.1 million barrels/day, according to preliminary CAS data, with 3.2 million b/d shipped from Yanbu and 1.9 million b/d from Ras Tanura, and the remainder lifted from non-Saudi locations.
CAS observed Yanbu crude loadings averaging 1.9 million b/d in September, down from 3.5 million b/d over the previous three months, as dark loadings from the country's west coast increased amid disruptions at two chokepoints.
Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline, with a capacity of up to 7 million b/d of crude, was attacked along its Riyadh and Madinah segments on Sept. 10, leading to its shutdown the following day, according to a Sept. 11 statement from the Saudi Press Agency.
Saudi crude loadings from Yanbu resumed Sept. 23, according to CAS.
Earlier this month, Saudi energy minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman said the pipeline had returned to operation within "five or six days" after the attacks and was pumping 5.8 million b/d as of Oct. 6, state media reported.

CHINA DATA: Sept gasoil exports reach 43-month high of 1.85 mil mt
Export margins drive gasoil shipment surge
Jet fuel volumes drop 23.9% in one month
Fuel oil exports rise 114% MOM to 2.59 mil mt
China's gasoil exports reached 1.85 million metric tons (459,000 b/d) in September, a 43-month high, data from the General Administration of Customs showed Oct. 18.
The previous high was 2.15 million mt in February 2023, according to GAC data.
Analysts, refiners, and traders said the surge in gasoil exports, which began in July from about 430,000 mt in June, resulted from tight supplies in the global market and pushed up export margins.
The country's gasoil export margin rose $2.86/barrel month over month to $44.62/b by late September, while gasoil export margins improved $1.19/b to $8.33/b, according to S&P Global Energy CERA's monthly report dated Sept. 30.

Market sources estimated China's clean oil product exports at 2 million-3.85 million mt in October.
Fuel oil exports rose 114% from August to 2.59 million mt in September, rising 16.1% year over year, GAC data showed.
Strong fuel oil exports pushed China's refined oil product outflow to a 45-month high of 7.16 million mt in September, according to GAC.

CHINA DATA: Sep crude throughput hits 6-month high of 14 mil b/d
Sep domestic crude output at 4.45 mil b/d Apparent stock draw reaches 531,000 b/d in Sep Q3 GDP expands 4.5% year over year

Chinese oil companies usually prioritize jet fuel exports among the clean products amid limited export quotas to maximize export profits.
Due to the sharp increase in gasoil exports, a 262.3% yearover-year jump and a 31.6% month-over-month rise, China's conventional jet fuel and sustainable aviation fuel exports declined to 1.94 million mt in September from 2.55 million mt in August, GAC data showed.
As a result, clean oil product exports declined by 3.7% month over month to 4.41 million mt, GAC data showed.
Chinese refineries have resumed clean oil product exports after a temporary suspension during the National Day holiday over Oct. 1-7 and have been granted 4.54 million mt of new export quotas.
Meanwhile, they were awarded 1.07 million mt of fuel oil export quotas.
Looking forward, "Clean oil product exports are unlikely to see a further significant increase, unless crude imports recover to cross over 10 million b/d levels," said Minmin Hu, senior principal analyst with CERA.

China's crude throughput recovered to a six-month high of 14.04 million barrels/day (57.48 million metric tons) in September, according to data from the National Bureau of Statistics on Oct. 19.
Despite an 8.4% year-over-year reduction among the same sample of refineries monitored by NBS, the volume crossed the 14-million-b/d mark for the first time since reaching 14.58 million b/d in March, the data showed.
In September, combined crude throughput at China's 49 state-owned refineries rose for a fourth consecutive month to a six-month high of 8.39 million b/d, Platts data showed. Based on a combined refining capacity of 11 million b/d, utilization reached 76.4%, marking a third consecutive monthly increase since June.
A month-over-month increase in the country's throughput, on a barrels-per-day basis, remained despite being partly offset by private refiners cutting throughput since mid-September amid higher feedstock costs, even as they recently received about 60.57 million mt of flash crude import quotas.
S&P Global Energy CERA estimated that state-run refineries' refining margins rose 5 cents/b month over month to $22.44/b, while independent refiners' margins eased by $1.62/b to $4.2/b, according to its monthly report dated Sept. 30.
On a barrels-per-day basis, the average throughput in September represented 0.8% growth from August. But on a metric-tons basis, the volume fell 2.7% month over month from 59.07 million mt, NBS data showed.

China's crude stocks fell to 1.252 billion barrels at the end of September from 1.307 billion barrels at the end of August and a record high of 1.382 billion barrels at the end of May, according to satellite data from Ursa Space.

UK workers on Apache's Forties, Beryl oil fields set to strike over pay
160 workers to strike Oct. 21 at 7 UK platforms Unite claims Forties pipeline faces disruption risk Apache pumped 32,000 boe/d in UK last year
Operations at a string of UK oil fields are set to be affected by strike action this week, labor union Unite said in a statement

Oct. 19, warning that it could impact the UK's largest crude pipeline system.
The 24-hour strike by 160 offshore workers of US producer Apache Energy will start on the morning of Oct. 21, Unite said, affecting operations at seven facilities across the Forties and Beryl oil fields.
It is scheduled to be followed by another 24-hour strike starting on the morning of Nov. 4 and includes electrical, instrument, mechanical and production technicians and radio operators, Unite said.
The impacted platforms are Forties Alpha, Forties Bravo, Forties Charlie, Forties Delta, Forties Echo, Beryl Alpha and Beryl Bravo, Unite said.
The union warned that the strike could lead to the UK's fuel supplies being "severely disrupted" and the closure of the Charlie platform, which could even take the Forties Pipeline System, the UK's largest crude pipeline, offline due to a lack of pressure.
About 80 offshore fields flow through pipelines into the Forties Pipeline System, which is operated by Ineos FPS and transports 30%-40% of the UK's crude supply, or around 200,000250,000 barrels/day.
The Charlie platform and FPS were unlikely to be significantly affected, an Apache spokesperson said in a statement to Platts, part of S&P Global Energy.
"Based on our current contingency plans, we do not anticipate that industrial action would affect other producers' ability to continue operating through the Forties Pipeline System," the spokesperson said.
"Our plans include maintaining experienced personnel on Forties Charlie to operate the plant and support continued third-party production, even if production from all five Apacheoperated Forties platforms were suspended. Any resulting reduction in pipeline pressure if Apache production was suspended would be comparable to that experienced during a routine maintenance outage."
Ineos FPS did not immediately respond to requests for comment from Platts.
The strike represents an escalation in an ongoing pay dispute, after Apache's workers rejected a pay offer from the company. The two sides are expected to hold talks with mediators prior to the strike, Unite said. Apache's spokesperson said the company hopes to resolve the matter through dialogue.
Sharon Graham, Unite's general secretary, called for Apache to make "an acceptable offer" to members and said the company was "putting UK fuel supplies at risk."
Stevie Davies, Unite's industrial officer, said Apache's recent pay offer amounted to a "real terms pay cut" for some of its members.
However, Apache said it had "engaged constructively" and believes its "final offer to the union is fair", with a proposed 4% pay increase for employees "whose earnings already place them among the highest earners in the UK."

UK's Serica lowers 2026 oil output guidance after lengthy Triton outage
Triton platform restart delayed to end-Nov
Still expects to hit 65,000 boe/d by year-end
Revenues double on year amid US-Iran conflict
UK producer Serica Energy has trimmed its 2026 full-year output guidance after longer-than-expected maintenance at its key Triton project, but still expects to lift daily production to 65,000 barrels by the end of the year, it said Oct. 19.
In a trading update, the North Sea-focused player noted an additional delay to the restart of its Triton platform, which will now resume operations at the end of November.
The production vessel went offline on July 27 for planned maintenance that the company thought would take just two months.
"During the final preparation steps and safety checks for planned restart, the need for an engine change out in one of the power generation units was identified," Serica said. "This has resulted in the operator revising its forecast for resuming production to around the end of November."
As a result, Serica expects its average 2026 production to come in "just below" 40,000 barrels/day of oil equivalent, having previously guided above that level, boosted by its acquisition of assets from fellow UK producer Spirit Energy. That deal completed a few weeks ago and adds 12,000 boe/d from the Southern North Sea.
On a quarterly basis, the extended outage at Triton and maintenance at its Bruce hub caused company production to dip in Q3 to just 22,100 boe/d, from 50,200 boe/d in Q2, Serica said.
Triton output was just 4,600 boe/d in the quarter, down from over 20,000 boe/d in Q2, while Bruce production fell some 10,000 boe/d to 8,300 boe/d, the company said.
However, Serica still expects to be producing 65,000 boe/d by

the end of 2026, once Triton resumes. Average output in the first nine months of 2026 was 44% higher than the same period last year, it said.
Serica is also in the process of securing a rig to execute a multi-well drilling campaign focused on near-field opportunities across its portfolio in 2027 and 2028, with a particular focus on its Bruce and Kyla assets.
The resulting campaign could add up to 30,000 boe/d of incremental production, Serica noted, helping keep output at 50,000 boe/d for the next decade.
On the financial side, Serica said its revenues in the first nine months of 2026 doubled year-over-year, reaching $905 million. It comes against the backdrop of soaring commodity prices fueled by the US-Iran war, which has kept Platts Dated Brent well above $100/b for a good chunk of the year. Platts is part of S&P Global Energy.
The company noted it "continues to screen multiple M&A opportunities, both in the UK North Sea and overseas" after walking away from a potential deal for Egypt and Vietnamfocused Pharos Energy.
It also hopes to move from London's AIM market to the FTSE 250 later this year.
"The benefit of an increasingly diversified portfolio means that current Serica production is around 40,000 boe/d, even prior to the delayed resumption from the Triton FPSO following its annual maintenance," said CEO Chris Cox in the statement.
"While the extended maintenance downtime required at the FPSO is frustrating, it is necessary, and we remain on track to hit production rates of 65,000 boe/d from our enlarged portfolio by the end of the year. The strength of the prevailing gas price in particular means that our guidance of $450-475 million of posttax [cash flow from operations] for the year remains unchanged."
Serica has been among the most active UK companies in terms of M&A in recent years, with unfavorable taxes and regulation and bans on new exploration being blamed for a wave of consolidations and IOC exits from the North Sea.

Nigeria seeks faster CNG adoption to counter higher fuel costs
Government calls for new push on CNG uptake
120,000 vehicles converted, despite 1 million target
Rising fuel costs create new momentum for transition
The Nigerian government is intensifying efforts to develop and deploy compressed natural gas as a cheaper alternative to liquid fuels, its energy ministry announced Oct. 18, as soaring gasoline prices have renewed national fears about consumer affordability.
A statement from Ekperikpe Ekpo, the minister responsible for gas, said that the government had put new focus on fast-tracking CNG uptake, with the aim of getting at least 1 million CNG-powered vehicles on the roads and broaden infrastructure access.

Encouraging the conversion of gasoline vehicles to make them CNG-ready has been a long-standing initiative of the Nigerian government, which in 2024 had previously targeted to hit the 1 million-vehicle milestone by 2027.
According to the website for the Presidential Initiative on Compressed Natural Gas and Electric Vehicles, the number of vehicle modifications currently stands at "over 120,000," compared to a total car market of roughly 12 million. In March, President Bola Tinubu ordered the urgent rollout of another 100,000 conversions, but no official figures have been released confirming completed numbers.
Ekpo said the government was resolved to tap into the country's abundant natural gas resources with new CNG uptake. According to the Nigerian Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Regulatory Agency (NMDPRA), peak 2026 supply to the local market has been 2.2 Bcf/day, equivalent to around a 40% share of the country's gas market.
The minister's comments follow a formal review of progress on CNG uptake, which was intended to cut consumer costs at a time when the government was ending a popular gasoline subsidy.
"There is a need to translate the Federal Government's auto CNG policy into tangible benefits for Nigerians in the form of cheaper transport fares," Ekpo said. He called the CNG transition scheme "imperative" after the removal of the state fuel subsidies in 2023.
Price pressure Since the policy was scrapped, the retail price of gasoline,
Nigeria's main fuel type, has shot up from under 200 naira/liter ($0.15/l) to roughly 1,500 naira/l ($1.13/l) in the retail market, in large part due to the Middle East war.
In 2024, the government unveiled plans to spend $500 million to support drivers in switching to CNG, promising discounted rates for domestic gas and aiming to roll out 100 new filling stations across the country. According to PI-CNG & EV, the country's total number of CNG filling stations remains around 90.
President Bola Tinubu has since set targets for all 36 Nigerian states to ensure that CNG uptake helps bring down prices, leading to new commitments in key jurisdictions. The Lagos Metropolitan Area Transport Authority, for example, stopped accepting new diesel buses into its network from the beginning of 2026.
In 2024, the government also commissioned a 5.2 million scf/ day CNG plant in Lagos and laid out plans for mass conversions of state-owned vehicles. However, local infrastructure constraints have contributed to long queues at CNG stations and truck deliveries being diverted, according to Nigerian media.
In August, Nigeria consumed 41.5 million liters of gasoline, or roughly 260,000 b/d, according to data from the downstream regulator. Demand has declined steeply since the outbreak of the US-Iran war in February, when consumption stood at 56.9 million liters/day.
In recent months, the country's Dangote refinery has supplied roughly 90% of its gasoline, although rising international crude

prices have kept prices elevated. Earlier this month, the Nigerian National Petroleum Corp. committed to a 30-day cap on the cost of gasoline at its retail stations, sparking new debate over the potential reinstatement of subsidies.
Should CNG replace gasoline as Nigeria's primary transport fuel, the government estimates the country could save between $2.5 billion and $3 billion annually.

Rwanda seeks stake in Dangote Kenya oil refinery
Country in talks with Nigerian refining business
$16 billion Lamu project targets 2030 launch date
Construction faces local environmental opposition
Rwanda has approached the Dangote Group over the possibility of owning a stake in its upcoming oil refinery in Kenya, its president Paul Kagame said on state-owned radio Oct. 18, making it the latest African government vying for an interest in the development.
Speaking to Rwanda Broadcasting Cooperation, the Rwandan president said the country was already in early discussions with the Nigerian-owned Dangote Group about acquiring a stake in the new development, which will be the company's second in its global portfolio.
"We shall be very happy to participate in the project if our talks become a success," Kagame said, without providing further detail on the potential stake under consideration.
In separate comments to Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, a Dangote refining executive confirmed that the company had been approached by Rwanda, but noted that the scale of the potential investment could make participation challenging for certain regional governments. Kenya has been discussing a 10% stake at $500 million, the company said previously.
The company has set a maximum 30% threshold it is willing to allocate to government stakeholders in the project, after previous engagement with Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania and Ethiopia, the executive said. At 700,000 b/d, the new refinery is expected to match the scale of the existing Dangote refinery near Lagos, Nigeria, and is expected to cost $16 billion to construct.
The Nigerian refinery was slated to include a 20% stake for the country's national oil company, NNPC, although the business eventually more than halved its planned stake after a period of delivering less crude to the facility than initially intended. The lasting relationship between Dangote and NNPC sees the refinery secure a proportion of its feedstock in the country's local currency, reducing its exposure to foreign exchange weakness.

Turkey targets 1 million b/d crude output from state-operator TPAO
Further expansion of Gabar and Adiyaman fields underway
Exploration continuing in Black Sea, more drilling in Somalia
TPAO targeting possible acquisitions in North America, Africa
Turkey wants to expand state upstream operator TPAO's long-term crude oil portfolio to 1 million barrels oil equivalent per day, its energy minister Alparslan Bayraktar said in a series of TV interviews October 17-18 after the dispatch of the company's floating production platform to its Black Sea Sakarya gas field.
Bayraktar said that Ankara plans to meet the 1 million boe/d target through a combination of expanding domestic crude production operations, growth outside Turkey in countries where the company holds stakes in exploration licenses, and acquisitions.
The minister estimated the company's existing production at roughly 350,000 boe/d and set an interim production target of 550,000-600,000 boe/d by 2028.
In Turkey, TPAO is producing 84,000 b/d from its south eastern Gabar fields, Bayraktar said, with the potential to rise to 100,000 b/d. The company is also close to disclosing a new reserve to the west of Gabar, but is not yet ready to annouce its estimated reserves, he said.
In addition, the state-backed operator has been working to expand production at its Adiyaman fields, also in southeast Turkey, where production has fallen under 10,000 b/d. New discoveries are expected to boost output to round 15,000 b/d he said.

Bayraktar also said that TPAO is still prospecting in the Black Sea, with work underway on its Akcabeyli well, in the west Black Sea.
"This time we are drilling a well with indications of oil," he said, confirming that TPAO plans to extend drilling to 6,000 meters below sea level.
Overseas operations Outside of Turkey, Bayraktar said that TPAO may drill a
second well off Somalia, but did not confirm any results from the company's first well, Curad-1, which it began drilling in April. The drilling campaign by TPAO marked the first on three offshore blocks in which the company has exploration and production rights.
TPAO's seismic survey vessel Oruç Reis, which conducted the Somali surveys, will now be dispatched to Pakistan, Bayraktar said. TPAO holds a 25% stake and operating rights for the Asian country's Eastern Offshore Indus Block-C, awarded in a licensing round in October 2025. The vessel's dispatch was previously delayed due to the situation in the Strait of Hormuz, according to Turkish officials.
Bayraktar also commented on operations outside Turkey where TPAO does not hold operating rights.
In Iraq, Bayraktar said that TPAO would begin to receive its share from the country's Kirkuk oilfields, home to an estimated 3 billion barrels of reserves, as early as 2027, after acquiring a 15% stake in the development in July. Other stakeholders in the BP Energy Company of Kirkuk Limited include BP (43%) and ConocoPhillips (42%), with the contract also covering work in Iraq's Bai Hassan, Jambur and Khabbaz fields.
Bayraktar also mentioned the possibility of TPAO becoming further involved in Libya, where the company holds a 40% share in offshore Block O7 along with operator Repsol (40%) and MOL Group (20%). For new acquisitions, areas of interest include North America and Africa, he said, without providing further details.

UKMTO reports 12 attacks on shipping in past week

Front-month ICE Brent settled at the highest level since May on Oct. 16 as traders as stepped-up attacks on shipping near the Strait of Hormuz pressured Middle East supply.
ICE December Brent climbed $3.94/barrel to $109.86/b and was the highest since May 19, while NYMEX November WTI settled $3.44/b higher at $101.41/b, the highest since Sept. 17.
"The Middle East conflict continues to dominate supply conditions," SAXO Head of Commodity Strategy Ole Hansen said in an Oct. 16 note. "Attacks on vessels in and around the Strait of Hormuz, combined with exceptionally high freight and insurance costs, continue to disrupt the economics of moving crude and refined products."
Twelve ships have been attacked in or near the Strait of Hormuz in the past seven days, according to a UK Maritime Trade Operations report released Oct. 16.
Middle East crude exports are trending lower in October, even as traffic through the Strait of Hormuz recovered to 16 transits on Oct. 15 from 12 the previous day, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea.
Total Middle East crude exports, including Saudi volumes from Yanbu, are tracking at 11.9 million barrels/day so far in October, down from 15 million b/d in September, preliminary CAS data showed.

Platts assessed Azeri Light CIF Augusta $5.33/b lower day on day at a $10.36/b premium to Dated Brent on Oct. 15, the largest daily move on record, after three trades were done in the Platts Market on Close assessment process on the day.

By the time Socar joined the offer side at around 1530 London time, BP and Itochu had lowered their offers to below $13/b, a drop of around $3/b from when they were first shown at around 1430.
Socar's offer was lifted by OMV at an $11.45/b premium, while BP and Itochu had their offers lifted at $10.20/b and $10.40/b, respectively, shortly before the MOC close.
Socar subsequently reoffered a cargo, which remained outstanding at the end of the pricing session at a $10.55/b premium to Dated Brent.
"It's like trying to catch a falling knife," one Mediterranean trader said of the rapid decline in differentials.
Offer levels fell in increments of 5 cents/b per minute, with BP and Itochu making more than 100 price moves over two hours before tradeable levels were found.

The correction in Azeri Light differentials came as other regional grades, including Es Sider and Saharan Blend, continued to soften.
"High prices that we saw last week eroded refining margins, and the market turned long with all the supply available," a second Mediterranean source said.
The trajectory of Azeri Light differentials, however, differed from that of North African grades, which are assessed on an FOB basis and did not receive the same support from elevated freight rates as delivered Azeri Light.

China unlikely to refill oil reserves without sharp price drop
Backwardation deters strategic buying plans
Imports rise as depleted commercial stocks cut additional supplies
December arrivals expected to drop on costs
China is unlikely to replenish its crude stocks unless oil prices and freight costs fall sharply, two crude procurement managers and a crude trader at Chinese state-run companies, as well as an executive at a state think tank, told Platts Oct. 16.
Improving imports over September-November would slow China's destocking activities, as logistics uncertainties have led to uneven crude arrivals, while inflows are likely to fall in December due to high prices, the sources added.
"The market is in backwardation, and we cannot rule out the possibility of a sudden drop in crude prices, making it an unfavorable time for stockpiling," the crude trader said.
On Oct. 16, the Singapore-assessed physical Dubai time spread was at $2.69/b, indicating backwardation despite narrowing from $3.695/b the day before, Platts data showed.
"We still largely take a hand-to-mouth approach amid the volatile market, but logistics uncertainties mean the pace of deliveries can fluctuate," said a crude procurement manager at a state-run refinery, adding that the company has had to increase crude imports only because its depleted commercial crude stocks can no longer provide additional supplies.

Russia's first cargoes of Vostok oil set to arrive in China by Oct. 19

The first two cargoes of Russian Vostok oil are due to arrive in China by Oct. 19, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea, as Russia bids to develop a major new oil supply network from Northern Russia.
Russia's largest oil producer Rosneft, which is developing Vostok Oil, officially launched the project on Sept. 6, when it loaded the first oil at the Bukhta Sever Port on the Kara Sea.

Most supplies are traveling via the Northern Sea Route, which runs through Russia's Arctic waters with a navigational season typically starting from June-July, and provides significant time and cost savings compared to alternatives.
CAS data shows two tankers -- the Vladimir Monomakh and the Vladimir Vinogradov -- both loaded with 100,050 mt of crude and condensate approaching China. Another tanker, the Ravelin, carrying Vostok Oil is traveling via the Atlantic and is due to arrive in China by the end of October.
Analysts, however, see winter weather conditions as a significant challenge to export plans.
Independent energy analyst George Voloshin said that without ice-breaker escorts, open-water and standard ice-class tankers must wrap up operations by late October, as the open-water navigation window in the Yenisey Gulf and Kara Sea closes rapidly between late October and mid-November.
"Given the current global shortage of Arc7 crude carriers dedicated to Vostok Oil, regular commercial liftings will taper off sharply once heavy ice forms, unless Russia diverts high-ice-class tonnage from other Arctic basins," he added.
Russia exported 8.3 million barrels of crude, condensate and fuel oil via the NSR to China or East Asian waters for offshore transfers in June-September, including 3.9 million barrels of Urals crude from Primorsk, according to CAS. This compares with 9.91 million barrels for the whole of 2025. The estimates do not take into account ships carrying cargo from the Russian Arctic and western ports that are sailing eastwards but have not yet passed the Bering Strait.

OIL FUTURES: US crude pushes to one-month high amid Middle East supply concerns
Four vessels hit near Hormuz since Oct. 16
WTI reaches its highest level since Sept. 15
Trump waives sanctions on Russian oil buyers
US crude futures settled at one-month highs Oct. 19, as continued attacks on shipping pressured Middle East supply outlooks.
NYMEX November WTI settled $2.25/barrel higher at $103.66/b, while ICE December Brent declined 31 cents/b to end the session at $109.55/b.
Four ship attacks have been reported since Oct. 16, including a rocket-propelled grenade attack on a tanker near Jubail, Saudi Arabia, in the Gulf region, according to UK Maritime Trade Operations.
The latest attack occurred Oct. 18, when a tugboat traveling southbound northeast of Khor Fakkan, UAE, outside the Strait of Hormuz, was struck by two unknown projectiles, resulting in damage and a "minor casualty," UKMTO said on its website. The tug subsequently continued its journey, according to UKMTO.

CHINA DATA: Sep crude throughput hits 6-month high of 14 mil b/d
Sep domestic crude output at 4.45 mil b/d
Apparent stock draw reaches 531,000 b/d in Sep
Q3 GDP expands 4.5% year over year
China's crude throughput recovered to a six-month high of 14.04 million barrels/day (57.48 million metric tons) in September, according to data from the National Bureau of Statistics on Oct. 19.
Despite an 8.4% year-over-year reduction among the same sample of refineries monitored by NBS, the volume crossed the 14-million-b/d mark for the first time since reaching 14.58 million b/d in March, the data showed.
In September, combined crude throughput at China's 49 state-owned refineries rose for a fourth consecutive month to a six-month high of 8.39 million b/d, Platts data showed. Based on a combined refining capacity of 11 million b/d, utilization reached 76.4%, marking a third consecutive monthly increase since June.
A month-over-month increase in the country's throughput, on a barrels-per-day basis, remained despite being partly offset by private refiners cutting throughput since mid-September amid higher feedstock costs, even as they recently received about 60.57 million mt of flash crude import quotas.
S&P Global Energy CERA estimated that state-run refineries' refining margins rose 5 cents/b month over month to $22.44/b, while independent refiners' margins eased by $1.62/b to $4.2/b, according to its monthly report dated Sept. 30.
On a barrels-per-day basis, the average throughput in September represented 0.8% growth from August. But on a metric-tons basis, the volume fell 2.7% month over month from 59.07 million mt, NBS data showed.

Stock draw The increase in throughput outpaced the rise in supply,
leading to an apparent stock draw of about 531,000 b/d in September, Platts calculations based on throughput, output and import data showed.
The higher daily throughput came as China's domestic crude output also hit a six-month high of 4.45 million b/d (18.21 million mt) in September, NBS data showed, while imports rose to a five-month high of 9.06 million b/d, according to customs data released Oct. 14.

In a video address to the World Petroleum Congress last week, US Energy Secretary Chris Wright acknowledged the possibility of easing sanctions on shadow-fleet tankers linked to Russia, Venezuela and Iran in a US bid to combat soaring freight costs.
Landed differentials of WTI Midland in Europe were again demonstrated lower during the Platts Market on Close assessment process Oct. 19.

barrel and $12/b over Dated Brent, were left outstanding at the London close.

Petroecuador resumes crude shipments from Colombia after nine-month pause
SOTE, OSLA pipelines handle 11,900 b/d from Colombia Ecuador and Colombia renew energy cooperation SOTE operating at capacity, Petroecuador says

Petroecuador has begun pumping crude from neighboring Colombia nine months after a trade war suspended operations.
Colombia's Ecopetrol and GeoPark subsidiary Amerisur are sending a total 11,900 barrels/day through Petroecuador's 360,000 b/d SOTE and 26-kilometer Lago Agrio-San Miguel, or OSLA, pipelines in a deal worth $16.8 million/year, Ecuador's state-run oil company said in a statement over the weekend.
Pumping operations restarted after investor-friendly Presidents Daniel Noboa of Ecuador and Abelardo de la Espriella of Colombia renewed energy cooperation agreements earlier in October.
De la Espriella's predecessor Gustavo Petro suspended electricity sales to Ecuador in January after President

Noboa imposed trade tariffs and cut off access to Colombian crude.
De la Espriella took office in August, pledging to renew ties with Ecuador, Peru, Chile and Argentina, with whom relations had been strained since Marxist President Petro rose to power in 2022.
The SOTE pipeline, which links Petroecuador's Lago Agrio oilfields with its 110,000 b/d Esmeraldas oil refinery on the northwestern Pacific coast, had been transporting oil from Colombia since 2013.
The pipeline is operating at capacity after repairing a leak and containing an oil spill in the week prior, the company added.
Ecuador was producing 466,080 b/d through Oct. 18, according to the Energy Ministry. Petroecuador accounts for about 80% of the country's output.

Europe's diesel market remains extremely tight despite jet fuel cracks outstripping gains in the motor fuel market for the first time in months, signaling a new and potentially more complex phase for European fuel markets.
After hovering around $60/barrel on average between Oct. 1 and Oct. 12, the European benchmark physical FOB ARA 10ppm diesel barge crack rose above $74/barrel on Oct. 16, for the first time since Sept. 30.
And while the European physical diesel crack stayed above the jet fuel crack since mid-June until mid-September, it now trades well below its counterpart.
The spread between the European jet fuel FOB FARAG bargeto-Brent crude crack and the ULSD FOB barge-to-Brent crude

crack was as wide as $11.75/b on Oct.16. "Everyone in Europe is so focused on diesel, even anticipating
a potential diesel crisis, and as a result refineries have not produced enough jet fuel," said James Simpson, head of aviation research at S&P Global Energy CERA. He added that in North America, it is a "very different story," with diesel prices trading around 5%-8% above jet fuel in recent weeks.
While the European benchmark diesel crack has rebounded in recent days since the beginning of October, it is still well below the peak of $98.01/b reached on Sept. 1.
But a higher jet fuel crack does not automatically mean that all refineries will maximize this fuel, with most of them potentially staying cautious, according to market participants.
"Gasoil cracks have pulled backed but are still very elevated, people continue to max out runs on diesel," a market source told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy.
The International Energy Agency said Oct.15 that it has specific plans in place to release more than 20 million barrels of diesel "as soon as possible." No details on the dates for release were provided. Only 8 million barrels of emergency oil stocks were released in September, marking a significant decline in inventories releases in recent months, according to IEA.
"Weaker [diesel] margins come from reinforcement on the crude side. With strong structure on the inputs, [we] automatically see weaker cracks. But nothing has changed on the products side," a European diesel producer told Platts.
New stock release announcements are not something which can support fuel market significantly, according to another market source. "Europe is releasing from an already announced quota and presenting that as news," the source said.
"Even if headlines are dictating price now, the structural and logistical problems should maintain tightness," according to the source, who asked for anonymity because of no authorization to speak publicly.
No lasting price relief in sight The diesel market situation is influenced by several factors,
including efforts to influence prices from the US, according to William O'Neil, principal analyst at S&P Global Energy CERA.
"On the federal level, this includes the dyed diesel waiver, supporting the G7 diesel inventory release, and the Russia diesel deal. Additionally, many US states have cut diesel taxes and loosened dyed diesel restrictions, echoing the federal waiver," O'Neil said.
S&P Global Energy assessed that half of US states have partially or fully waived dyed diesel rules, as of Oct. 16. The more important factor for the global diesel market is the perception that a US diesel export ban is becoming increasingly unlikely, according to O'Neil.
"But the levers of softening (with the exception of tax cuts in the US) probably aren't significant enough to deliver lasting price relief. We don't think the Russia deal, for example, is likely to substantially and durably lower global diesel prices unless Ukraine is on board," he said.

Six VLCCs The six VLCCs that transited Hormuz on Oct. 17 all exited the
Gulf: the Liberia-flagged Arzanah carrying Basrah Heavy crude from Iraq, the Cameroon-flagged Matelo, the Saudi Arabiaflagged Samha, the Kuwait-flagged Umm al Aish, the Liberiaflagged Emerald Shine and the Panama-flagged Kiku operated by Trafigura, according to CAS data. Kiku left the Gulf around Oct. 17 in laden condition, although the cargo is not known, CAS said.
Trafigura said it had no comment on its ships. South Korea's Sinokor-operated VLCC Peru reappeared off India's west coast on Oct. 18 after leaving Hormuz around Oct. 16, laden with Saudi Arabia's Arab Medium grade crude and was last seen heading for Vadinar, India, where it is expected to conduct ship-to-ship transfers, CAS said. Sinokor's Egypt Prosperity exited Hormuz on Oct. 16 in laden condition and was last seen heading toward offshore Fujairah, UAE, CAS said. The Panama-flagged LPG carrier Yellow Stone left the Gulf on Oct. 17 after loading at Sitra, Bahrain, in August, CAS said. The ship had spent nearly two months between Port Rashid in Dubai and Mina Saqr in Ras al Khaimah, both in the UAE, CAS said. For the week ending Oct. 18, total Strait of Hormuz traffic was estimated at 154 transits, down from 160 transits in the week prior, CAS said. Over the same period, crossings through the US naval blockade line dropped to 430 transits from 443 transits, while Bab al-Mandab traffic dropped to 266 ships from 279, according to CAS data.

Strait of Hormuz transits climbed to 23 ships on Oct. 18, with more product tankers and bulk carriers taking the chokepoint into and out of the Gulf, S&P Global Commodities at Sea said in an Oct. 19 report.
Product tanker transits rose to five from four ships, while bulk carriers had eight crossings on Oct. 18, up from zero on Oct. 17, CAS said. Total Hormuz transits rose from 15 ships on Oct. 17, according to the CAS report.
In line with the increased product traffic, refined product exports through Hormuz rose to 1.1 million barrels on Oct. 18 from 995,335 barrels on Oct. 17, according to CAS figures.
Crude exports fell to 7.35 million barrels from 7.94 million barrels over the same period, according to CAS.
VLCC traffic dropped to just one ship on Oct. 18 from six on Oct. 17, according to the CAS report. Transits through the Bab al-Mandab Strait into the Red Sea dropped to 34 ships on Oct. 18 from 39 on Oct. 17, according to CAS data.
