IEA cuts oil demand outlook as diesel shortage extends into 2027
Diesel accounts for a quarter of the decline
Refining margins diverge as freight costs surge
Global refinery runs forecast to fall in 2026
The International Energy Agency cut its forecasts for global oil demand this year and slashed its estimate for next year's demand recovery, Oct. 14, warning that diesel supplies will remain tight well into next year as the Middle East war continues to hit supplies and fuels pump prices.
World oil demand is now set to decline by an average of 2.6 million barrels/day in 2026 as the impact of the war in the Middle East weighs on markets and demand growth is not expected to return to positive territory until March 2027, the IEA said in its latest monthly Oil Market Report. The agency kept its average 2026 world oil demand at 102.4 million b/d but cut 2027 demand to 104.4 million b/d from 105 million b/d in its September report, leaving 2027 consumption about 620,000 b/d, or 0.6%, below 2025 levels.
The IEA forecast global demand falling by 2.5 million b/d year over year in the fourth quarter of 2026, following contractions of 5 million b/d in the second quarter and 3.7 million b/d in the third quarter. In September, the IEA had forecast a 2.5 million b/d decline in global oil demand in 2026 and a 2.6 million b/d rebound in 2027.
"As the Iran war manifests itself through restricted product availability, soaring fuel prices and a deteriorating economic outlook, diesel will register 25% of the drop," the IEA said.

INTERVIEW: India seeks to attract upstream capital with offshore reforms, deepwater drilling support
Upstream reforms to address investment risks across full cycle
Scope to build partnerships with national oil companies
Samudra Manthan conceived as long-term energy security initiative
India has opened a large swath of previously inaccessible offshore basins as it seeks to attract investment in exploration and production, combining new acreage with economic incentives and support through the Samudra Manthan program, Neeraj Mittal, secretary at the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, said Oct. 13.
The reforms are designed to address investment risks across the full exploration and production cycle, from evaluating geological prospects to developing discoveries and bringing them on stream. However, the government recognizes that offshore exploration remains uncertain and capital-intensive, requiring patient, long-term capital, technical expertise and disciplined execution, Mittal told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, during an oil markets podcast.
"My message to companies, global companies, is to examine the opportunities closely and engage with us. There is considerable scope to build partnerships, if required, with national oil companies, which we think would be commercially rewarding and would contribute to India's domestic energy supply as well. The opportunity ahead will turn a new accessible frontier into a sustained program of exploration and development," Mittal said.
Investors will have access to stable contracts, deepwater fiscal incentives, publicly funded seismic surveys, shared infrastructure and conditional government support for frontier drilling, he added.

China's Huajin Aramco receives first Saudi crude cargo ahead of startup
300,000 b/d facility nears commercial operation
Joint venture receives Arab Medium crude on VLCC
Huajin Aramco Petrochemical Co. has received its first Saudi crude cargo, marking a key milestone in establishing feedstock supplies as the northeastern China refining and petrochemical complex moves closer to startup, Saudi Aramco said Oct. 14.
The cargo's arrival underscores progress at the Panjin-based project in Liaoning province, which is currently in the production preparation stage, according to a statement posted on Aramco's WeChat account.
Huajin Aramco, or HAPCO, is a joint venture between Norinco Group, Panjin Xincheng Industrial Group and Saudi Aramco, with shareholdings of 51%, 19% and 30%, respectively.
The project includes a 300,000 b/d refinery, a 1.65 million mt/ year ethylene plant and a 2 million mt/year paraxylene plant.
The crude cargo was carried by the Saudi-flagged VLCC Kassab. The vessel loaded Arab Medium crude at Saudi Arabia's Juaymah terminal in the Persian Gulf around Sept. 3 and discharged at HAPCO's terminal on Oct. 6, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea data.
The latest shipment follows the arrival of HAPCO's first VLCC crude cargo in September, a key step in commissioning logistics and storage facilities ahead of startup.

MALAYSIA DATA: Aug total oil product output up 10.6% YOY; fuel oil, gasoline falls
August combined output rises 7.1% MOM
Crude oil imports, exports fall
Malaysia's combined oil product output rose 10.6% year on year to 3.64 million metric tons in August, preliminary data from the Department of Statistics showed Oct. 12, with kerosene, LPG, gasoil and naphtha production increasing.
The combined output of oil products in August also grew 7.1% month over month, led by kerosene, LPG and gasoil, the data showed.
Malaysia's combined oil products include gasoline, gasoil, fuel oil, kerosene, LPG and naphtha.
In the first eight months of 2026, the combined output of Malaysia's oil products totaled 26.82 million mt, up 5.2% year over year, with kerosene, gasoil, gasoline and naphtha production increasing over the period.
Malaysia produced 1.84 million mt of gasoil in August 2026, up 8.22% from 1.70 million mt a year earlier and up 12.50% from 1.63 million mt in July, the data showed.
Production of kerosene surged 43.88% year over year and rose

15.20% month over month to 415,849 mt in August. The increase in middle distillate output coincided with an
uptrend in refining margins. The Platts-assessed FOB Singapore 10 ppm sulfur gasoil
derivative crack spread against the front-month Dubai swap -- a measure of the product's relative strength against the crude from which it was refined -- averaged $67.42/b in August, up from $61.79/b in July and $44.98/b in June
Similarly, the FOB Singapore jet fuel/kerosene derivative crack spread against the front-month Dubai swap averaged $64.45/b in August, up from $60.26/b in July and $44.86/b in June, Platts data showed.
Among light ends, Malaysia's gasoline production fell 9.11% month over month and 1.37% year over year to 546,400 mt in August.
Malaysia's gasoline output could rise on the back of the return of the 300,000 b/d Malaysia's Melaka refinery from its planned maintenance works in early October, Platts reported earlier.
Malaysia's naphtha production fell for the second consecutive month, declining 8.40% month over month to 371,770 mt in August. Year over year, production grew 25.85%.
From January to August, naphtha output was up 15.88% to 3.29 million mt.
Platts assessed the benchmark C+F Japan naphtha price at an average of $792.71/mt in August, down slightly from $798.18/ mt in July.
Meanwhile, Malaysia produced 276,878 mt of LPG in August, the country's highest output since May 2025, when it produced 314,688 mt. The country's August LPG production rebounded 34.82% from July and 11.12% from August 2025, after two consecutive monthly declines and four consecutive months of year-over-year declines.
Consumers primarily use LPG for household cooking, with the remainder largely exported as pressurized cargoes to countries such as Thailand, Vietnam and the Philippines.
According to S&P Global Commodities at Sea, Malaysia exported 116,000 mt of LPG in August, down more than 17% from 140,000 mt in July. The bulk of its exports went to China, with 50,000 mt, Cambodia, with 19,000 mt and Thailand, with 13,000 mt.
Separately, Malaysia produced 2.19 million mt of LNG in August, down 1.05% year on year and 13.09% from July. Its LNG production in the first eight months of the year totaled 17.78 million mt, down 2.90% year on year, the data showed.
The country exported 19.82 million mt of LNG in JanuaryAugust, up 17% year on year, the department said Sept. 28.
Malaysia imported 24 million mt of refined products in January-August, up 9.6% year on year, the data released Sept. 28 showed. The country also exported 22.88 million mt of oil products in the period, down 2.7% year on year.
Malaysia exported 1.52 million mt of oil products to Vietnam in the first eight months, up 33.7% year on year, making it Vietnam's third-largest refined product supplier during the period, preliminary data from Vietnam Customs showed.

BP eyes next phase of GTA gas project 'quite soon' as Senegal woos oil investors
2.5 million mt/y gas field came online in 2024
Senegal offers 104 licenses to new investors
Dakar takes more pragmatic tone towards industry
The next phase of the huge Greater Tortue Ahmeyim gas project on the Senegal-Mauritania border could come "quite soon," operator BP PLC's country head said Oct. 14, while Senegal's energy minister signaled a shift in tone toward the oil and gas industry.
During a panel discussion at the Africa Energy Week conference in Cape Town, Massaer Cisse, BP's vice president and head of country, said GTA required "quite a unique approach to developing shared resources," emphasizing the importance of cooperation among the companies and a pair of host governments.
"We are looking forward to progressing it," Cisse said. "We are working with our partners to make sure the next phase of this project comes along quite soon."
He added that the growing importance of LNG in international energy markets - and the competition to secure cargoes - means GTA "has a big role to play."
Phase 1 of GTA came online at 2.5 million metric tons/year in 2024, with its first cargo loading earlier last year. It could reach 5 million mt/y in its second phase and 10 million mt/y in its third

and final phase. However, Senegalese officials have bristled publicly at the
pace of the ramp-up, complaining in particular about the limited domestic gas supply from the project as is. A Phase 1+ is now expected to precede a full second phase, with a guaranteed domestic supply built in.
US-based player Kosmos Energy Ltd. also holds a nonoperated interest.
Cisse's comments follow a softening in approach by Dakar, with President Bassirou Diomaye Faye dismissing populist Prime Minister Ousmane Sonko in May and recently replacing Senegal's energy minister as part of a broad reshuffle.
Addressing the conference, new minister El Hadji Abdourahmane Diouf said Senegal was seeking new partners for its 104 available onshore and offshore oil and gas blocks, after five were signed by Italian major Eni SpA.
"Sangomar and GTA demonstrate the basin's potential, but much remains to be evaluated," Diouf said, referring to Woodside's 100,000 barrels/day Sangomar oil project, which he said shipped 36 million barrels last year.
He said the country was abiding by international processes, working to "ensure that potential investors know the country is ready to work with them" and "extending its hands" for partnership. The agenda included assurances of investor returns and clarification of procedures for potential partners.
Tone shift It marks a shift in tone from Diouf's predecessor, Birame
Souleye Diop, who floated potential contract renegotiations and audits alongside vocal demands for domestic gas supply.
Ibrahima Noba, the director general of hydrocarbons at the energy ministry, told the conference that Senegal was focused on accelerating exploration to make new discoveries and promised to "review our legal framework...to attract investment."
Diouf noted, however, that the country continues to prioritize projects with built-in domestic gas supply, adding that even associated gas from Sangomar could be used locally.
On Sept. 10, Senegal laid the first pipeline for the northern segment of its new national gas network, a key effort to expand gas supply for power and industry, Diouf said.
It is also hoping to secure a new partner for the YakaarTeranga gas field, which Kosmos previously operated before handing its stake back to state-run Societe Des Petroles Du Senegal in April. The offshore field contains as much as 25 Tcf of gas, according to Senegalese officials.
Diouf was speaking just weeks after Petrosen agreed with Turkey's Yamata to build a second 4 million mt/year oil refinery to process Sangomar crude.
The $2 billion-$3 billion project should supply the local market and create opportunities in distribution, storage and petrochemicals, Diouf said.

Novak says Russian oil production set to rise after plant restarts
Refineries resume after maintenance downtime
US-Russia energy talks progress at multiple levels
Diesel exports target 3.5M mt/month by end of 2026
Russia's Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said Oct.14 the country's oil production is set to recover and grow as refineries return from maintenance, while negotiations with the US on energy supply contracts are actively underway.
"The situation is improving. A number of our plants have resumed operations following maintenance, and the situation today is significantly better than it was a month or two weeks ago," Novak said on the sidelines of the Russian Energy week, according to TASS.
Russian oil production will "recover and increase in line with the capabilities" of infrastructure and refining facilities, Novak said, adding that the deal with the US is "a positive signal for the economy, linked to increased refining volumes at oil refineries, higher production levels, and additional tax revenue for the budget," he said.
On Oct.10, the Russian government partially lifted a diesel export ban implemented at the beginning of July. The restrictions were weakened after negotiations with the US administration.
The US Treasury Department issued a sanctions waiver on Oct. 9, allowing the sale, delivery and import of Russian-origin diesel fuel, including importation into the US.
Novak said negotiations between the Russian and US energy ministries on supply contracts are ongoing at both the ministerial and corporate levels, though no agreements have been finalized.
"A mechanism will be found to facilitate these supplies," Novak said.
Russia's Deputy Prime Minister said that any supplies agreed under the framework would move to global markets, including to the US, through direct contracts.
Novak also acknowledged that attacks on energy infrastructure remain a material risk to supply recovery, though he said defensive measures are being strengthened.
"Of course, much depends on how the situation regarding attacks on energy infrastructure unfolds; although our plants are being defended, we are strengthening that protection. In this sense, this represents one reason for improving the situation," he said.
Novak reiterated that Russia is ready to increase its diesel exports to global markets to as much as 3.5 million metric tons/ month by the end of 2026.
Before the start of the refining crisis triggered by Ukraine's attacks on the country's energy infrastructure, Russia was the second-largest diesel exporter. Russian diesel and gasoil exports averaged about 3.3 million mt/month over January-May, roughly in line with the 2025 volumes, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea. Exports have started to decline from June, reaching 479,000 mt in September, the lowest monthly volume on

record, according to CAS data, which dates back to 2016. About 45% of Russia's primary refining capacity was offline as
of the end of September, according to S&P Global Energy CERA analysts.
Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said that Russia must stop striking Ukraine's energy system and killing Ukrainians if it wants Ukraine to halt its strikes on Russian oil refineries and oil exports.

Houthis target Saudi Aramco facilities in Dhahran, Riyadh
Group vows more strikes on Saudi oil facilities
Dhahran administrative building catches fire: SPA
Houthi militants have attacked Saudi Arabian Oil Co.'s sites in Dhahran, home to the company's headquarters, and in Riyadh, while vowing to continue targeting the company's oil facilities, according to an Oct. 14 statement from the Yemen-based group.
"The Yemeni Armed Forces have carried out a qualitative military operation targeting Aramco Company in Dhahran in the Eastern Province, King Khalid International Airport and the Aramco refinery in Riyadh," Houthi Spokesperson Yahya Saree said in a statement on X, adding that the operations would "continue in the coming days, striking the Saudi enemy's airports and oil facilities."
An Aramco administrative building in Dhahran was targeted Oct. 13 and caught fire, the state-run Saudi Press Agency said Oct. 14, citing an official energy ministry source. There were no injuries or fatalities, SPA said.
Aramco did not immediately respond to an email request for official comment on the Houthis' statement Oct. 14.
On Oct. 3, Houthis targeted "the Aramco Company" in the Saudi capital, Riyadh, using "ballistic missiles and drones," according to a post on X by Saree, who said a fire had erupted at the targeted sites. The Saudi energy ministry did not confirm the attack.
On Oct. 11, Houthis said Aramco's Shedgum gas plant and its Tanajib oil facility in the Eastern Province were targeted and struck, resulting in fires at both locations. Prior to the strikes on Aramco's facilities on Oct. 11, Houthis said they had launched missile and drone attacks on Riyadh's King Khalid Airport and Dammam's King Fahd Airport.

VENEZUELA DATA: Crude output edges up to 1.218 million b/d in Sept.
Orinoco Belt crude production 690,000 b/d
West output leads East, Barinas-Apure production
Offshore output 14,100 b/d; PDVSA Gas 4,900 b/d
In September, average crude production from Venezuelan state oil company PDVSA and its foreign partners, including output under productive production contracts, or CPPHs, edged up to 1.218 million barrels/day from 1.213 million b/d in August,

according to preliminary data from the Ministry of Hydrocarbons reviewed by Platts, part of S&P Global Energy.
Venezuelan production remained broadly stable, although it was affected by prolonged power outages, especially in the west, and delays in upgrading the infrastructure that supports oil operations, according to the report.
The Ministry of Hydrocarbons report contains preliminary, unofficial information that may change after consolidation. The preliminary data does not include condensate production, which the report estimated at 66,000 b/d for August.
Western fields Output from western fields in the states of Zulia and Trujillo
remained at 359,000 b/d in September, unchanged from the average for August.
The preliminary report showed that production from PDVSAoperated fields in the west remained at an average of 70,000 b/d. Production from joint ventures and productive production contracts remained at an average of 288,000 b/d in September.
Production at Petroboscan, owned 60% by PDVSA and 40% by Chevron Corp., fell to 115,307 b/d in September from 116,757 b/d in August.
At the end of August, the US departments of State and War reached a deal with North American Blue Energy Partners, or NABEP, to expand oil production in Venezuela. As part of the deal, NABEP will have operating control of 17 fields, including five Petrozamora fields in Zulia state that it already controlled.
Petrozamora's production rose to 98,369 b/d in September from 97,816 b/d in August. Production at seven other fields in Zulia state awarded to NABEP -- Lagunillas Lago, Intercampo Norte, Tia Juana Lago, Lagomar, Rosa Medio, Lago Medio and Lago 5 -- totaled 51,493 b/d in September, down from an August average of 53,626 b/d.
Until August, the Lagunillas Lago and Lago 5 fields were operated by China Concord Resources under productive production contracts signed with the Venezuelan government in 2024. Meanwhile, the Intercampo Norte field was operated by the Sinovenezolana joint venture, according to previous reports.
In September, production at selected oil fields in Zulia state was as follows: Petroregional del Lago, owned 60% by PDVSA and 40% by Maurel & Prom SA, produced 22,131 b/d; Petroquiriquire Occidente, owned 60% by PDVSA and 40% by Spain's Repsol SA, produced 35,750 b/d.
Fields in Barinas and Apure states operated by Colombia's Colven under a productive production contract produced 10,000 b/d in September.
PDVSA's production and participation contracts, or CPPs, now called primary hydrocarbon activity contracts, were incorporated as a business model under the reform of the Hydrocarbons Law approved at the end of January.

Venezuela's PDVSA eyes 1.5 mil b/d crude output by end-2027
PDVSA expects to reach 1.3 mil b/d by Dec. 31, 2026
Projects 18,000-20,000 b/d growth per month for 2027
Increasing oil output requires more electricity: expert
State-owned company PDVSA lowered its 2026 oil output target, estimating that Venezuela will reach 1.3 million barrels/ day by Dec. 31, according to Hector Obregón, the company's president, who spoke to business leaders in Caracas.
"We project sustained growth of 18,000-20,000 b/d per month throughout 2027, ending the year at nearly 1.5 million b/d," Obregón said in a summary of his speech published by PDVSA on

the social media platform Telegram on Oct. 9. PDVSA is expected to reach crude oil production of 1.37
million b/d by December, the company's executive vice president, Jovanny Martinez, said during a presentation to oil industry executives in Caracas on April 27.
"Oil production has grown 8% over the past 12 months," Venezuela's Interim President Delcy Rodríguez said Oct. 8 at the opening of the business event, according to a post on Telegram.
Rodríguez presented business leaders with a figure of 1.218 million b/d, including condensates and natural gas liquids, for September, representing an increase of 90,000 b/d from September 2025.
In August, Venezuela reported average OPEC production of 1.201 million b/d, while secondary sources estimated production at 1.145 million b/d, according to the Monthly Oil Market Report.
"To date, PDVSA has signed 61 strategic agreements in the oil and gas sector," Rodríguez said.
Obregón said that 44 negotiations are still ongoing and that the company projects closing out 2026 with a total of 105 signed contracts.
These agreements include memoranda of understanding, partnerships and productive production contracts, or CPPHs, signed following the reform of the hydrocarbons law in late January. This reform provides greater flexibility in both the tax framework and other legal aspects, such as arbitration and private-sector participation throughout the oil industry.
Obregón did not explain the reasons for the adjustment to the targets.
Operational hurdles Business leaders who attended the event highlighted several
challenges hindering oil production growth. "Without [enough] electricity or oil-cement, it is very difficult
to accelerate oil production plans in the short term," said a business leader from the construction sector who spoke with Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, on condition of anonymity.
"There is not enough Class G cement -- which is resistant to high pressures and aggressive fluids -- used in drilling, completion and well reentry operations."
Power shortages affect the entire country, particularly the oil industry, which depends on the national power grid.
According to available figures, the total infrastructure has a theoretical capacity of 30,000-34,000 MW; however, actual electricity generation is about 13,000 MW, representing less than 40% of the installed capacity.

Platts Oilgram News

October 14, 2026

September milder in Europe, but colder in Canada and Japan

September was milder in Europe, but colder in Canada and Japan than a year ago, according to data obtained from CustomWeather and gathered by Platts.

A total of 19 cities geographically representative of Europe had 26.1% fewer heating degree days in September than in September 2025. The total for the SeptemberSeptember period was 26.1% fewer degree days than the similar period last year and 35.1% fewer than normal.

Eight cities geographically representative of Canada had 48% more degree days in September than in September 2025. The total for the September-September period was 48% more than the similar period last year and 4.7% fewer than normal.

Four cities geographically representative of Japan had 45.9% more degree days in September than in September 2025. The total for the September-September period was 45.9% more than the similar period last year and 27.5% fewer than normal.

US degree days for September

The degree days indicator for US heating oil demand for September was 121.54% greater than for September 2025 and 44.11% less then normal.

The indicator is comprised of the average degree days for 18 geographically representative US cities, weighted to reflect the cities relative consumption of home heating oil.

The degree days data were compiled by Platts from reports by CustomWeather.
