JAPAN DATA: Sept US crude imports rise nearly 15-fold to 532,776 b/d
US supplies Japan 27.5% of crude imports Winter kerosene demand boosts appeal of WTI Midland Refiners take rare Argentine Medanito crude

Sanctioned tankers lift Russia's NSR oil exports to new record: CAS
Arctic oil shipments exceed 13 million barrels Trade lane can save bunker and charter costs Sanctioned fleet raises environmental worries

Japan's reliance on US crude continued to grow in September amid ongoing disruptions to tanker flows in the Middle East, with shipments from the North American supplier rising almost fifteenfold year over year to 532,776 barrels/ day and reaching a record high for the first three quarters, according to trade ministry data and industry sources contacted over Oct. 30-Nov. 2.
Japan imported 1.94 million b/d of crude oil in September, down 17.7% from a year earlier, while US crude accounted for 27.5% of total imports, or 532,776 b/d, up from 36,183 b/d a year earlier, according to the latest data issued by the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry on Oct. 30.

Russia has lifted its oil exports via the Northern Sea Route to a new high this year, with sanctioned tankers continuing to play a dominant role in the emerging Arctic trade.
During the NSR's navigational season in 2026, 13.2 million barrels of crude and petroleum products were loaded from Russian ports for shipments to Asia, compared with the previous record of 12.2 million barrels in 2025, and 10.8 million barrels in 2024, according to data from S&P Global Commodities at Sea.
This year's volumes include the first crude cargoes from Vostok, Rosneft's flagship Arctic oil project at Bukhta Sever Port on the Kara Sea, as well as the first fuel oil shipment since commercial ships started to utilize the NSR in 2019, according to CAS data.
Russia could reduce freight costs when delivering to Asian buyers via the trade lane compared with the usual Middle Eastern route, where security risks have increased due to the USIsraeli war with Iran, some analysts said.
"Major conflicts are pushing shipping northward," Mikhail Korostikov of the Carnegie Russia Eurasia Center said in a web note. "It's not the innate qualities of the Northern Sea Route that explain its current growing popularity, but the unusual extent of global conflict."
Tanker transit time from Russia's western oil hubs to East Asia via the Suez Canal typically takes six weeks or more, whereas the NSR route can theoretically cut the transit time by up to 40%.

Vietnam reinstates zero gasoline excise tax, cuts power price 2%
Government extends gasoline tax exemption to 2027
Retail fuel prices drop under new policy
Electricity rates decrease for all customers
Vietnam reinstated the zero special consumption tax on gasoline through March 2027 and cut average retail electricity prices by 2%, extending energy-cost relief as the government seeks to support macroeconomic stability and reduce costs for consumers and businesses, according to a government statement issued Oct. 31.
The special consumption tax on gasoline was reduced to zero from Nov. 1 through March 31, 2027, under Resolution 45/2026/ NQ-CP, signed by Deputy Prime Minister Nguyen Van Thang on Oct. 31. The statutory tax rates are 10% for conventional gasoline, 8% for E5 gasoline and 7% for E10 gasoline.
The government may shorten or extend the period depending on socioeconomic development requirements, energy security and the need to stabilize the domestic fuel market. Under the resolution, the Ministry of Industry and Trade may submit a proposed adjustment to the Ministry of Finance for government consideration.
Retail gasoline prices were lowered from Nov. 1 following the tax decision, with E5 RON92 falling by Dong 1,804/liter to no more than Dong 24,223/liter and E10 RON95-III dropping by Dong 1,575/ liter to no more than Dong 24,159/liter, according to the Ministry of Industry and Trade.
The ministry said the E10 RON95-III ceiling was Dong 64/ liter below that for E5 RON92. The ministry continued to set contributions to and spending from the gasoline price stabilization fund at Dong 0/liter for biofuel gasoline.
Vietnam previously reduced the special consumption tax on gasoline to zero from March 26 through April 15, then extended the exemption from April 16 through June 30.
The environmental protection tax remained at Dong 0/liter for gasoline, excluding ethanol, and for diesel, kerosene, fuel oil and jet fuel. The products also remained exempt from VAT declaration and payment, while preferential import duties on certain petroleum products remained at zero through Dec. 31.
Vietnam restored the special consumption tax on gasoline after the earlier exemption expired at the end of June. In considering its petroleum tax policy for July-September, the Ministry of Finance proposed retaining zero import duties, a zero environmental protection tax and the VAT exemption while restoring special consumption tax rates under the country's Special Consumption Tax Law.
Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, assessed Gasoline Unl 92 FOB Spore Cargo at $127.64/bbl on Oct. 30, down $7.46/bbl day over day.

Power prices cut Separately, state power utility Vietnam Electricity lowered
the country's average retail electricity price by 2% to Dong 2,159.9037/kWh, excluding value-added tax, effective Nov. 1.
The electricity price reduction followed a government meeting on gasoline and electricity pricing chaired by Deputy Prime Minister Nguyen Van Thang on Oct. 27. At the meeting, the government instructed EVN to review costs and maximize savings to enable a reduction in the average retail electricity price from Nov. 1.
The Ministry of Industry and Trade subsequently issued Decision 2892/QD-BCT on Oct. 31, setting retail electricity prices for customer groups and retail electricity suppliers from Nov. 1. EVN said the reduction was aimed at contributing to macroeconomic stability and supporting households and businesses.

INTERVIEW: Shipowners emphasize reliable bunker supply amid geopolitical uncertainty
Supply uncertainty heightens focus on reliability, port options
Share of term contracts increases
Company targets methanol service standards by end-2028
Shipowners are placing greater emphasis on reliable bunker supplies and a wider choice of refueling ports amid geopolitical uncertainty, Shanghai Chimbusco Marine Bunker's general manager Hang Xingping told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, in an interview.
International instability this year has heightened concerns about the security and consistency of conventional fuel supplies, encouraging shipowners to increase their use of term procurement, Hang said.
"Uncertainty over securing fuel supplies is increasing, so shipowners are more willing to use term contracts to ensure supply stability," he said.
Asked whether shipowners remained willing to use term contracts in a high-price environment, Hang said concerns about future fuel availability could make supply security a greater priority.
"They may now be giving greater priority to supply security and stability," he said.
"One clear change we have observed is that shipowners are more willing to enter into term contracts."
Hang said the share of term procurement was increasing, although he did not say it had overtaken spot buying or provide an updated percentage.
The company supplies high- and low-sulfur fuel oil and marine gasoil, sourcing fuel from both China and overseas, Hang said. It was increasing its focus on high-sulfur fuel oil while maintaining its established low-sulfur business, he added.

Reliable supply for alternative fuels Demand for biofuels, methanol and LNG was also becoming
more sustained, moving beyond small demonstration purchases, Hang said. The shift was particularly evident among leading shipowners that had invested in dual-fuel vessels, rather than across the entire industry.
However, buyers were still weighing the price difference between conventional and alternative fuels against the value of carbon-related cost savings before committing to supply agreements.
"Mutually binding term contracts for alternative fuels may still be relatively scarce," Hang said.
Green methanol suppliers and shipowners faced interdependent constraints, he said, adding that producers were reluctant to increase output without dependable demand, while buyers were hesitant to increase consumption without dependable supply.
"Shipowners need to make more attempts, and suppliers need to have more confidence," he said.
Larger physical deliveries illustrate the growth in alternativefuel activity. Chimbusco's Zhong Ran LV Neng 85 bunker vessel supplied approximately 7,500 metric tons of green methanol at Ningbo-Zhoushan on Sept. 30, according to a company statement on the same day.
Separately, Shanghai's methanol bunkering volume reached 51,200 mt in the first half of 2026, up 57.5% year over year, according to figures presented by Shanghai International Port Group at a July 22 municipal government briefing. The total included 14,700 mt of domestically produced green methanol, up 144%.
Platts assessed low-carbon methanol FOB Shanghai at $815/ mt on Oct. 30, unchanged from the day prior.
Methanol service standards Shanghai Chimbusco is also developing standardized
methanol bunkering procedures as the sector expands. In an Oct. 27 announcement, the company said it had
launched a municipal-level pilot project to standardize methanol bunkering services at an Oct. 20 meeting and training session.

Selected under Shanghai's 2026 standardization pilot program, the two-year project is scheduled to complete its work by the end of 2028.
The planned standards will cover personnel, equipment, materials, methods and the operating environment, encompassing procedures before, during and after bunkering, the company said.
Shanghai Chimbusco will draw on its methanol bunkering experience to develop the standards with technical support from Shanghai Academy of Quality Management Co., Ltd. The partners aim to produce practices that can be replicated and adopted more widely.
The initiative addresses the delivery side of a market that Hang said still needed time to develop.
"Shipowners also need to give port bunkering companies time to develop," he said.

Russia's ESPO Blend crude loadings rise to 41 ships in October: CAS
One cargo headed to India, rest to China
Dec ESPO Blend trade slow to pick up
Russian ESPO Blend crude loadings from the Kozmino port rose to around 41 ships in October, three ships higher than the month prior, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea data and multiple trade sources Nov. 2.
Of the 41 ESPO Blend shipments in October -- each about 100,000 metric tons -- all but one appeared to be destined for China, while the remaining vessel was pointed to India, CAS data showed.
October-delivery cargoes had traded over early August at discounts of around $1/b to premiums of up to $2/b to ICE Brent crude futures on a delivered basis to China, Platts reported earlier, with a Chinese state-owned company and an independent refiner heard to have snapped up at least two-thirds of the October program for the grade.
Market sources had then said that ESPO Blend cargoes were competitively priced, with arrivals viewed as more reliable than those from the Middle East due to recurring disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz and Bab al-Mandab strait.
Closer to the present, early traded levels for December-arrival ESPO Blend cargoes were heard at premiums of up to $25/b to ICE Brent crude futures on a DES China basis, three Asia-based trade sources said. Spot activity for December cargoes has been slow to pick up, as differing views on the grade's outlook have led to a wide bid-offer spread between buyers and sellers, the trade sources added.
This comes as Iranian crude feedstock supply has tightened for China's independent refiners. However, falling refining margins as well as increased purchases of regular Middle Eastern grades such as Iraqi Basrah crudes have helped cap demand for ESPO Blend.

UAE crude exports surpass prewar levels after OPEC exit: Mazrouei
Emirati oil shipments reach 4.09 million b/d in June
ADNOC expanding Fujairah pipeline capacity
Minister says global infrastructure lacks resilience
UAE crude exports exceeded prewar levels at certain points during the ongoing shipping disruption through the Strait of Hormuz, Emirati Minister of Energy and Infrastructure Suhail alMazrouei said Nov. 2 at the ADIPEC conference in Abu Dhabi.
S&P Global Commodities At Sea data as of Nov. 2 showed that 4.09 million barrels/day of Emirati crude were exported in June, whereas prewar levels were typically under 4 million b/d each month.
Before May, the UAE had been constrained by its OPEC quota of 3.411 million b/d and held a share of its production offline, even as it targets a 5 million b/d production capacity by 2027.
The UAE left OPEC and the OPEC+ alliance at the end of April. At the time, the Gulf state, which joined OPEC in 1967, said the decision was based on its "national interest" and commitment to "meeting the market's pressing needs."
Since June, UAE crude exports have leveled off, totaling 3.2 million b/d in September. Preliminary data showed October export volumes at 2.77 million b/d, according to CAS.
Mazrouei declined to comment on specific export volumes during the panel, but said the UAE's pipeline via Fujairah as well as its fleet of ships have helped it to reroute barrels and deliver them to customers. The existing pipeline's capacity is about 1.8 million b/d and a new pipeline is under construction that will roughly double the UAE's export capacity via Fujairah.
Asian customers are the biggest buyers of ADNOC crude, with India importing 864,000 b/d and Japan importing 764,000 b/d in September, according to CAS data.
Mazrouei said the UAE had worked to keep prices manageable throughout the war.
"We feel consumer pain and understand if the price goes too high, it's going to destruct demand in years to come," he said. "We owe it to our long-term partners."

ICE January Brent crude futures were trading at $112.10/barrel at 0600 GMT Nov. 2. The average crude price in October was the highest since the war started at the tail end of February.
"I cannot tell you what's going to happen, but I can tell you we are going to be much more prepared than we were before Feb. 28," Mazrouei said.
"After this crisis, many companies are going to look, think again and design or redesign the facilities to be more resilient in the future," Mazrouei told reporters on the sidelines of his panel discussion.
"So you need to invest, for example, in the needs of carriers that can take your crude to the countries. Just producing them from the wellhead and having an export is not good enough."
The UAE's ports, refineries and pipelines were targeted in the first round of fighting earlier this year.
"Any price shock is going to be passed on to the consumer," Mazrouei said, citing the need for resilient global infrastructure. "The infrastructure we have worldwide is not resilient enough."

ADNOC CEO calls for more energy storage, flexible refining capacity
Global oil stocks drop 1 bil barrels in 8 months: Jaber
Says shipping costs rise to 30% of oil prices
Abu Dhabi National Oil Company CEO Sultan al-Jaber called for expanding strategic energy storage and product stockpiles amid the ongoing Middle East conflict in his opening remarks at the ADIPEC conference in Abu Dhabi Nov. 2.
"We need expanded strategic storage, larger stockpiles of products and flexible refining capacity closer to consumer markets," Jaber said.
"Second, routes to market. Supply only counts when it arrives. Infrastructure must be built before it is needed and enabled by advanced logistics," he added.
In eight months of regional conflict, nearly 1 billion barrels have been drained from global inventories, diesel stocks are at their lowest seasonal level in 35 years, and there is a 600 billion cubic feet LNG supply gap, Jaber said.
"Simply put, the world has eroded its supply buffers," he said, adding that shipping and logistics now account for more than 30% of the price of a barrel, whereas before the crisis, that figure was around 5%.
While Middle East crude exports rose month over month in September as producers found workarounds via pipelines that bypass the Strait of Hormuz and via the so-called shuttle ships that ferry crude to ship-to-ship transfer hotspots outside the strait, exports were still below prewar levels.
Middle East crude exports in September and October of 15 million b/d were roughly 3.4 million-3.9 million b/d above the June level, according to the November global crude oil short-term outlook from S&P Global Energy CERA. That figure excludes what S&P Global Commodities at Sea data terms as around 385,000 b/d of Iraqi exports via Turkey.

Ukraine will continue attacks on Russian refineries: Zelenskyy
Russia's offline refining capacity rebounds
Ukraine resumes attacks after two-week pause
Zelenskyy also calls for new Patriot missiles to pause attacks
Ukraine will continue attacking Russian refineries until Moscow stops striking its energy infrastructure or until Washington meets a separate air defense request, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said in a TV interview Nov. 1.
"We said that if you want us not to attack Russia's refineries, they have [to stop] attacking our energy system," Zelenskyy said.
Ukraine has faced pressure from the US administration in the past weeks to stop attacking Russian refineries, with President Donald Trump attributing high diesel prices to the RussiaUkraine war.
Ukraine paused attacks on Russian refineries from Oct. 16 to Oct. 29, which marked the longest strike-free period for Russia's refining system in more than seven months. It resumed strikes Oct. 30, with an attack on the 164,000 barrels/day Novokuybishev refinery in Samara Oblast.
The 142,000 b/d Novoil refinery in Bashkortostan came under Ukraine's attack Oct. 31, while the 340,000 b/d Norsi refinery in Nizhny Novgorod was struck Nov. 1, according to Russian media outlet Astra.
Zelenskyy also said Ukraine would pause strikes for one month if the US supplied new Patriot air defense systems for the same period to counter Russian ballistic missile attacks.
The US Treasury Department issued a sanctions waiver to Russia Oct. 9, allowing the sale, delivery and import of Russian diesel, including imports into the US. Russia partially lifted its diesel export ban Oct. 10.
Russian authorities have said the amount of diesel exported in the coming months will partly depend on the frequency of attacks on its refineries.
The easing of refinery strikes in October allowed offline Russian refining capacity to rebound to about 2.1 million b/d as of the end of October, according to S&P Global Energy CERA analysts. They estimated offline primary refining capacity at about 3.1 million b/d at the end of September.

Kazakhstan halts Atyrau-Samara crude flows after drone attack
Ukraine drone strike damages Samara hub
Pipeline ships 7.573 million mt by September
Technical teams assess infrastructure damage
Kazakhstan has halted crude flows through the AtyrauSamara pipeline after a Ukrainian attack damaged the Samara production and dispatch hub, the Kazakh ministry told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, Nov. 2.
The Samara hub came under Ukraine's drone attack the night of Nov. 2, the Kazakh ministry said in a statement. "Consequently, at 0609 Astana time Nov. 2, oil transportation via the AtyrauSamara trunk pipeline was temporarily suspended," the ministry said.
It is unclear when the flows through the pipeline could be resumed, according to the ministry. "Specialized technical teams are currently assessing the condition of the infrastructure. The resumption of oil intake along this route is expected in the near future," the ministry said.
Kazakh state pipeline operator Kaztransoil, which operates the Kazakh section of the Atyrau-Samara line, said in an Oct. 12 statement that it shipped 7.573 million metric tons from January through September. The route has served as an alternative to Kazakhstan's main supply route -- the Caspian Pipeline Consortium -- which experienced significant disruptions due to drone attacks and technical and weather-related incidents.
Kaztransoil and Russia's Transneft, which operates the Samara hub, were not immediately available for comment.
Local residents said they heard explosions and saw a column of smoke rising toward the production and dispatch station, according to Russian media outlet Astra.
Governor of Samara Oblast Vyacheslav Fedorishchev confirmed a drone attack on the region in a post on Russian social media platform Max, but did not provide any information on the consequences of the strike. The hub has come under attack from Ukraine several times in October, according to media reports.
-- Alexandra Vladimirova, Rosemary Griffin

Argentina hikes biodiesel, ethanol prices in November on month
Biodiesel rises 2.1%, ethanol by 2%
Government is seeking to contain pump prices
Argentina's Energy Secretariat said Nov. 2 it raised biodiesel and ethanol prices by up to 2.1% in November on the month for blending into refined products, twice the increase in October.
The secretariat increased the price of biodiesel, which is produced mostly from soy oil in Argentina, by 2.1% to Pesos 2,074,474/mt ($1,344/mt) in November from Pesos 2,032,310/mt in October, it said in a resolution.
The November price is up 23% from Pesos 1,688,961/mt in the year-earlier month, according to secretariat data.
Sugarcane ethanol prices were increased 2% to Pesos 1,104.9/ liter in November from Pesos 1.083.2/l in October, while the price of corn ethanol was increased by the same percentage to Pesos 1,012.7/l from Pesos 992.8/l over the same period, the secretariat said.
The prices of both products have risen 20% over the past year. With international crude prices running at above $100/ barrel on global supply concerns, Argentina's government has

been taking steps to contain pump prices. These include a postponement of a hike in diesel and gasoline pump taxes until Dec. 1 and lobbying for legislative approval to increase the blends to 10% from 7.5% in diesel and 15% from 12% in gasoline.
Argentina produces all its own ethanol and biodiesel to meet the required blends.
The biggest fuel retailer in the country is the state-run YPF, with a 55% share of diesel and gasoline sales, according to Surtidores, a local trade news outlet. It is trailed by Shell at 23%, Pan American Energy's Axion Energy at 12% and Trafigura's Puma at 5.5%.

Democrats' midterm win could push Trump toward Iran escalation: analysts
Midterm results may accelerate Iran escalation
Democrats consider war funding restrictions
A Democratic takeover of Congress in the US midterm elections could increase the chance that President Donald Trump will turn his focus to foreign policy and potentially speed an escalation in the war with Iran, analysts say.
Trump's decision to send a third carrier strike group to the Middle East gives him the option to escalate the Iran war, and he might do so during the lame duck period after the election, said Jason Campbell, a senior fellow at the Middle East Institute.
"If he has it in mind that he wants to escalate with Iran, and the Democrats win one or both houses in November, he is likely going to be incentivized to try to do this during that interim period," Campbell told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy.
Democrats may question the Trump administration's $1.5 trillion defense spending request, which is the highest ever sought, Campbell said. And they are likely to press the administration on the US strategic objectives in Iran and military leadership changes, he said.
The risk of a resumption of military conflict with Iran is rising, and may be higher if Democrats sweep both houses of Congress and the president turns to foreign policy as his primary theater of action, David Goldwyn, chair of the Atlantic Council Global Energy Center's Energy Advisory Group, said. "The risk of elevated crude and product prices due to increased conflict in the Strait [of Hormuz] is high."
Recent shifts in WTI forward structure suggest traders are moving away from earlier expectations that crude price spikes would be relatively short-lived, in favor of a more sustained higher-price environment.
While prompt-dated futures prices have rallied back to near wartime highs this month, backwardation along the forward curve has eased as forward-dated contracts have found support amid concerns of longer-term supply disruptions. Front-month WTI has averaged a roughly $10/barrel premium compared with the sixth-month contract during the back half of October, data from Platts showed, compared with over $13/b during the first weeks of the war.

Strait of Hormuz shipping traffic rose to its highest monthly level since July in October, supported by stronger dry-bulk and LNG movements despite a decline in VLCC traffic and mounting regional threats, S&P Global Commodities at Sea data showed Nov. 2.
The rise came amid an "increasingly volatile" operating environment at Hormuz and the Bab al-Mandab Strait, with "reported projectile strikes, vessel fires and other security incidents" throughout October, CAS said.
"In the month of October, we registered 30 attacks, if I remember correctly, which is one of the highest we have," International Maritime Organization Secretary-General Arsenio Dominguez told journalists on Nov. 2.
While Iranian aerial attacks continue to threaten shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, mine collision incidents also remain a threat to vessels traversing the chokepoint, as "there's no confirmation that there are not mines in the region," Dominguez added.
"I cannot guarantee that it's safe. That's why we are not recommending anyone to go through the Strait of Hormuz, and I keep saying for the ships to stay away from it," Dominguez said during a press conference.
Despite elevated security risks over the month, observed and assumed Hormuz traffic totaled 750 transits in October, up from 749 in September, as VLCC traffic fell to 156 transits in October from 184 the previous month, but the declines in crude tanker transits were more than offset by other vessel classes, according to the CAS report.
Bulk carrier traffic climbed to 149 transits, the highest since June, according to CAS. LNG carrier crossings, which fell steeply since Qatar LNG production units suffered damage at the start of the Iran war, also climbed, reaching 32 transits, or their highest since the start of the war.

LPG tanker traffic fell month over month to 36 transits from 40 on weaker inbound activity, while outbound movements were steady, CAS reported.
Daily crossings over the previous three days totaled 24 transits on Oct. 30, 28 transits on Oct. 31, before easing to 17 crossings on Nov. 1, according to CAS.
Prominent outbound crude movements between Oct. 30 and Nov. 1 included Bahri VLCCs Lawhah, Aslaf, Majra and Platinum Shine, as well as Sinokor-operated Jamaica Prosperity, Vietnam Prosperity and Colombia Prosperity, CAS reported. Also crossing outbound were Suezmax tankers Tilos I, Ottoman Equity and Cronus, all of which are carrying Gulf crude to India.
Headed inbound the strait were Bahri VLCCs Kassab and Gold Shine, Trafigura-linked Kiku and ADNOC's Al Adlah, according to CAS assessments. Dynacom-operated Aframax Astypalea also appeared to have entered the Gulf for the first time since March.
Three LPG tanker transits were recorded from Oct. 30 through Nov. 1, all outbound, CAS reported. Bahri-operated Gas Aqua exited after loading ammonia at Ras Al Khair, while Iranian-linked VLGC Xavia moved out of the Gulf and later positioned near Sohar ahead of an understood ship-to-ship transfer.
The Iran-linked Ocean Unity crossed outbound Nov. 1 after remaining offline since mid-July and appeared to be in ballast, according to CAS, which has noted that "[the tanker's] cargo status may become clearer as the voyage progresses."
Five vessels assessed to have crossed Hormuz during the three-day period were listed on the Iranian Persian Gulf Strait Authority's blacklist, according to the authority's website. The Iranian authority had listed 240 vessels as violating its Strait of Hormuz transit guidelines and previously threatened noncompliant vessels with fines, seizure or confiscation.
In the Red Sea, Bab al-Mandab traffic rose to 1,196 transits in October from 1,154 in September, while tanker movements were stable at 408 versus 404, CAS reported. Northbound traffic increased to 647 transits, about 54% of the monthly total, from 578 in September.
Saudi-linked Bab al-Mandab traffic continued to decline sharply, as observed by CAS, falling to 21 transits in October from 52 in September, according to the report.

Platts Dubai crude down 11% on peace talk hopes
The war in the Middle East may end up reducing global oil demand by 3 million barrels/day by the end of 2026, and it will take another year until consumption is back to 2025 levels, Vitol CEO Russell Hardy said at the ADIPEC conference in Abu Dhabi Nov. 2.
"Supply has shrunk and the demand has to shrink to meet that daily supply quantity," Hardy said.
OPEC has cut its 2026 global oil demand growth forecast since the war began. It expects demand growth to be 200,000 barrels/day in 2026, according to its monthly oil market report released Oct. 13. Before the war, it expected 2026 global oil demand growth at 1.4 million b/d.
Demand needs time to recover "partly because of the real change taking place in China in terms of electric vehicles and avoiding high-priced oil imports," Hardy said.
"It is difficult to find a situation where supply and demand daily requirements are meeting daily supply at the moment," he said, adding that supply needs to be kept where it is to keep the market balanced.
The Platts Dubai oil benchmark fell 11% to $105.30/barrel Nov. 2 on signs of diplomatic progress to end the war. Platts is part of S&P Global Energy.
Over the past two days, talks have taken place in Doha between Iranian and Qatari mediators.
"The reality is the system is on low inventory and there isn't a lot of spare capacity on the product side globally," Hardy said.
The few weeks heading into Christmas typically show a "lull in demand," which then picks up around February when refined products are more in demand, he said. "There is still a lot of risk in the system over the next six months."
For Vitol, operating during the war has meant it is "obligatory to carry compulsory stocks," Hardy said.

"Carrying additional stock where we are making commitments to customers has been our way of offering the supply chain to make sure that if anything doesn't quite go to plan, you've got some inventory."

"Middle East exports have increased, but so have crude oil prices," analysts at S&P Global Energy CERA said Oct. 29, adding that China's buying had soaked up most of the rise in Middle East exports. Apart from the marginal 0.3% growth in July, global air passenger demand has declined year over year since April, when RPK fell for the first time since the COVID-19 pandemic.
Middle Eastern airlines' traffic fell 8% over the period, marking a slight improvement from an annual contraction of 14.6% in August. Excluding Middle Eastern figures, however, global RPK rose 0.3% year over year in September, supported by stronger demand for international travel, IATA said in its statement.
European airlines' RPK inched up 0.3% year over year, with growth in international passengers offsetting a decline in domestic traffic.
Meanwhile, RPK across Asia-Pacific carriers increased 0.5% year over year, with both domestic and international traffic rising marginally over the period. The increase came despite traffic on routes within Asia falling 0.9%, according to IATA.
According to aviation statistics company OAG, low-cost carriers in Asia have faced greater pressure to reduce capacity, particularly on short, price-sensitive routes across Southeast Asia and the broader Asia-Pacific region.
"If geopolitical disruption keeps fuel prices elevated, filed schedules may change through winter, with capacity reductions possible within Southeast Asia and on short-haul across the wider Asia Pacific region," Diedre Fulton from OAG said in an Oct. 28 analysis.

"Air traffic demand fell 0.5% in September, reflecting weaknesses in the Middle East and North America. Nevertheless, this was an improvement from August, with a welcome return to growth in most international markets. These patterns have more-or-less repeated themselves throughout much of the year, and we can expect this to continue amid geopolitical uncertainty and volatile fuel prices," said IATA's Senior Vice President Sustainability and Chief Economist, Marie Owens Thomsen.
Looking ahead, global scheduled seat capacity is expected to remain above 2025 levels in October and November, up 0.6% and 1.5%, respectively.
