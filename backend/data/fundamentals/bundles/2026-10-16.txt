China unlikely to refill oil reserves without sharp price drop
Backwardation deters strategic buying plans
Imports rise as depleted commercial stocks cut additional supplies
December arrivals expected to drop on costs
China is unlikely to replenish its crude stocks unless oil prices and freight costs fall sharply, two crude procurement managers and a crude trader at Chinese state-run companies, as well as an executive at a state think tank, told Platts Oct. 16.
Improving imports over September-November would slow China's destocking activities, as logistics uncertainties have led to uneven crude arrivals, while inflows are likely to fall in December due to high prices, the sources added.
"The market is in backwardation, and we cannot rule out the possibility of a sudden drop in crude prices, making it an unfavorable time for stockpiling," the crude trader said.
On Oct. 16, the Singapore-assessed physical Dubai time spread was at $2.69/b, indicating backwardation despite narrowing from $3.695/b the day before, Platts data showed.
"We still largely take a hand-to-mouth approach amid the volatile market, but logistics uncertainties mean the pace of deliveries can fluctuate," said a crude procurement manager at a state-run refinery, adding that the company has had to increase crude imports only because its depleted commercial crude stocks can no longer provide additional supplies.
Another crude procurement manager said state-run refineries can now pay market prices for crude barrels drawn from commercial storage, rather than physically returning them within a specified timeframe, as was required previously.

Oil-focused Africa Energy Bank to open early Nov, says APPO's Ghezali
Opens with $500 mil capital, will fund in 2027
Initial focus on cross-border oil, gas project
Eyes stronger NOCs, domestic market buildup
The long-awaited Africa Energy Bank will formally open in early November with $500 million of capital to deploy, and could fund as many as three oil and gas projects in 2027, the new secretary general of co-owner APPO told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, in an interview.
Speaking on the sidelines of the Africa Energy Week conference in Cape Town on Oct. 15, Farid Ghezali, who took over the African Petroleum Producers' Organization earlier this year, said "administrative formalities" were being concluded ahead of the launch.
Co-owned by APPO and Afreximbank, the institution - which will be headquartered in Abuja - aims to tackle longstanding financial challenges facing African energy projects, following the flight of western capital from fossil fuels. The initial endowment has been raised entirely from APPO members and Afreximbank.
"We have the capital, $500 million," Ghezali said. He now aims to "go to the market, we can raise $5 billion. And the goal is to achieve $10 billion by the end of next year".
The secretary general said the initial focus will be on crossborder projects, such as regional refineries and pipelines like the Central African Pipeline System, which would span six countries.
"[The bank] will be making investment decisions in 2027. My wish is to have at least three projects financing in 2027," he said.

INTERVIEW: Japan hopes for predictability, stability in Australia's gas reservation scheme
Lack of predictability, stability may deter investment
Diversifying crude oil, LNG supply sources key focus
Strategic Buffer LNG currently available year-round
Japan hopes to see predictability and stability in Australia's domestic gas reservation scheme legislation amid ongoing discussions on the matter, a top official overseeing energy resources policy told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, on Oct. 16.
"The government is watching this issue with considerable interest, and we have maintained ongoing communication with the Australian government on the matter," Tetsuya Azuma, director of the Energy Resources Development Division at the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry, said in an interview.
"Predictability and stability in the investment environment are extremely important. In particular, it is essential that existing contracts are properly respected," Azuma said. "In terms of predictability, it is also important that the rules are not applied retroactively. That may essentially be another way of saying that existing contracts should be respected, but these principles are extremely important."
"If such principles are not respected, there is a risk of discouraging investment. That would ultimately be an issue not only for the region but also for Australia itself, because it is important that investment in Australia continues," he added. "From that perspective, we have consistently conveyed that we expect transparency, predictability and stability to be properly maintained."
The Australian government introduced the national domestic gas reservation legislation on Oct. 14. The new domestic supply obligation is set at 20% of LNG exports, calibrated to forecast domestic demand, and is scheduled to take effect on Jan. 1, 2028.
Azuma's comments come as Japanese companies express concern about Australia's policy development. JERA Co. Inc., for one, said Oct. 15 that it is concerned about the "unintended consequences" of Australia's domestic gas reservation scheme legislation, "like many other long-term customers of and investors in Australian LNG."
"We have told the Australian side that it is important to listen closely to the views of companies... we are asking Australia to ensure that these principles are properly safeguarded through its institutional framework," Azuma said. "So rather than saying, 'If this happens, Japan will do this,' our position is that Australia should design the system properly so that we do not reach that situation in the first place."
Azuma took the helm of the division in July, having previously served as director of the ministry's Fuel Supply Infrastructure Policy Division, and immediately responded to crude oil supply disruptions through the Strait of Hormuz following the start of the conflict in the Middle East.

Asked about his policy priorities, Azuma said, "Put simply, the priority is ensuring stable energy supplies. In particular, since around March, amid changes in the Middle East situation and continued difficulties in transiting the Strait of Hormuz, we believe energy security has become increasingly important."
In the absence of risk-free sources of crude oil and LNG supplies, Azuma said, "Given that reality, I think it has once again become extremely important to ensure sufficient diversification and spread the risks."
The Japanese Cabinet approved a set of bills on Oct. 13, including one to rationalize the use of designated sea lanes to ensure the stable transport of crude oil and oil products. Under the bill, importers such as refiners, petrochemical producers and trading houses will receive grants when they procure crude oil and oil products from suppliers outside the Strait of Hormuz.
Asked how much Japan needs to reduce its dependence on the Strait of Hormuz for shipping, Azuma said that is something to be discussed going forward.
Once the new framework comes into operation following the enactment of the legislation, Azuma added that the government is expected to establish something along the lines of implementation guidelines, which would include targets for rationalizing the use of designated transportation routes.
LNG security To bolster LNG supply security, Azuma said Japan is working to
diversify its supply sources, expand global markets and increase its buffer of hard-to-store LNG.
"So it is important to maintain a well-diversified portfolio, and if we can diversify it even further, all the better," Azuma said, adding that expanding global LNG transactions would, in itself, help enhance the market's ability to balance supply and demand.
"The volume and breadth of the market increase, the global [LNG] market naturally becomes more flexible and better able to respond to supply shocks," he added.
Unlike Europe, which has underground gas storage facilities, Azuma said, "In Japan, we are not necessarily ruling out such options, but in more practical terms, and particularly in terms of measures that can be implemented in the near term, we already have mechanisms such as SBL (Strategic Buffer LNG), which in a sense provide a buffer by effectively holding a call option."
"There was also the recent arrangement between JOGMEC [Japan Organization for Metals and Energy Security] and [Malaysia's] Petronas concerning emergency procurement. That is essentially a call option, giving Japan the right to take a certain volume in an emergency," he added.
JOGMEC said Sept. 11 that it has agreed with Petronas on a framework for cooperation to ensure stable LNG supplies during emergencies.
"Essentially, we first create a basic template or framework. The details of how it would actually work in each individual case would probably need to be worked out more thoroughly, but we establish a basic template that sets out the fundamental understanding that a certain type of supply will be provided in

Japan to subsidize difference between domestic kerosene wholesale and import prices: minister
New kerosene subsidy system to be in place from Nov until March 2027
Government addresses winter supply concerns
Refiners urged to maintain normal sales levels
Japan is set to introduce a new system to subsidize the difference between domestic kerosene wholesale and import prices amid high import prices of the middle distillate, Minister of Economy, Trade and Industry Ryosei Akazawa said Oct. 16.
Amid high kerosene import prices, "We will therefore introduce

a system under which the difference between import prices and domestic wholesale prices will be subsidized through an imported kerosene adjustment rate," Akazawa told reporters in Tokyo.
Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, assessed the FOB Singapore jet fuel/kerosene outright price at an average of $175.60/b to date in October, compared with September's average of $169.50/b and the 2025 average of $86.42/b.
The minister's comments came as METI is aware that some wholesalers and other businesses are placing orders substantially above the usual level of demand, and that some refiners and distributors have been asking their customers to place orders in line with expected demand to maintain stable supplies.
"We are aware that these developments have raised concerns among some members of the public about kerosene supplies during the winter. However, I want to reiterate that we are by no means in a situation where supplies are running short. I would first like to make that point clear," Akazawa said.
Amid kerosene supply concerns, Akazawa said, "We will ask refiners and distributors, import trading companies, wholesalers and other businesses to keep sales and orders at the same level as in the corresponding month of the previous year."
Aakazawa added, "We will establish a new direct kerosene sales scheme, under which oil refiners and distributors will sell kerosene directly in cases where supply becomes uneven, or bottlenecks develop in distribution."
"The subsidy scheme for the price difference will be effective from Nov. 2026 to Mar. 2027, though its details will be finalized later," Nobuhiko Kikkawa, Director of Fuel Distribution and Retail Office in the Agency for Natural Resources and Energy, part of METI, said at a press briefing in Tokyo. "The current price difference is a little less than ¥20/l."
According to Kikkawa, METI has set up a consultation platform for supply disruptions in oil products or lubricant markets on its website since March, which is also to be used for consumers or traders who struggle to procure kerosene.
"METI will help consumers or traders find refiners' spots that supply kerosene cargoes to those in need of kerosene as fast as possible, while consumers or traders have to talk with refiners on how much kerosene cargoes cost," Takehiko NAGAI, Director of Industrial Safety Policy Division in METI, said.
Asked about South Korea's imports, Kikkawa said there is no problem for Japan's refiners or importers to procure kerosene cargoes from South Korea as far as METI acknowledges.

already preparing term and spot shipments for the NovemberMarch cold season, product marketing managers at two refiners based in Ulsan and Seosan told Platts on Oct. 16.

SINGAPORE DATA: Middle distillate stocks rise 10.72% to near 6-month high
Singapore flips to net importer of gasoil Jet fuel exports rise nearly sevenfold
Singapore's onshore commercial middle distillate stocks rose 10.72% week over week to 10.38 million barrels over Oct. 8-14 amid an influx of gasoil imports, data from Enterprise Singapore showed Oct. 15.
Stocks were last higher in the week ended April 29 at 10.92 million barrels, marking a near six-month high, historical data showed.
The city-state flipped to being a net importer of gasoil over Oct. 8-14 as inflows rose fivefold to 255,694 mt over the period. Net imports were last seen over April 30-May 6, historical data showed.
China was the largest supplier with 102,512 mt, the most since Aug. 13-19 when the country shipped 136,753 mt of gasoil to Singapore, Enterprise Singapore data showed.
The remaining imports came from Malaysia with 64,799 mt, South Korea with 55,459 mt and India with 32,922 mt.
In contrast, exports slipped 29.85% week over week to 203,720 mt over Oct. 8-14, the data showed.
The Netherlands and Cambodia were the top destinations at 59,989 mt and 36,390 mt, respectively.
This was followed by Sri Lanka (32,480 mt), Malaysia (21,274 mt), and Togo (14,604 mt).
Exports to Indonesia fell to 12,576 mt from 54,583 mt the week before, while 10,010 mt flowed to Spain, according to the data.

The bulk of the remaining outflows was received by six other countries, with South Africa leading with 7,466 mt.
Jet fuel exports rise Singapore returned to being a net exporter of jet fuel/
kerosene over Oct. 8-14 as imports fell to 6,176 mt from 41,995 mt the week before, according to Enterprise Singapore data.
The downswing came amid a lack of arrivals from China, India, and Malaysia, which had bolstered imports in the week ended Oct. 8. In comparison, South Korea was the primary supplier in the week to Oct. 14, with 6,170 mt.
Bucking the decline, exports rose nearly sevenfold to 92,940 mt over Oct. 8-14, the data showed.
Australia and the Netherlands were the top destinations at 40,320 mt and 38,385 mt, respectively.
The remaining outflows were largely absorbed by five other countries, led by Reunion (8,994 mt) and Fiji (2,790 mt).

Côte d'Ivoire plans $10 billion investment in new refining, storage: SIR
Second $7.2 billion refinery set to launch in 2034
Modernization plan for existing refinery includes FSRU
Bouaké terminal rehab to add 48,000 cu m inland storage
Côte d'Ivoire's state-backed refiner Société Ivoirienne de Raffinage plans to spend $10 billion on new refining capacity, logistics and efficiency measures before 2040, its commercial head has announced, as it aims to combat energy security risks and drive new growth for its downstream sector.
Core to Côte d'Ivoire's downstream road map is the construction of a new 170,000 b/d refinery in San Pedro, first announced in 2025 and now targeted to come onstream in 2034, M'Baye N'Deye Coura Myriam, SIR's director of commercial affairs, said during a panel discussion at African Energy Week in Cape Town.
At an expected cost of $7.2 billion, the second refinery development was prioritized over previous plans to expand the existing refinery in Abidjan. Nevertheless, Côte d'Ivoire plans to modernize the site to ensure its long-term competitiveness, Coura Myriam said.
Among the upgrade measures for the 80,000 b/d Abidjan site, SIR plans to commission new catalytic reforming and isomerization units to reduce benzene and aromatics content and enhance fuel octane levels, with a target launch date in 2033, it said in a project prospectus.
Honeywell UOP has been contracted to conduct the upgrade, the prospectus said. Work on a new gasoil hydrodesulfurization unit backed by the African Development Bank and BOAD, another Africa-focused lender, is already underway and due to be completed by 2029.

US extends Lukoil sanctions waivers for filling stations, Bulgarian assets
Treasury extends transaction permits to April
Funds blocked from transfer to Russian parent
The US extended its sanctions waivers until April 24, 2027, for conducting transactions with LUKOIL International GmbH's overseas filling stations and the company's Bulgarian assets.
The US Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control issued two new licenses on Oct. 15, as the previous waivers were set to expire Oct. 29.
The list of Bulgarian subsidiaries includes the Bulgarian retail arm Lukoil Bulgaria EOOD, the refining business Lukoil Neftohim Burgas, and Lukoil Aviation Bulgaria and Lukoil Bunker Bulgaria.
The waivers allow Lukoil subsidiaries to avoid transaction bans and continue operations. Although the subsidiaries are authorized to receive funds, the assets cannot be transferred to the parent company in Russia, which has been sanctioned by the US since October 2025.
On Aug. 13, the UK, which also sanctioned Lukoil in 2025, extended its sanctions waivers for Lukoil's international subsidiary, including its Bulgarian assets, until Feb. 26, 2027.
Since October 2025, all Lukoil's international subsidiaries have required special permissions from the US and the UK to maintain their operations and negotiate with potential buyers.
In January 2026, Lukoil agreed with US investment firm Carlyle to buy its international subsidiary, but Carlyle did not receive OFAC approval, and the agreement expired at the end of July.
Carlyle told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, that it is no longer "actively pursuing" the purchase of Lukoil's international assets.
Lukoil's international portfolio includes the Shah Deniz project in Azerbaijan, the West Qurna 2 project in Iraq, and upstream assets in Egypt, Cameroon, Nigeria, Uzbekistan, Ghana, Mexico, the UAE, and the Republic of Congo. In Romania, Lukoil holds the EX-30 Trident exploration permit in the Black Sea. Lukoil also has stakes in several projects in Kazakhstan.
Lukoil has stakes in a retail network of fuel stations across Central and Eastern Europe, as well as in three European refineries, including Bulgaria's Neftohim Burgas facility, Romania's Petrotel refinery, and the Zeeland plant in the Netherlands, with a combined processing capacity of 400,000 barrels/day.

Iraq hands Kirkuk oil fields over to BP, targeting 450,000 b/d crude output
Four fields have been handed over
Output targets 500 MMscf/d of gas
ConocoPhillips holds 42% project stake
Iraq has transferred operations of its Kirkuk oil fields to BP with plans in motion to boost crude production to 450,000 barrels/day and gas output to 500 million standard cubic feet/ day, Iraq's oil ministry said in a statement Oct. 15.
Four oil fields -- Kirkuk, Bai Hassan, Khabbaz and Jambur -- previously operated by Iraq's state-owned companies will now be operated by BP, the statement said.
BP did not respond to a request for comment Oct. 15. In February 2025, BP and Iraq reached a production-sharing deal to redevelop the Kirkuk fields and establish a new operator comprising personnel from BP and Iraq's state-owned North Oil Company and North Gas Company. BP agreed in July to sell a 42% interest in the Kirkuk oil development project to ConocoPhillips. The Kirkuk fields produce about 320,000 b/d, with some volumes feeding domestic refineries and others exported via Turkey's Ceyhan port. The agreement between BP and Iraq for the Kirkuk fields includes production of more than 3 billion barrels of oil equivalent from the Baba and Avanah domes of the Kirkuk oil field and three adjacent fields -- Bai Hassan, Jambur and Khabbaz. There is also potential for investment in exploration under the deal, with the area possibly holding 20 billion boe, BP said previously. Production at the fields has long been in decline, following a peak of about 1 million b/d in the decades after the reservoir's discovery in 1927. The original Kirkuk discovery is central to BP's early heritage. Despite ample remaining reserves, the Kirkuk fields needed significant rehabilitation following the Iraq War in 2003. BP undertook technical studies at Kirkuk intermittently between 2013 and 2019, in parallel with its role at the Rumaila field in the south of the country.

Syria's oil, gas pipelines come under further attack
Gas pipeline explosion halts power supply
Sabotage targets Banias-Homs product line
Crude imports drop to zero since August
Syria's state energy company reported two separate pipeline attacks on Oct. 15, including an explosion on a key domestic

gas transmission line, as the country's energy systems face a mounting wave of attacks while it works to attract investment and repair its war-torn infrastructure.
A 16-inch al-Jabsah gas pipeline was struck by an explosion in the al-Arabidi area in northeastern Syria, the Syrian Petroleum Company said in a statement. Emergency teams shut sectional valves at both ends of the damaged section -- at the al-Jabsah gas plant and the Deir ez-Zor gas station in the al-Bughailiyah area -- while firefighting and security teams were dispatched to the scene, the company said.
The al-Jabsah line connects to Syria's central gas network, supplying Homs and surrounding areas with power. As of January, the pipeline was carrying approximately 1.2 million cubic meters/ day, SPC said earlier this year.
The Banias-Homs petroleum products pipeline was also struck in what SPC described as an act of sabotage carried out for the "purpose of theft" near Homs, resulting in a gasoline leak. Repair efforts are underway, SPC said. The 24-inch pipeline carries products inland from Syria's Banias port.
Escalating attacks The twin strikes on Oct. 15 are the fourth and fifth incidents
in a series of attacks that have targeted Syrian oil and gas infrastructure.
A Syrian oil pipeline in the al-Hasakah area was damaged in an attack on Oct. 7. In the nearby Deir ez-Zor province, a fire broke out on a gas pipeline in the Shula area on Sept. 29, knocking some power stations out of service, while on Oct. 1, an explosion at the Tishreen pressure reduction station damaged a pipeline, though pumping resumed the following day, according to SPC statements.
In mid-August, an explosion on the same al-Jabsah gas line halted gas flows and disrupted electricity generation, which SPC described at the time as a "deliberate act of sabotage."
Syria is working to rebuild its oil and gas sector following a prolonged civil war, but the country continues to face security threats, including from remaining pockets of Islamic State fighters, though the militant group's activity has fallen to record low levels, according to a September analysis by the Middle East Institute. Local regional media reported that suspects have been arrested in connection with the latest attack, though neither the perpetrators nor a motive have been formally identified.
The attacks have coincided with widespread diesel shortages and a mid-September price hike that helped spur protests over the cost of living. Syria's energy ministry said at the time that the price increases were temporary and reflected higher procurement costs and the country's dependence on imported supplies.
Since the ouster of former President Bashar al-Assad at the end of 2024, Syria has relied primarily on Russia for crude imports and scattered donations from other nations, including Saudi Arabia. However, S&P Global Commodities At Sea data showed that Syria imported between 35,000 and 90,000 barrels/day of crude per month throughout most of 2026, but no imports were recorded in August, September or so far in October.

Brava Energia makes gas discovery in exploration well at onshore Sanhacu field
Located in Potiguar Basin
Evaluates output potential
Follows Ecopetrol arrival
Brazilian independent oil and natural gas producer Brava Energia discovered hydrocarbons in an exploration well drilled at the onshore Sanhacu field in Rio Grande do Norte state, according to a regulatory filing.

MSR debate EU member states agreed on Sept. 23 to double the threshold

The European Parliament has taken a different position, backing a lower threshold of 650 million allowances effective Feb. 1, 2027, and rejecting the commission's earlier proposal to scrap automatic cancellation altogether.
The Council is hoping to conclude negotiations with the Parliament on the MSR file by end-2026.
The MSR was introduced in 2019 to address a persistent oversupply of allowances in the EU carbon market that had suppressed prices and undermined the system's ability to incentivize decarbonization investment.
The MSR works by automatically adjusting the volume of allowances in circulation based on the total number held in the market. Reforms to its intake and cancellation rates have been a recurring source of debate among member states and the European Parliament.

ETS2 extends carbon pricing to road transport, buildings and small businesses outside the existing EU ETS, directly affecting households through higher fuel and heating costs. The market targets a 42% reduction in emissions from 2005 levels by 2030, with allowance supply expected to tighten in the years following launch.
