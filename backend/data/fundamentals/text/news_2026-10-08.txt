US crude exports remained elevated at 3.5 million b/d in September, edging up from 3.4 million b/d in August, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea data, but well below the May peak of 4.4 million b/d. South Korea was the largest single destination at 568,600 b/d, followed by the Netherlands at 459,000 b/d, with Japan, France, Canada and the UK also among the top receivers.
Volumes into Asia climbed across the region, including Northeast Asia, where US exports rose from 793,000 b/d in August to 1.1 million b/d. Exports into Southeast Asia climbed from 292,000 b/d to 528,000 b/d, while volumes into the Indian subcontinent climbed from 68,000 b/d to 220,000 b/d over the same period.
Asian volumes in September largely reflect cargoes fixed before VLCC freight on USGC-Asia routes roughly doubled. The Hormuz disruption initially drove Asian refiners toward US crude as an alternative supply source, but its knock-on effect on tanker markets has since eroded the economics of those trade flows.
Fixtures to Asia, including some UKC options, climbed to 7.8 million metric tons in September compared to 4.8 million in August.

France has agreed to release 10 million barrels of diesel from its emergency reserves to try and curb rising fuel prices after a G7 commitment to draw down more stocks, its Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu announced in a national address Oct. 7.
"Last Friday, President Macron convened an emergency meeting of the G7, which decided to mobilize strategic reserves in a coordinated manner. As part of this effort, we will make 10 million barrels of diesel from our reserves available," Lecornu told reporters, saying the decision could shave prices at the pump by around 0.12 to 0.18 per liter.
His comments follow a pledge from the International Energy Agency to try and accelerate the pace of its 400 million barrel stock release, which was announced in March but still has 100 million barrels left to complete. After mounting pressure from the US for other countries to release stocks, the G7 said its member states would prioritize getting diesel to the market within 20 days.
As part of the collective action in March, France committed to release 14.6 million barrels of oil, without specifying whether those volumes would be crude or oil products. In a Oct. 7 note, analysts at US investment bank JP Morgan estimated that France had already released 3 million barrels of diesel, and still has around 61 million barrels in reserve.

China slashes Oct clean fuel exports; third quota batch short of expectations
State refiners cut clean oil products exports
New clean fuel quota likely at 4.5 million mt
Third LSFO quota batch totals 1.07 million mt
China's state-owned refiners have sharply reduced October exports of clean oil products following fresh restrictions on oil product exports despite of new quota allocations, refinery and market sources said Oct. 8.
"We have drastically cut the exports of both gasoline and jet fuel, with only one cargo of jet fuel to be carried out for October," a source at a PetroChina refinery in North China said.
According to the source, one MR-sized gasoline cargo and two MR-sized jet fuel cargoes scheduled for October exports have been canceled.
"The situation may change as headquarters of the stateowned oil companies are still applying for more export volumes," the source added.
Four refinery sources with Sinopec also affirmed restrictions on clean oil product exports for October.
"Signing new deals is not allowed for the time being," a Sinopec refinery source in South China said.
A second Sinopec source said that the overall volume for October would be sharply lower than previous expectations.
Clean oil product exports in October were estimated at 2 million mt, including shipments to foreign destinations, Hong Kong and Macao, as well as jet fuel supplied for bonded bunkering at the country's international airports, falling from the previous expectation of 3.85 million mt.
Ahead of the Oct. 1-7 National Day holiday, Chinese refiners halted clean oil product exports to overseas destinations, Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, reported earlier. Similar to measures implemented in mid-March, outflows are currently restricted to Hong Kong and Macau, while jet fuel flows are limited to supplies for China's international airports.
Smaller clean fuel quota allocation Market sources pegged China's latest batch of clean oil
product export quotas at about 4.5 million mt, released before the holiday, significantly below pre-holiday expectations of around 8 million mt.
"The new quotas mean only around 2 million mt of oil products can still be exported for a month, which would be a sharp decline from August and September levels," a Beijingbased analyst said.
The allocation would also be substantially lower than the 8.395 million mt issued in the third batch of clean oil product export quotas in mid-September 2025.
Platts reported Sept. 24 that this year's third-round allocation was likely to be smaller than the corresponding batch issued a

year earlier, implying a volume of less than 8 million mt. China's clean oil product exports surged 79.6% month over
month to 4.58 million mt, or 1.16 million barrels/day, in August, the highest level since January 2023, as shipments of jet fuel, gasoil and gasoline all increased, according to customs data.
Third LSFO quota batch issued China has allocated a third batch of low sulfur fuel oil export
quotas totaling 1.07 million mt to state-owned oil companies, sources with knowledge of the matter said Oct. 8.
The latest issuance lifted China's year-to-date LSFO export quotas to 14.07 million mt, up 5.6% from a year earlier.

South Korea, Egypt seek deeper oil, energy, Suez logistics ties
KNOC expands upstream oil projects in Egypt
Refiners use Sidi Kerir terminal for Arab Light deliveries
Suez zone offers South Korean industrial complex site
South Korea and Egypt are seeking closer cooperation in upstream oil development, energy infrastructure and Suez-linked logistics, as security risks in the Red Sea complicate South Korean refiners' Middle Eastern crude shipments, according to the Korea Chamber of Commerce and Industry, the trade ministry and Korea National Oil Corp. Oct. 7.
The sectors featured prominently at the South Korea-Egypt Business Roundtable in Seoul Oct. 7, held during Egyptian President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi's visit, the KCCI said in a statement.
The meeting was the first joint public-private consultation since the countries formally launched efforts in January to pursue a Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement. It focused on converting their broader economic partnership into investment and business opportunities, including in energy and infrastructure, the KCCI and the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Resources said in separate statements Oct. 7.
State-run KNOC aims to expand energy cooperation with Egypt using its previous experience in upstream oil development projects there, the KCCI said, citing the KNOC President Sohn Joo-seok, who attended alongside South Korean Trade Minister Kim Jung-kwan and KCCI Executive Vice Chairman Kim Jeong-il.
Other key corporate representatives included POSCO International President Lee Kye-in, who chairs the Korea-Egypt Economic Cooperation Committee, as well as executives from Hanwha Ocean, Hyundai Rotem and Samsung C&T, according to the KCCI.
Egypt's delegation included El-Sisi, Investment and Foreign Trade Minister Mohamed Farid Saleh, Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty, Transport Minister Kamel El-Wazir, Industry Minister Khaled Hashem and Suez Canal Economic Zone Chairman Mustafa Sheikoun.

Philippine energy secretary unveils oil reserve, fuel transition plans
Philippines targets 15%-35% oil cut by 2050
1 mil barrel oil reserve facility due Dec 2026

Philippine Energy Secretary Sharon Garin unveiled two landmark energy policy frameworks on Oct. 8, outlining a roadmap to reduce the country's heavy dependence on imported oil while accelerating the buildout of renewable energy.
Speaking at the Philippine Energy Investment Forum in Manila, held alongside the ASEAN Energy Business Forum, Garin formally presented the 2026 National Oil and Gas Co-Investment Plan and the fuel transition plan, which together set out a coordinated strategy for managing fuel supply under current conditions while progressively reducing the Philippines' exposure to global oil market disruptions.
The announcements carry direct implications for crude and refined products trade flows into the Philippines, which relies on the Middle East for around 90% of its crude oil imports and where oil accounts for 47.7% of final energy consumption, Garin said.

South Korea has been importing medium-sour Arab Light crude loaded at Yanbu and transported south through Bab alMandab toward Asia. As security concerns intensified, several VLCCs were instead fixed to sail north through the Suez Canal into the Mediterranean, then around Africa's Cape of Good Hope to the Far East.
The circuitous route adds three to four weeks to delivery times and substantially raises freight costs. Two Yanbu-Suez-CapeSouth Korea VLCC voyages were fixed in early August at about $20.2 million each, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea data.

Energy security The urgency behind the new policy frameworks was
underscored by the energy emergency declared earlier this year, Garin said. On March 24, President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. issued Executive Order 110, declaring a national energy emergency in response to a sharp rise in global oil prices linked to the conflict in the Middle East.
The Department of Energy said it maintained domestic fuel stocks at 50 days of supply throughout the crisis, which is above the regulatory minimum of 30 days, and implemented weekly supply adjustments to prevent hoarding and distribution disruptions. A temporary suspension of the wholesale electricity spot market was also credited with averting a 7- to 12 Philippinepeso increase in consumer electricity bills.

The NOGCP establishes a systematic framework for managing fuel investment priorities and government response protocols during supply disruptions, detailing the roles of the DOE, the Department of Transportation, and local government units, Garin said.
The fuel transition plan sets out sector-by-sector pathways to reduce oil dependence, with a baseline scenario targeting a 15% reduction by 2030 and 35% by 2050, she added.
Under a more aggressive scenario, the Philippines is targeting a 30% reduction by 2030, 50% by 2045, and more than half by 2050, according to the DOE.
Transport, which accounts for 70% of domestic oil demand, is identified as the primary lever for reducing consumption, Garin said.
The government is targeting electric vehicles to comprise 25% of the national vehicle fleet by 2030 and 80% by 2040, supported by at least 7,000 electric vehicle charging stations nationwide by 2028, Garin said. To bolster physical supply security, the DOE said it is advancing the Philippine Strategic Petroleum Reserve under the Philippine National Oil Corp.

Proposed locations include Bataan and Palawan, with the government seeking joint venture partners or independent investors for the expanded infrastructure, including oil storage tanks and potential refinery capacity, Garin said.
On the supply side, Garin said peak electricity demand has reached 20.1 GW, while installed capacity stood at 32.5 GW as of July 2026. Electricity demand is growing at 6 to 7% annually, and as high as 14% in some urban centers. Net energy imports currently account for 51.5% of the total primary energy supply.
The government has also committed to a renewable energy share of 35% of the generation mix by 2030, 50% by 2040, and more than 50 percent by 2050.

Philippines DOE, JICA sign deal to cooperate on oil security
Japan shares expertise on oil stockpiling
Countries assess supply-demand balance
Philippines works to establish SPR program
The Philippines Department of Energy on Oct. 8 signed a joint statement with the state-owned Japan International Cooperation Agency to cooperate on oil security.
The DOE and JICA will cooperate to strengthen the Philippines' energy security through knowledge sharing from Japan and policy recommendations based on an assessment of the country's oil supply-demand balance and stockpiling

system, according to a briefing document. "There could be a regional stockpiling (in the Philippines).
It's a location where countries can, one option, is to have a common stockpiling for several countries or for the Philippines, for example will allow other countries to store their inventory here in the country," Philippine Energy Secretary Sharon Garin told reporters, following the end of the Asia Zero Emission Community (AZEC) 4th Ministerial Meeting at the Philippine International Convention Center in Pasay City.
"So that's what we're studying with JICA and METI... for the preparatory work needed for the feasibility study to determine if we establish it in the Philippines, where and how the logistics will be," Garin said.
The development comes after an energy emergency was declared in the Philippines earlier in 2026. On March 24, President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. issued Executive Order 110, declaring a national energy emergency in response to a sharp rise in global oil prices linked to the conflict in the Middle East.
"In the Philippines, the effects of the global energy crisis or oil crisis have been felt in fuel prices, transport fares and electricity bills. These pressures reach households and businesses alike, reminding us how closely energy security is tied to economic stability and the everyday lives of our people," Garin said during the AZEC 4th Ministerial Meeting earlier in the day.
"Alongside the joint statement, we will consider measures to strengthen preparedness and coordination in the event of future supply disruptions," she said.
The joint statement was signed alongside 17 other announced agreements at a ceremony covering cooperation on energy security, gas-fired power/LNG and energy transition on the sidelines of the AZEC meeting.
This cooperation is being implemented under the Partnership on Wide Energy and Resources Resilience Asia, or POWERR Asia, which was launched April 15 by Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi. POWERR Asia is a new cooperation framework comprising both emergency and structural responses with midto long-term perspectives.
The signing follows Japan saying May 28 in Tokyo that it would help build strategic petroleum reserves in the Philippines, as leaders from the two countries shared long-term plans to enhance energy security and supply chain resilience amid the current geopolitical situation.
The initiative between Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry and the Philippines' Department of Energy was announced after a summit meeting between Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi and President Romualdez Marcos.
The move comes as the Philippines works to establish an SPR program, including the development of new stockpiling facilities.
"Following this session, we will engage with the delegations from Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates. As co-chair, the Philippines looks forward to a productive exchange," Garin said.

Europe, Middle East and Africa
UK sanctions more Russian oil companies, shadow fleet tanker
Oil companies Zarubezhneft and INK Capital sanctioned
New listings include 12 new shadow fleet tankers
UK now sanctions 90% of Russian oil production capacity
The UK has sanctioned the Russian oil companies Zarubezhneft and INK Capital, along with a dozen new "shadow tankers" involved in evading restrictions, it announced on Oct. 8, as it seeks new routes to mount pressure on Moscow over its four-year war with Ukraine.
A statement from the UK's foreign office said that by listing the two Russian oil companies, it was cutting off revenue sources for the country's war economy, as well as closing avenues for larger energy companies to obscure their operations and avoid existing measures.
The addition of 12 more oil tankers to the UK sanctions list was additionally designed to prevent the continued trade of Russian oil above the UK's price cap, which sets a $44.10/b threshold for the maximum value at which Russian crude can be legally traded. The additional shadow tanker sanctions takes the total number of listed vessels to over 600, the statement said.
The new listings follow a raft of previous UK sanctions, which already cover the majority of the Russian oil sector and a vast proportion of the shipping network used to support its trade. With its existing sanctions on Rosneft, Gazprom, Lukoil, Surgutneftegas and Tatneft, the UK was the first G7 member to sanction Russia's four leading oil companies.
The state-owned Zarubezhneft has upstream oil and gas projects in Russia, Vietnam, Indonesia, Uzbekistan, Cuba and Egypt, the largest of which are run by its Vietsovpetro and Rusvietpetro joint ventures. The former focuses on the continental shelf of southern Vietnam, while Rusvietro produces roughly 60,000 b/d of oil from 14 northern Russian fields.
S&P Global Commodities at Sea data detected six tankers shipped by Zarubezhneft in 2026, which delivered a total of 3.8 million barrels of Urals crude to Turkish and Indian refineries from the Baltic port of Primorsk.
In Bosnia and Herzegovina, the company also has a significant downstream footprint, operating a refinery in Brod and a motor oil plant in the nearby town of Modrica. Through its subsidiary Nestro Petrol, the company operates 86 fuel stations in Bosnia and Herzegovina focused mostly on LPG and compressed natural gas, while a separate entity, OPTIMA Grupa, focuses on Balkans trading.
INK Capital is the main holding company of Russia's Irkutsk Oil Company, an independent producer with oil and gas projects in Eastern Siberia's Irkutsk, Krasnoyarsk and Republic of Sakha regions. In 2025, it also commissioned a 650,000 mt/year polymer plant, which processes Eastern Siberian feedstock to produce high-density polyethylene (HDPE) and linear low-density

polyethylene (LLDPE). Neither of the companies was immediately available for
comment. With its new bundle of designations, the UK government
estimates its sanctions now cover over 90% of Russia's total oil production capacity, while tanker listings remain a focus to prevent Russia from capitalizing on surging energy prices.
"Through this action, we're not only targeting ships already on the water, but going after those who facilitate the supply chain used to move Russia's oil around the world," the government said in its statement.
Previous sanctions waiver The new targets mirror previous actions by the UK
government, which has recently broadened its focus from the energy sector to target more financial networks and companies involved in sectors such as drone manufacturing. It has previously floated even tougher energy restrictions, but has recently rowed back on escalation due to domestic price concerns.
Westminster has previously aligned closely with the EU in its efforts to amplify pressure on Russia, and in February offered its support for a full maritime services ban that was ultimately abandoned by the EU. In a policy mirroring EU law, the UK was also due to clamp down on imports of fuel made from Russian crude in third countries like India and Turkey, but later opted to defer a full-fledged ban.
The UK formally implemented its "refining loophole" ban in May, but issued a separate waiver exempting its two largest oil product imports, diesel and jet fuel. After significant media backlash over the relaxed Russian restrictions, the government has committed to the waiver, which does not have a fixed expiry date, by no later than 2027.

Syria oil pipeline attacks continue amid domestic fuel shortage
Third pipeline incident in 10 days
Crude imports drop to zero in recent months
Shortages trigger protests across country
A Syrian crude pipeline was damaged in an attack Oct. 7, in the third incident at oil and gas facilities in the country in around a week, the Syrian Petroleum Company said in a statement the same day.
Repair works are underway on the pipeline in the Al-Hasakah

area in Syria's northeast, the statement said. "This attack comes in the context of a series of attacks that
have occurred in recent times on a number of oil [pipe]lines and facilities in Syria," the statement said.
In the nearby Deir ez-Zor province, a fire broke out on a gas pipeline in the Shula area Sept. 29, knocking some power stations out of service, while on Oct. 1, an explosion at the Tishreen pressure reduction station damaged a pipeline, although pumping resumed the next day, according to statements from SPC.
In mid-August, there was an explosion on the Jabsa gas export line that halted gas flows and affected electricity generation in an attack SPC at the time described as a "deliberate act of sabotage."
Syria is working to rebuild its oil and gas infrastructure and industry after a long civil war, but the country has faced ongoing security challenges, including from remaining pockets of Islamic State fighters. However, the militant group's activity has fallen to record low levels, according to a September analysis from the Middle East Institute.
Local regional media reported that suspects have been arrested for the latest attack, though neither the perpetrators nor a motive have been identified.
The attacks on oil and gas infrastructure have coincided with widespread diesel shortages and a mid-September price hike that helped spur protests amid a backlash over the cost of living.
The energy ministry said at the time that the price increases were temporary and reflected higher procurement costs and Syria's dependence on imported supplies. Since the ouster of former President Bashar al-Assad at the end of 2024, Syria has relied primarily on Russia for crude imports and a few scattered donations from other nations, including Saudi Arabia.
Syria imported 35,000-90,000 barrels/day of crude each month throughout most of 2026, but no imports have been recorded in August, September, or so far in October, according to S&P Global Commodities At Sea. Syria has two refineries at which crude is processed into products that typically serve the domestic market.
The country imported 43,000 b/d of gasoline and diesel in September, up from an average of around 25,000 b/d throughout much of 2026, CAS data showed.
Syria's domestic crude production has climbed over the last year, but still remains below required levels to meet domestic demand.

US Gulf producers shut in 1.283 million b/d, 1.13 Bcf/d ahead of Hurricane Isaias
Chevron shuts in five facilities Occidental monitoring situation 121 manned platforms evacuated
Producers in the Gulf of Mexico have shut in 1.283 million barrels/day of oil production and 1.13 billion cubic feet/day of

natural gas output ahead of Hurricane Isaias, the Marine Minerals Administration said Oct. 8.
The shut-in volumes represent nearly 63% of the current daily oil production and 57% of the natural gas production in the Gulf of Mexico, MMA said in its latest update at 2 pm ET.
The curtailed volumes increased from 511,619 b/d and 350 MMcf/d on Oct. 7, MMA said at the time.
"Based on data from offshore operator reports submitted as of 11 am CT today [Oct. 8], personnel have been evacuated from a total of 121 production platforms, 32.61% of the 371 manned platforms" in the Gulf, it said, adding that personnel have been evacuated from five non-dynamically positioned rigs in operation.
A total of four of the 17 dynamically positioned rigs have moved off location out of the storm's path as a precaution, MMA said.
