Shell urges conventional jet investment as fuel security concerns sharpen
Conventional jet to remain aviation's main fuel for decades: Shell
Middle East jet/kerosene output forecast below 815,000 b/d in Q4: CERA
Airlines urge investment in independent supply routes
Aviation must keep investing in conventional jet fuel supply chains alongside sustainable aviation fuel, as Middle East disruptions expose weaknesses in infrastructure and supply arrangements, a Shell Aviation executive said Nov. 10 at the IATA Aviation Energy Forum in Guangzhou.
Conventional jet will remain central to aviation for decades, requiring investment in alternative supplies, trading capabilities and storage to strengthen energy security, Doris Tan, head of Shell Aviation Asia-Pacific and Middle East, said at a panel.
"Conventional jet is the main fuel that will be needed in aviation for many decades to come," Tan said. "We need to really invest in supply contingency and the infrastructure projects."
Middle Eastern jet/kerosene production is projected to remain below 815,000 barrels/day in the fourth quarter, compared with 1.05 million b/d a year earlier, according to S&P Global Energy CERA's November aviation outlook. Refined product exports through the Strait of Hormuz are expected to remain low through year-end and potentially into the first half of 2027.
The disruption is also feeding into Asian jet fuel economics with Singapore jet/kerosene-Dubai cracks strengthening $10.4/b to $66.2/b in October, according to CERA. Pertamina aims 32,000 b/d oil boost from Algeria in 2027, eyes overseas M&A
Pertamina pursues M&A in North Africa, South America
Indonesia aims 1 million b/d output by 2030
Indonesia's state-owned Pertamina expects its Algerian oil production to increase by about 32,000 barrels/day from early 2027 following its planned acquisition of interests in three onshore fields, as the company steps up overseas acquisitions to bolster crude supplies and diversify sourcing away from conflict-exposed regions.
The additional production is expected to translate into about one cargo of crude oil shipped from Algeria to Indonesia each month, Pertamina Deputy President Director Oki Muraza said in a discussion Nov. 9.
"Given the current global situation, with disruptions to one-fifth of the world's oil supplies through the Strait of Hormuz, we are stepping up efforts to bring barrels home. For example, we have just secured an acquisition in Algeria, which is expected to add around 32,000 b/d of oil production. In theory, this would translate into one cargo arriving from Algeria in Indonesia each month," Muraza said.
The acquisition is part of Pertamina's efforts to secure oil supplies from regions outside conflict-affected areas, particularly the Middle East, he said.
Pertamina, through its affiliate PT Pertamina Algeria Eksplorasi Produksi (PAEP), agreed to acquire Spanish energy company Moeve's interests in three onshore oil fields in Algeria's Berkine Basin. The deal covers Moeve's stakes in the Rhourde el Khrouf, Bir el Msana and Ourhoud fields.
Completion remains subject to customary conditions, including approval from Algerian authorities. Neither company disclosed the transaction value, the companies said in separate statements Nov. 4.
Pertamina said Nov. 4 that the acquisition was part of its "Bring the Barrel Home" initiative, which aims to optimize production from overseas upstream assets to support oil and gas supplies to Indonesia.
In December 2025, Pertamina shipped its first 1-million-barrel crude cargo from Algeria after extending the production-sharing contract for Block 405A, securing its operations in the country for another 25 years. The cargo departed from Port Arzew in Oran.
In June 2026, a tanker carrying about 450,000 barrels of Algerian crude was scheduled to arrive at Pertamina's Cilacap refinery in Central Java to support refinery operations and domestic fuel supplies.
Outside Algeria, Pertamina also holds upstream interests in Iraq and Malaysia, with part of its international portfolio managed through its 71.09% stake in France-listed Maurel & Prom.
Expansion The company is looking to expand the overseas portfolio
further through acquisitions of oil and gas assets in North Africa

and South America, as it seeks to diversify its crude supplies away from regions exposed to geopolitical conflicts, Muraza said.
"We are targeting mergers and acquisitions for oil and gas fields in North Africa, where there is no war, as well as in Latin America or South America," he said.
The company was also finalizing an expansion into Latin America through its subsidiary Maurel & Prom, Muraza said.
"We are also finalizing our move into Latin America through our subsidiary, M&P. As we continue to increase national production capacity in line with the government's target of reaching 1 million b/d by 2030, we need to secure supplies ahead of 2030," Muraza said, adding that Pertamina currently accounts for 69% of Indonesia's national oil production.
"We are pursuing significant inorganic expansion through mergers and acquisitions, acquiring and buying oil and gas fields. We have successfully done this in North Africa, and we are now working on opportunities in South America," he said.
Alongside its overseas expansion, Pertamina is continuing to raise output from domestic fields and step up exploration, Muraza said.
"We are continuing to increase domestic production while also securing supplies before 2030. We are pursuing a fairly significant expansion through inorganic growth, including mergers and acquisitions," he said.
Domestically, Pertamina is pursuing enhanced oil recovery at the Minas field and steam flooding at the Duri field in Sumatra, while accelerating development of new discoveries and exploration activities. The company also plans to drill more than 130 exploration wells through 2030, Muraza said.
Pertamina has allocated about 75% of its capital expenditure to upstream activities, he said.
"We are working toward the president's directive to reach 1 million b/d of oil production by 2030. We are pursuing various innovations and allocating the largest share of our capital expenditure to the upstream sector, with around 75% of our capex dedicated to upstream activities," he said.
Deputy Energy and Mineral Resources Minister Yuliot Tanjung, during a meeting with the Regional Representatives Council (DPD) Nov. 9, broadcast on YouTube, said Indonesia was targeting oil production of 900,000-1 million b/d by 2029.
The target would be supported by the offering of 118 new oil and gas working areas and efforts to optimize output from existing fields using enhanced oil recovery and unconventional technologies.

Saudi crude loadings reach wartime high in Oct as Yanbu flows climb
Yanbu port lifts surge 900,000 b/d in October
Loadings in Egypt's Sidi Kerir rise to 1.4 million b/d
China boosts imports in Oct, Saudi Aramco CEO says
Saudi Arabia's visible gross crude loadings during October reached a wartime high of 7.5 million barrels/day, according to the latest S&P Global Commodities at Sea data, as Yanbu loadings recovered rapidly from September's East-West pipeline disruption and the country used alternative export routes to move barrels around the Strait of Hormuz.
Visible Yanbu loadings averaged an estimated 2.8 million b/d in October, up 900,000 b/d from a wartime low of 1.9 million b/d in September, according to the data, when an attack on the EastWest pipeline interrupted shipments from Saudi Arabia's Red Sea coast.
The 7.5 million b/d total includes re-exports from Egypt and ship-to-ship transfers outside the Strait of Hormuz.
Saudi Arabia's East-West oil pipeline, with a capacity of up to 7 million b/d, was attacked on Sept. 10 along its Riyadh and Madinah segments, leading to its shutdown the following day, according to a Sept. 11 statement from the Saudi Press Agency.
Despite market estimates that repairs would take weeks for the pipeline to operate again, flows to the country's western ports resumed "within days," Saudi Arabian Oil Co. CEO Amin Nasser told analysts during a third-quarter results call on Nov. 3.
The pipeline returned to operations within "five or six days" after being attacked and was pumping 5.8 million b/d of crude Oct. 6, state media reported Saudi energy minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman as saying that day.
Visible Saudi crude loadings from Yanbu resumed Sept. 23 after appearing to pause on Sept. 12, according to CAS.
At the east coast, detected crude loadings from the Gulffacing Ras Tanura port declined from 2.4 million b/d over September to around 2 million b/d in October, CAS data showed.

Turkey plans to boost Gabar oilfield output to 100,000 b/d in H2 2027
Gabar fields currently produce 84,000 b/d
Two new reserves hold 135 million barrels
Well count rises from 184 to 230 units
Turkey plans to boost output from its Gabar oil fields to over 100,000 barrels/day in the second half of 2027, President Recep Tayyip Erdogan said in a speech to the Turkish cabinet late Nov. 9.
Erdogan said that the Gabar fields are producing 84,000 b/d and that the additional output will come from two new reserves that commenced production in October.
The new reserves, named Gaziler and Cardakli, have the potential for 135 million barrels of oil in place, he said.
In order to boost output from the Gabar fields, the number of producing wells will be increased from 184 to about 230, he said.
Turkey's produces around 120,000 b/d of oil, Erdogan added. The country produced 6.47 million metric tons of crude in 2025, up 22% year over year, according to data from Turkey's General Directorate of Mineral and Petroleum Affairs.

EIA raises 2027 Brent oil price outlook by $2/b on Middle East supply constraints
Brent crude forecast rises to $86/barrel
Middle East shut-ins average 2.5 mil b/d
Refinery crack spreads widen through 2027
The US Energy Information Administration raised its 2027 price outlook for Brent crude oil by $2/barrel compared with last month's outlook to $86/b, amid ongoing supply constraints.
The EIA raised its Brent price outlook for next year because the agency expects global oil inventories to rebuild slightly more slowly than projected in October, EIA spokesperson Lee Tucker told Platts, a part of S&P Global Energy.
"The main reason is that we no longer expect Iran's oil production to increase in 2027," Tucker said.
The EIA also raised its gasoline outlook by 17 cents to $3.73/ gallon, and it increased its outlook for 2027 diesel prices by 54 cents to $5.03/gal, according to the agency's Short-Term Energy Outlook released Nov. 10.
Regarding gasoline and diesel prices, the EIA said it now assumes the global petroleum product market will face continued supply constraints through the end of 2027.

Global oil inventories fell by an average of 1.2 million b/d in Q3 2026, but will begin building in Q4 2026, the EIA said.
The agency said, however, "The heightened risk of supply disruptions and longer shipping routes will limit the near-term impact of global stock builds until geopolitical risk dissipates and inventories begin to return closer to pre-conflict levels."

damage to refineries in the Middle East and Russia, and spreads will remain high for most of next year due to ongoing disruptions to global markets, the EIA said.

Occidental exceeds production targets using new drilling technology
New Mexico output surges 23% since January
AI surveillance cuts well failures by 22%
Company identifies 4 billion boe in EOR resources
Occidental Petroleum beat its own US onshore production targets in the third quarter as the company rolled out a technology-driven strategy to unlock billions of barrels of additional resources across its Permian Basin, Gulf of America, and international portfolio, the company said Nov. 10.
US onshore production outperformed the midpoint of guidance by 20,000 barrels of oil equivalent/day in the quarter, driven by record output in New Mexico and a surge in Powder River Basin volumes, Oxy CEO Richard Jackson said during a third-quarter earnings call.
In New Mexico, Occidental reported record gross operated production of 284,000 boe/d, up about 23% since January, according to the company's earnings materials. In the Powder River Basin, the company brought a record 27 wells online in the third quarter, driving more than 70% production growth versus the first quarter to average net production of 23,000 boe/d in the third quarter.
"Across US unconventional, our 2025 and 2026 wells have delivered top-tier performance in every basin with at least 10% above the industry average on a six-month cumulative oil per lateral foot basis," Jackson said.
In the Delaware Basin, Jackson said, increasing the number of wells per bench in some areas maintained individual well performance while boosting recovery per drill spacing unit by up to 2.5 times, a development that could materially increase recoverable volumes without similar increases in capital spending.
On enhanced oil recovery, Jackson said the company holds 2 billion boe of resource, much of it on its 1.2 million-acre Permian Central Basin Platform position.
The company is applying unconventional drilling and fracking techniques to historically bypassed conventional zones and drilled eight conventional grassroots horizontal wells in 2026 that are competitive within its portfolio, featuring high oil-cut production and lower decline rates.
Sidetrack program An EOR sidetrack program launched in late February had
drilled about 80 sidetracks by the end of the third quarter, with

500 additional sidetrack candidates identified. The sidetrack program and conventional horizontals
together added 10,000 boe/d as of the end of the third quarter, according to the company's earnings report. New conventional horizontal opportunities contribute more than 200 million boe of development-ready resource, with further upside through appraisal and CO2 EOR, Jackson said.
In unconventional EOR, Occidental has identified another 2 billion boe of resources. Its first three commercial projects are progressing toward injection by 2029, with more than 30 additional development-ready opportunities in the pipeline.
These projects over the last 10 years have demonstrated about 45% production uplift, with broader technology combinations showing potential to increase estimated ultimate recovery by 70% to 140% per well.
"We have identified roughly 450 million boe of waterflood resource at less than $40 breakeven, providing more than a decade of development runway today," Jackson added, referring to the Gulf of Mexico.
Occidental's first Gulf waterflood project, the King dumpflood, began first injection in July. Third-party gas pipeline constraints and maintenance activity affected regional third-quarter production. Production was fully restored by late October.
International volumes were slightly lower in the quarter, mainly because of a change in gas processing mode to ethane rejection at Dolphin in the Middle East.
CO2 strategy "We have signed offtake agreements with two project
developers to deliver 2 million metric tons per year of anthropogenic CO2 captured from third-party gas processing plants for use in our Permian EOR operations, which represent about 15% of our 2026 purchased CO2," Jackson said.
Around 50 million tons per year of CO2 are available from proximate sources, including around 10 million tons per year from gas processing and more than 15 million tons per year from natural gas power generation in the Permian, according to company estimates.
On digital operations, Occidental said about 9,000 wells, or 68% of its artificial lift wells, are under AI surveillance with predictive intelligence, supporting up to 7% annual production uplift and reducing failure frequency by 22% versus 2023.
About 50% of that AI-surveilled subset is operating autonomously, including roughly 450,000 barrels/day of production, particularly in the Delaware Basin. In Oman, 600,000 barrels per day of steam injection is now optimized well by well and pattern by pattern using these tools, Occidental said.

making progress on non-technology-related repairs and had captured more than 2,000 metric tons of atmospheric CO2 to build pellet inventory.

US Treasury may allow purchase of sanctioned tankers on case basis
OFAC may be targeting 50-80 tanker deals
Higher freight rates spur a shift to compliance
The US Treasury Department may allow companies to buy sanctioned shipping vessels and return them to legitimate shipping on a case-by-case basis, according to a government source and a person with knowledge of the matter.
"Treasury may consider proposals from legitimate shipping actors to purchase and restore shadow fleet vessels to the licit shipping industry on a case-by-case basis," a US official told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy.
This approach "ensures that no sanctioned person receives any benefit, and reduces the size of the fleet overall, while helping address pressures on the global energy market," the US official said.
Another source with knowledge of the matter said Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control could be targeting 50-80 sanctioned tankers owned by non-sanctioned companies. There could be 20-30 owners willing to move quickly, the source said.
At the end of September, there were a total of 1,225 tankers sanctioned by the US, UK or EU, S&P Global Commodities at Sea said in an Oct. 29 shadow tanker report. Of those, 560 were sanctioned by the US.
Shadow tankers are increasingly being drawn into the compliant market due to rising freight rates, according to a separate CAS market impact report issued Oct. 23.
In 2026, 41 shadow-fleet crude tankers entered compliant trade through Oct. 20, with 68% remaining fully compliant so far, compared with just 17.5% of vessels that remained compliant in 2025.
The shift to continued compliance was particularly notable in the third quarter when mainstream tanker earnings surged, the report said. Dirty Arab Gulf-China VLCC freight averaged approximately $197/mt in September, up 77% from August, the report said.
CAS defines the shadow fleet as tankers that have transported Iranian or Russian oil in the last two years, vessels subject to US sanctions, and vessels with unknown or suspected false flag registrations.

October milder in Europe and Japan, but colder in Canada

October was milder in Europe and Japan, but colder in Canada than a year ago, according to data obtained from CustomWeather and gathered by Platts.

A total of 19 cities geographically representative of Europe had 10.9% fewer heating degree days in October than in October 2025. The total for the September-October period was 15% fewer degree days than the similar period last year and 19.9% fewer than normal.

Eight cities geographically representative of Canada had 9.7% more degree days in October than in October 2025. The total for the September-October period was 17.9% more than the similar period last year and 4.9% fewer than normal.

Four cities geographically representative of Japan had 26.8% fewer degree days in October than in October 2025. The total for the September-October period was 21.7% fewer than the similar period last year and 22.4% fewer than normal.
