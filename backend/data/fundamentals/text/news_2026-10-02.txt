G7 orders 100 million barrel oil stock release, rejects energy export bans

The G7 has set a four-month deadline for its member states to release the last 100 million barrels of oil stocks they committed to in March after collectively pledging to refrain from energy export restrictions within the organization.
In a statement Oct. 2, the seven industrial countries called on the International Energy Agency to "monitor the immediate and full implementation of the March 2026 commitments." Adjusting for stocks already released to the market, the group pledged to oversee the release of 100 million barrels of oil by February next year.
The decision included a commitment for the G7 and partner countries to frontload a "substantial diesel release" in the next 20 days, with members to discuss the possibility of additional releases in coordination with the IEA. The group will also seek to coordinate refinery maintenance schedules to avoid simultaneous shutdowns and increase utilization rates, it said, advocating for engagement with non G7-countries that hold additional capacity.
The statement followed an announcement from US President Donald Trump on Truth Social declaring that Europe has agreed to immediately begin releasing a "massive amount" of its "heavily stocked diesel," after reports of White House requests for leading EU countries to release extra reserves, or potentially face US export bans.

Vietnam extends fuel tax relief through 2026 amid supply diversification
Zero import duties retained on gasoline, diesel, jet fuel
Gasoline ceiling prices rise despite relief
PetroVietnam signs US crude supply deals
Vietnam has extended reduced taxes on petroleum imports, sales and refinery feedstocks through the end of 2026 as it seeks to contain domestic prices and diversify energy supplies, according to Resolution 43/2026/NQ-CP issued on Sept. 30.
Extended from Oct. 1 through Dec. 31, the measures, such as the preferential import duty on gasoline, remain at zero, compared with its normal rate of 10%, while duties on diesel, fuel oil, kerosene, and jet fuel remain at zero instead of 7%, according to the government.
Import duties also remain at zero for several refinery and petrochemical feedstocks. The measures cut duties on xylene, condensate, and paraxylene from 3%, and on certain other cyclic hydrocarbons from 2%.
The environmental protection tax remains at Dong0/liter for gasoline, excluding ethanol, diesel, kerosene, fuel oil, and jet fuel.
The covered products also remain exempt from the requirement to declare, calculate, and pay value-added tax, while suppliers can continue to deduct input VAT.
However, the resolution does not extend the earlier zero special-consumption tax on gasoline. That tax continues to be applied separately under Vietnam's 2025 Special Consumption Tax Law and related regulations.
The government said the extension would support energy security, inflation control, and business activity while enabling importers to diversify supplies and reduce their reliance on traditional markets. The Industry and Trade Ministry may propose shortening or extending the measures if required by economic conditions, energy security, or fuel market stability, subject to government approval.
Extending the relief prevented the tax concessions from expiring Sept. 30 but did not result in across-the-board retailprice reductions.
From 3:00 pm local time Oct. 1, Vietnam raised the ceiling price for E5 RON 92 gasoline by 172 dong/liter ($0.0066/liter or $1.05/barrel) to 26,569 dong/liter ($1.02/liter or $162.5/b) and increased the ceiling price for E10 RON 95 gasoline by 102 dong/ liter to 27,189 dong/liter.
The diesel ceiling declined by 780 dong/liter to 29,717 dong/ liter, while the fuel-oil ceiling increased by 919 dong/kg to 20,391 dong/kg.
The Industry and Trade Ministry attributed the mixed adjustment to changes in international product prices and the dong-US dollar exchange rate.

India's September LPG imports from Middle East highest since start of war: CAS
Middle East LPG shipments to India rise 20% MOM
US LPG exports to India decrease 38% in Sept.
UAE overtakes US as India's top LPG supplier
India took delivery of the highest volume of LPG from the Middle East in September since the start of the Middle East war and the disruption to cargo flows via the Strait of Hormuz in late February, according to the latest S&P Commodities at Sea data on Oct. 2, while the volume of LPG inflows from the US fell by

more than 38% from a month ago to its lowest since the start of the war.
India imported 955,400 mt of LPG, comprising 449,000 mt propane and 506,000 mt butane, from the Middle East in September, up 20.34% from a month earlier. The Middle East contributed 64.99% to India's overall imports in September, up from 39.51% in August, with the largest volume of imports arriving from the UAE at 485,400 mt, followed by Oman at 238,000 mt, Iran at 135,700 mt and Qatar at 96,300 mt, the CAS data showed.
Since the start of tensions in the Middle East, the US has been India's largest LPG supplier until September, when the top spot was overtaken by the UAE. India imported 478,800 mt of LPG from the US last month, down 38.34% from August when the latter exported 776,500 mt of LPG to India, the data showed.
According to a report released Sept. 29 by analysts at CAS, US LPG exports declined approximately 27% week over week to about 1.3 million mt in the week ended Sept. 25, reflecting weaker loading activity across key US Gulf Coast terminals.
"The reduction appeared to be driven by logistical constraints, including Panama Canal restrictions and vessel rerouting via the Cape of Good Hope, rather than weaker demand," the report showed.
It added that following higher flows in the previous week, US LPG exports for the week ended Sept. 25 eased slightly amid ship availability and scheduling constraints.
"The decline was led by shipments to the US-Far East route, which fell nearly 29% week over week to 612,000 mt, while no LPG volumes were directed to India," the report showed.
India took delivery of 1.470 million mt of LPG, comprising 760,000 mt of propane and 710,000 mt of butane, in September, falling 7.66% from a month earlier and 31.69% from a year ago.

Russia could partially open diesel exports in case of surplus output: Novak
Deputy PM says exports to begin after return to surplus
Putin previously said sanctions would stop diesel exports
Diesel export ban to stay in place until end of October
Russia could consider partially opening diesel exports once it returns to a production surplus, Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak told its Federation Council Oct. 2, describing the market as balanced and sufficiently supplied, local media reported.
Traditionally the world's number-two diesel exporter, Russia started shutting in its diesel production in July following persistent Ukrainian drone attacks on its refining sector, now extended until the end of October. For non-producers, which include traders and refineries with capacities of less than 1 million metric tons/year, the ban will remain in place until the end of January.
The deputy prime minister added that given Russia produces more diesel than it consumes, it is necessary to avoid accumulating more stocks than the country has the capacity to store, which would force refineries to reduce throughput and affect the output of other products.
Novak's comments come a day after Russia's President Vladimir Putin said at the Valday Forum in Moscow that Russia had sufficient diesel but warned the fuel wouldn't make it to international markets because of foreign sanctions.
"We already have enough diesel fuel. But it won't reach global

markets because of the bans and sanctions against our oil and petroleum products," Putin was reported to have said at the forum, according to the transcript posted on the Kremlin website.
By the end of August, analysts at S&P Global Energy CERA estimated that almost half of Russia's primary refining capacity was offline due to Ukrainian drone strikes, with persistent and targeted attacks often hitting the same sites repeatedly during the month. On Oct. 1, Putin estimated that Ukrainian strikes had shaved off about 1% of Russia's GDP.
Novak also said Oct. 2 that once the fuel market is balanced, Russia will return to consuming its own gasoline and exporting it to other countries. Russia produces around 10%-15% more gasoline than it consumes, he said, calling the imports a temporary measure.
Gasoline was the first type of fuel export banned by the Kremlin back in April, and the restriction has since been extended through to the end of January 2027. The measure has seen Russia turn to foreign suppliers to import local fuel supply, exacerbating global concerns over international tightness.
He added that Russia will not stop attacks on Ukrainian ships and sensitive sites in exchange for halting Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries.
The Russian president's comments come amid growing international concerns over rising diesel prices, particularly from the administration of US President Donald Trump, ahead of the country's upcoming midterm elections.
Speaking to local broadcasters Sept. 27, Trump recently called the price spikes "a Russia problem" rather than a consequence of Middle Eastern trade shut-ins, and urged Ukraine to pull back drone attacks targeting the country's refineries.

Canada's 1 million b/d Pacific Coast oil pipeline crosses major hurdle
Pipeline to be called now Pacific Link
Construction to start in Q3 of 2027
Pacific Dilbit discount continues to widen
A 1 million-barrel/day crude oil pipeline to the Canadian Pacific Coast, from the prime oil sands producing region of Alberta, has received a major boost, with the federal government listing the proposed infrastructure as a "project of national importance"

and kickstarting an environmental and regulatory process, along with engineering works and stakeholder consultations, top government officials said Oct. 1.
A project of national importance would signify that the federal government-backed Major Projects Office now has sole authority to handle regulatory permits and grant approvals within a specified timeline.
"We didn't get here overnight, and the announcement today comes after an MoU we signed last November," Alberta Premier Danielle Smith told reporters on a webcast from Fort McMurray, the heart of the oil sands producing areas of Athabasca, Cold Lake and Peace River. "In July, we submitted a project application for the West Coast pipeline for being listed as a project of national interest, and our aim now is to start construction on Sept. 1, 2027."
The pipeline is now formally to be called Pacific Link (compared with West Coast earlier) and will sustain over 3.5 million b/d of crude oil production from the oil sands region, Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said on the same webcast, adding the new pipeline will primarily target Asia and also provide an opportunity for Western Canadian Sedimentary Basin to wean away from its traditional market of the US and find new customers.
"This will be a tremendous opportunity for oil sands producers to narrow the price differentials and reach out to a new market," Carney said. "There's a structural challenge of energy security and the existential challenge of climate change. And these challenges are forcing countries to think differently of where their energy comes from and on who they can rely to supply."
Clear path forward A year ago, the planned pipeline was a vision, but today "we
have a clear path forward," Smith said, adding the construction of Pacific Link will result in the province's oil sands producers utilizing the latest technology to add incremental barrels through what would likely be brownfield expansions.
The West Coast pipeline will originate in Bruderheim, Alberta, and terminate at the Roberts Bank terminal in the southwest coast of British Columbia, the Alberta government said separately in a release, adding the proposed infrastructure will be "largely" following the existing 890,000-b/d Trans Mountain pipeline corridor to a deep-water, VLCC-capable port terminal at Roberts Bank.
Leveraging this existing corridor will significantly reduce regulatory and logistical barriers, while minimizing land disturbances, it said.
The West Coast pipeline will be a private-public partnership, with participants including the Alberta Petroleum Marketing Commission (representing the provincial government), Trans Mountain Corp. (representing the federal government) and Calgarybased midstream player Pembina Pipeline Corp., Smith said.
"The PONI listing provides greater certainty through a clearer, more efficient and predictable federal review process that will maintain environmental standards and Indigenous consultation, which will be led by the Major Projects Office and supported by the Canada Energy Regulator," Pembina Pipeline Corp. said in a statement on Oct. 1.

ALASKA DATA: September oil output rises on Pikka gains
Santos, Repsol increase output at Pikka North Slope field Pikka output reached 70,000 b/d at end of September Companies near phase one goal of 80,000 b/d for Pikka
Alaska North Slope production gained sharply in late September as Santos Ltd. throttled up output at its new Pikka

field on the western North Slope. As of Sept. 27, Pikka was producing 70,000 b/d and nearing its
first phase target of 80,000 b/d, a Santos official said, asking not to be identified. Pikka is owned 51% by Australia-based Santos and 49% by Repsol, based in Madrid.
Alaska Department of Revenue production data showed North Slope production gaining 49,817 b/d between Sept. 1 and Sept. 30, reaching 551,247 b/d on the final day of the month. The increase came mainly in the last few days of September as the Santos increased the injection of seawater into the Pikka reservoir, which stimulated production of crude oil.
Pikka's oil is metered by the state revenue department through the Kuparuk field pipeline system.
Santos previously said it expects to achieve the phase-one goal of 80,000 b/d by the third quarter of the year. The company said it is planning a phase two expansion at Pikka with the addition of more wells and facilities, which could bring Pikka to about 160,000 b/d when it is complete.
The phase two expansion has not been officially announced, but company officials have said that the engineering and planning are underway. Santos and Repsol have also discovered new oil deposits south of the Pikka field and are now doing the planning and engineering for the development of those.
Other North Slope fields performed as expected in September, according to the revenue department data. Prudhoe Bay, the largest field on the slope, averaged 285,914 b/d in September, up from a 263,548 average in August. September production is roughly on par with September, 2025, according to the data.
The increase was expected because field production facilities tend to perform better as cooler weather begins in autumn on the North Slope. Prudhoe is operated by Hilcorp Energy and owned in roughly one-third shares by Hilcorp, ConocoPhillips and ExxonMobil.
The small Lisburne field produced an average of 16,651 b/d in September, on par with a 16,508 b/d average in August but down somewhat year over year from an average of 17,388 b/d in September, 2025. Lisburne's production includes output from smaller satellite accumulations, which is metered along with the Lisburne field itself. Hilcorp Energy is the owner and operator at Lisburne, which is located in the eastern part of the Prudhoe Bay field, also operated by Hilcorp.
The Alpine field, the farthest west of the North Slope producing fields and located near the new Pikka field, continued to lag production in September, according to the revenue department data. Alpine produced an average of 38,877 b/d compared with an average of 39,300 b/d in August. Alpine is down an average of 3,900 b/d year-over-year from September 2025. Alpine is owned and operated by ConocoPhillips.
Alpine production has been affected by problems in managing rising gas-oil ratios in nearby satellite fields, GMT-2 and GMT-1, in the federally owned National Petroleum Reserve-Alaska. Mixed oil and gas fluids and water are shipped by pipeline to processing facilities in the nearby Alpine field.

ExxonMobil, TotalEnergies win production-sharing oil cargoes at PPSA auction
Total volume of 2 million barrels
Volatility continues to mute sales
Remaining lots to be sold Oct. 7
Global energy heavyweights ExxonMobil and TotalEnergies submitted the winning bids for two cargoes of crude oil from Brazil's Bacalhau and Buzios production-sharing fields at a recent spot-market auction, but international market volatility continued to undermine interest in the sales, according to the government subsalt management company PPSA.
ExxonMobil claimed a 1 million-barrel cargo of crude from the Equinor-operated Bacalhau field at the auction on Sept. 30, PPSA said in the results of the regulator's ninth spot-market sale published Oct. 1 after markets closed. A portion of Bacalhau's reservoir lies within the Norte de Carcara production-sharing area. The vessel will be the first ammonia-capable ship on the Singapore Registry of Ships, which has already registered eight methanol-fueled vessels since 2020 as shipowners increasingly adopt alternative marine fuels, according to MPA's statement released at the SRS Forum.

A record 74 vessels from 28 companies qualified for MPA's Green Ship Program in 2026 for adopting solutions exceeding prevailing environmental regulatory requirements, the authority said Oct. 2, recognizing Seaspan Corporation for contributing the highest net tonnage to the SRS in 2025-26.
The registry has grown to more than 4,400 vessels. Its tonnage reached a record 137 million gross tonnage at the end of 2025, up 27% from a year earlier, making Singapore the world's fourthlargest ship registry, according to MPA.
"With an average vessel age of about 11 years, the SRS is also currently the youngest fleet among the world's top ten ship registries," said MPA.
