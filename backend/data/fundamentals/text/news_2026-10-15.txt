The Iran war is accelerating a redesign of Middle East oil export infrastructure as Gulf producers expand routes that reduce tanker exposure to the Strait of Hormuz, but funding, political and security hurdles remain, according to analysts and company executives.
For decades, Gulf producers optimized around a single reality: most exports would move by tanker through the Strait of Hormuz. The US-Iran war has exposed the vulnerability of that model. Governments, national oil companies, traders and importers are now treating Hormuz disruption as a recurring strategic risk rather than a remote contingency. A key challenge, however, is that bypassing Hormuz reduces concentration risk, but it does not eliminate geopolitical risk, according to the analysts.
More than 20 million b/d of crude and refined products, roughly 20% of global trade, transited Hormuz before the war began in February 2026, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea. Although crude flows have recovered in recent weeks -after collapsing to below 5 million in August- total oil shipments through the chokepoint remain below pre-war levels, CAS data shows.
Faced with the largest oil supply disruption in history, the response has focused on new regional pipelines, ports and storage hubs.

Crude and condensate floating storage inventories in Southeast Asian waters fell at the end of September to their lowest level since November 2019, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea, as Iranian barrels accumulated closer to the Gulf region amid a US naval blockade and tighter sanctions pressure.
Total floating storage stocks in Southeast Asia fell 51.2% month over month to 11.24 million barrels end-September from 23.01 million barrels end-August, according to CAS data compiled Oct. 12. The volume was also down from 35.97 million barrels July 14, when the US reinstated its blockade of Iranian ports, and 49.19 million barrels April 13, when Washington imposed its first blockade.
Iranian crude and condensate accounted for most of the remaining Southeast Asian inventory, but those volumes also declined to 9.25 million barrels endSeptember from 17.8 million barrels a month earlier, CAS data showed.
Iranian floating storage has now fallen about 65% since July 14 and nearly 78% since April 13, when it stood at 26.76 million barrels and 41.45 million barrels, respectively, the data showed.
According to CAS, a ship is considered floating storage if it is laden and does not move more than 10 nautical miles per day for at least seven consecutive days.

Asia-Oceania ultra-light crudes likely to sustain premiums as Middle East supply stays tight
Ichthys premiums averaged $16.32/b in October Thailand takes Alba condensate amid high Australian premiums Seoul seeks to cut costs on Malaysian, Australian
feedstock intake
Southeast Asian and Oceania ultra-light crude cargoes are expected to maintain spot-market premiums amid ongoing uncertainty in Middle Eastern deodorized field and low-sulfur condensate supplies, as South Korea streamlines customs procedures and revises tariffs to lower feedstock import costs, industry sources and Korea Customs Service said.
Refiners and petrochemical producers with condensate splitters for naphtha output are heavily relying on Australian, Malaysian and African ultra-light crudes, after struggling to secure steady Middle East DFC and LSC supplies since the Middle East war started in March, feedstock managers at Japanese, South Korean, Chinese and Thai refiners in Ibaraki, Incheon, Seosan, Fujian and Rayong, as well as Singaporebased traders, told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, in market discussions Oct. 9-15.
Among recent spot deals, a South Korean refiner purchased a 600,000-barrel cargo of Australian Ichthys Field condensate scheduled to load Oct. 28-Nov. 1 at a premium equivalent to around $8/barrel over Platts Dated Brent, FOB.
Japan has made occasional purchases of LSC spot cargoes, but has struggled to secure even a single cargo so far this year.
Meanwhile, Japan saw its crude and condensate imports from Qatar tumble 84% year over year to 3.9 million barrels in the first eight months, the latest data from the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry issued Sept. 30 showed. Japanese refiners have received some medium-sour Qatar Marine crude so far this year, but no shipments of DFC or LSC, METI data showed.
However, Japan has been bringing home some of its equity ultra-light crude barrels from Australia, with 626,000 barrels of Ichthys condensate discharging back in August, METI data issued Sept. 30 showed.

Most recently, Japan's Mitsubishi was heard to have moved its equity Australian North West Shelf condensate cargo for loading over Oct. 20-24.
Reflecting East Asia's heavy reliance on Australian ultralight crudes, Platts assessed Ichthys condensate at an average premium of $16.32/b to Dated Brent, FOB to date in October, compared to the September average premium of $11.87 and the 2025 average of $3.01/b.
Platts also assessed North West Shelf condensate at an average premium of $3.33/b to Dated Brent, FOB to date in October, compared to the September average premium of $2.24 and the 2025 average of minus $2.12/b.
With Australian condensate premiums elevated, some downstream players are looking further afield for ultra-light crude supply. Feedstock and logistics managers at Thai and Chinese refining and petrochemical companies in Rayong and Fujian told Platts that African grades are emerging as an alternative.
Thailand imported 14,818 b/d of Alen and Alba condensate from Equatorial Guinea in August, compared with zero shipments from the African supplier in the previous month and the same month a year earlier, according to the latest Thai customs data.
In China, shipments of ultra-light crude and natural gas liquids from Russia's Sakhalin 2 project to Dongshan Bay of Gulei in Fujian province surged 133% year over year to 9.75 million barrels in the first nine months of the year, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea data.
Sakhalin 2 is an oil, gas and condensate project located in the Sea of Okhotsk offshore Russia's Far East.

Customs Service is taking steps to lower feedstock procurement costs for domestic refiners and petrochemical producers. KCS said in a statement issued Oct. 12 and its October industry briefing report that it is expanding customs support to facilitate imports of Malaysian condensate and Australian natural gas liquids.

CHINA DATA: Independent Russian crude imports hit 6-month high; Iranian flows fall
Jan-Sep Russian crude imports rise 71%
Russian gains fail to offset declines in Iranian supply
ESPO premiums drop $3/b
China's independent refiners increased imports of Russian crude in September as tighter Iranian supplies drove a shift in feedstock, with volumes rising 26.2% month over month to a sixmonth high of 5.4 million metric tons, or 1.32 million barrels/day, according to data collected by Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, from industry sources Oct. 15.
However, higher Russian inflows were insufficient to offset the decline in Iranian crude imports, which fell 57.3% month over month to 2.2 million mt, reaching their lowest level since January 2022, when deliveries were 2.18 million mt, the data showed.
Combined imports of Russian and Iranian crude by China's independent refining sector fell 22% month over month to 7.59 million mt in September, according to the data.
China's independent refiners imported 3.4 million mt of ESPO crude in September, equivalent to 34 cargoes of 100,000 mt each, up from 3.2 million mt in August, the data showed.

ESPO premiums fall Mainstream offers for December-delivery ESPO crude were
reported at premiums of about $25/b to ICE Brent futures on a DES Shandong basis on Oct. 15, down from about $28/b in the week ended Oct. 9, refinery sources said. No deals were concluded at the current level.
"With prices for alternative crudes falling, independent refiners are waiting for ESPO prices to come down," a Dongyingbased refinery source said.
The refinery source said ESPO cargoes are unlikely to clear at current premiums, increasing the likelihood of further price declines.
"Deals for December cargoes are difficult to conclude because prices remain too high for buyers," a Shandong-based trader said.
Market participants indicated that offers for December ESPO had fallen to around $20/b as of Oct. 15, with some independent refiners targeting prices as low as $16/b or even $13/b, according to two refinery sources in Shandong.
"Those levels are too low to be reachable at the moment," a refinery source in Dongying said, referring to the $13/b-$16/b range.
November-loading ESPO cargoes were reported to be traded at premiums of about $30/b to ICE Brent futures on Oct. 15, according to refinery sources. Premiums were about $12/b at the beginning of September.

Indonesia adds 52,800 mt/y of LPG capacity at Cilamaya plant to curb imports
West Java plant produces 160 mt/day LPG
Indonesia imports 7 mil-8 mil mt/y LPG
Government explores CNG as cheaper option
Indonesia will add 52,800 metric tons/year of liquefied petroleum gas production capacity with the start of operations at local company PT Energi Nusantara Perkasa's Cilamaya plant in West Java, the company's President Director, Sardjono, said Oct. 15. The project could help Indonesia reduce its dependence on LNP imports.
The plant is designed to produce 160 metric tons/day of LPG and 320 barrels/day of condensate, Sardjono said at the inauguration, which marked the start of operations and production.

"We believe national energy independence must be built through real investment, stronger domestic industry and synergy between the government and businesses," Sardjono said.
Indonesia produces no more than 1.8 million mt/year of LPG domestically, compared with annual consumption of 8.5 million mt, Energy and Mineral Resources Minister Bahlil Lahadalia said. Including industrial demand, total consumption rises to around 11 million-12 million mt/year, while imports amount to around 7 million-8 million mt/year, said Lahadalia, who also serves as Coordinating Minister for Downstreaming and Energy Transition.
Geopolitical conflicts heightened uncertainty over energy supplies and strengthened the case for countries to develop domestic resources, Lahadalia said.
"Almost every country in the world is now thinking about how to develop its own domestic energy sector," Lahadalia said, citing ongoing geopolitical conflicts.
The government is also exploring CNG as an alternative to LPG to leverage domestic gas resources and reduce reliance on imported fuel.
CNG is already used in some restaurants and hotels, while 3 kg cylinders are being tested and 12 kg cylinders are already in use, Lahadalia said. He said CNG could be 30%-40% cheaper, but did not specify the basis for the comparison.
Lahadalia said Indonesia's gas production was rich in methane and ethane, known as C1 and C2, which he said were not suitable for LPG production.
The Cilamaya facility has a gas processing capacity of 40 million cubic feet/day, with a 10% design allowance that could raise its maximum capacity to 44 MMcf/day. The remaining gas is returned to Pertamina's Offshore Northwest Java operations for further use, Sardjono said.
The plant uses mechanical refrigeration and turbine expander technology to improve LPG recovery. The relocation cost $12 million and the plant's assets were valued at $24 million, bringing the total investment in Cilamaya to around $36 million, Sardjono said.
ENP also operates an LPG processing plant in Gresik, East Java. The two facilities have a combined installed gas processing capacity of 140 MMcf/day. ENP has produced about 1 million mt of LPG over nearly 19 years of operating the Gresik plant, which has a capacity of 100 MMcf/day and has been run under a jointoperation arrangement with Pertamina since 2007, Sardjono said.
Sardjono asked the government to extend the Gresik plant's operating life, citing its contribution to domestic LPG production, and also called for reliable gas supplies, coordination among relevant stakeholders and technical evaluation to keep the facility operating.
Lahadalia instructed the ministry's oil and gas directorate general to expedite the extension process once the company had met all requirements, asking officials to put completed applications on his desk within a week for approval.
Lahadalia said LPG subsidies amounted to around 87 trillion rupiah ($4.85 billion) when the Indonesian crude price was $70/b and could approach 100 trillion rupiah when prices averaged $90-$100/b.

Equatorial Guinea plans Zafiro field output boost, major gas expansion
Zafiro field could hit 50,000 b/d with partner
Eyes 10 gas fields to feed key EG LNG plant
Issues new laws to lure upstream investors
Equatorial Guinea is hoping to hit 30,000 b/d at its flagship Zafiro oil field - which ExxonMobil exited in 2024 - through an upcoming well reconnection project, but could reach 50,000 b/d by bringing a partner on board, the head of state-run GEPetrol said Oct. 15.
In a press conference on the sidelines of the Africa Energy Week conference in Cape Town, Bienvenido Nguema Envo, director general of the African country's national oil company, said 7,000-11,000 barrels/day of crude output could be added in 2027 by reconnecting old wells.
"Together with the workovers they present an initial phase to ramp up production to just north of 30,000 b/d for Zafiro," Envo said.
The country has exported just 14,000 b/d of medium sweet Zafiro crude so far this year, according to data from S&P Global Commodities at Sea.
Envo added: "There is a much bigger project, the redevelopment of block B and the Zafiro field. There are enough studies right now to justify the project. So we are looking for some partners to be able to execute that."
"Numbers vary on how far we can take the production, but certainly north of 50,000 b/d" he said.
That will require new wells and an analysis of whether the existing facilities on the field can handle additional volumes, Envo said.
The Zafiro field has historically been the cornerstone of Equatorial Guinea's oil sector, but was taken into state hands in the second quarter of 2024 when ExxonMobil exited the country. It pumped some 280,000 b/d at peak in 2004 but has faced major natural declines.
GEPetrol has limited experience operating assets, especially of Zafiro's size. Envo did not specify any potential partners for the development.
At the same press conference, hydrocarbons minister Antonio Oburu Ondo said that the small OPEC member was working hard to attract new upstream investment, by "improving our legal and regulatory competitiveness" to be "absolutely competitive and to modernize our terms."
The country is introducing a new hydrocarbons law this month, and a special economic zones law, to attract new investors, he said.
Equatorial Guinea once pumped above 300,000 b/d but currently produces some 50,000 b/d, according to official figures, amid underinvestment and field maturation.

INTERVIEW: KazMunayGas seeks consortium to develop deep Caspian oil reserves
Iskaziyev says replenishing reserves is top priority
KMG expects all disputes to be resolved
US sanctions stall Kalamkas-Khazar project
KazMunayGas is assembling a multinational consortium including Shell, Chevron, Sinopec and CNOOC to explore ultradeep formations in Kazakhstan's Caspian region, opening a new frontier in one of the world's most significant hydrocarbon provinces, Kurmangazy Iskaziyev, first deputy chair of KazMunayGas' management board, said during an interview in Almaty.
"Replenishing reserves and the resource base is a top priority for us. We have worked on this issue for a long time, conducting a regional study of 15 sedimentary basins in the Republic of Kazakhstan," Iskaziyev told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, on Oct. 1.
The initiative targets "highly promising" formations between 5.5 and 10 kilometers deep -- well below the current maximum depth of discovered fields in Kazakhstan -- and will require largescale, high-resolution seismic surveys covering 60,000 linear kilometers over three years, according to Iskaziyev. The project is set to begin next year, he said.

The push to replenish Kazakhstan's reserve base comes as one of the country's flagship producing projects, Karachaganak, has started to mature, while the Tengizchevroil project is nearing its plateau and Kashagan has reached its plateau. All three projects face contract expiration windows between 2033 and 2041, creating urgency to identify the next generation of commercial fields to sustain export volumes via the Caspian Pipeline Consortium and other routes.
Consortium talks Negotiations on forming the exploration consortium are
currently underway with Shell, Chevron, Sinopec and CNOOC, Iskaziyev said, with CNOOC's deepwater experience in Guyana cited as directly relevant to the technical challenges posed by Kazakhstan's deep-seated geological traps.
"We have held discussions with the companies interested in participating, and negotiations regarding the formation of a consortium are currently underway," Iskaziyev said.
The involvement of Shell is particularly notable given that the company is simultaneously engaged in arbitration proceedings with Kazakhstan. Kazakhstan is pursuing multi-billion-dollar compensation from international companies operating in the country for claimed cost overruns and delays to the project's expansion.
Iskaziyev expressed confidence that current disputes would eventually be resolved, framing Shell's continued engagement as a signal of confidence in Kazakhstan's investment climate. "It reflects Kazakhstan's image and its recognition of our resource potential," he said.
Shell declined to comment on the KazMunayGas statements. Chevron's participation can also mark a strategic expansion for the US-based producer, which has historically confined its upstream Kazakhstan activities to its stake in Tengizchevroil and involvement in Karachaganak, according to Iskaziyev. He said Chevron had "never ventured beyond those projects" but is now ready to invest despite current challenges. Chevron said Oct. 6 it is constantly evaluating opportunities but does not comment on commercial matters. In a further sign of renewed international interest, Iskaziyev said BP, which exited Kazakhstan in 2009, is now returning and that a contract for a major block is likely to be signed before the end of the year. BP did not answer a request for comment Oct. 6. Iskaziyev also said that KazMunayGas is ready to form a joint venture with ExxonMobil to expand the Kashagan oil field, but the companies have not yet reached any agreements, in contrast to recent media reports. Bloomberg reported Aug. 6 that ExxonMobil flagged to Kazakhstan its readiness for a US$80 billion joint investment in Kashagan, which is contingent on resolving a long-running US$150 billion dispute between the government and international companies. The proposed investment, in an equal joint venture with KazMunayGas, would tap the undeveloped western part of the massive Kashagan, according to the publication.

Somali piracy surged Jan-Sep, but global incidents at 34-year low: IMB
Global maritime incidents drop to lowest since 1992
Somalia accounts for 94% of hostage-takings
Singapore Straits sees 82% decline in attacks
Global piracy and armed robbery incidents against shipping fell to their lowest level in more than three decades in the first nine months of 2026, even as Somali piracy surged and violence against crew increased, the International Maritime Bureau said Oct. 15.
The IMB, part of the International Chamber of Commerce, recorded 51 incidents in January-September, down from 116

in the same period of 2025 and the lowest nine-month figure since 1992.
Of the incidents, 36 ships were boarded, eight hijacked, four fired upon and three reported attempted boardings.
Somali pirates were responsible for eight ship hijackings and 94% of the 90 seafarers taken hostage globally during the period, according to the IMB. Most of the hijacked ships have since been released with their crews reported safe.
"While incident levels are at their lowest in more than three decades, the recent uptick in Somali piracy activity is a reminder that the threat remains," IMB Director Michael Howlett said.
The EU naval force operating in the region also highlighted the resurgence.
"In recent months, the resurgence of piracy activity off the coast of Somalia has once again highlighted the persistent nature of this threat," Vice Admiral Ignacio Villanueva Serrano, EUNAVFOR ATALANTA operation commander, said in a statement Oct. 15. "At the same time, the ongoing conflict in Yemen and the wider instability affecting the Red Sea are adding further complexity to the regional situation."
The Gulf of Aden is a key transit route for crude oil, refined products and containerized trade moving between Asia, Europe and the Mediterranean via the Suez Canal. The resurgence in Somali piracy has added another security risk for shipowners already facing threats linked to the conflict in Yemen and instability in the Red Sea.
Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, assessed the MR tanker rate for transporting 35,000 metric tons of clean petroleum products from the Red Sea to Japan at $247.52/mt on Oct. 15, the highest since the assessment began in October 2021 and up from $63.71/ mt on Dec. 31.
Threats to seafarers The IMB said 105 crew were directly affected in January-
September, compared with 72 in the same period of 2025, while weapons were identified in 53% of reported incidents.
The latest figures follow the release in late September of three tankers seized by Somali pirates between April and August. Somali authorities freed the 3,089-dwt Honour 25, 32,200dwt Sibu 1 and 3,313-dwt Eureka, although five seafarers were killed and several others seriously injured during the operation to free the Honour 25, according to the International Maritime Organization.
Elsewhere, incidents declined sharply in the Singapore Straits, a key chokepoint for oil and other commodity flows between the Middle East and Asia.
The IMB recorded 13 incidents in the Singapore Straits in January-September, down from 73 in the same period of 2025. Two crew were taken hostage, two threatened and one injured. Perpetrators were armed with guns or knives in more than 69% of incidents and successfully boarded all targeted vessels.
The Gulf of Guinea recorded just two incidents during the nine-month period and none in the third quarter, while Indonesia recorded six incidents, down from nine a year earlier.

TotalEnergies expects 'exceptional' downstream results for Q3
Refining margins surge to record $33.2/b
Hydrocarbons output rises to 2.5 mil boe/d
Middle East conflict cuts production by 5%
French energy major TotalEnergies SE said Oct. 15 it expects its downstream results for the third quarter to be "exceptional" due to higher refining output and historically high refining margins in Europe and globally.
TotalEnergies expects to report a 10% increase in refining throughput in Q3, which has allowed it "to fully capture" exceptionally high refining margins in Europe and worldwide, the company said in its report on main indicators. The company produced an average of 1.4 million barrels/day across its refining network in Q2.
The company said that in Q3 the European comparative refining margin marker was $33.2/b, more than double the $13.5/b in Q2. TotalEnergies reported a $2 billion increase in refining and chemicals cash flow compared to Q2.
Talking about its hydrocarbons production, TotalEnergies said it expects output at 2.45 million-2.5 million b/d of oil equivalent, up from 2.4 million boe/d in Q2 and roughly in line with 2.5 million boe/d produced in Q3 2025.
The increase in production is attributed to a reduced impact from the Middle East conflict, TotalEnergies said. It clarified that the conflict is expected to affect about 5% of the company's total production.
The company said in July that the Middle East war, which has affected the company's assets in Qatar, Iraq and the UAE, accounted for an 8% annual production loss.
Other output excluding Middle Eastern assets is expected to grow by more than 4% year over year, exceeding the annual guidance of 3%, the company added.
"Exploration & Production results are expected to reflect production growth and the sensitivity to lower oil prices and higher gas prices compared to the previous quarter," TotalEnergies said.
TotalEnergies also said that its oil trading activities continued to outperform in Q3, at a level consistent with the previous two quarters.
CEO Patrick Pouyanne said Oct. 5 that amid recent market disruptions the company has benefited from its structure, which incorporates upstream, midstream, and downstream activities.
"The lesson is that we are entering a new world of disruptions," Pouyanne said. "Funnily, I prefer the world of disruption than the peaceful world. It creates for us more opportunities, but you need to have the tools. You need to have the assets."

TotalEnergies' average liquids price was $87.1/b in the third quarter, down from $91.6/b in Q2.
TotalEnergies is due to release its full quarterly earnings on Oct. 29.

South Africa aims to make its new strategic oil stocks policy into law by the end of 2026 after proposing a set of measures that would increase its minimum level of reserves to cover 60 days of net imports, Tshepo Mokoka, CEO of the country's stateowned Central Energy Fund, said on the sidelines of Cape Town's annual African Energy Week conference Oct. 14.
After publishing a proposal text of the new oil stocks law in July, the government is awaiting cabinet approval to greenlight the law, following a public consultation process, Mokoka said.
"By the end of this year, the cabinet should make a decision," Mokoka said.
Marking the first major change since the 1970s when the country's stockpiling policy was conceived, the proposed law would raise the government mandate for emergency oil reserves from 40 days of net imports to 60 days. "I think the Middle East crisis has taught us a lot," Godfrey Moagi, CEO of SANPC, said in a speech at the event Oct. 14. "We're going to move to 60 days instead of 40 days, and that is going to be very important to make sure that we secure the energy for this country."

South Africa consumes about 27 billion liters of petroleum products every year, and risks a 24 million South African rand ($1.4 million) shock to its economy in the event of a 20% supply shortfall over a two-week period, according to government estimates.
With state-owned reserves concentrated in Saldanha Bay on the country's West Coast, it takes a minimum of 21 days for imports to reach South African ports, and at least another 10 for products to be offloaded and transported inland, according to the estimates.

Mexico sets rules to resolve legacy hydrocarbon permits two years after new constitution
CNE sets maximum permit terms up to 30 years
Fuel marketing permits limited to two years
New rules resolve pending regulatory disputes
Mexico's National Energy Commission has established criteria to determine the duration of unresolved hydrocarbon permits under the previous regulatory framework, setting maximum terms that range from two years for fuel marketing to as much as 30 years for major transportation and storage infrastructure.
The rules, published in the Official Gazette on Oct. 14, apply to permit applications submitted under the former Hydrocarbons Law that remained pending when the new Hydrocarbons Sector Law took effect in early 2025. These rules do not apply to new permit applications.
The measure provides the National Energy Commission, or CNE, with a framework to determine permit durations based on investment requirements, infrastructure complexity, expected returns and the risks associated with each activity.
Although the regulation is expected to have a limited impact on Mexico's broader hydrocarbons industry, it will provide greater certainty to a small number of applicants whose projects became entangled in regulatory disputes during the previous administration, according to Bernardo Cortés, a partner at law firm Cortés Quesada Abogados who specializes in Mexico's energy sector.
"Some companies challenged unusually short permit durations through injunctions, known as amparos, but their cases remained unresolved," Cortés said.
Mexico overhauled its energy legislation in early 2025 and replaced the previous regulator, the Energy Regulatory Commission, or CRE, with the CNE.

"The new criteria provide a framework for resolving those outstanding cases, offering greater legal certainty to companies whose projects have remained in regulatory limbo," Cortés said.
Permit durations Under the criteria, permits to transport hydrocarbons and
petroleum products through pipelines, as well as certain storage activities, may be granted for up to 30 years.
Non-pipeline transportation permits for gasoline, diesel and other petroleum products may have a maximum duration of 15 years, while natural gas transportation by other means may receive authorizations lasting up to 20 years.
Retail fuel sales permits may extend for up to 20 years. However, permits for the commercialization of hydrocarbons, petroleum products and LPG would be limited to two years, reflecting the regulator's distinction between capitalintensive infrastructure and commercial activities that require comparatively limited fixed investment. The government is putting particular emphasis on retail operations as it tries to combat the sale of illicit fuel, Cortés said. "Reducing the duration of permits would allow them to maintain closer supervision of those involved in the fuel business," Cortés said. The durations represent maximum terms rather than automatic entitlements. The CNE may grant shorter authorizations depending on its assessment of individual projects, but it must justify its decisions.
Legal disputes prompt new criteria The measures follow legal challenges involving permits issued
by the CRE, particularly authorizations to market fuels granted for periods of two years or less.
Federal courts subsequently required the regulator to establish clearer criteria for determining permit durations, addressing disputes over the basis for the terms assigned to individual companies.
The new framework establishes six evaluation factors, including investment requirements and capital recovery periods, infrastructure complexity, regulatory compliance, geographical and market conditions, and risks associated with the permitted activity.
The CNE must explain the characteristics of each project, the factors considered and the reasons supporting the duration granted or a decision to deny an application. The provisions come as Mexico continues implementing its 2025 energy reforms.
Limited impact on investment Although the measure provides greater certainty, Cortés said
the maximum permit durations may not necessarily correspond to the economic life of individual projects.
For example, non-pipeline transportation projects may receive authorizations lasting no longer than 15 years under the criteria, even when their expected operating life extends beyond that period, Cortés said.

The US Federal Energy Regulatory Commission Oct. 15 proposed two major changes to its natural gas project reviews, in what Chairman Laura Swett characterized as "monumental steps" to ease restrictions hindering gas from getting to market.
The agency proposed a rulemaking to create a streamlined, blanket certificate program for LNG projects, with three tiers of review depending on the type and scope of the proposed project (RM26-2).
FERC also approved a draft policy on gas project reviews that would cut out its practice of requiring air dispersion modeling to support its National Environmental Policy Act reviews. Swett called the models "convoluted" and often duplicative, saying they have proven to be an "unnecessary source of confusion, delay, and litigation risk."
Commissioner Judy Chang dissented on the draft policy (PL26-3), describing air dispersion modeling as "the best tool we have for understanding the likely local and regional impacts of the projects," outside of having physical monitors. Without it, she said, FERC will lack key information about local impacts before authorizing projects.
The proposed changes come as FERC commissioners have largely aligned on the goal of reducing unnecessary procedural hurdles in energy infrastructure permitting.
"While legislative changes are essential to permitting reform, FERC has to do whatever it can now under the confines of

existing law to avert the precipice of electricity generation we face if we don't build more," Swett said at FERC's open meeting. "We cannot do that without more gas."
The streamlined, three-tiered LNG program would authorize activities at LNG plants without case-specific authorization orders under Natural Gas Act Sections 3 and 7.
The first tier would allow automatic authorization, subject to landowner and community notification, for projects that remain within plant design limits and do not increase capacity.
The second tier of review would entail a 60-day notice and protest period for activities that expand capacity but stay within existing footprints.

Asked what types of projects FERC was trying to exclude from the program with the 25-acre delineation, Swett said that "each of my colleagues [has] different thoughts on that issue, which is precisely why we are noticing it for comment."
The program would include export facilities as well as peak shaving facilities.
For LNG uprate projects, Swett asked staff to quantify how much the program would help the US meet growing LNG demand.
The time saving under the 60-day prior notice procedures would have allowed as much as an additional 1.45 trillion cubic feet of natural gas or about $39 billion worth of natural gas to be available for export sooner, FERC staff said.

A total of 19 cities geographically representative of Europe had 26.1% fewer heating degree days in September than in September 2025. The total for the SeptemberSeptember period was 26.1% fewer degree days than the similar period last year and 35.1% fewer than normal.

Eight cities geographically representative of Canada had 48% more degree days in September than in September 2025. The total for the September-September period was 48% more than the similar period last year and 4.7% fewer than normal.

Four cities geographically representative of Japan had 45.9% more degree days in September than in September 2025. The total for the September-September period was 45.9% more than the similar period last year and 27.5% fewer than normal.
