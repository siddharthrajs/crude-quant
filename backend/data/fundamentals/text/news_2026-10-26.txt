In a letter to Stuart Payne, chief executive of the upstream regulator the North Sea Transition Authority, Michael Shanks, the minister of state for energy, reiterated plans to introduce Transitional Energy Certificates allowing companies to go after known hydrocarbon deposits close to existing fields, while maintaining its ban on new exploration.

China clean product exports likely to fall in Nov: refiners, analysts
Gasoil exports fall 40% on supply shortage Refinery margins turn negative on freight costs Domestic stocks hit lowest since Jan 2023: JLC
Chinese refineries are likely to slash their clean oil product exports in November, led by gasoil, due to tight domestic supplies, while weak refining margins weigh on production, six refiners and two market analysts told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, on Oct. 26.
One of the refiners said the total volume of oil companies allowed to export would fall by 30%, with gasoil volumes down about 40%. However, he did not provide details on the basis for the reductions.
China was estimated to export about 3.7 million metric tons of oil products, comprising about 40 million mt of gasoline, about 1.1 million mt of gasoil and 2.2 million mt of jet fuel, in October, Platts reported earlier. The country's gasoil exports reached a 43-month high of 1.85 million mt in September, pushing total clean oil product exports up 3.7% month over month to 4.41 million mt, according to customs data.
"Exports in November are set to fall as the domestic market is so tight," said a source with a state-run refinery in southern China, adding that Chinese refineries are required to prioritize meeting domestic supplies rather than capturing export profits.
China's gasoil stock fell about 4% month over month to 10.78 million mt on Oct. 23, the lowest since Jan. 6, 2023, according to data from local information provider JLC.
Meanwhile, five of the six refiners who spoke to Platts said they would cut their utilization in November, capping production, as they expected to make losses due to high freight and insurance costs.
"For us, feedstock is sufficient, but the refining margins in the domestic market will worsen next month as soaring freight costs are largely not reflected in China's gasoline and gasoil pricing mechanism," said a source with another state-run refinery. He

added that the refinery plans to cut utilization to about 80% in November from about 93% in October.
Under China's current oil product pricing mechanism, introduced in 2013, the government adjusts retail ceiling prices for gasoline and gasoil every 10 working days based on a basket of benchmark crude prices, reportedly including ICE Brent, NYMEX WTI and DME Oman, weighted according to China's crude import profile.
Market sources said freight and insurance were set at a combined $5/b in the formula, much lower than the current level.
Dirty freight for a 270,000-mt VLCC carrying crude from the Gulf to China has hovered around $230/mt since Sept. 16, according to Platts. The assessment fell to $234.93/mt on Oct. 23 from a record $255.72/mt on Oct. 12, Platts data showed. A year ago, the assessment was at $18.85/mt on Oct. 23, 2025, according to Platts data.
"Margins are deeply negative. Luckily, we will be undergoing maintenance next month," a second source with a state-owned refinery in East China said.

China PDH operating rates edge up on restarts; tight LPG supply caps recovery
PDH margins turn positive at 201 yuan/mt
1.35 mil mt/year capacity due back from maintenance
Tight LPG imports remain key constraint on run rates
China's propane dehydrogenation plant operating rates edged higher in the second half of October as units restarted from maintenance, but tight imported LPG availability continued to constrain a broader recovery in run rates, market sources said.
PDH plant utilization rose to 66.33% as of Oct. 26, from 65.49% a week earlier, according to data from local energy information provider JLC. The rate, however, remained below the September average of 71.39%, suggesting October utilization was likely to remain below the previous month's level.
"The utilization rate improved with a few plants restarting units," a JLC analyst said.
Operating rates could recover further, with plants totaling 1.35 million mt/year of PDH capacity scheduled to restart following maintenance, potentially providing additional support to China's demand for imported propane.
The returning capacity includes Zhenhua Petrochemical's 750,000 mt/year PDH unit and Jvzhengyuan's 600,000 mt/ year facility, according to the analyst. Guoheng Petrochemical's 660,000 mt/year PDH unit restarted in the week to Oct. 25.

Galp, Moeve call off Iberian downstream merger plan
Negotiations fail on strategic terms
Three refineries remain separate entities
Portugal cited sovereignty over Sines plant
A proposed merger of downstream assets belonging to Portugal's Galp Energia SGPS SA and Spain-based Moeve SA has resulted in no agreement, the two companies said in separate statements Oct. 26.
"Further to the announcement of January 8, 2026, regarding a non-binding agreement between Galp and Moeve's shareholders, Mubadala Investment Company and The Carlyle Group, and

following extensive negotiations between the parties, no agreement has been reached," Galp said.
In a separate statement Oct. 26, Moeve said that it and Galp "did not reach final terms that met their respective strategic and financial criteria to support an enduring and value-accretive partnership."
"Following extensive negotiations and a strong commitment from all parties to explore the potential of this combination, and despite all the efforts, we have been unable to reach an agreement on an opportunity of significant potential to create a major European downstream player," Galp Chairperson Paula Amorim said, adding that the parties had been unable to achieve the necessary commercial alignment.
"We remain fully focused on executing our transformation with financial discipline and delivering sustainable long-term value," Moeve CEO Maarten Wetselaar said.
According to Wetselaar, the potential downstream "combination with Galp" represented "an opportunity to accelerate value creation, but it was never a condition for its delivery."
The merger aimed to combine the downstream businesses of the two companies, creating a unified competitor to marketshare leader Repsol in the Iberian peninsula.
Galp operates the 226,000 barrels/day Sines refinery in Portugal, while Moeve operates two refineries in southwest Spain, at Huelva and Algeciras, with capacities of 220,000 b/d and 240,000 b/d, respectively.
The plan entailed creating an "IndustrialCo" to focus on refining, petrochemicals and supply activities across the three industrial sites, with Moeve becoming the majority partner and Galp taking around a 20% stake. Under the proposed plans, a second business, "RetailCo", was to be co-controlled by Galp and Moeve, holding balanced stakes, combining the oil products sales to direct clients of around 6.5 million mt in 2025 and a combined 3,500 service points between them.
In July, Portuguese Environment and Energy Minister Maria Graca de Carvalho said in a Portuguese parliamentary that the government wanted to ensure "sovereignty" over the country's only refinery at Sines, in the face of the proposed merger.
The government would expect strategic control of the refinery to remain in Portugal, enabling it to provide or guarantee supply in crisis situations, Graca de Carvalho said.

Equinor, Aker BP collaboration eyes higher Norway oil and gas output
Companies to collaborate on tight reservoirs
NOD sees 2.8 billion boe recoverable potential in such reservoirs
Equinor aims to sustain NCS output into 2035
Norway's Equinor and Aker BP are beginning a strategic collaboration around tight reservoirs aimed at boosting oil

and natural gas development and recovery on the Norwegian Continental Shelf, both said in statements on Oct. 26.
The companies will collaborate on technology development, standardization and knowledge sharing to support work on prospects, discoveries and projects in tight reservoirs across the NCS where oil and gas flow particularly slowly though rock, they said.
The Norwegian Offshore Directorate has estimated some 2.8 billion barrels of oil equivalent in recoverable resource potential from such mapped reservoirs, which include many of the roughly 90 discoveries that have yet to get a development decision, both said.
"The Norwegian Offshore Directorate has therefore encouraged companies to work together," Aker BP said in its statement.
"Scale is the key," Aker BP CEO Karl Johnny Hersvik said. "By considering opportunities across both portfolios, we can create the basis for increased activity, faster learning and lower costs. Together, we can develop solutions more quickly and deploy them at a larger scale. We are starting this work immediately."
Equinor's Executive Vice President for Exploration & Production in Norway, Kjetil Hove, said: "By viewing our portfolios in context and developing common solutions, we can reduce costs and develop and qualify technology faster."
NCS outlook State-owned Equinor aims to sustain production of about
1.3 million barrels of oil equivalent/day on the NCS into 2035. It has estimated that about 70% of this output will come from new wells and developments.
Gas output on the NCS is expected to hold roughly steady over the coming years, thanks to new developments and exploration, before falling toward the end of the decade, the Norwegian Offshore Directorate said in its latest annual report on the NCS in January.
Gas output across the NCS is projected to fall to 107.1 Bcm by 2030, from some 119.8 Bcm in 2025, according to the NOD.
Norway is a key gas supplier for Europe. Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, assessed the month-ahead Dutch TTF gas price at 92.78/megawatt-hour on Oct. 23. The index is at its highest point since December 2022.

US crude oil stocks expected lower amid strong refinery demand
Inventories expected to hit four-week low
Refinery runs likely edge up to 93.6% of capacity
Exports decline as arbitrage economics weaken
US crude oil inventory draws likely extended during the week to Oct. 23, analysts surveyed by Platts, a part of S&P Global Energy, said Oct. 26, against a backdrop of stronger refinery demand.
Commercial crude stocks likely declined 1.2 million barrels to around 411.3 million barrels during the period, analysts said. This fourth straight weekly draw would put stocks at the lowest level since late July and leave them 3.6% behind the seasonal five-year average of US Energy Information Administration data.
Nationwide refinery utilization likely climbed 0.2 percentage point to 93.6% of capacity, analysts said, a five-week high and 6.8% above average for this time of year.
Refinery margins have broadly declined during October but remain robust, S&P Global CERA data showed. The US Gulf Coast WTI MEH cracking margin has averaged $48.72/barrel to-date this month, down from $50.13/b during September.

Distillate cracks remained very strong. The USGC ULSD crack versus WTI MEH has averaged $100.13/b in October compared with $101.03/b in September, CERA data showed.
US distillate stocks likely climbed 600,000 barrels to around 104.9 million barrels during the week to Oct. 23, analysts said, while gasoline stocks were seen up 500,000 barrels over the period at around 205.8 million barrels.
Weekly US crude exports slowed to 3.19 million b/d, according to data from S&P Global Commodities at Sea, falling to the lowest level since early August.
Export arbitrage economics remained supportive along key routes, but have weakened in recent weeks. The incentive for moving WTI MEH into Rotterdam versus North Sea Forties has averaged $14.36/b to date in October, CERA data showed, but averaged just $6.83/b during the week to Oct. 23.
The incentive for exporting Mars crude into China versus Dubai fell to minus $1.57/b during the reporting period, compared with an October-to-date average of $4.76/b, CERA data showed.

US lawmakers warn banks, ports against facilitating Russian diesel trade
Lawmakers challenge Russian diesel legality
Tanker carrying Russian fuel heads to NY
Refinery damage constrains export capacity
US lawmakers urged US banks and port authorities not to facilitate Russian diesel transactions and warned of potential criminal and civil liability, challenging the legal basis for the Trump administration's effort to reopen the trade to ease fuel prices.
In an Oct. 24 letter sent to executives of Goldman Sachs, JP MorganChase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, Morgan Stanley and port authorities in Houston, New York, New Jersey, Beaumont, Texas and Corpus Christi, Texas, US senators and one US representative demanded that the companies preserve records related to proposed or actual Russian diesel transactions and disclose whether they had participated in, or knew of, arrangements to import the fuel into the US.
"Any importation of Russian diesel into the US raises significant legal questions and may be subject to legal action, as well as congressional investigations," US Senators Jeanne Shaheen, Democrat-New Hampshire, Elizabeth Warren, Democrat-Massachusetts, Ron Wyden, Democrat-Oregon, Chris Coons, Democrat-Delaware, Sheldon Whitehouse, DemocratRhode Island, Thom Tillis, Republican-North Carolina and representative and House Foreign Affairs Committee ranking member Gregory Meeks, Democrat-New York, wrote.
The letters followed the Treasury Department's Oct. 9 issuance of General License 135, which allows the sale, delivery and importation of Russian diesel, including into the US, through April 7, 2027. US President Donald Trump announced the exemption alongside commitments to sell additional Russian supplies into American and global markets.

The lawmakers argued that the license does not resolve statutory restrictions on Russian energy imports. Their letter cited the Ending Importation of Russian Oil Act of 2022, which they said generally prohibits such imports unless the president consults Congress and certifies that Russia has ceased military hostilities in Ukraine, poses no immediate military threat to NATO members and recognizes Ukrainians' right to choose their government.

The warning extended beyond importers of record. The preservation demand covers contracts, shipping and customs documents, payment records, terminal reservations and communications with counterparties and US government officials beginning Sept. 1. The letter does not establish that Goldman Sachs has participated in a transaction.

The Malta-flagged medium-sized tanker Agioi Fanendes is heading to New York carrying Russian diesel, according to S&P Global Energy Commodities at Sea data, with the Kremlin expecting deliveries to continue "on a regular basis," Kremlin spokesperson Dimitry Peskov said Oct. 23.

Brazil's oil, gas sector to maintain growth after Lula's victory
Fourth non-consecutive term
Oil output record level in August
Opening up domestic gas market
Brazil's oil and natural gas industry will stay on pace for higher production and increased exploration activity to rebuild reserves after President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva was reelected for a fourth term, but Latin America's largest economy also will seek to leverage renewable energy sources to attract investments in data centers and AI.
Brazil's energy policies weren't much of a focus during the election, except for some increased chatter surrounding the potential privatization of state-led oil company Petrobras, government and industry officials told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy. Bolsonaro had dismissed talk that his administration would sell off stakes in state-controlled entities as "fake news."
Lula, meanwhile, sought investors to build data centers powered by renewable energy sources and provide the government with 10% capacity to develop local technology and AI initiatives, officials noted. Lula also wants to develop and refine Brazil's deposits of rare earth minerals needed for the

global energy transition. Lula and his Workers' Party, or PT, have favored a state-led
development model that included national champions such as Petrobras as key investors in strategic areas such as refining and fertilizers. Lula also has suggested that Petrobras needs to return to the retail distribution market after its non-compete clause against the former distribution unit Vibra ends in mid-2029.
Despite that interventionist approach, the PT's management of the oil and gas sector has generated relatively little controversy and delivered favorable results, helping sustain investment and production growth, said Marco Oviedo, senior economist and strategist at XP Inc, in Sao Paulo.
"It's one of the sectors that has shown strong momentum in recent years," Oviedo said. "The potential remains, and it will continue to be a sector that generates growth."
Petrobras' controlling stake in Vibra was sold in two stages, completed in July 2019 and July 2021 as part of privatization efforts conducted under the Bolsonaro administration. Lula ended Petrobras' asset sales program when he was inaugurated for a third non-consecutive term in January 2023.
Lula will also likely maintain existing subsidies on diesel sales and imports, officials said. The subsidies have been funded by cuts to fuel taxes and revenues, including a 12% tax on crude oil exports that is also likely to stay in the near term.

The US is taking on a growing role in funding African oil and gas projects as it has shifted its support for green energy and rejected forecasts of fossil fuel demand reaching a plateau, according to government officials, analysts and private investors.
A lack of Western funding from climate-conscious lenders is often cited by Africa's oil and gas advocates as producing an unfair disadvantage for the continent, where many countries lack the financing to develop their natural resources.
However, the policy shift under US President Donald Trump's second term appears to be directing new attention to Africa, both from state entities and the US oil majors betting on sustained demand growth for the world's hydrocarbons.
In a sign of its new focus on partnerships to develop African energy and mineral resources, the US sent its largest-ever delegation to Cape Town's African Energy Week from Oct. 12-16, Joshua Volz, a special envoy for the US Department of Energy, said at the event.
Now in its fifth year, the conference gathers a variety of African energy investors, ranging from international energy

companies to delegations from Russia, China and the Middle East -- all regions that have assumed new prominence amid a Western withdrawal.
Spending rebound According to analysis from S&P Global Energy CERA, capital
expenditure on African exploration and production peaked at $57 billion in 2013 before retreating to $20 billion in 2021, largely due to IOCs, including Chevron and ExxonMobil, slashing spending.
However, from its floor in 2025, investment has rebounded strongly in 2026. In the year to date, Africa has already attracted 70% more funding, marking the largest annual increase in more than two decades.
Addressing delegates in Cape Town, Volz emphasized a renewed appetite from the White House "double down on commercial opportunities" in Africa, which, at $8 billion, is already responsible for the largest share of spending from the US ExportImport Bank.
Increasingly, the US Development Finance Corporation, a federal government agency focused on advancing its foreign policy and economic development, is also shifting its focus from debt to equity positions in Africa, where it has $14 billion invested, Volz said.
Among the assets under consideration by the DFC are those owned by Russian energy giant Lukoil, according to reporting from the Financial Times, which wrote that the agency is part of a consortium with US businessman Todd Boehly and several Gulf investors bidding for its international portfolio.
Lukoil, which was sanctioned by the US in 2025, has a significant African oil and gas footprint spanning Egypt, Cameroon, Nigeria, Ghana and the Republic of Congo. A DFC official declined to confirm its involvement because of the project's commercial sensitivity.
Industry response The US pivot has been interpreted by some as a green light for
an African spending spree. "The transition might be dead, and if I have a chance, I want
to kill it in Africa," said NJ Ayuk, executive chairman of the African Energy Chamber, in his opening address to the group's conference in Cape Town.
Borrowing from a favored Trump slogan, the African lobby group also launched a "Drill Baby Drill" wine range to coincide with its event, underscoring potential synergies between the White House and the continent's oil producers.
The shift in government stance has had ripple effects beyond the public sector. JJ Enoch, a senior associate at geopolitical risk advisory firm JS Held, said "a meaningful shift" in White House support could improve IOC confidence in the long-term policy environment and draw private funding into state-backed projects.
Chevron, which currently produces roughly 300,000 b/d from sub-Saharan Africa, has increased its acreage position by around 70% in recent years, said Brent Gros, the company's head of offshore exploration, at the African Energy Week event.
