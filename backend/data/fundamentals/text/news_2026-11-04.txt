Middle East oil exports near wartime high in October as 230 tankers ship Gulf cargoes
Total oil exports reach 72% of pre-war level
Maritime attacks surge to 35 incidents in Oct
Tankers sail dark through Strait of Hormuz

What could come next for the US oil market following the Democratic Party sweep?
Does Trump make a deal or escalate with Iran?
Congress likely to investigate Venezuela deals
Oversight into US leasing plans expected

Middle Eastern oil exports remained near a wartime high in October despite the heaviest wave of maritime attacks during the ongoing US-Iran conflict, as some 230 ships engaged in Gulf trades helped keep oil flowing to global markets, according to industry estimates.
Latest S&P Global Commodities at Sea data shows 15.2 million barrels per day of crude shipments from the Middle East last month, or 80% of the average level between December 2025 and February 2026 before the war broke out in end-February. Total exports of petroleum products reached 3.6 million b/d, or 51% of the pre-war level.
When crude and products were combined, October's total oil exports reached 18.8 million b/d, just off the wartime record of 18.9 million b/d in September and amounting to 72% of the prewar level, according to CAS.
The figures comprise direct shipments from Persian Gulf ports via Hormuz to non-Middle Eastern locations, loadings from other tankers in the Gulf of Oman, Fujairah and Saudi Red Sea ports. They could be revised later as more data becomes available.
JP Morgan separately estimates Middle Eastern oil flows have recovered to 20.7 million b/d currently, and that global oil markets were already in a surplus of 1.1 million b/d in September and 1.4 million b/d last month when rising supply elsewhere and weaker demand were taken into account.

CHINA DATA: Independent refiners' Iranian crude imports reach record low in October; Iraqi barrels up
Iranian crude imports slump 80% YOY Iraqi crude leads replacement barrels
China's independent refineries imported 1.16 million metric tons, or 275,000 barrels/day, of Iranian crude in October, the lowest monthly volume since Platts began collecting the data in January 2022, as supplies dried up.
October Iranian crude imports to China dropped 80% year over year and 49% from September, according to data collected by Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, and released Nov. 4.
Only five Iranian crude cargoes were discharged during October, down from 13 in September, the data showed.

Iraqi barrels fill supply gap Small Chinese independent refiners, which heavily relied on
Iranian oil, have stepped up purchases of alternative barrels from Iraq and the UAE, Platts data showed.
The refiners imported a combined 3.12 million mt of crudes from Iraq and the UAE in October, nearly four times the 790,000 mt imported in September, according to Platts data.
Iraqi crude accounted for the bulk of the increase, with imports rising to about 2.31 million mt in October from 520,000 mt in September.
Taking into account the frequent buyers, including the private mega refineries, the independent sector imported 5.33 million mt of crude from the UAE and Iraq in October, rising from 3 million mt in September, Platts data showed.
"I think those Basrah crudes can almost offset the loss of Iranian crudes," said a trader in Shandong.
Basrah Medium crude for December delivery was offered at premiums of about $13-$14/b to ICE Brent crude futures on a DES Shandong basis Nov. 4, broadly stable from a week earlier, according to two trader sources.

South Korea's exports on track to top $1 trillion as oil product values surge

Iranian crude flows to China's independent refining sector have declined sharply since reaching an eight-month high of 1.81 million b/d in April, as the US blockade of Iranian shipping increasingly constrained supplies.
"There have been no offers for the grade for months as there are no cargoes available anymore," a Shandong-based trader said.
Iranian crude and condensate on the water in Southeast Asia stood at 2 million barrels and 2.2 million barrels in the Far East Oct. 19, compared with a combined 56.9 million barrels Feb. 28 when the war in the Middle East started, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea data.
China's independent refiners have relied heavily on discounted Iranian crude as a source of cheap feedstock since 2022. Imports reached a record 1.91 million b/d, or 8.07 million mt, in March 2025 and averaged about 1.5 million b/d over 2025, Platts data showed.
Flows have fallen sharply since geopolitical tensions in the Middle East escalated at the end of February.
Over January-October, China imported 48.06 million mt of Iranian crude, down 23% from 62.09 million mt in the same period a year earlier, according to Platts data.

South Korea's cumulative exports exceeded $900 billion for the first time in January-October, putting the country on course to surpass $1 trillion in exports for 2026, as refined oil product sales contribute to the milestone amid robust refining margins, according to the trade ministry and industry sources over Nov. 2-4.
The country would become only the fourth nation in the world to achieve annual exports of $1 trillion, with the threshold expected to be crossed in November, the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Resources said in a statement Nov. 2.
South Korea's exports rose 93.7% year on year to $115.28 billion in October, the second-highest monthly total on record after September's $121.05 billion. Exports for January-October reached $930 billion, leaving $70 billion needed in November to reach the annual milestone, according to MOTIR.
Semiconductors remained the leading export item and the main driver of growth. Semiconductor exports surged 259.4% year over year to $56.44 billion, supported by strong global demand for memory chips used in artificial-intelligence applications and elevated prices as major technology companies expanded datacenter and other AI-related capital spending, MOTIR said.
Refined oil product exports also made a significant contribution. Export value rose 80.0% year over year to $6.92 billion, even though shipment volume declined 7.5%, according to MOTIR.

Singapore's bunker sales seen at nine-month high in October: CAS
Bunker fuel sales up 11.9% MOM at 5.23 million mt Bulk carriers lead with 3,134 bunkering events HSFO premium hits record $83.26/mt average in October
Singapore's bunker fuel sales are estimated to have risen 11.9% month over month to 5.23 million metric tons in October, the highest level since January, as bunkering activity and aggregate receiving tonnage increased sharply, according to the latest S&P Global Commodities at Sea nowcast.
The surge in demand came as the world's largest bunker market faced tight availability of prompt fuel. Delivered high sulfur fuel oil premiums reached a record monthly high in October, while the front-month low sulfur fuel oil crack recently climbed to its strongest level in more than four years.
October sales were also estimated to be up 9.9% year over year, CAS said in its Nov. 2 report.
The October estimate reflected a 12.5% month-over-month increase in aggregate bunker-receiver tonnage and an 8% rise in bunkering events. CAS identified 10,167 bunkering events in October, up from 9,411 in September and marking the highest monthly total since January 2024.
Bulk carriers accounted for the largest share, with 3,134 bunkering events, up from 2,883 in September. Chemical/product tankers followed with 1,579 events, up from 1,470 a month earlier, while crude tankers recorded 1,047, up from 857. Container ships accounted for 981 events, and product tankers for 953.
CAS estimated October HSFO sales at 2.17 million mt, LSFO sales at 2.63 million mt and marine gasoil sales at 437,000 mt, representing market shares of 41.4%, 50.2% and 8.4%, respectively. The balance between high- and low-sulfur fuels

broadly reflected scrubber installation rates across the calling fleet, CAS said.
September's actualized fill rates, which represent bunkered volumes relative to vessel tonnage, remained broadly stable, with an increase in the low-sulfur segment offset by a slight moderation in the high-sulfur segment.
Supply crunch shadows demand rebound The stronger bunker demand came as buyers faced tight
prompt availability, with Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, reporting a significant supply crunch in the first half of October, when most LSFO and HSFO suppliers could offer deliveries only after mid-October.
The tightness was particularly pronounced in HSFO. The delivered 380 CST HSFO bunker premium over the FOB Singapore benchmark averaged $83.26/mt in October, the highest monthly average on record.
The previous monthly high was $63.86/mt in March, based on Platts assessments. Meanwhile, the average Singapore-delivered LSFO bunker premium rose 23% to $76.01/mt in October from $61.80/mt in September.
Fuel oil cracks strengthen Firmness was also evident further upstream in the fuel oil
complex, with cargo supply tightening amid reduced Middle Eastern inflows as high freight rates curbed cargo movements.
The Singapore front-month 0.5%S marine fuel crack against ICE Brent reached $30.50/barrel on Oct. 30, up 17% week over week and marking its strongest level since July 2022. LSFO cash differentials also strengthened toward the end of the month amid concerns about near-term supply and uneconomic arbitrage flows from Western markets.
HSFO refining margins also strengthened sharply in October amid persistent supply tightness. The Singapore 380 CST HSFO crack rose about 196% during the month through Oct. 26 to $9.77/b, although market sources said elevated cracks were dampening refinery feedstock demand for the residual grade.
Singapore's commercial stocks of heavy distillates stood at 18.68 million barrels in the week ended Oct. 28, down 0.9% week over week and 24.6% year over year, according to Enterprise Singapore data.

India seeks higher foreign direct investment in oil and gas with policy reforms
Upstream, petrochemicals seen as key growth areas
Refining capacity to continue expanding
Energy transition opens cooperation opportunities
India is seeking deeper collaboration with international energy companies and aims to attract greater foreign direct investment across its oil and gas sector as it advances policy reforms to make the industry more investor-friendly, Petroleum Secretary Neeraj Mittal said Nov. 3.
Speaking at the Abu Dhabi International Petroleum Exhibition and Conference (ADIPEC), Mittal said India is seeking partnerships across the upstream, midstream and downstream segments and aims to establish itself as a global manufacturing base for the oil and gas industry.
"We are here to collaborate and forge linkages with multinational companies, invite them to India and make India the destination for global manufacturing for the oil and gas sector. We hope that we will be able to attract a significant amount of foreign direct investment into the space," Mittal, who is attending the conference, said in a video message.
Indian delegates attending ADIPEC said New Delhi is courting foreign investors amid policy reforms aimed at expanding domestic exploration, strengthening energy infrastructure and encouraging greater private and foreign investment in the sector.
Mittal said Samudra Manthan, the program focused on deep-water and ultra-deepwater exploration, was designed to expand exploration in technically challenging offshore areas while reducing the financial risks associated with high-cost drilling.
"This is a very unique program because India is offering almost 50% government support to de-risk deep-water drilling costs," Mittal said.
The federal Cabinet approved investments of up to 840 billion Indian rupees ($8.8 billion) over five years in late July to advance offshore oil and gas exploration under the National Offshore Exploration Scheme. The initiative is expected to help add more than 600 million metric tons of oil equivalent to India's reserves by fiscal year 2030-31 (April-March), the Petroleum Ministry said July 31.
The government will fund up to 50% of eligible deep-water exploration drilling costs, capped at $71 million per well. By partially covering these costs, the scheme could significantly improve the economics of frontier exploration.
Downstream growth There are also investment opportunities in refining,
petrochemicals and downstream manufacturing, as India's demand for petroleum products is expected to grow for at least the next two decades, Vikas Kaushal, chairman and managing director of state-owned Hindustan Petroleum Corp. Ltd., said at the conference.
"In India, we do have a fantastic refining base," Kaushal said,

adding that the country already exports significant volumes of refined products and has developed strong operational capabilities through sustained investment in new refineries.
India's refining capacity, currently about 267 million metric tons/year, is expected to approach 290 million mt/y within the next year and reach 320 million mt/y by 2032, Petroleum Minister Hardeep Singh Puri said Sept. 25. The expansion will involve both new refineries and upgrades to existing facilities.
According to S&P Global Energy CERA, India's refining capacity will reach 5.85 million barrels/day at the end of 2027, up 600,000 b/d from 2025. Capacity expansions at Indian Oil Corp. Ltd.'s facilities in Panipat, Koyali and Barauni are expected to be the main contributors, adding 346,000 b/d to the country's capacity.
Kaushal said India's refining capacity and position as a major fuel market could provide a platform for further investment in downstream products, including petrochemicals.
He added that India's petrochemical consumption remained relatively low compared with global levels, while the country imports about 9 million-10 million mt/y of petrochemicals.
Kaushal said HPCL intends to build on both its oil-related activities and downstream businesses while expanding its petrochemical investments. "That's something where I would want to invest in the next five to 10 years," he said.
Alongside efforts to increase oil and gas production, India is also presenting its energy transition as an area for international cooperation, delegates said at ADIPEC.
Mittal and other Indian energy industry leaders attending the conference said India's energy program is part of its broader effort to develop alternative fuels while addressing waste management and energy security. It also creates opportunities for foreign companies in technology, project development, equipment manufacturing and infrastructure.

UK introduces legislation to lower energy bills, end new North Sea oil licenses
Energy Independence Bill laid before Parliament
Focuses on consumer relief, ending oil licensing
Changes will 'bring bills down': Burnham
The UK government introduced sweeping energy market legislation to Parliament on Nov. 4, laying the groundwork for lowering consumer energy bills and ending new North Sea oil and gas exploration licenses.
The Energy Independence Bill combines near-term consumer relief with structural changes to grid infrastructure, renewable power and offshore oil and gas licensing, according to a news release.
Building on measures announced in the UK's Oct. 28 budget, the government said it will bring forward amendments to the bill to support plans to scrap the daily standing charge on household gas bills, a fixed cost levied regardless of consumption.
Around 23 million households would be affected, with approximately 60% of gas account holders expected to save an average of £45 a year, the government said.
Affordability is a central focus for UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham, who said the legislation is designed to address structural vulnerabilities in Britain's energy system.
"For too long, families have paid the price for an energy system that isn't working for them. Bills are too high and events around the world can send costs soaring," the prime minister said in a statement. "Together, these changes will bring bills down for good, give families greater security and put Britain back in control of its energy future."
Among other measures, the bill gives UK energy regulator Ofgem greater powers to protect consumers from "rogue" energy companies and mis-selling, which are expected to save businesses around £100 million per year, the government said.
The government added that it would bring forward a further amendment to enshrine in law Great British Grid, the UK's new publicly owned investment body for electricity networks. Separately, the bill extends an existing policy -- moving 75% of domestic renewables obligation costs from energy bills onto general taxation -- through March 2029.

Switzerland allows for second time release of jet fuel stocks
Up to 50,000 cubic meters draws allowed until Nov. 29
Refinery restrictions and rail issues cause strain
Rhine water levels disrupt oil products transport
Switzerland has authorized for a second time the release of jet fuel stocks due to temporary shortages, the Swiss Federal Office for National Economic Supply, or FONES, said Nov. 3.
Previously, Switzerland allowed the release of jet fuel stocks

between Oct. 15 and Nov. 1 to maintain supplies at Zurich Airport. In its latest decision, the country allows up to 50,000 cubic
meters to be drawn until Nov. 29. Including the already authorized draws of up to 30,000 cubic meters, the total volume represents 17.4% of the obligatory jet fuel stocks, FONES said.
The release has been attributed to restrictions in refinery operations in southern France and in Germany, further compounded by rail freight disruptions.
Market sources have reported weather-related disruptions to refinery operations in southern France over the recent weeks.
"Despite low demand in November 2026, the airports of Geneva and Zurich are therefore relying on withdrawals from mandatory reserves," FONES said.
FONES also reiterated that Switzerland's supply of oil products had been negatively impacted for months due to lower Rhine water levels, which had put additional strain on the use of alternative transportation.
Currently, Switzerland also allows the use of diesel stocks until Nov. 22.

US distillate stocks fall to 23-year low as refiners push to boost output
Distillate stocks fall 3.57 mil barrels; lowest since 2003
Refiners push runs, distillate output in Q3
Commercial crude stocks fall 870,000 barrels
Unseasonably strong demand pushed US distillate stocks to 23-year lows during the week ended Oct. 30, according to US Energy Information Administration data released Nov. 4.

Panama Canal planning ahead for more drought years: administrator
Canal plans to add water storage to combat drought
Light 2026 rainfall prompted weight, transit limits
New reservoir project targets 2032 operations
As drought years become more common for the Panama Canal, its governing body is trying to plan ahead rather than react and add water storage, the Panama Canal Authority's top administrator said Nov. 4.
"It's our main resource," Ilya Espino de Marotta said at the

Houston International Maritime Conference. "We have been very resilient in a volatile world."
She said drought impacts severe enough to limit transits and cargo weights on ships have happened before, but the number of years between droughts has shrunk. In late 2022 and 2023, the PCA reacted as issues arose, but in 2026 the PCA took proactive steps to conserve water amid forecasts of the worst El Nino since 1950.
May-August 2026 rainfall was 34% below average, and watershed inflows were 44% below average. Seeking to conserve water ahead of the January-April 2027 dry season, the PCA in June began announcing a series of draft restrictions that required ships to lighten loads before transiting the waterway. The normal draft of 50 feet was slated to be reduced step by step to 47.5 feet by September and daily transits fell from 36 in August to 32 in September.
The draft limits required container ships carrying imports to the US from Asia to reduce weight before entering the canal, and helped fuel a rise in rates on the North Asia-US East Coast run to a four-year high of $11,800/FEU by mid-September, which held at that level for six weeks before rates began a slow retreat. Rates last reached that level in 2022 when demand spiked as economies emerged from COVID-era lockdowns.

IETA backs EU carbon overhaul but urges faster, clearer action
Urges EU to define carbon credit rules within two years or fall short
IETA puts EU ETS predictability at heart of post-2030 push
Biochar inclusion and early removal review urged
The International Emissions Trading Association has backed the European Commission's overhaul of the EU Emissions Trading System beyond 2030, but warned that the international credits framework must be built well before it is needed or risk arriving too late to matter, the group said in a position paper published Nov. 3.
IETA said the EU ETS review provides a strong foundation for the post-2030 period but needs sharper timelines, clearer procurement commitments and broader eligibility for carbon removals to deliver on its promise.
"Preserving confidence in the EU ETS throughout the legislative process will be critical," IETA said. "The reform should maintain the efficiency of a market-based instrument and provide visibility for investment."
The Commission's July 17 legislative package slowed post2030 emissions cuts, added 6 billion in free allowances, created a 100 billion Industrial Decarbonisation Bank and opened controlled access to carbon removals and international credits in response to industry competitiveness concerns.
International credits The most urgent call is on international credits. IETA said
developing projects at the scale and integrity the framework will require takes years, meaning governance, procurement and eligibility rules must be defined within 12 to 24 months of the review's adoption, well ahead of the 2031-2035 pilot phase.
The Commission has proposed that from 2036, companies will be able to use international credits to meet up to 2% of their

compliance obligations, creating additional flexibility as Europe pursues its binding 2040 emissions target.
IETA called on the EU to clarify pilot modalities including volumes, procedures, timelines and procurement models well before 2031, and recommended decoupling the post-2036 Linear Reduction Factor from the 2033 assessment of international credits, arguing the cap trajectory should not depend on the outcome of a single review.
"The EU should provide market participants with more visibility on the ETS trajectory, contributing actively to building a reliable framework for international credits that supports EU climate goals, international cooperation and EU competitiveness," IETA said. "A predictable commitment to procure the international credits towards the 2040 climate target will strengthen the demand signal for stakeholders, giving greater confidence to scale international credit supply in time."
Rules-based ETS On market functioning, IETA said a predictable, rules-based
EU ETS remains its core priority, with compliance and noncompliance entities both contributing to liquidity and price formation. The group reiterated strong support for EU-UK ETS linking, calling for alignment opportunities to be advanced through the current review to reduce future adjustment costs.
On carbon removals, IETA said current eligibility is too narrow and called for biochar to be included from the outset under already-approved Carbon Removal Certification Framework methodologies.
It urged the EU to bring the scheduled 2034 review of broader removal eligibility forward to before 2030, warning that waiting risks missing a critical window given the pace of sector innovation.
The Commission has proposed integrating up to 250 million metric tons of high-quality permanent domestic carbon removals into the ETS, certified under the Carbon Removal Certification Framework and subject to monitoring and verification rules.
On aviation, IETA said the proposed interaction between the EU ETS and the Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation requires further assessment to avoid double payment and unintended distortions to price formation and hedging incentives in the CORSIA market.
On free allocation, IETA pushed back against the proposed 80/20 conditionality mechanism, arguing it increases uncertainty over allowance supply and adds administrative complexity.
Baseline free allocation should remain unconditional for sectors exposed to carbon leakage risk, the group said, with any conditionality recognizing early movers rather than penalizing operators decarbonizing at varying speeds.
IETA also called for the Industrial Decarbonization Bank to become operational as soon as possible and for the minimum share of EU ETS revenues earmarked for decarbonizing ETScovered sectors to be raised beyond the proposed 50%. ETS2, covering buildings and road transport, should remain outside the current ETS1 review to preserve its 2028 operational timeline, the group said.

October milder in Europe and Japan, but colder in Canada

October was milder in Europe and Japan, but colder in Canada than a year ago, according to data obtained from CustomWeather and gathered by Platts.

A total of 19 cities geographically representative of Europe had 10.9% fewer heating degree days in October than in October 2025. The total for the September-October period was 15% fewer degree days than the similar period last year and 19.9% fewer than normal.

Eight cities geographically representative of Canada had 9.7% more degree days in October than in October 2025. The total for the September-October period was 17.9% more than the similar period last year and 4.9% fewer than normal.

Four cities geographically representative of Japan had 26.8% fewer degree days in October than in October 2025. The total for the September-October period was 21.7% fewer than the similar period last year and 22.4% fewer than normal.
