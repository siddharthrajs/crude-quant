With shifting projections around peak oil and wars in Ukraine and Iran spurring renewed interest in African oil and gas exploration, countries across the continent have been working hard to lure IOCs to their basins in the past year.
Those efforts were on show again at Africa Energy Week - a giant annual oil and gas conference in Cape Town over Oct. 12-16 - with countries from Senegal and Algeria to Namibia and Uganda pitching investors on their upstream opportunities.
Against this backdrop a new deal type has emerged on the continent: low-commitment Memorandums of Understanding to examine geological data across offshore licenses. And the world's largest oil companies have been using these arrangements to hoover up acreage up and down the continent.
But will these deals lead to actual investment, drilling, production and revenues, or are they just a low-stakes way for IOCs to fill their portfolios with promising acreage?
'We're hot, you're not' The past year has seen a slew of reconnaissance agreements
and MOUs between African countries and oil majors, leading some industry watchers to declare an "exploration renaissance".
For years, IOCs were lukewarm on African exploration amid a backlash from environmental groups against projects like TotalEnergies' EACOP pipeline in East Africa and dominant energy transition narratives.

US Lower 48 States shale oil and gas producers were eyeing another round of mergers and acquisitions in the current quarter, with elevated commodity prices and 2026 crude oil output forecast to reach 11.33 million barrels/day, industry participants said Oct. 22.
"There hasn't been a major surge in [M&A] activity that was expected [with WTI prices over $100/barrel]," Gavin Spencer, manager at LX Oil and Ranch Ltd., told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy.
"Government interventions through the summer, and policy actions are still moving prices around the globe and continue to create a lot of uncertainty," Spencer said. "But, there's a shift happening on the margin: buyers are starting to re-rate assets toward higher price levels, and that's slowly closing the gap between what buyers will pay and what sellers will accept."
The first quarter of 2026 saw "relatively big" upstream deals valued at roughly $38 billion, according to Spencer, who added that the figure fell to about $9 billion amid the war in Iran.
"That slowdown was not a lack of appetite, rather a pricing standoff, and deals are coming back now," he said, citing recent mergers-and-acquisitions deals such as Magnolia-WildFire, Matador-Paloma, and Crescent-Devon.
Global upstream M&A activity declined by about 25%, falling to 14 deals in September from 19 in August, reflecting reduced activity across both North American and international markets

India's Reliance says open to partnering with US companies in oil, new energy
Rubio visits Jamnagar refinery to view scale, operations
Reliance discusses mutual commercial interests with Rubio
Mukesh Ambani outlines Jamnagar complex's long-term vision
India's Reliance Industries Ltd. is open to collaborating with US companies across oil and gas and new energy, as the country's largest private refiner aims to provide affordable energy amid a changing landscape and growing demand, the company said in a statement Oct. 21.
The comments came after Reliance Chairman and Managing Director Mukesh Ambani and Executive Director Anant Ambani held discussions with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio during his visit to the Jamnagar complex, which houses Reliance's refinery and new energy gigafactories.
Rubio was given a tour of the refinery complex and offered a firsthand view of its scale, advanced technology and integrated operations, according to the statement.
"The visit included wide-ranging discussions on the global energy landscape and opportunities for commercial partnerships across conventional and new energy. The exchange covered reliable and affordable energy supplies, technological advances, resilient supply chains and the investment required to meet growing demand," Reliance said in the statement.
Jamnagar refinery's Nelson Complexity Index of 21.1 -- the highest in the world -- gives the asset the flexibility to process almost all crude oil grades and meet the increasingly differentiated and demanding product specifications of global markets, Debangsu Ray, cluster president and head of the Jamnagar Refinery and Petrochemical Supersite, told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, on Sept. 8.
The refining complex, with crude processing capacity of 1.4 million barrels/day, has so far processed more than 240 different crude oil grades, Ray said.
"The interaction provided a constructive opportunity to exchange industry perspectives and explore areas of mutual commercial interest," Reliance said.

US crude oil flows to India averaged about 149,000 b/d in 2024, marked by a strong midyear surge followed by a secondhalf pullback. Flows were generally higher but more uneven in 2025, averaging about 238,000 b/d, according S&P Global Commodities at Sea data.
However, US crude oil flows to India weakened in 2026, averaging 128,000 b/d over January-October -- roughly 43% below the 2025 average for the same period, CAS data showed.
Footprint in Venezuela Ahead of his visit to Jamnagar, Rubio said discussions with
Reliance would focus on understanding the company's current position and assessing potential opportunities for crude oil production in Venezuela.
Mansi Anand, principal analyst for research on national oil companies at S&P Global Energy CERA, said Venezuela's challenge has never been its resource potential, as the country possesses one of the world's largest heavy-oil resource bases. The difficulty has been converting those reserves into reliable production, cash flow and shareholder returns.
"Reliance, however, could approach Venezuela from a very different position. Jamnagar is one of the world's premier heavycrude refining systems and Reliance is already a significant Venezuelan crude buyer," Anand said.
"However, moving from crude purchases to new upstream investment is a much bigger step. Any material upstream commitment would likely require durable sanctions clarity, secure export channels, contractual stability and an investment structure that limits country-risk exposure," she added.
Long-term vision Mukesh Ambani and Anant Ambani outlined Reliance's long-
term vision for the Jamnagar complex, including its development into a centre for deep tech, innovation and advanced manufacturing, according to the statement.
"Discussions also explored the increasing intersection of energy and digital infrastructure, including the role of dependable power in supporting data centres and the expansion of the digital economy," the statement said.
Ray said the future of the Jamnagar complex would involve transforming it from a traditional fuel refinery into an integrated energy and materials complex, as Reliance seeks to balance capital allocation between maintaining its core refining strength and scaling up its new energy gigafactories.
Primary capital allocation will follow two megatrends: deep petrochemicals expansion and world-scale green energy manufacturing, Ray said. By placing solar, bioenergy and advanced energy storage ecosystems in close proximity to its refining footprint, Reliance aims to create a symbiotic loop where clean energy powers low-carbon refining, which in turn yields the high-tech materials needed for the transition.
-- Sambit Mohanty, Ratnajyoti Dutta

Zhoushan port bonded bunker sales rise 75.8% MOM to 778,800 mt in Sep
LSFO refinery output rises 8.4% YOY in Sep
Typhoon Champi threatens port operations
Bonded bunker fuel sales at China's largest bunkering hub, Zhoushan, rose 75.8% month over month and 9.2% year over year to 778,800 metric tons in September, as the market recovered from typhoon disruptions and slower demand in August, according to data from traders.
Total sales were the highest since May, when they were recorded at 734,200 mt, the data showed.
Bunkering operations at the port were disrupted in August due to severe weather and typhoons, leading to an order backlog and a shift in demand to other ports.
Data from local energy information provider JLC showed that low sulfur fuel oil output at Chinese refineries reached 1.136 million mt in September, up 1.1% from a month earlier and 8.4% higher year over year.
LSFO storage available for lease in Zhoushan rose 11.1% month over month and 14.4% year over year to 1.26 million cubic meters in September, according to Zhejiang Mercantile Exchange data.
Weather reports showed that Typhoon Champi, located southeast of Taiwan, was expected to reach its peak intensity around Oct. 25, raising concerns about disruptions to bunkering operations at the port.
Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, assessed the Zhoushandelivered 380 CST fuel oil at $843/mt on Oct. 21. The premium over benchmark FOB Singapore 380 CST fuel oil cargoes rose $4.43/mt day over day to $77.82/mt.
Platts assessed the Zhoushan-delivered LSFO at $925/mt on Oct. 21, down $20/mt day over day. The premium over the benchmark FOB Singapore 0.5%S marine fuel fell $5.32/mt to $70.50/mt.

CFR India bitumen prices rise on higher freight costs, tighter import supply
Freight costs rise to $350/mt from Middle East
Vessel shortages threaten construction supply
Imports drop 12.5% year over year
Indian bitumen importers are facing tighter Middle Eastern supplies and elevated freight costs as geopolitical tensions constrain vessel availability, just as the country's post-monsoon construction season begins, according to market participants.
Importers said securing vessels for shipments from the Middle East has become increasingly challenging amid disruptions along regional shipping routes, limiting their ability to import fresh cargoes despite expectations of improved seasonal demand.
"Finding a vessel is very difficult now. Even if material is available, getting a ship to move it is a challenge," said a trader based in Mumbai.

Freight rates for bitumen shipments from the Middle East to India's west coast have risen sharply in recent weeks, with rates reported at $350/metric ton, up from prewar levels of $60-$70/ mt, according to market sources. Elevated transportation costs, coupled with firmer offer levels from Middle Eastern suppliers, have increased the cost of securing imported material.
Shipping constraints have also raised concerns about the timely availability of imports, with traders and importers struggling to arrange new shipments from traditional Middle Eastern supply sources.
Meanwhile, India's post-monsoon construction season is underway, with roadbuilding and infrastructure activity expected to support a seasonal recovery in bitumen consumption.
"Demand has picked up as construction activity resumes, but the worry now is whether there will be enough material coming in," said an importer based in Kolkata. "With freight so high and vessels difficult to secure, we could face supply shortages in the coming weeks."
Market participants said a prolonged shortage of available vessels could further tighten domestic supply and bolster import prices.
India's bitumen imports fell 12.5% year over year to 168,000 mt in August from 192,000 mt a year earlier, according to data from the Petroleum Planning and Analysis Cell.
Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, assessed CFR West Coast India VG30 and VG40 bitumen at $660/mt and $665/mt, respectively, on Oct. 21, both up $65/mt week over week. CFR East Coast India VG30 and VG40 were assessed at $700/mt and $705/mt, respectively, both up $65/mt from the previous week.

Middle East oil shipments reach 19 million b/d
IMO records 29 maritime attacks in October
War risk premiums rise to 12.5% of hull value
Middle Eastern oil exports rebounded to more than 19 million barrels per day by mid-October, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea, despite continued Iranian attacks on merchant ships in and around the Strait of Hormuz.
Latest CAS data shows 15.6 million b/d of crude shipments from the Middle East on Oct. 12-18, or 82% of the average level between December 2025 and February 2026 before the outbreak of the US-Iran war. Total exports of petroleum products reached 3.5 million b/d, or 51% of the pre-war level.
On a seven-day moving average basis, Middle Eastern oil exports fell from nearly 21 million b/d on Oct. 1 to 15.2 million b/d Oct. 12 before the latest recovery to 19.2 million b/d on Oct. 18, equivalent to 74% of the pre-war level, according to CAS.

Valero sees high tanker costs boosting USGC crude advantage
Freight surge widens crude discounts globally
VLCC rates to Asia jump sixfold from 2025
Heavy sour barrels flood discounted markets
Surging tanker freight rates have dramatically reshaped global crude trade flows, handing Valero's US Gulf Coast refining system a significant competitive advantage, as the cost of moving oil from the USGC to Asia has increased to as much as $35/barrel, company executives said in its third-quarter 2026 earnings call.
"The U.S. Gulf Coast continues to be one of the most advantaged regions globally, supported by abundant domestic crude production and access to cost competitive feedstocks," CEO Lane Riggs said on the Oct. 22 call.
The freight spike -- driven by ship congestion around the Strait of Hormuz, insurance risk premiums and transshipment delays -- is forcing crude sellers to widen discounts to offset shipping costs, making USGC barrels among the cheapest available globally and amplifying margins for refiners with access to nearby supply.
Valero's USGC advantage Randy Hawkins, who oversees crude supply at Valero, said on
the call that the USGC was "one of the most advantaged crude markets globally," and that the freight environment "only further supports that," signaling Valero expects the dislocation to persist long enough to materially benefit its refining margins in the near term.
Hawkins said Very Large Crude Carrier rates from the USGC to Asia have surged to $30-$35/barrel, compared with a 2025 average of roughly $5/b, according to Platts freight assessments from S&P Global Energy.
The cost of moving West Texas Intermediate crude from the USGC to Singapore on a VLCC has averaged $252.47/metric ton, or $34.44/b, so far in the fourth quarter, as of Oct. 22, compared to $95.47/mt, or $13.02/b, in Q3, according to Platts assessments.
The sharp rise is being compounded by ship-to-ship transfer operations in the Gulf of Oman, which are tying up global tanker capacity and adding transshipment delays and higher bunker costs to the overall freight burden.
The knock-on effect on crude pricing has been severe. Canadian crude discounts have widened to as much as $27-$30/ barrel under ICE Brent futures, as the prohibitive cost of routing Canadian barrels to Asia-based buyers has forced sellers to redirect supply toward closer markets at steep concessions.
Heavy sour grades from Latin America -- including Venezuelan and Mexican crude -- are also carrying a freight advantage into the USGC versus Asia, further expanding the pool of discounted feedstock available to Valero's USGC system.

International demand drives deepwater spending growth into 2027: Oceaneering
Rigs relocate from Gulf, boosting global ops
ROV market share reaches 59% of floating rigs
Brazil, Guyana lead 2027 expansion strategy
Rig transits out of the US Gulf Coast to regions in Europe, West Africa and the Middle East meant a slowdown in US offshore activity during the third quarter, Oceaneering International CEO Roderick Larson said during an earnings webinar Oct. 22, as international demand offset lower North American deepwater spending.
The company faced additional mobilization costs as rigs relocated to other regions from the USGC, Larson said. Specifically, the Gulf saw a slowdown in Oceaneering's Subsea Robotics segment and a "less favorable project mix" in the Offshore Project Group.
As of Sept. 30, Oceaneering had remotely operated vehicle contracts on 80 of the 136 floating rigs under contract, or 59% of market share, Larson said. The company maintains a fleet of 250 ROV systems, he added.
In Subsea Robotics, greater activity in Europe, Guyana and West Africa largely offset lower activity in the USGC, while ROV pricing improved, with average revenue per day utilized rising to $11,781 from $11,254, according to the company. Increased survey activity and a one-time contractual cost recovery related to US Gulf operations also contributed to higher margins.
"Our backlog as of Sept. 30, 2026, was $435 million and was largely sustained by order intake during the quarter of $160 million," executives said, adding that the book-to-bill ratio was 0.77 for the trailing 12-month period. "Based on these recent bookings, together with our sales funnel, we remain confident in our ability to achieve a full-year, book-to-bill ratio of 0.9 to 1.0."
