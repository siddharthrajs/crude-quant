Russia targets year-round Arctic shipments with launch of new icebreaker: Rosatom
Northern Sea Route has historically run in summer only
Could reshape trade flows with Asian markets from 2027
Deliveries from Vostok Oil started in September
Russia's state nuclear corporation Rosatom expects the Northern Sea Route to begin year-round operations from early 2027, a milestone that could significantly reshape commodity trade flows between Russia's Arctic energy projects and Asian markets, a senior company official told Interfax.
The transition to continuous navigation could begin with the nuclear-powered icebreaker Chukotka expected to enter service in early 2027, bringing Russia's nuclear icebreaker fleet to nine vessels, Vladimir Panov, director for Northern Sea Route and Arctic development at Rosatom's Arctic Directorate, said, according to an Interfax report Oct. 27.
"We are now in the final stage of preparation for regular eastbound voyages on the Northern Sea Route," Panov said.
The fleet expansion comes on the heels of Russia's largest oil producer Rosneft beginning shipments from its flagship Vostok Oil project in the Arctic in September.
Rosneft officially launched the project on Sept. 6, when it loaded the first cargo of Vostok oil at the Sever Bay port on the Kara Sea. The first vessel from the project to reach China landed at the port of Yantai on Oct. 19, having traveled by the Northern Sea Route, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea.
In early November, two more tankers carrying oil from the project are expected to reach China, while another one is destined for India. Loadings at Sever Bay averaged 89,000 b/d in September and 71,000 b/d in October, according to CAS.

Indian refiners favor delivered crude in procurement shift as shipping costs mount
HPCL looks to maintain term-spot buying flexibility
BPCL adjusting crude slate to delivered economics
Indian state-run refiners are increasingly seeking crude on a delivered, or CIF, basis rather than on FOB terms, as geopolitical tensions drive sharp swings in freight and insurance costs, petroleum ministry officials and refiners said Oct. 26.
Under CIF arrangements, sellers cover the cost and risk of transporting crude to the buyer's destination. This allows Indian refiners to secure a more predictable landed cost and avoid exposure to sudden increases in freight rates, which have risen sharply amid disruptions and attacks involving vessels in the Gulf region, they added.
"We are buying crude on a delivered basis these days and not on FOB as we used to buy earlier," a senior Indian refinery official told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, on Oct. 26.
Since freight costs for crude shipments have risen sharply in recent weeks compared with previous months, refiners are increasingly concerned that an apparently attractive purchase could become uneconomical by the time it reaches an Indian refinery, the official added.
Hindustan Petroleum Corp. Ltd. Chairman and Managing Director Vikas Kaushal told an investor call Oct. 23 that the staterun refiner was looking at buying crude on a landed basis, except when it could send a vessel to pick up cargoes at a relatively lower cost.
"It is now a volatile situation, on how the market is reacting," Kaushal said.

Asian refiners view US crude premium as cost of stable supply, logistics security

East Asia continues to rely heavily on US crude as a leading non-Middle Eastern supply source amid efforts to diversify procurement, with double-digit premiums for WTI Midland and West Texas Light delivered into Asia potentially reflecting the value of enhanced supply reliability and logistical security, according to industry sources and government ministries during Oct. 22-27.

Indonesia shifts crude, fuel, LPG procurement to government-led deals
Jakarta takes control of energy imports New system bypasses Pertamina tenders Lemigas to manage overseas purchases
Indonesia is shifting overseas procurement of crude oil, refined fuels and LPG away from Pertamina-led business-tobusiness tenders toward deals negotiated through governmentto-government and government-to-business channels, giving Jakarta a more direct role in securing the country's imported energy supplies.
The change is set out in Presidential Regulation No. 107/2026, signed by President Prabowo Subianto, and is intended to strengthen supply certainty amid geopolitical disruption,

Coordinating Minister for Downstreaming and Energy Transition and Energy and Mineral Resources Minister Bahlil Lahadalia said Oct. 26.
"Until now, Pertamina has used a B2B process, and the tenders have taken a long time. Under this regulation, we are moving to G2G and G2B. This means we can now choose which countries to source supplies from," Lahadalia said at a press conference.
The move marks a significant change in Indonesia's procurement system, shifting a key part of the purchasing process from state-owned Pertamina to the government at a time of heightened concern over the security and reliability of imported energy supplies.
Under the new mechanism, the government will negotiate procurement arrangements with supplying countries, while a public service agency, or Badan Layanan Umum (BLU), will handle purchases and sell the crude, fuel, or LPG to Pertamina, Lahadalia said.
"There will be no other companies involved in the procurement chain. It will be country-to-country," he said.
Lahadalia said the existing B2B system had exposed Pertamina to supply uncertainty even after it secured tenders at competitive prices, with suppliers sometimes receiving higher offers from other buyers after tenders had been awarded.
The government plans to use Lemigas, the Energy and Mineral Resources Ministry's oil and gas testing center, for the new import mechanism. Deputy Energy and Mineral Resources Minister Yuliot Tanjung said in May that the government planned to optimize existing institutions rather than establish a new BLU, including using Lemigas to import crude oil, refined fuels and LPG.
Supply security The overhaul comes amid uncertainty over energy supplies
linked to geopolitical tensions in the Middle East. The ministry's oil and gas director general said Oct. 5 that
fuel import tenders had attracted almost no participants amid the conflict, while securing alternative supplies could entail higher costs.
The new regulation retains a route for Pertamina to pursue direct imports when national reserves reach critically low levels or geopolitical disruptions affect a supplying country. Such purchases would have to be coordinated with the BLU, Lahadalia said.
Jakarta also plans to develop crude oil storage facilities in Indonesia, allowing imported barrels to be delivered directly into the country rather than transiting through third countries, according to Lahadalia.
"Our crude oil imports will also be supported by storage facilities that we plan to build here. The BLU will develop the facilities with several companies that have already partnered with it. This will allow imported crude to be shipped directly to Indonesia, without stopping in country A, country B or country C," he said.

ASEAN plans to boost gas infrastructure on rising demand: Singapore minister
GasCo launches first open season, LNG procurement exercise
Governments, gas industry to work together for energy security
Bloc can potentially produce biomethane with existing infrastructure
The Association of Southeast Asian Nations is expected to add about 24 gigawatts of gas-fired generation capacity from 2025 to 2030, with the bloc set to build an additional 34 million metric tons/year of LNG regasification capacity and Singapore continuing to boost its gas infrastructure amid rising demand, Minister of State for Energy, Trade and Industry Gan Siow Huang said at an industry event Oct. 27.
"The Middle East conflict has reminded us how vulnerable and volatile global energy supply chains can be. This is not the first time that we are seeing disruptions to energy supplies, but there are indications that there could be more to come," she said at the Asia Gas Markets Conference by S&P Global Energy during the Singapore International Energy Week 2026.
"Natural gas powers 95% of Singapore's electricity. So certainly, we are very deeply interested in what is happening in the gas markets," she said.
Singapore GasCo Pte. Ltd. has launched its first open season and LNG procurement exercise to secure term supply as the city-state strengthens its energy infrastructure amid uncertain geopolitics, she said.
Global electricity demand is expected to rise by more than

a quarter over the next decade, with Asia accounting for a significant share of that growth, driven by electrification, rising incomes, cooling demand, data centers and AI, she said.
She highlighted the need for governments and the gas industry to work together for energy security.
Governments must assess energy security needs and safeguard supply, while LNG producers, traders, shippers and terminal operators ensure reliable deliveries, she said.
In Singapore, five new gas-fired power plants will come online by 2031, while the second LNG terminal will raise its LNG regasification capacity to 15 million metric tons/year, she said. Singapore is also studying the need and feasibility of a third LNG terminal at Pulau Tekong, she added.
"Realizing these investments requires both public-sector planning and private-sector participation," she said.
"Governments can plan ahead for future infrastructure needs but we need the industry to bring its technology and expertise to develop and operate these facilities."
Natural gas will be an important transition fuel as lowercarbon energy alternatives are developed, she said, highlighting ASEAN's potential to produce biomethane using existing gas infrastructure and supply chains.
The Southeast Asian economic union's agricultural and palm oil industries generate substantial organic waste that could be converted into biomethane, with several projects already unveiled in Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand, she said.
Singapore appointed Keppel Energy, Sembcorp Utilities and Tuas Power as aggregators under its 300 MW Biomethane Sandbox to test the commercial viability of importing and using biomethane for power generation and industrial decarbonization, she added.
On Oct. 27, Gan also addressed the Asia Clean Energy Summit (ACES) 2026 at SIEW.
According to Gan, electricity imports remain a key thrust in Singapore's efforts to decarbonize the power sector and diversify its energy sources.
Singapore's Energy Market Authority has granted Sembcorp Utilities conditional approval to import 1.3 GW of electricity from or through Peninsular Malaysia, she said. Working with consortium partners from Malaysia and Vietnam, Sembcorp plans to source low-carbon power from multiple sources, including Vietnamese offshore wind and Malaysian solar, she noted.
The approval brings the number of conditionally approved projects from Peninsular Malaysia to three.
"If all three projects materialize, up to 2.2 GW of low-carbon electricity could flow from Peninsular Malaysia to Singapore," she said.
"Beyond electricity imports and solar as near-term pathways, Singapore is also exploring promising low-carbon technologies for the future," Gan said.
Nuclear and geothermal energy options are being "seriously" studied while also testing other promising solutions such as biomethane, she added.

Eni, TotalEnergies complete Nigerian onshore oil divestments
Eni completes asset transfer to Apex Global
TotalEnergies concludes onshore deal with Vaaris
IOCs shift focus to Nigerian deepwater assets
Italy's Eni and France's TotalEnergies have completed the divestments of a string of Nigerian onshore oil assets, transferring them to indigenous producers, as international oil companies focus on deepwater projects in the African country.
In a statement released late Oct. 26, Eni said it had concluded the sale of its Nigerian subsidiary, Agip Energy and Natural Resources, or AENR, to Apex Global Energy, a company wholly owned by Nigerian firm Sterling Oil Exploration and Energy Production.
The transaction comes two years after Eni sold four Nigerian onshore producing oil blocks - OMLs 60, 61, 62 and 63 - as well as stakes in the Brass terminal, exploration concessions and power plants, to another Nigerian upstream operator, Oando.
The latest divestment sees Eni selling off AENR's interests in onshore and shallow-water oil and gas exploration and production assets in Nigeria, including a 5% stake in the Renaissance joint venture, where it was partnered with TotalEnergies and state-run Nigerian National Petroleum Co.
"The transaction, which has received approval from all relevant authorities, is in line with Eni's strategy to optimize its upstream activities by rebalancing the portfolio and divesting non-strategic assets," the Italian energy giant said.
"Eni will continue to maintain a presence in the country through exploration and development of deep offshore assets as well as through its participation in Nigeria LNG," it added.
Eni holds a 10.4% interest in Nigeria's flagship liquefied natural gas facility, Bonny LNG.
The company also partners with Shell in the prolific Bonga deep-water field, where the companies are currently embarking on a major field expansion through the Bonga North and Bonga Southwest projects. These developments have the potential to add over 300,000 barrels/day of oil production, according to estimates by the project partners.
Eni also holds stakes in Oil Prospecting Lease 245, which has been broken up after a long-running dispute was recently settled. The license could potentially yield some 200,000 b/d when onstream.
TotalEnergies deal Separately, on Oct. 27, TotalEnergies said it had completed the
divestment of its 10% non-operated interest in the Renaissance JV licenses to local company Vaaris for $800 million.
The deal, first announced in January, covers 15 licenses, which are mainly producing oil onshore, with net output of 16,000 b/d in 2025.

"TotalEnergies retains full economic interest in the three gas licenses of the Renaissance JV (OML 23, OML 28 and OML 77), which currently account for 50% of Nigeria LNG gas supply," it added.
The French company's previously planned $860 million deal with local company Chappal Energies for a 10% stake in the Renaissance JV collapsed in September 2025.
Many international oil companies, including Shell and ExxonMobil, have sold their onshore and shallow-water assets in Nigeria's Niger Delta region in the past couple of years to shift their focus to deep-water projects and gas developments. Nigerian President Bola Tinubu has announced a string of fiscal incentives and sweeteners to entice majors to invest in offshore projects.
For years, onshore assets were plagued by crude theft and sabotage of production facilities, which alongside underinvestment caused a material drop in Nigerian production.

INTERVIEW: Uganda's new energy minister eyes infrastructure push beyond first oil
First oil due in 2026, exports to start in 2027
Third bid round expected to launch imminently
Dangote joins Kenya Pipeline Company as co-owner
Uganda is on the brink of becoming the world's youngest oil producer by the end of this year, with the launch of the contentious East Africa Crude Oil pipeline set to link the country's oilfields to the world market after almost a decade in the making.
However, the long-awaited pipeline suffered new setbacks in the wake of the US-Iran war, while a massive new Kenyan refinery project has overhauled expected trade routes for the country's oil, its energy minister told Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, in an exclusive interview.
Speaking on the sidelines of African Energy Week in Cape Town earlier this month, new energy minister Monica Musenero said that Uganda should achieve first oil from its CNOOCoperated Kingfisher oilfields by the end of 2026, although the first exports will not leave Tanzania until early 2027.
The Ugandan oil project involves two developments, Kingfisher and Tilenga, which are being developed by CNOOC and TotalEnergies, and will rely on the EACOP to shuttle up to 230,000 barrels/day of medium-to-heavy sweet crude to the Tanzanian coast.
However, after years of delays linked to financing and environmental opposition, the pipeline project hit another speedbump when the US-Iran war trapped vital equipment in the Persian Gulf. "We had to airlift," Musenero said, adding that the developers were left with no alternative when parts were left stranded beyond the Strait of Hormuz.
The delay has meant a missed opportunity for Uganda to capitalize on crude prices that have traded above $100/b over the last two months, well above the $60/b it had used as its benchmark.
"I wish I were selling crude now," Musenero said, adding that a long-delayed third licensing round should also be coming soon

Arctic Gateway signs deal with Dubai to develop Canada's Port of Churchill
CentrePort Canada also a signatory
Group eyes integrated transportation corridor
Manitoba-based Arctic Gateway Group has signed an agreement with DP World and CentrePort Canada Inc. to develop growth opportunities at the Port of Churchill that include the

construction of infrastructure to export multiple commodities - crude oil, LNG, hydrogen, grain, zinc, potash, and uranium - to global markets from the Canadian Arctic Ocean.
The deal focuses on port operations, logistics services, and the potential to develop an integrated transportation corridor linking the Port of Churchill, the Hudson Bay Railway, CentrePort Canada, and DP World's global logistics network, AGG said late Oct. 26.
DP World is part of Dubai Ports World and specializes in port and terminal operations globally.
AGG owns and operates the Port of Churchill, the Hudson Bay Railway, and the Churchill marine tank farm and has a mandate to further develop facilities at the Port of Churchill with private sector investment, it said.
At present, the port is shipping a range of commodities, including critical minerals, grain, and potash, AGG said.
Manitoba's key inland and coastal ports are poised for even further growth, AGG said, including the potential to connect Europe and Asia intermodally through Canada, thereby establishing new trade options.
The Port of Churchill provides direct access to Europe, the Middle East, Africa, and South America via the Atlantic, without the need to build a pipeline across the Great Lakes and the St. Lawrence River, AGG said.
Year-round shipping In early September, AGG said studies indicated the feasibility
of maintaining year-round operations at the Port of Churchill and exporting additional commodities.
"Our next steps are focused on the practical work required to scale the corridor: consulting with rights holders, determining the appropriate modernization of the port, advancing the railway upgrade, continuing marine planning and validation, and working with governments, Indigenous partners, customers, and private investors on the investments required," AGG CEO Chris Avery told Platts then.
Platts is part of S&P Global Energy. "This season [July to October] is already demonstrating that commercial opportunity," Avery said. "Churchill is experiencing the most diversified shipping season in its history, including the return of grain exports for the first time since 2020." The port is also handling northern resupply, zinc concentrate, and its first-ever export shipment of Manitoba-produced potash, he said, noting VLCC loadings of crude oil and LNG exports, too, are in the cards.

Noble sees tighter global drillship market, accelerating recovery in 2027
Contracted utilization hits record 99% mark
Day rates climb to $500,000 from $400,000
Exploration expected to drive 2027 demand
The global ultra-deepwater drilling market is tighter than current rig activity suggests, with contracted utilization at a record 99% and rising demand set to lift day rates and accelerate earnings growth through 2027, Noble Corp. management said Oct. 27.
Of the 110 ultra-deepwater rigs marketed worldwide, 109 are contracted for current or future work, above the 2024 peak of 105, CEO Robert Eifler said during the company's Q3 earnings call.
Only 88 rigs are currently working, Eifler said, about 10 fewer than at the 2024 peak, leaving a backlog of commitments yet to turn into active drilling.
"We've reached a point now where the number of pending contract startups for currently idle rigs modestly exceeds the

number of contract rollovers over the next six months or so," Eifler said.
That could expand the active fleet in coming quarters, further tightening capacity and supporting day rates.
Recent high-end drillship contracts have set leading-edge day rates from the mid-$400,000s to $500,000/day, up from near $400,000/day earlier in the recovery, Eifler said, adding that rising utilization and a growing pipeline of future work were already influencing pricing, even before the active fleet nears full employment.
Contract awards have also reaccelerated in recent months after a slowdown between May and July, Noble said.
Exploration to drive next phase Higher oil prices have yet to lift offshore drilling demand,
Noble said. Producers remain disciplined and reluctant to make long-term
investment decisions on prices that could retreat as geopolitical conditions change, Eifler said.
Instead, the recovery has been driven mainly by previously delayed development projects now moving ahead.
Noble expects exploration, especially frontier drilling to replace reserves, to become a bigger demand driver as producers set 2027 budgets.

In an Oct. 27 statement, European Shipowners, also known as ECSA, said the proposed Sustainable Maritime Alternative Propulsion mechanism should be increased to offer at least 330 million EU Allowances from the European Commission's proposed 110 million over 2028-40.
In July, the EC proposed SMAP as part of a wider carbon market reform. EU lawmakers and member states aim to agree on the exact incoming rules next quarter.
"Shipping pays almost 20% of total Emissions Trading System revenues, although it accounts for only 10% of the emissions covered by the system, and, unlike other sectors, it has never received a single free allowance," ECSA Secretary General Sotiris Raptis said in a statement.
Shipping companies are responsible for about 10% of greenhouse gas emissions covered by the EU ETS while purchasing approximately 90 million EUAs per year, equivalent to almost 20% of all allowances auctioned under the system, according to ECSA.
"The European Parliament and the Council should at least triple the ETS revenues earmarked for SMAP to send a credible market signal for the scale-up of sustainable maritime fuels and technologies, including energy efficiency," Raptis said.
Subsidy scheme Under the current proposal, SMAP would recycle ETS
allowances to partially offset the cost differential between conventional and lower-carbon marine energy.
EUAs would cover 55% of the price differential for biogas and advanced biofuels, 80% for low-carbon hydrogen and low-carbon fuels, and 90% for renewable fuels of non-biological origin. Eligible zero-emission propulsion technologies would receive 90% support.
But if claims from shipping companies exceed the available allowance reserve, the support provided for each claim would be reduced proportionately, according to the EC proposal.
September's average bunker costs for very low sulfur fuel oil in intra-EU trades were $1,005.74/metric ton in Rotterdam, compared with $1,378.55/mtVLSFOe for B30 bioblends and $2,057.17/mtVLSFOe for bio-LNG, according to the Platts bunker cost calculator when the ETS compliance costs were taken into account. Green ammonia cargo for delivery into Northwest Europe was $1,570.80/mtVLSFOe.
Assuming an EUA price of 100/metric ton of CO2 equivalent, ECSA said the shipping industry needs a total of 330 million allowances in 2028-2040 to cover the price gap between conventional, oil-based fuels and sustainable energy.
The nearest-December EUA contract has averaged 79.78/ mtCO2e so far this year, according to Platts assessments. Platts is part of S&P Global Energy.

ECSA said its modeling showed that the Commission's 110 million-EUA reserve would cover only one-third of what it estimates is needed to create a market for sustainable maritime fuels and technologies, leaving a shortfall of about 220 million allowances.
Sustainable fuels could be on average four times more expensive than conventional fuels, making predictable support important for investment decisions, according to ECSA. Its model assumes annual shipping energy demand of 27.5 million mt of oil equivalent within the relevant EU ETS.
"Predictability is essential: companies need certainty, before they take investment decisions, that the price gap between sustainable and conventional fuels will be partly covered," Raptis said.
ECSA also called for SMAP eligibility to extend to all technologies that deliver carbon savings, including energyefficiency projects, and said companies should be able to seek support in advance rather than only after sustainable fuels or technologies are used.
Separately, the association backed proposed changes to ETS rules for offshore vessels but opposed having different compliance systems for 2027-30 and from 2031, arguing a single regime would reduce administrative burdens.

September milder in Europe, but colder in Canada and Japan

September was milder in Europe, but colder in Canada and Japan than a year ago, according to data obtained from CustomWeather and gathered by Platts.

A total of 19 cities geographically representative of Europe had 26.1% fewer heating degree days in September than in September 2025. The total for the SeptemberSeptember period was 26.1% fewer degree days than the similar period last year and 35.1% fewer than normal.

Eight cities geographically representative of Canada had 48% more degree days in September than in September 2025. The total for the September-September period was 48% more than the similar period last year and 4.7% fewer than normal.

Four cities geographically representative of Japan had 45.9% more degree days in September than in September 2025. The total for the September-September period was 45.9% more than the similar period last year and 27.5% fewer than normal.
