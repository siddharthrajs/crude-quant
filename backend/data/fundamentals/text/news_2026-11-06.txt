October US crude exports edge higher despite soaring freight costs
US crude exports rise to 4.2 million b/d
VLCC freight rates triple in two months
Asian refining margins fall 37% in October

Russian crude exports fall on G7 tankers as Greek operators flee for better earnings
Greek tankers exit Russian routes for profits, security
G7 tankers' market share in Russia hits 7-month low
Non-G7 fleet maintains shipments amid relief talk

US crude exports edged up to 4.2 million barrels/day in October, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea data, despite soaring VLCC freight rates that weighed on arbitrage economics to Asia.
South Korea was the largest named destination for US WTI crude in October, receiving 14.8 million barrels, although that was slightly below 15.6 million barrels in September.
Shipments to the Netherlands fell to 10.1 million barrels from 11 million barrels, while exports to Canada increased to 8.5 million barrels from 7 million barrels.
Medium and heavy crude exports showed a pronounced shift toward Northeast Asian buyers in October.
Japan received about 4.65 million barrels of medium and heavy crude and South Korea took 3.90 million barrels. Germany received 700,350 barrels, though these figures include re-exports of Canadian-origin crude from the US Gulf Coast.
In September, Japan had received about 1.01 million barrels and South Korea 1.42 million barrels, while the October data showed no medium or heavy shipments to China, India or Spain.
Freight surge squeezes Asia economics The October export increase came despite a steep rise in
tanker costs. USGC-China VLCC freight averaged $244.70/mt, compared with $156.19/mt in September and $79.21/mt in August. Rates therefore more than tripled in the space of two months.

Russian crude exports on G7 tankers fell sharply in October as Greek operators were enticed by strong earnings elsewhere and steered away from Black Sea conflicts, according to S&P Global Commodities at Sea and Maritime Intelligence Risk Suite data.
The data shows tankers flagged, owned or operated by companies based in G7 countries and their allies, or insured by Western protection and indemnity clubs, loaded roughly 906,000 barrels per day of Russian crude in October, or 24.2% of total exports.
The share was the lowest in seven months. In September, G7 tankers lifted 1.2 million b/d, or 29.2% of total exports.
Shipments by tanker operators in Greece, the top shipowning nation within G7/EU, more than halved to 8.3 million barrels in October from 17.3 million barrels in September, according to CAS/ MIRS. October's loadings were the lowest since December 2024.
The development came as tanker rates were around their all-time highs in mainstream markets, boosted by larger ship requirements from offshore transfers east of the Strait of Hormuz and high risk premiums during the ongoing US-Iran war.
October's average Suezmax time-charter-equivalent earnings for transporting Russian crude from the Baltic to West Coast India was $46,257.92 per day lower than the Global Suezmax Index, compared with a discount of $23,225.69/day in September, according to Platts assessments for non-scrubber, non-eco ships. Asian refiners eye North African crude to diversify despite softer Dubai structure
Platts Dubai price structure falls to prewar level
Pakistan bags El Sharara crude at Platts Dated Brent plus $20/b
Thailand triples, South Korea doubles North African imports
The recent weakening of the Dubai crude market structure may signal some improvement in sour-crude flows through the Strait of Hormuz, but Asian refiners plan to continue diversifying their feedstock by increasing US crude purchases and actively seeking spot cargoes from North Africa, according to government ministries and industry sources over Oct. 26-Nov. 5.
It is far too early to assume that tanker flows through the Strait of Hormuz will fully normalize based solely on the US midterm election results, feedstock managers at refineries in Negishi, Karachi, Chonburi and Seosan, as well as Pakistani and South Korean government ministries, said over Oct. 26-Nov. 5.
Platts assessed the spread between Cash Dubai and samemonth Dubai futures at 30 cents/b on Nov. 2, the lowest level since minus 30 cents/b on July 17 and below the prewar February monthly average of 92 cents/b. Platts last assessed the spread at $1.46/b on Nov. 5. The spread -- widely known as the Dubai M1-M3 market structure -- is a key component of the monthly official selling price calculations used by major Middle Eastern producers.

Hungary's MOL Q3 output up 2.3% YOY to 94,200 boe/d
Hungarian, Iraqi crude growth offset by lower Azeri oil, CEE gas
Q3 upstream capex up 30%, driven by Croatia offshore
Profits soar on record refinery margins, oil/gas price surge
Hungary's MOL Group reported Nov. 6 hydrocarbon output of 94,200 barrels/day of oil equivalent for the third quarter of 2026, up 2.3% year over year, with growth in crude production in Hungary and Iraqi Kurdistan offset by a drop in Azerbaijani crude output and produced gas volumes.
MOL's third-quarter output was slightly below the company's full-year 2026 guidance of 95,000-97,000 boe/d and brought output for the first nine months to 95,100 b/d. MOL said its hydrocarbon output is estimated to have totaled just 89,600

boe/d in October, primarily due to planned turnarounds in Hungary, Kazakhstan and Pakistan, as well as lower entitlements from Azerbaijan.
MOL's organic capital expenditure totaled $394 million in Q3, about the same as a year earlier and bringing nine-month organic capex to $982 million. MOL said earlier it is targeting organic capex of $1.7 billion in 2026, about the same as it spent last year.
Within total capex, organic upstream investments grew by almost 30% year over year to $115 million in Q3, including $5.6 million spent on exploration -- a three-year low -- and $86.2 million on field development, up 52% year over year. Field development capex remained steady and was highest at the ACG field in Azerbaijan at $34.2 million, spent mostly on offshore projects. Development spending more than doubled to $32.6 million in Croatia, where MOL is carrying out a major offshore well-drilling program.
Downstream capex totaled $92 million in Q3, barely growing from a year earlier, and was focused on crude intake diversification, the building of a product pipeline between MOL's Bratislava and Danube refineries, and repair works at the AV3 distillation unit at Danube, heavily damaged in an October 2025 fire.
Within total Q3 hydrocarbon output, crude production at MOL's fully consolidated operations was 38,600 boe/d, down 3.2% year over year. The drop was led by the ACG field, where MOL's share of output was down 14% to an all-time low 11,300 boe/d as high oil prices reduced MOL's entitlement. According to the terms of the field's production-sharing agreement, MOL's share of the Azerbaijani field's output is not fixed at its 9.57% ownership stake, but calculated using a formula that is, among other variables, tied to crude oil prices.
Output at the Shaikan field in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq also fell 18% year over year, not yet fully recovering after a Q2 shutdown because of the Iran war.
In contrast, crude output in Hungary was up 8.5% to 13,300 boe/d, thanks to production optimization and a low base affected by outages, while crude production at the Pearl joint venture in Iraqi Kurdistan -- less affected by the Iran war -- was up 39% to 8,900 boe/d, thanks to higher seasonal demand.
Gas and downstream results MOL's natural gas output at its fully consolidated operations
was down 3.1% year over year in Q3 at 33,700 boe/d. Hungary and Croatian onshore fields led the drop, down a combined 9.5% to 26,800 boe/d due to natural decline and turnarounds. This was partly offset by Croatian offshore operations, where output recovered by 31% year over year to 2,700 boe/d, driven by new wells and well workovers. Elsewhere, output at MOL's Kazakhstani joint venture, which produces mostly natural gas, was up 58% year over year to 5,700 boe/d, as production continued to ramp up following a mid-2024 launch.

SPAIN DATA: Q3 crude imports fall 2% on year, weighed by Middle East
Crude imports decline to 15.8 million mt
Americas supply 7.94 mil mt in Q3, up 1%
Nigeria becomes Spain's top crude supplier
Spanish crude imports in the third quarter declined 2% year over year to 15.8 million metric tons (1.26 million barrels/day) amid declining Middle East volumes, according to data published Nov. 5 by Spanish reserve corporation CORES.
Spain's refining capacity is 74.7 million mt/year across nine refineries, equivalent to about 18.7 million mt in each quarter, according to CORES.
For September alone, crude imports fell 7% year over year to 4.77 million mt, according to CORES, while year-to-date import volumes fell 1% year over year to 44.9 million mt.
In the third quarter, volumes from the Middle East declined 57% year over year to 779,000 mt, comprising 462,000 mt from Saudi Arabia, down 56%; 132,000 mt from the UAE, up from zero in the yearago quarter; and 185,000 mt from Iraq, down 75% year over year.
Spain's main refinery operator, Repsol, reported a distillation rate of 81% in the period, down 5% year over year, primarily due to an ongoing production issue at the Cartagena refinery, whereas competitor Moeve reported a 100% operating rate for the period. No data was available for the BP-operated refinery at Castellon.
The Americas region was Spain's main source of crude in the third quarter, supplying 7.94 million mt, up 1% year over year, led by 2.43 million mt from US, down 8%; 2.08 million mt from Brazil, up 16%; 1.73 million mt from Mexico, down 6%; and 1.28 million mt from Venezuela, from zero.
Volumes from Africa in the third quarter increased 6% year over year to 5.80 million mt, with volumes from the three largest suppliers

rising. Nigerian volumes increased 25% year over year to 2.52 million mt, making it Spain's largest supplier in the third quarter; Libyan volumes rose 27% year over year to 1.41 million mt and Algerian volumes rose 11% year over year to 1.13 million mt, the data showed.
-- Gianluca Baratti

FACTBOX: Democratic gains, data center backlash set new course for US energy policy
Democrats sweep midterms, win House, Senate
Voters, regulators target data center expansion
Democrats swept the Nov. 3 US midterm elections, winning majorities in the US House of Representatives, US Senate and numerous statehouses in a "blue tsunami" in which energy prices and infrastructure were central.
Surging data center electricity demand collided with voter anxiety over power bills, forcing both parties to address who pays to serve large users. Voters also weighed fossil fuels against renewables, seeing diverging platforms often along party lines.
Democratic gains in Congress, governors' offices and utility commissions could bring tougher oversight of utility rates and spending, new conditions on data center growth and a changed outlook for natural gas demand, renewable procurement and utility regulation.
The following highlights key races and ballot measures relevant to energy markets:
US Senate races Alaska Overview: In a race too close to call Nov. 6, former Democratic
Representative Mary Peltola led Republican Senator Dan Sullivan by a few hundred votes but not enough to avoid the state's ranked choice voting process with 74% of votes counted.
Energy impact: A Sullivan win keeps a leading Senate advocate for Arctic drilling and the Alaska LNG project. A Peltola win, from a Democrat who has tied energy prices to President Donald Trump's policies, would likely make it harder to pass legislation expanding oil and gas development.
Michigan Overview: Democrat Abdul El-Sayed defeated Republican Mike Rogers to hold retiring Senator Gary Peters' seat. Energy impact: El-Sayed campaigned on enforceable conditions for new data centers, including local rate protections. North Carolina Overview: Democrat Roy Cooper, a former governor, defeated Republican Michael Whatley to flip retiring Senator Thom Tillis' seat, the state's first Democratic Senate win since 2008. Energy impact: Cooper, like Whatley, opposed a federal ban on new data centers, but he backed municipalities' right to halt development.

China's WAF imports fell 400,000 b/d YOY in Q3
West African crude differentials have begun to bounce back from a dismal two weeks amid easing freight costs, according to oil traders and pricing data, but sluggish Chinese demand and competition from rival producers remain obstacles to further gains.
Platts, part of S&P Global Energy, assessed Angola's Girassol at an $11.15/barrel discount to Dated Brent on Nov. 6, up $1/b on the day, while Congo's Djeno gained $3.25/b in the week to hit a $27.95/b discount on Nov. 6.
The higher differentials come as freight costs for the West Africa to Asia route have dropped 30% from record levels in early October amid a broad freight market correction, Platts data shows.
Heavier West African barrels, such as Angola's Girassol and the Republic of Congo's Djeno, typically depend on Chinese buyers, while light sweet Nigerian grades like its flagship Bonny Light stream tend to find buyers across the world.
And while the immediate aftermath of the US-Iran war saw a rush to obtain cargoes of West African crude, Angolan and Congolese grades have been pressured in recent weeks by slower Chinese buying, forcing African producers to compete with the Brazil and returning Middle East suppliers for a smaller pool of buyers.
Data from S&P Global Commodities at Sea shows China importing just 600,000 barrels/day of West African crude in the third quarter, down some 400,000 b/d year over year. The trend has continued in October, with imports of just 680,000 b/d, CAS data shows.

The effect has been felt across heavy West African crude differentials, which have plummeted in the past two weeks. Djeno's differential lost $28/b between late September and mid-October, hitting a record discount of $34.60/b on Oct. 19, according to Platts data.
In the same period, Angola's Girassol collapsed from a premium of $4.60/b to Dated Brent to a $15.90/b discount.
By contrast, light sweet Nigerian crudes, which have a broader base of buyers - from the Americas and Europe to Asia and the huge local 700,000 b/d Dangote refinery - have largely retained their value, generally trading at healthy premiums to the benchmark for most of the conflict, even though differentials have dipped in recent weeks.
Platts assessed Nigeria's Forcados at a $1.49/b premium to Dated Brent on Nov. 6, up 59 cents/b on the day.
Freight impact WAF crude traders and analysts said soaring freight rates
were primarily to blame for the pressure on differentials. "Lower WAF differentials are partly due to high shipping costs,
which have come off a bit but remain steep," said Rebeka Foley, a senior analyst at S&P Global Energy CERA.
"This has been dividing regional crude and product markets, leaving barrels unable to clear and weighing on prices in exporting regions (while boosting prices in import-dependent markets)."
Chinese buying has also been affected by government policy this year, with Beijing imposing controls on refined fuel shipments to safeguard domestic supplies, although so-called "teapot" refineries have continued to buy, according to Platts reporting.
In addition, the Dated Brent forward structure has disadvantaged long-haul arbitrage this year, with crude markets in strong backwardation, penalizing cargoes that spend several weeks at sea.
Rates fall However, the outlook appears to be changing for WAF crudes,
according to traders, with freight rates softening in recent days. The cost of shipping a VLCC crude cargo from West Africa
to the Far East fell from a record high of $269/mt on Oct. 9 to $169.70/mt on Nov. 6, according to Platts.

Iraqi crude exports rise 19% in October as more oil sails through Hormuz
Exports total 3.5 million b/d
Ceyhan pipeline hits highest flow since 2022
Iran opens special routes for Iraqi vessels
Iraqi crude exports rose 19% month over month in October, as more Iraqi oil passed through the Strait of Hormuz, according to data from state oil marketer SOMO, obtained exclusively by Platts Nov. 5.
The data showed that exports hit 3.543 million barrels/day in October, up 577,000 b/d from the September total of 2.966 million b/d.
The October volume brought Iraq's crude exports to just 1% below the pre-conflict February level of 3.57 million b/d, before disruptions from the Middle East war and shipping constraints.
October exports included 3.121 million b/d from the southern Gulf terminals, up 550,000 b/d month over month. This was up from 2.571 million b/d in September, and approaching the February rate of 3.335 million b/d.
The substantial increase in Iraqi exports from its Gulf BOT terminals in September and October is due to the increased number of tankers allowed to pass through the Strait of Hormuz to lift Iraqi oil.
Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi stated in an interview with the Iraqi state channel, Oct. 20, that Iran has opened "special routes" for Iraqi ships through the Strait of Hormuz to help Iraq mitigate the cost of the ongoing crisis in the waterway. In March, Iraq said the country was in talks with Iran to allow some tankers carrying Iraqi crude to pass out of the Persian Gulf through Hormuz.
That arrangement, as well as the incentive of around $30/b discount on the FOB price, has driven that increase in exports from the terminals.
October Basrah Gulf terminal exports consisted of 2.028

million b/d of Basrah Medium crude and 1.093 million b/d of Basrah Heavy crude. This compares to 1.968 million b/d and 603,000 b/d in September, respectively, according to SOMO data.
Exports remain just below February levels of 2.120 million b/d of Basrah Medium crude and 1.215 million b/d of Basrah Heavy crude.
Basrah Medium has a nominal API gravity of 29 degrees, while Basrah Heavy carries a gravity of 23-24 API.
Iraq also substantially increased exports via Ceyhan in October, the data showed. Ceyhan exports, which consisted of oil from Kirkuk and the Kurdistan region, as well as Basrah oil, rose from 254,000 b/d in September to 422,000 b/d in October, according to the SOMO data.
This Ceyhan export rate is the highest since exports resumed in September 2025 and since late 2022, before the shutdown of the ITP in March 2023, according to the data.
The SOMO data did not provide a breakdown of the grades currently comprising exports via Ceyhan, but sources close to SOMO informed Platts, on condition of anonymity, that they consisted of approximately 50,000 b/d of Kirkuk crude, 182,000 b/d of KRG crude, and 190,000 b/d of Basrah crude.

BUNKERWORLD: Marine fuel prices fall to two-month low on weak crude, mixed demand
VLSFO, HSFO indices correct sharply
Singapore inventories rise amid export decline
Australia completes first green ammonia bunkering
Global marine fuel prices have fallen to their lowest in about two months, pressured by weaker crude futures and a mixed demand picture across the main trading hubs.
The Platts Bunkerworld Marine Fuel 0.5% Sulfur Index ended the week to Nov. 5 at $843/metric ton, the lowest since Sept. 10 and down $20/mt day over day, $91/mt week over week and $20/ mt month over month.

The Platts BW380 Index, which represents value for 3.5% sulfur fuel oil, ended the week at $674/mt, the lowest since Sept. 23. This was down $37/mt day over day, $131/mt week over week and $57/mt month over month.
The Hi-5 spread, the difference between 0.5%S and 3.5%S fuel oil, widened to $169.43/mt Nov. 5, its highest since Sept. 23.
Crude futures weakened significantly following the Democratic Party's sweeping US midterm election victory, which raised expectations of accelerated efforts to end the war in the Middle East.
Democratic US Senate minority leader Chuck Schumer and House Democratic minority leader Hakeem Jeffries have called on the Trump administration to end the Middle East war.
In Singapore, the world's largest marine refueling port, commercial inventories of heavy distillates rose 2.4% in the week ended Nov. 4 to a six-week high of 19.14 million barrels, according to Enterprise Singapore data.
The stock build came as Singapore's fuel oil exports fell 29% from the preceding week to about 328,265 mt in the week to Nov. 4, with shipments to China dropping for a second straight week.
Several market participants told Platts recently that Middle East bunker demand should improve further in November as prices ease following increased activity at key UAE bunkering hubs in October.
In terms of alternative fuels, Australia has completed its first green ammonia bunkering of a dry bulk carrier in Hedland, marking another step toward the use of ammonia as a loweremission marine fuel.
West of Suez In the Northwest European bunker market, very low sulfur fuel
oil availability was sufficient to cover requirements, while high sulfur fuel oil availability remained comparatively tight across the Amsterdam-Rotterdam-Antwerp hub.
Spot trading in North America remained thin recently as the market continued to weigh the post-election landscape and an uncertain outlook for the Middle East.
The BW Indexes are daily-weighted indexes composed of price assessments from 20 key bunkering ports. To achieve a representative geographical spread, the ports were selected based on their size and geographical importance.
The BW 0.5% Sulfur Index ports are Hong Kong, South Korea, Shanghai, Singapore, Japan, Las Palmas, Durban, Fujairah, Gibraltar, Piraeus, Rotterdam, St. Petersburg, Houston, Los Angeles, New York, Balboa and Santos.
The BW380 Index ports are Busan, Canary Islands, Colombo, Durban, Fujairah, Gibraltar, Hong Kong, Houston, Los Angeles, New York, Offshore Nigeria, Panama Canal, Piraeus, Rotterdam, Santos, Shanghai, Singapore, St. Petersburg, Suez and Tokyo.

Energy Transition
IEA sees renewable transport energy demand rising about 60% by 2031
EVs drive almost half of renewable growth
Biofuel mandates accelerate amid oil disruptions
The International Energy Agency forecast Nov. 6 that global renewable energy consumption in transport will surge by almost two-thirds over the next six years, with Middle East conflictdriven oil market disruptions pushing governments to accelerate biofuel mandates and electrification policies.
Renewable energy use in transport is projected to rise to 9 exajoules by 2031, up from 5.5 EJ in 2025, accounting for 6% of global transport energy demand, according to the IEA's forecast in its Renewables 2026 report.
The disruption to oil supplies, prices and key shipping routes caused by the ongoing US-Israel war with Iran is acting as a catalyst, with governments in North America, South America and Asia Pacific accelerating new and previously announced liquid biofuel mandates to reduce exposure to oil market volatility and improve transport fuel affordability, according to the IEA.
Almost half of the increase in renewable energy in transport will be driven by greater deployment of electric vehicles.
China is the dominant regional driver, with continued deployment of light-duty passenger EVs and medium- and heavyduty electric trucks, as well as the development of domestic battery supply chains. Europe follows, with increasing availability of electric vehicles across passenger and commercial segments, according to the energy agency.
About 35% of the growth is expected to come from broader consumption of liquid biofuels in road transport, with blending mandates and rising transport fuel consumption underpinning the expansion. An additional 10% will be attributed to liquid biofuels in marine and aviation transport, IEA added.
Growth in biofuel consumption in both marine and aviation sectors will be driven by European regulations, including the European Union's long-term emissions-reduction targets under the FuelEU Maritime regulations and the EU and UK's long-term sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) mandates. The ReFuelEU Aviation regulations require a 6% SAF blend in jet fuel by 2030, while the UK's SAF mandate requires 10% SAF blending by 2030.
Biomethane, renewable hydrogen and hydrogen-based fuels are expected to account for the remaining 10% of renewable energy growth in transport, according to the IEA.
"The widespread adoption of EVs over the next decade is, in a way, less a technology or economics question than a political one," Jeff Meyer, director of mobility and energy research at S&P Global Energy CERA, wrote in a note Oct. 14.
Intensifying competition from Chinese automakers may be a more enduring "force of change" than fuel prices, which, like all commodities, will have their ups and downs, according to CERA.
