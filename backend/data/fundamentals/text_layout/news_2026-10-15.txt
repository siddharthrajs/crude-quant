US crude stocks fall to multi-month low
on Gulf Coast demand

Inventories drop 8.5 million barrels to 415.6 million
Gulf Coast supplies reach July 31 levels
Refinery utilization climbs to 93.5% capacity

    US crude oil stocks fell to multi-month lows during the week to
Oct. 9, US Energy Information Administration data showed Oct. 15,
as global demand continued to pull US Gulf Coast oil supplies.

    US commercial crude stocks declined 8.5 million barrels over
the week to Oct. 9 to 415.6 million barrels, a record low not seen
since July 31 when inventories were at 406.9 million barrels, the
data shows.

    The draw put stocks 2.45% below the seasonal five-year
average, marking the second time they have fallen below normal
since early August.

Iranian oil in floating storage
    Despite the decline in Southeast Asian inventories, the overall

volume of Iranian crude and condensate held in floating storage
across the tracked Southeast Asian, Far Eastern and Middle East
Gulf waters was virtually unchanged month over month, the CAS
data showed.

    Combined Iranian floating storage in the regions stood at
70.89 million barrels end-September, compared with 70.95
million barrels end-August. However, the figure was 43.1%
above the 49.52 million barrels recorded on July 14, 37.3%
above the April 13 level and 46.6% higher year over year,
CAS said.
